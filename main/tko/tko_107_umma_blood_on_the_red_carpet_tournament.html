TKO Issue: #107
Date: June 2014
Franchise: UMMA
Event: Blood on the Red Carpet Tournament
Location: Scotiabank Place (Ottawa, Ontario, Canada)

Team Competition

Eight championships were decided here in Ottawa, with sixteen fighters reaching the final. Of those sixteen, six were RSI women, with two bringing home the hardware. To say I'm proud would be an unprecedented understatement. RSI has had a number of memorable moments in our 18+ months of operation. But none exceeds what happened here at this tournament. And yet, with all the happiness, there was heartache.

The saga of Stephanie McMahon is no secret. She's been having a tough time of it, but she seemed poised to start turning things around. A good run at this tournament would definitely go a long way to getting her back on track. And her run was better than good, it was superb... fighting her way through the likes of top SCQ contender Uma Thurman and ESL champion Gemma Atkinson. In the final, though, she just didn't seem to have it... her opponent taking it to her right from the opening bell. Steph struggled to gather herself, desperately trying to get some space. But, her tormentor just kept coming, and when Steph collapsed to the canvas after a brutal kick to the midsection, I knew her storybook turnaround had just come to an abrupt and painful halt. Clutching her side to protect her freshly broken ribs, the hurt in Steph's eyes reflected much more than physical pain. No doubt she was mourning the opportunity that slipped away, and lamenting the long recovery that now lay before her. A lesser fighter at this stage of her career might think about retirement. But Steph won't do that. Because I won't let her. Not after what I saw from her here.

I'm not going to spend too much time on this, for a number of reasons. Does this suck? Yep. Is it unfair? Yep. Will she come back from it? You bet your ass. RSI is committed to Steph in every possible way. We will get her healthy. We will get her ready. Steph's been a part of the foundation from the beginning, and never more so than now. We won't let her down.

By the way, if you're wondering why I'm rushing through Steph's story, it has a lot to do with her opponent in that final... and not wanting to detract from what is easily the most stunning achievement of Torrie Wilson's tumultuous career.

That's right... not Kim Kardashian. Not Maria Sharapova. It was RSI's own Torrie Wilson who stormed her way up from the bottom half of the bracket to take the Super Heavyweight crown here in Ottawa. And, trust me, she did not take the easy road. Her path took her through not only Stephanie, but also top contender Jennifer Ellison and even the winningest fighter in the weight class, Sharapova. Torrie wasn't just winning these fights, either... she was tearing it up... taking each victory in a decisive, commanding style. No doubt, Torrie made a statement at this event, and I'm certain the rest of the UMMA took notice.

The way it ended was unfortunate, though... Torrie's eyes watering as she knelt down beside her friend and training partner in the center of the octagon. Steph pushing her away, telling her to go enjoy her moment. But, it was clear Torrie wasn't really enjoying anything. In fact, I think her newly won championship was just about the furthest thing from her mind.

Things didn't get much better at the post event conference. The new champion looked well enough as she entered the room, but, when she went to speak, no words came out. Soon, the tears started to flow again, and Torrie seemed powerless to get them under control. After a few moments, I'd seen enough. But, as I rose to head up front and get her, there was a commotion at the back of the room.

Anybody care to take a guess?

That's right, Stephanie had just walked in, the protective vest clearly visibly under her Rampage Sports t-shirt. At the sight of her friend, Torrie rushed over, but Steph put her hand out and gave the universal "easy does it" signal as she gestured toward her tender ribs. Torrie approached gingerly, but the two soon found themselves face to face. I heard Torrie say she was sorry, but Steph shook her off. Then, they leaned into each other... their foreheads touching... and shared a private moment. I couldn't hear a word that was said, which is exactly as it should have been. When they were finished, Steph gestured toward the microphone, but Torrie shook her head. Refusing to let her friend give up her moment, Stephanie picked up the microphone, and, looking back at Torrie, announced, "Ladies and gentlemen... your super heavyweight champion... Torrie Wilson!" As the assembled media broke into applause, Torrie had little choice but to return to the interview table. Steph found herself a chair and stayed for the duration, and having her there made all the difference. Torrie slowly started to enjoy her accomplishment. By the time it was over, she was smiling broadly and the excitement in her voice was clear.

Nice save, McMahon.

Truth is, there's nothing for Torrie to feel bad about. Shit happens, and no one knows that better than Stephanie. She'll recover, and she'll be back out there. And, when she does, she'll have some new competition, as Torrie's success in this tournament has vaulted her up the SCQ super heavyweight rankings. I suspect there will be a chance for Steph to even the score with her friend in the not too distant future.

In the meantime, Torrie, the disastrous outcome takes nothing away from your big win. I knew you had this in you. When you signed with RSI, you told me you simply weren't getting the results you felt capable of. You promised me nothing more than hard work and dedication, and I promised you every resource we had available. Your performances since then have been absolutely outstanding. And, now, you have a shiny new belt to show for your efforts. So wipe those tears away and enjoy your win while you can, because your training is about to get a helluva lot more intense. You're playing with the big girls, now.

Talk about taking the hard road. Both Tiffany Mulheron and Emma Watson faced off against some tough talent in this tournament. For Tiffany, that included a picture perfect victory over SCQ champion Portia de Rossi. No easy task, but Emma did her one better, grinding out a hard earned victory over ESL champion Nadine Coyle, followed by a dominating win over top SCQ contender Grace Park. And so it was that, once again, two RSI fighters found themselves facing off for the belt.

Early on, this one looked like it was going to go Emma's way. But, Tiffany proved her toughness, hanging on through the early onslaught. And that turned out to be the key, as the effects of her two previous matches seemed to catch up with Emma, allowing Tiff to gain control. Emma, being Emma, held on tenaciously... probably much longer than she should have. But giving in is just not in her character. And hey... she damn near pulled it off, as she was within 15 seconds of surviving the round before the ref stepped in to end things.

There's nothing for Emma to be ashamed of here. She was fantastic in this tournament, especially against Coyle, who never gives an inch against anyone. And how unlucky can you be, to have to vie for the championship against the one fighter who knows you, inside and out? Before the title fight even started, I knew it was going to come down to who had more left in the tank. Tonight, that fighter was Tiffany.

Afterward, Tiff wasn't shy about ribbing her teammate. "Ever since I got here, I've been hearing about how good Emma is," she said with a playful smile. "She didn't seem that tough, to me." Still, if you looked closely, you could see that something was off with Tiffany. With the championship belt lying on the table in front of her, she looked wistful and even a little depressed. That was until Emma slipped in behind her, that is. The two shared a warm embrace, and Emma joined Tiff for the remainder of the Q&A. And, though I'm sure she was disappointed, Emma was graceful about the loss. "If I had to lose, this is definitely the way I would want it to happen," said the young actress. "I've seen the work that Tiffany puts in, and I know how good she is. She deserves this, and I'm thrilled for her." Then, that mischievous smile appeared as she continued. "Besides... I've never won one of these tournament titles," she said, lightly touching Tiffany's new belt. "So you can bet your ass I'll be coming for you now, blondie." Oh, Emma! What happened to the graceful approach?

Life is just never easy for Emily VanCamp. Constantly tormented by nemesis Irina Sheik, Em has proven herself to be among the best there is time and time again. She and Irina have traded the ESL back and forth a few times now, but VanCamp has yet to get her hands on a tournament belt. But, that seemed destined to change here in Ottawa. Destiny allowed Em to open up with a trio of quick and decisive victories. Destiny had Emily at her absolute best in a commanding victory over Koda Kumi. And, destiny had Em way ahead and in control in the final. Then, destiny turned around and punched her right in the face.

Oh, no... wait. That was Ali Larter.

It just didn't seem real. Em looked fantastic out there, spending the opening round picking apart the SCQ champion with precise and brutal strikes while deftly smothering Larter's attempts to respond. She opened the second with a tentative kick, and then looked to move in for the kill. But, Larter timed it perfectly, looping a devastating left out and around Emily's guard. Boom... just like that, it was all over.

A little luck, a little desperation and a healthy dose of veteran guile. Say whatever you want... Larter knew what she needed to do, and she made it happen. She knew Em was beating her, and she knew she needed something big. Though every measurable statistic said Emily was the better fighter, there is absolutely no way to argue with a knockout. And Em didn't even try. "That was... that was fantastic, really," said the ESL champion with a measure of respect. In fact, Em made it sound like she's found a kindred spirit in Ali. "I look at Ali, and I think we have a lot in common," said the Captain America actress. "It's never come easy for either of us. We have to fight for it. I had her on the ropes, but Ali knows her way around the cage. She took a big chance, and it paid off. You have to respect that."

I have no shame in admitting that Emily is taking this a lot better I am. I mean, I may have to respect Ali's courage and toughness, but I don't have to be happy about it, do I?

The last time we had one of these tournaments, Jennifer Stano made history when she eliminated the practically unbeatable Doutzen Kroes. Though the win was earth-shattering, the joy didn't last long, as Stano lost to teammate Britney Spears in the semi-final round. But, just for good measure, Jen followed up with yet another victory over the UMMA's greatest fighter at the next ESL event. Suddenly, she was all anyone could talk about. So, coming into this event, no one knew what to expect. Could Jennifer go deeper into this tournament? Could she somehow overcome Kroes again?

Yes and no. In that order.

Once again, Stano showed skill and intelligence far beyond her experience. She simply ate up the competition in the opening rounds, including a blowout win over the far more experienced Denise Richards. But then, things got a little more interesting, as Stano faced off against SCQ champion Jennifer Connelly. Though not quite on the level of Kroes, Connelly is among the UMMA elite. And, she proved a tough test for Stano right from the opening bell, as she scored an immediate takedown. The mat is not a place where Stano likes to be, which is exactly why Connelly put her there. Stano survived long enough for the referee to stand things up, but poor technique had her back on the mat within a minute. It seemed like Stano spent the entire first round trying to survive. But, god-damn... she did it. And, in the second round, she managed to stay on her feet and utilize her prolific striking game. Stano has the ability to simply overwhelm most fighters with her strikes. Jennifer Connelly, though, is not one of those fighters. The two waged war over the final two rounds, with Stano seeming to score more often and do more damage. The judges agreed, handing RSI's young phenom yet another unbelievable victory.

So, after a dominating semi-final win over Lindsay Ellingson, it was time for the rematch everyone was hoping for. Once again, Jen found herself face to face with the previously indomitable Doutzen Kroes. Could the young fighter pull off a third straight win over the legend? Not quite... but she gave it everything she had. The two stood toe-to-toe, exchanging vicious blows for almost the entirety of the three round matchup. However, this time around, Kroes got ahead early and stayed ahead. There would be no turn-around. No dramatic knockout. Stano fought until the bitter end, which came when the referee determined that Jen's eye was too swollen to continue. I'm not overly thrilled with the stoppage, but I doubt it would have mattered much. Kroes was ahead on the cards, and Jen had little hope of pulling off a miracle in the less than two minutes that remained.

Now, look... I know this was a bit of a disappointment after the stunning victories Jen pulled off the last two times. But, let's be realistic... a fighter with only 13 total fights coming into this tournament made it all the way to the championship fight. And she did it by going through three fighters who have considerably more experience... most notable Connelly, who came in with nearly 80 fights to her credit. By any measure, that's a tremendous success. I'm damn proud of Jen, and I'm not ashamed to admit she's showing me far more than I'd expected. I knew she'd be great, but I still managed to underestimate her, somehow.

So, all in all, a great event for RSI. And, I'd be remiss not to point out another great run by Catherine Bell. Sure, she got destroyed by Louise Glover. Again. But, look around... who hasn't? We'll keep working on it, and I'm still confident she'll find a way. There were a few other disappointments for RSI here in Ottawa, but... oh, gee... look at the time.

It's back to SCQ for our next event. That rib injury will keep Steph on the sideline, and UMMA safety regulations will keep Catherine and Kelly Hu out of action, as well. But, there'll still be plenty going on, as Torrie looks to keep things going against Abi Titmuss. Also, Roselyn Sanchez will get her shot against Emily's new nemesis, Ali Larter. And Marzia Prince will be in for tough night, as she faces off against veteran Famke Janssen. I'll be taking a nice long vacation (go ahead... guess where I'm going), so I don't know how much reporting I'll be able to do from the road. Danni's running the show while I'm gone, with a little help from the Britney and Elisha. Should be... interesting. Take care.
