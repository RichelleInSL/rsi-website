TKO Issue: #62
Date: February 2013
Franchise: I Hate You (later renamed to Elite Starlet League)
Event: Clinic for the Insane
Location: Rod Laver Arena (Melbourne, Australia)

You Win Some, You Lose Some

Clinic was certainly a mixed bag of emotions for the RSI group. From the disappointment of seeing Britney Spears lose to Laura Vandervoort once more, to the thrill of watching Elisha Cuthbert and Kirsten Dunst fight it out tooth and nail, there certainly is a lot to talk about. We'll start at the bottom, and work our way up.

First of all, there isn't really anything positive to take away from Erica Rose Campbell's performance tonight. The game plan was for her to avoid the dangerous feet of Serinda Swan and get the fight to the ground. She failed to accomplish either of those things, and that is simply not acceptable. Serinda has certainly shown she is an incredibly talented fighter, but she is still a rookie. And being totally torn apart by a rookie is not something that should happen to someone of Erica's experience. Erica was being tended to by the RSI medical staff, so she was unavailable for comment after the fight. But, rest assured, we'll be talking to her.

Next, we have Britney's championship fight. Certainly, this match shows that she has made some big strides under RSI's direction. Given her recent history against Laura, just getting the fight to the cards was quite an accomplishment. But, we'd be lying if we said we weren't disappointed. When asked for comment, Britney simply stated, "I expected to be better than that." We agree. Laura still dominated the match, while Britney mostly just showed a newfound ability to survive in there. It's clearly not enough, but we're hopeful she can continue to improve with further training.

When she came to us, Michelle Marsh's typical fight strategy was to simply slug it out with her opponent, and her results were expectedly mixed. Certainly, she has displayed the power needed to produce some spectacular knockouts, but she has also found herself staring up glassy-eyed into the lights far too often. The RSI team felt Michelle really needed to develop her ground skills, and she was very receptive to what they had to say. The result was a beautiful performance against Ivanka Trump, tonight. Credit to Trump for hanging in there as long as she could, but Michelle used her strength and conditioning to really wear her opponent down. "I really felt going in that I had a solid game plan and the skills to pull it off," Marsh said afterward. "It worked out great. I felt like I was in control throughout the entire fight." When asked specifically about her finishing technique, Michelle was very honest. "I've never been able to control a match that way before. I usually just concentrate on getting the win. But, the fans here tonight were awesome, and I know what they like. I knew Ivanka was done, and I really wanted to break her, ya know? Show that I have what it takes to win consistently. I'm just sorry the ref stopped it before I got her to cry. I don't know why, but I just wanted to hear her beg me to stop. I'm not usually like that. I don't know what came over me, but, to be honest, I kinda liked it." A little confidence is a very dangerous thing, folks.

Coming into tonight, Danielle Lloyd had been seriously considering walking away from the octagon. It wasn't just losing 7 out of 10, but the way she was being totally woman-handled in the cage. Her opponent, Harumi Nemoto, had won 7 of her last 10, including a tremendous knockout victory over Elize Du Toit in her last outing. Clearly, there was a lot riding on this one for Danielle. Danielle has tremendous kickboxing skills, though, and her new RSI team certainly noticed. Strenuous training and game planning have honed her feet into lethal weapons, and she used them to literally chop Harumi down, tonight. "My coaches worked hard, training me to keep my distance and work my opponent's legs and body with kicks," a clearly relieved Lloyd said after the fight. "It worked out perfectly, but I still feel like I have work to do. My clinch and ground games are not where they need to be, yet. I think we'll get there, though." Apparently, we need to take some of Michelle's excess confidence and give it to Danielle. We'll see what we can do.

Last but not least, Elisha Cuthbert clearly earns the fight of the night for the RSI group. Blessed with blazing speed and tremendous power, she is clearly a gifted fighter. However, with only 4 wins in her last 10 fights, she wasn't getting the results you'd expect from such skills. Well, that all changed tonight. Not that Elisha dominated the fight. In fact, the opposite was true. Kirsten Dunst is a formidable opponent, and this fight was just tremendous to watch. Kirsten had Elisha in trouble a few times, but Elisha kept her cool and used her defensive skills to keep herself in the fight. That's the mark of a great fighter. It's one thing to outclass a lesser opponent. But, when you're opponent tests you, can you show you're up to it? Kirsten tested Elisha repeatedly tonight, and Elisha answered every time. She stayed with it, weathering everything Kirsten threw at her until she found her opening. And, when that opening came, she exploited it perfectly and attacked without mercy. Afterward, she made no excuses. "It's unfortunate the fight ended the way it did, with the injury and all. But my job is to keep going until the ref pulls me off. That's what I'm trained to do."

So, in the end, there's a lot for the RSI crew to celebrate tonight, but there's also a lot of work to be done. Speaking of which, RSI fighters are facing some tough, tough matches coming up at Ultimate Force. Aly Michalka, Ashley Tisdale, Julianne Hough and Rhian Sugden have all shown that have what it takes to compete at this level. Now, it's time to show they can do better than just competing. Krystal Forscutt, meanwhile, has shown flashes of talent in what has thus far been an unsuccessful career. We'll see if RSI has helped her turn that talent into victory.
