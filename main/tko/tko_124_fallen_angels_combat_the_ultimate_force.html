TKO Issue: #124
Date: April 2015
Franchise: Fallen Angels Combat
Event: The Ultimate Force
Location: American Airlines Center (Dallas, TX)

Her Turn

If Julianne Hough and Vicki Blows didn't spend so much time trying to smash the crap out of each other, you might get the impression they were friends. After all, they sure do seem to know how to share.

Way back... when Jules won her first divisional title... it was Vikki she took it from. When next the two met, Vikki took it back. The FAC event after that, Jules reclaimed it. Last time they met, Vikki took it back again.

Do I actually have to tell you who won tonight?

After injury put this rivalry on ice for a while, the women finally got back in there with the title on the line. And Jules left no doubt that tonight was not going to be the night the pattern was going to change. She tore into Vikki right from the opening bell... making use of a few unorthodox moves in the process. Normally, I'd take issue with that, but Vikki is well known for fighting the same way. So, it might not have been the cleanest fight, but it was par for the course for these two.

When it was over, Julianne was asked to reflect about the years of back and forth between she and her rival. "I think it's about hunger," claimed the blonde dancer and actress. "When you have the belt... no matter how hard you try, you just can't have that same hunger you get when you don't. That's what drives you." So, does Jules have a plan for next time? "I'm thinking of maybe giving the belt to someone... having them hide it from me until I defend it. Maybe that'll do the trick."

Who am I to argue with that logic?

RSI has a number of women I refer to as "cyclical champions." The round and round between Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik is well known. More recently, the rivalry between Tiffany Mulheron and Nadine Coyle has taken on that same sort of feel. But, Jules and Vikki is the one that started it all. On top of that, Jules was the first champion who actually grew out of the RSI program. So, while things have been really good for us recently, we all know it can't last. But, I think Julianne can. I truly believe she has a long and successful career in front of her.

Some things never change, huh? Alyson Michalka and India Reynolds are known for the defense-be-damned, all out brawls they've had in the past. Though it's been a while since the two have gotten together, they picked up right where they left off here in Texas. Most of the time, the winner is first one to land a solid hit on her opponent. And Aly won that race tonight, in a big way. She forced the clinch right from the outset, and the move seemed to take Reynolds completely by surprise. India tried to thrash her way way free, but Aly wasn't having it... completely destroying her opponent with vicious knees and combinations until she collapsed to the canvas.

The first question Aly was hit with after the event had to do with whether or not she felt any remorse about the way she had torn India apart. "Absolutely not," she answered, stunned that the question was even asked. "There's no mercy in that cage. If things went the other way, she'd have done the same thing to me. Hell... she HAS done that to me. That's what we're trained to do." She was then asked if she was surprised about how easily she'd won, tonight. "Not really," Michalka said. "This is the way it seems to go with us. It's usually all or nothing. Tonight, I got it all, and she got nothing." When the next reporter seemed to ask the same question phrased a little differently, Aly decided enough was enough. "Look, I don't know what you guys are driving at. If you're hoping I'm going to say something about being so much better than India, you can forget it. I beat her to the punch tonight. I had a plan, and I made it work, and I'll credit for that. But, I'm not going to trash her. She'll be back. She ALWAYS comes back."

Couldn't have said it better, myself.

The rematch between Emma Watson and Xenia Deli ended the same way as the first, but it took a heck of a lot longer this time. Though Emma was in complete control throughout, Xenia hung tough... pushing things into the third round. Unfortunately, her reward for that toughness was an injury that's going to force her to the sidelines for a little while. Trust me, we at RSI know how that goes. Just ask Ashley Tisdale. However, Xenia's "injury" was nothing more than swelling... enough to stop the fight, perhaps, but certainly not anything that should force her out of action. I think referee Lauren Budd had good reason to stop this one, but she should have called it a TKO. I think Xenia would even agree. But, I think we can all agree that declaring an injury that's going to cost the supermodel time away from the cage was a step too far. And no one took greater exception to it than her opponent.

"That was outrageous," Emma declared in the post-fight press conference. "If the ref thought it was time to stop the fight, then just do that. Say she's not defending herself... whatever. I don't think anyone would have questioned it. But, this was wrong." Watson went further, recognizing the effort Xenia put into tonight's fight. "I know heart when I see it, and she has tons of it. If her skills ever catch up to her heart... that's going to be a bad day for me. She showed a lot of toughness, and it's a shame she was punished for it."

I have to second Emma's opinion on this. We've all been bitten by the UMMA's injury rules, but this is really something different. Swelling as an injury? That's insane. If the ILF wants to appeal Xenia's injury suspension, I'd happily support them on it. As long as she checks out medically, she deserves the chance to fight again when the time comes.

Oddly enough, Emma was something of a factor in Shelby Chesnes's fight tonight, as well. Though it's been years since she's seen the inside of a cage, Roberta Murgo's last fight was a one-sided submission victory over a very new Emma. Knowing that your opponent once defeated one of your most successful teammates... not to mention the champion of your division... could not have been a comforting thought for Shelby. Add to that the fact that Shelbs has been having a rough time of it herself, and you can certainly understand why I had some questions about her mental state going into this one.

Well, regardless of how she felt beforehand, I'm sure she feels better, now.

Shelby was focused, determined and merciless in the cage tonight, completely spoiling Murgo's return. She came flying out at the bell, scoring with a flurry of punches Roberta didn't seem prepared for. From that point forward, Chesnes never looked back.

Shelby made no apologies for the take-no-prisoners approach she took tonight. "I needed the win," she said afterward. "My career has been very up and down, so far. Just when I thought things were starting to go my way, I had a couple of tough losses. I needed to get things turned around, and I was willing to do whatever I had to do get this win."

I'm sure she meant whatever she had to within the rules, right?

Well, look who learned a thing or two. In her last fight, Dianna Dahlgren fell in sudden fashion to an acrobatic submission move by Lacey Banghard. As they came together tonight for the rematch, Dianna seemed determined not to let it happen again. Lacey came with one takedown attempt after another, and Dianna stuffed almost all of them. When Banghard was able to grapple her to the mat, Dahlgren worked patiently and intelligently to keep herself out of danger and get back on her feet. And when they were standing, Dianna was basically unstoppable. Though Lacey had her moments, RSI's youngest heavyweight stayed calm and worked her way out of every bad spot she got caught in. In the end, the judges really had no choice but to call this one for Dianna.

"I've had some tough fights recently, and things have not been going my way," admitted the fitness star afterward. "Tonight was big, for me. I never really lost faith in myself, but it's nice to see the results I want from the effort I put in." As for Lacey, Dianna was suitably impressed. "She's tough to really measure," she said. "She can kill you standing up or on the mat... striking or submissions. You have to really stay focused to keep ahead of her."

I don't think you can really be any more focused than Dianna was tonight. Nice win, Miss Supercross.

Brittany Tacy stepped into the cage to face off against Joss Stone tonight, and she seemed to relish the challenge her more experienced opponent presented for her. Unfortunately, she then promptly got her ass whipped.

Losing is a part of the game, but I know from experience how it feels when a stand up fighter gets beaten at her own game. You fall to a sneaky submission. Hey, them's the breaks. But, when you get the kind of fight you want but still get destroyed... that's hard to swallow. The image of your opponent standing over you while you lie bloodied on the canvas is not something you get over quickly.

Don't believe me? Just ask Brittany.

"I guess I proved how well I can take a beating," said a bitter Tacy afterward. "Any time Joss needs a life size punching bag to work with... hey, I'm available." Not enough? Wait, there's more. "Does she like pounding on blonds?" the young fighter added sullenly. "Maybe I can make some fantasies come true for her."

Does that sound like the words of a young fighter with a winning record and victories over some quality opponents? Nope. It sounds like a fighter who's shopping around for a bridge to jump off of. Worry not. We won't let that happen, nor will we allow her to wallow in self pity for very long. But, for the moment, I'm just staying out of her way.

More submission problems for the women of RSI, as both Jennifer Lawrence and Sabine Jemeljanova were taken out quickly and quietly by tough opponents. This is something that's always been a bit of a problem for us, but it's really getting bad now that some of the newer fighters are moving up the ranks. As Steph said last time, good submission defense comes with experience. Unfortunately, there seems to be quite a bit of painful humiliation in store for these women in the meantime.

One more note before I go... I don't often hand out accolades to other stables, but what happened here in Dallas deserves a mention. With fourteen fights on the card, the Bad Ass Bunnies had their hands full. And I'll be damned if they didn't handle it all. Fourteen fights, fourteen wins... you don't see that too often. So, for those who haven't been paying attention, now you know... those Bunnies aren't screwing around.

Well, it's team time again. This is usually the portion of our show where I turn the proceedings over to Frick, Frack and... I don't have a name for Aly, yet. But, that's not going to happen today, because the ladies are mad at me. Between injury substitutions and trying to deal with mismatch problems, I've been asserting my authority more than usual for this event. The team leaders... and a few of the team members... don't like that, and I don't blame them. But, I'm doing it anyway because it's the right thing to do. I'm not sure what all of the changes will be, yet, but I can tell you one of them. Amanda Seyfried should be facing Candice Swanepoel in this upcoming event. If the thought of that matchup gets you excited... well, I'm right there with you. But, a small cut Amanda suffered in her last outing resulted in an injury stoppage. That means she's not available to represent her team. The only substitute I have available is young Anna Kendrick. Now, I love Anna to death. I even love her crazy, no filter approach to life. But, I'm not going to send her out to get the crap kicked out of her by Swanepoel. I'm sure she'd like the shot, but there's just no way I can let that happen. No... there's only one welterweight on the RSI roster capable of dealing with Candice, and her name is Danneel Harris. That's right... Harris versus Swanepoel. That help you get over the disappointment of Amanda's absence? I thought it might.

On top of that, Catherine Bell will face off against the legendary Adriana Lima. And speaking of legendary, how about Britney Spears against the practically flawless Doutzen Kroes? Britney didn't fair too well the last time this pair got together, so you can bet she'll be looking to put on a better show this time around. Hmmm... did I leave anything out? Oh, yeah... how about RSI's youngest champion, Emma Watson, against the unflappable Rachel McAdams? In a night loaded with awesome matchups, I think that one may turn out to be the best of them all.

Get out there and get your tickets, now. If you already have plans, cancel them. A night like this isn't likely to ever happen again.
