TKO Issue: #128
Date: June 2015
Franchise: Diamond Edge Fighters
Event: Never Again
Location: Blue Cross Arena (Rochester, NY)

Making Her Mark

Danneel Harris is widely known for her humility. She does not ridicule her opponents. She does not trash-talk. She is one of the best fighters the UMMA has on it's roster, but you'll never hear her say that. In fact, she rarely speaks of her numerous accomplishments, both in and out of the cage. She simply goes about her business... effectively and reliably. It's why I count on her. It's why every one of her RSI teammates love her.

It is why she leads us.

It is also why it was so shocking to hear the following statement come out of her mouth.

"My goal tonight was to remind everyone that this is still MY division."

Woah.

Tonight was the night it was all supposed to begin. The shakeup generated by the UMMA franchise reorganization had brought Danni her greatest challenge in years. Cheryl Cole... the woman many consider to be the greatest welterweight in the UMMA... had been elevated to the DEF franchise. The move meant Cheryl had to surrender her position in the battle for the ESL divisional title. It also meant she would immediately get a shot at the DEF belt.

Danni's belt.

With one minor exception, Danni has held that belt for years. True, it slipped away from her briefly after a stunning loss to Kristanna Loken. But, Danni took it back in emphatic style the very next chance she got.

Cheryl, on the other hand, has battled for years with greats such as Megan Fox and Catrinel Menghia. Though the title passed among them a few times, there was never any doubt that Cole is a force to be reckoned with. In fact, coming into tonight, Cheryl was the top ranked welterweight in the UMMA.

Care to guess who was second? That's right... Danni. The difference between them? 0.46 points. And, no... I did not misplace my decimal point.

At any rate, Danni knew what she was up against tonight. Cheryl is a knockout artist whose power and aggressive approach mean her bouts rarely carry into the second round. Twice before, she and Danni met in tournament action. Both experiences were short, and neither was a particularly happy memory for our girl.

But Danni seemed determined that things would be different, this time around. From the moment the fight was announced, she was a woman on a mission. She always takes her opponents seriously, but her preparation and attitude in the run up to tonight was like nothing I've ever seen from her. She was intense and focused in the gym, and quiet and subdued outside of it. She spent more than her usual amount of time with Stephanie McMahon, our development coordinator, and Amanda Seyfried, her top training partner. Word has it that she also found a volunteer to work with her after hours. Through it all, she refused to say much of anything about the upcoming matchup with Cheryl. But we all knew. We all knew what she was after, and we could see how important it was to her.

And all of that extra work sure paid off when the time came.

As expected, Cheryl came out swinging at the bell. Danni blocked and countered to force Cole back, then made ready for the next attack. And that's when Cheryl went off script. Springing a rare takedown attempt, Cheryl revealed she'd been working on some new tricks for this fight. It was a brilliant move few would have seen coming. But Danni was ready... refusing to yield and forcing Cole to pull guard. Suddenly, the war was being fought on an unexpected front, as the women battled for position on the mat. It was reversal after reversal between the two of them, until Danni finally secured the mount position she wanted. And, then...

Well, then Danni smashed her head into Cheryl's face.

The move was totally out of character, and referee Autumn Reeser was quick to pull the women apart and penalize Danni for it. But the lost point didn't seem to matter much to her. She knew this one wasn't going to the cards.

Reeser gave Cheryl a moment to recover, then the battle resumed. After a few quick exchanges, Cole came with another takedown attempt. This time, Harris stuffed it completely, and made her pay. She hit home with a vicious combination, then scored again with a clean kick to the head as Cheryl tried to scramble away.

After that, it was all Danni.

Cheryl hung tough, and Danni stayed patient. She knew how quickly Cole could turn things around, so she kept up her defensive style and took her chances where she found them. Cheryl kept coming, but Danni seemed to have an answer for everything. By the end of the round, Cheryl was bloodied and exhausted. Still, Danni was cautious, letting Cole open up the action in the second. But it didn't take long for Harris to take control, again. Less than a minute later, it was all over.

It's been clear for weeks that Danni wanted this. She wanted it more than anything I've ever seen. And now that it's over, you don't just have to take my word for it.

"I felt like it was important for me to get this win," she said afterward. "Competing with Cheryl is a new experience, for me. I've fought some of the best over the years, and I think I've held my own. But Cheryl coming to the division is more than just a fight. This is going to be a constant battle. I don't know how it's all going to go. No one does. But I wanted to be the one to fire the first shot. My goal tonight was to remind everyone that this is still MY division."

Like I said... woah.

Danni went on to make it clear that she likes the way this new rivalry is pushing her.

"I like this feeling," she said. "I like what it felt like, getting ready for tonight. The work, the planning, the nerves... I've seen Emily and Julianne and Tiffany... I've seen how hard it is for them, having to constantly compete with the best. But I've also seen how much they love it. Now, I know why. Getting ready for the moment that bell rings... it's a rush. And I think I may be hooked."

As the adrenaline subsided, shades of the RSI captain's usual modesty began to reappear. When asked what she felt such a dominant victory meant for the future of the rivalry with Cole, Danni was having none of it.

"No one dominates Cheryl Cole," she said dismissively. "She's too good for that. Things went my way tonight, and that's fine. But all that means is Cheryl will be back... stronger and faster next time. I need to be ready, because there's no doubt she's going to be."

That sounds more like the Danni we know. But what about that headbutt, Miss Harris?

"Just saying hello to my new friend," she said with a sly smile. "I know Cheryl won't complain about it. She loves to bend the rules as far as she can. I just wanted her to know I came ready to play."

OK, I guess. Just remember we have a reputation to uphold, alright?

Congratulations on a great fight, Danni. I don't know what this rivalry has in store for you, and I don't really care. I've never seen you have this much fun... before, during or after a fight... and it looks good on you. I've said it before and I'll say it again... the only reason your title is important to the rest of us is because it's important to you. I hope we did our part to get you ready for tonight. And when the time comes for the rematch, just let us know what you need. Every single one of us stands ready to assist in whatever way we can.

Louise Glover's MMA career did not start of well, at all. Her first two fights were embarrassing... and that's being kind. She pulled off a nice win in her third fight, only to get destroyed again the fourth time out. Some fighters might have quit. Certainly, most would have seriously questioned if they had what it takes to succeed.

Louise, however, turned herself into a human wrecking ball, obliterating anyone who stood in her way. She's had forty-two fights since that third loss. All but two of them were victories. Looking for a championship belt? Just ask Louise. Not only did she permanently affix the ESL divisional title top her wall, but she has the heavyweight strap from all three annual PPV events, as well. The list of fighters she's beaten along the way reads like a who's who of the heavyweight class. Catherine Bell, Caroline Wozniacki, and Adriana Lima have all fallen to Glover at one point or another. Of those three, only Lima has ever pulled out a win over the RWB juggernaut.

Petra Cubonova, meanwhile, has faced Louise twice, and neither was an enjoyable experience. She was completely wiped out the first time, and only faired a little better on the second attempt. So, it's fair to say Louise has been a difficult puzzle for Petra. Now, the reorganization has put the two of them in the same division, and forced Glover to surrender the divisional title she'd held for so long. Needless to say, she was hungry to get her hands on Cubonova's DEF belt. So, Petra wasn't going to get a lot of chances to figure out Louise's secret.

Luckily, she only needed one.

From the very first moment, nothing about this bout went the way you'd expect. Though Louise attacked first, it was Petra who landed the first meaningful blows of the fight. After that came a high kick out of nowhere from Cubonova. The high-risk move landed untouched, and suddenly it looked like Louise was in real trouble. Glover fought back, pressing the action to keep Petra at bay. But Cubonova defended well and waited for her chance. A failed clinch attempt by Louise left her vulnerable, and Petra made the most of it. She landed a clean one-two combination that put Glover on her heels. Louise tried to press again, but Petra saw it coming and landed a counter punch that staggered her opponent badly. After that, Louise just couldn't seem to gather herself as Petra used her boxing skills to chip away again and again. With a ton of time left in the round, Louise sensed that things were slipping away from her and tried to contain the damage by taking Petra to the mat. But the Czechian beauty was all over it... pushing Glover off and hitting her with another high kick that landed unchecked. Louise stumbled for a moment, then dropped to the canvas. And that was all she wrote.

When questioned afterward... well, let's just say Petra's emotional state was not hard to determine.

"I first beat Lima... FINALLY. Now, I win against Glover!" she cried enthusiastically. "I would say now is good time for me, yes?"

Oh, hell yes.

At the last regular event, Petra proved that the seventh time's the charm as she finally tore the divisional belt from Lima's unyielding fingers. Afterward, many questioned if she was really good enough to be considered one of the top heavyweights, or if the win was little more than a blip on the radar screen. After all, with one win in seven tries, it was easy to call it a mere demonstration of the law of averages.

Well, now those would-be mathematicians can go average their asses off, as far as I'm concerned. When you back up a victory over Adriana Lima with a win over Louise Glover, then any discussion of the best heavyweights in the game has to include your name. End of debate.

The secret to Petra's success tonight was that she refused to fight scared. Sounds simple enough, but it's a hard thing to accomplish when your opponent has the skills and the resume that Louise does. It would have been easy to shrink back and get overly defensive... putting all your focus into avoiding the big mistake. Easy, but also ineffective. The only way to win against an opponent like Louise is to go out there and fight your fight. Lay it all on the table and see what happens. That's exactly what Petra did, attacking decisively when she saw her chances and taking risks when she felt they'd pay off. Her instincts were dead on and she never missed a step. And that's why she's taking that title belt back home with her.

She's going to need every bit of that fearlessness if she intends to stay on top, though. Her next challenge is likely to come from Lima, who'll undoubtedly be looking to un-blip that loss she suffered last time.

Wow... fending off both Adriana AND Louise? How'd you get so lucky, P?

New rival. Same old Daniela.

While the franchise reshuffle brought Cheryl to Danni and Louise to Petra, it took both Daniela Hantuchova and her main rival, Olivia Wilde, away from the ESL and into the DEF. Not a big deal, until you remember that the DEF already has a light heavyweight champion. After wrenching the divisional title away from Erika Christensen, Holly Willoughby has been riding atop the division for well over a year, now. The franchise move cost Daniela the ESL crown she'd held for so long, which put her first in line for a shot at unseating Holly.

With a chance to establish herself against a new rival, the tennis star made good on her opportunity. And she was not at all nice about it.

The bout started with a gradual feeling out period. Then, just as it looked like the fighters were settling in, Daniela caught her opponent with a crazy spinning back kick that the popular TV host never saw coming. The blow left Holly staggered and vulnerable, and Daniela swarmed in to press her advantage. She was absolutely relentless, but Willoughby toughed it out for as long as she could. Had the kick come a little later in the fight, she probably would have survived and had the chance to regroup between rounds. But, the blow landed just over a minute in... leaving far too much time for Daniela to finish what she'd started. And finish, she did... knocking the British beauty unconscious with about a minute and a half left in the round.

No doubt about it... Daniela dominated the action tonight. But she wasn't ready to predict she would also dominate this new rivalry.

"What does this mean, one fight?" the Slovakian fighter asked. "I am happy with win, but there is long way to go. She has beaten many great fighters. So, one fight is not much. She will train and she will learn. Then, we will see."

Obviously, Daniela has a point. With victories over Caprice Bourret, Brooklyn Decker and RSI's own Alyson Michalka, Holly has repeatedly proven she's up to whatever challenge lies ahead of her. So, Daniela will certainly have her work cut out for her if she wants to keep her new belt. But, for now, I'm proud of the way she handled herself, and I'm happy to see her start this rivalry off with a win.

The next stop on the reorg express found Tiffany Mulheron launched from one tremendous challenge to another. Though the shift temporarily freed her from her vicious war with Nadine Coyle, it tossed her in with a familiar foe in Kate Lawler. Coming into tonight, Tiff and Kate faced each other four times, splitting the victories between them. The last one was so close that nobody can say for sure who won. Though the judges handed Tiffany the victory, Kate - and her stable owner - had some legitimate questions about the decision.

In other words, something had to give. And for the first few minutes, it looked like that something was going to be Tiffany.

Rule number one for Tiffany was not to let Kate take the action to the ground. Mission not accomplished. The opening bell was still echoing through the arena, and Tiffany was already on her back. Such let downs have spelled the end for many an RSI fighter, in the past. When your game plan is blown instantly like that, it's hard to maintain the confidence needed to recover. But Tiff shrugged it off quickly and refused to back down from Lawler. They traded blows on the mat, with Tiffany showing a lot more skill there than I'd expected. Eventually, the action cooled, and referee Natasha Yi stood the fighters up. That sounds like good news for Tiffany, until you realize that Kate's striking game is just as lethal. They traded blows for a few exchanges before Lawler rocked Mulheron with a vicious combination. Tiffany was badly stunned, and things were looking pretty bleak. I don't even know how she managed to stay conscious, let alone get her defenses up. She hung on through a few exchanges, until Lawler scored another takedown late in the round. Mulheron scramble to escape a scary submission attempt, and managed to ride out the rest of the round.

The second round started off a little weird, but Tiffany finally got into the fight about two minutes in. A pretty combo snuck past Lawler's defenses and sent her reeling. Tiff was quick to follow-up and establish control, but Kate refused to be bottled up... continuing to attack from the outside. The attacks kept Tiffany busy, but she stayed focused on the goal and used her inside game to accumulate heavy damage on Lawler's body. That damage eventually wore down Kate's defenses and allowed Tiff the chance to finish as the round moved toward it's final minute. Thankfully, one chance was all she needed as she set Lawler up with a vicious high kick and then put her away with flurry of punches that hammered into her unprotected face. The final blows landed with such force that, for a moment, there was legitimate concern that Kate may have suffered a devastating injury. But word came down a short time later that things had looked much worse than they were.

It's hard to say how I feel about this fight. Much like their last clash, neither fighter was clearly dominant. Though there's no doubt Tiffany was the winner this time, I think there was a lot of doubt about who was winning prior to the last few exchanges. Kate's aggressive style and Tiffany's defensive-minded approach seem to cancel each other out in a way you rarely see.

"She may be my most difficult opponent," Mulheron said of Lawler. That's quite a statement, given her history with Coyle. "With Nadine, I knew I could win if I just avoided the big mistake," she said. "She could take over the fight if you gave her the slightest chance, but, as long as I fought my game, I would be OK." Her experiences so far with Lawler have taught her that being mistake-free isn't always going to be enough. "It's different with Kate. My style isn't very effective against her. She's very difficult to game plan for, and I feel like I constantly have to adjust to make any progress against her."

Our strategist-in-chief agrees. "Lawler creates a lot of problems for Tiffany," Steph McMahon said. "Trying to form a specific plan against her is almost impossible. I've spent a lot of time with Tiff, talking about the different options and approaches she might take. When it's fight time, it's up to her to make whatever changes she needs to as she goes. She can't wait to make adjustments between rounds. It could be too late, by then."

When faced with an opponent as complex as Lawler, it's helpful to have a top-flight fighter to train with. Tiff definitely has that base covered.

"It was such an incredible fight," Emma Watson said of her experience as a spectator. "I think I stopped breathing at least twice." One of the hidden benefits of Tiffany's move to the DEF is that it allows for her training partner and soul mate to be in attendance, since Emma has more time to work with before the FAC goes into action. Well, it's nice for Tiff, anyway. For Emma, maybe not so much. "I'm sure it was exciting for the fans. For me... it was like torture."

Well Emma, you'd better get adjusted quick, because the rankings dictate that Tiffany and Kate will be doing this for a while. So, back to the gym for both of you. It's never too early to start getting ready for next time.

Four titles in one night is a tremendous accomplishment, and I'm proud of every one of these women. But I'm also proud of the one that got away.

Three attempts. Two different opponents. All failures. Tonight represented Britney Spears' third shot at a divisional title as a member of RSI. Lengthy injuries on both sides prevented her rivalry with Malin Akerman from being as storied as it could have been, but it certainly was exciting. They faced each other six times, with each woman taking three victories. On the one hand, two of those victories came as Brit marched her way to PPV titles. On the other, the women faced off for the divisional crown twice, with Malin taking both.

That rivalry is on hold, as the UMMA reorganization has pushed Malin on to the SCQ franchise. In the meantime, Britney was set to resume a rivalry with an old nemesis, tonight. The tales of her struggles with Laura Vandervoort are long and numerous. The two competed for years, starting nearly on the day they each signed with the UMMA. In fact, they met for the first time in what was just the second fight for each of them. Britney took the win that day, but Laura's gone on to grab a sizable advantage in their head-to-head matchups since then. Though Britney has turned her game around and reinvigorated her career since joining RSI, she continues to struggle with Laura.

Tonight was going to be different, though. Coming off a stunning, end-to-end victory over the vaunted Doutzen Kroes, this was the night that Brit would show she had what it takes to stand up to Laura. This was the night she would remind people that she is one of the UMMA's top middleweights for a reason. Above all, this was the night she would finally grab hold of that divisional title.

Well, I guess two out of three ain't bad.

Britney was absolutely Britney, tonight. She was relaxed and confident, never letting things get away from her. Laura came out flying at the bell, and took control early... including the timely use of an underhanded move or two. She seemed determined to take the action to the mat, but, even there, Britney proved she was up to the challenge. She weathered everything Laura threw at her, relying on her power and scoring when she could. It was more of the same in the second as Laura came out swinging, and Britney defended and countered. But then, just as Britney looked like she was starting to gain control, Laura launched an attack that landed right on the button, sending the RSI fighter off to dreamland.

It was a disappointing turn of events, made all the more so by the fact that Britney was fighting exactly the way she'd trained to. I mentioned earlier that Britney is one of the UMMA's top middleweights. Well, have a look at that list, and guess what? Laura is right there with her. And if we're using success against Doutzen Kroes as the bellwether... well, Laura was the one who took the ESL crown away from Doutzen. Not once, not twice, but three times. So, Britney knew the challenge she faced, tonight. Holding her own and fighting smart was the plan going in, and she was executing it to perfection. And then... well, shit happens.

I'm proud of the way she fought tonight, and I told her not to hang her head over the loss. I think she acquitted herself quite well against Laura, and I think she showed why she's considered one of the best. But Britney wasn't really interested in moral victories.

"I lost," she said simply. "Putting on a good show is great, but, in the end, it's all about winning. I didn't do that." She was a little easier on herself when asked about her continuing struggles against Laura. "I just can't quite seem to figure it out," she said. "I think I was better tonight than I've been in the past against her. I felt more in control, for sure. But I guess I still have some work to do."

Indeed, it appears Doutzen is not the only bellwether in the middleweight group, these days. Laura has long shown she deserves the attention of the fans. Given her more recent performances, I think you could make a case that she might just be the best there is. Britney's going to need everything she has if she's going to turn the tide in this rivalry.

Can she do it? Well, there's no way I'd be willing to bet against her, that's for sure.

While RSI provides the training and facilities Britney needs to improve her game, it appears she also doesn't have to look far for a sympathetic ear. Her close friend and fellow team leader, Elisha Cuthbert, also suffered a difficult loss here in Rochester. Her opponent, Lindsay Ellingson, is not a fighter to be taken lightly. Elisha knew she was in for a tough fight, and she trained with even more determination than usual. And anyone who knows Leesh will tell you that's saying something. But all of that effort came to naught, as Ellingson walked away with the win.

When interviewed afterward, Elisha appeared to keep things in perspective. "I knew fighting Lindsay was going to be an uphill battle, for me," admitted the former Girl Next Door. "She has the talent. She has the training. And she has some of the longest limbs I've ever seen." Despite Ellingson's reach advantage, it was clear Elisha didn't live up to her own expectations, tonight. "I thought... I just thought I'd have more for her, you know?" she said. "It started out well. She made me grind it out on the ground for a good part of the first round, but I survived it. Then, I felt like I had it going there in the second." But as anyone who's ever faced Lindsay knows, things can change in a hurry. "She landed a few hard strikes, and then... she was just relentless. Before I knew it, it had all gotten away from me. I tried to pull it back together, but I just didn't have anything left."

I've said before that Elisha isn't like Britney. Britney is strength and speed and savvy. Leesh is all grit and guts. She gets most of her wins by running on pure heart, and that scrappy determination was on display again, tonight. It just wasn't enough. So, she'll head home to the RSI facility and get back to work, determined to make herself better... and we'll be there to help her do it. Because that heart she shows is what we love about her, and we're going to do everything we can to make sure it IS enough, next time.

They say familiarity breeds contempt. It also makes it pretty hard to surprise an opponent you frequently train with. Such was the case as Cobie Smulders prepared to take on Sophia Bush, who also serves as the leader of the Dragon's Den stable. With the stables training together much of the time, Cobie and Sophia know each other much better than most opponents do. Unfortunately, that knowledge did a lot more for Sophia than Cobie, tonight.

Truth is, this was destined to be a short fight. Both women favor a highly aggressive style, so it was likely that the first one to land a meaningful blow would take the win. Sophia showed her experience advantage, side-stepping a pair of early attacks from Smulders, then seizing her chance while Cobie was out of position. And, once Sophia took command, it was over in no time.

For Cobie, this was a disappointing setback, but it didn't come as a total surprise.

"I know Sophia's struggled in the past, but I haven't seen any signs of that since the stables began training together," Smulders said. "She has a lot of experience, and she's putting it to good use. I'm still figuring things out, I guess. So, I wanted the win tonight, but I knew it was going to be tough."

As the post-fight questions continued, Smulders was reminded that some of Sophia's reemergence can be credited to someone Cobie knows quite well. "Yeah, I have to remember to thank Danni for the job she did," she said jokingly. "I guess she didn't think about what it would mean for the rest of us. After all, it's not like she's the one who has to deal with Sophia in the cage."

That's true... at least, for now.

With the friendship between Danni and Sophia being common knowledge, RSI's leader faced some Sophia-related questions at her own presser. One reporter decided to cut right to the chase, asking Danni if she felt her friend had the skills to get up there and compete for the title. "I'm not going to put that kind of pressure on her," she answered. "She's improving with each fight, but she's still figuring out what she's really capable of." A follow-up question asked what it would be like to compete with her friend for the title. "Not yet," Danni said. "I'm not going to start talking about that stuff, yet. Sophs still has a big hill to climb, and I have Cheryl to keep me busy. So, ask me again when that time comes."

Danni may be playing things close to the vest, but I think she may have tipped her hand a little when she said 'when' and not 'if' that time comes. So, we'll have to wait and see if her tacit prediction comes true.

Meanwhile, Cobie wasn't the only RSI fighter having Dragon troubles. Kristen Bell found herself matched up against sometime training partner Natalie Portman of the Den. This time, familiarity bred a pretty interesting fight. Though Nat controlled most of the action in the first round, Kristen got a few licks in toward the end that must have made the Thor actress sweat a little. But Portman started fast again in the second, and Kristen just didn't have anything left to get it turned around once more.

Afterward, the House of Cards actress was pretty matter-of-fact about the loss.

"She was just better," Kristen said. "She came out fast and took some chances that worked for her. I just couldn't get anything going against her." Bell also talked a little about her relationship with Natalie as a whole. "We get along pretty well," she said. "The two of us are in similar situations. Neither of us has had a very successful career, and we're both trying to earn a second shot at it while we still can. So, I'm happy to see her succeed. I just wish it didn't have to come at my expense."

Most of the time, a fighter needs to stick to her game plan to win. But every once in a while, you have to just go with the flow and throw the plan out the window. That's what happened tonight with Danica Patrick. She's had a lot of trouble with Tila Tequila in the past. On paper, it's simple... Tila wants to get the action to the ground, while Danica needs to everything she can to stay on her feet. Trouble is, everything she can has not always been enough. It happened again tonight, as Tila got the takedown she wanted almost immediately.

Only this time, Danica rolled with the punches.

As soon as they hit the canvas, Danica closed her guard and kept Tequila at bay. A few seconds later, rolled her opponent over and worked herself into mount position. That's when the punches started raining down. Tequila managed to tie Danica up, but not before the NASCAR driver opened up a gash on her opponent's forehead. On top of that, the tie-up forced the ref to stand the fighters back up... which is where Danica wanted to be to begin with. Patrick's stunning performance on the ground left Tequila unsure of how to attack, and Danica set to work on picking the model apart with her striking game. After that, it was just a matter of time before Danica completed her victory.

So, here we are again. Another excellent performance from Danica, which usually means her next fight will be a disaster. Can she buck the trend and get herself on track? Here's hoping.

Rounding out the RSI portion of tonight's card, Jennifer Morrison put on a massively impressive show against actress Noureen DeWulf. It didn't start out that way, as DeWulf drew first blood... splitting Jen's lip with an uppercut a few seconds into the bout. However, what followed was probably not what Noureen was hoping for. Morrison touched a thumb to her face, looked down to confirm she was bleeding and then proceeded to beat the ever-lovin' crap out of her opponent. Punches and kicks landed like heat-seeking missiles, with Jen blasting her way through every defense DeWulf tried. Noureen finally cooled the action by pulling guard after a failed takedown, but that did little more than stretch out the beating. Morrison held her own on the ground, then went straight back to work after the two regained their feet. DeWulf held on for as long as she could, and suffered the consequences for her toughness. She was a bloody mess by the time the final knockout happened.

With two solid victories in a row, the hope is that Jen now has something to build on. The brutal start to her career has left her deep in the hole, but I have confidence she's going to get it all turned around. After all, the savior can't fail, can she?

Phew! What an event! And now, RSI has ten more fights coming up in Orlando, including title fights for Julianne Hough and Emily VanCamp against new rivals? Geez... I need a vacation.

Wait a second. Vacation... Orlando... Hot dog! I think I just got myself an idea.
