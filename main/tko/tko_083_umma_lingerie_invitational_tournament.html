Breaking Through

If you wanted to make a list of the greatest light heavyweight talents in the UMMA, what would it look like? You would have to include SCQ champion Caprice Bourret and DEF champion Holly Willoughby, right? I'm sure you'd also include past FAC champion and top competitor Erin Heatherton. And, it certainly would be hard to overlook Mallika Sherawat and her 42 career victories, wouldn't it? So, the question for RSI's Alyson Michalka was simple: Was she good enough to beat one of these women to win the Lingerie Invitational tournament title?

What if I told you she beat them ALL?

That's right. In a strange seeding quirk, Aly faced an impossible gauntlet of UMMA talent in this tournament. And, quite simply, she made the impossible possible.

It started simply enough with an opening round match-up against former RSI stablemate Erica Rose Campbell. A quick victory there set up a date with Holly. So, round 2, and Aly was already up against a reigning champion. This one started out looking like the women were going to completely destroy each other. But, some great footwork by Aly setup a tremendous kick that left Holly out on her feet. Then it was on to Mallika, she of the previously mentioned 42 victories. Once again, both fighters pounded each other from the opening bell. And, once again, awesome technique allowed Aly to unleash the high kick from hell. Three down, two to go.

Next in line, Caprice Bourret. A little background on Caprice. She has held the SCQ championship for over two years, defending it five times over that span. Over that same span, she has fought in an astounding 53 matches. She has lost only 11. So, Aly knew what she was up against. And, as always, she showed not an ounce of fear. Caprice tagged her early in the fight, drawing first blood. Aly shook it off and brutally hammered Caprice in the clinch, literally destroying the champion one blow at a time. It's a testament to Bourret's experience and toughness that she made it to the end of the round. But, she was still bleeding heavily and looked throughly beaten as she came out for the second. Knowing a battered Caprice is still extremely dangerous, Aly showed no mercy. And, once again, it was that brutal high kick that ended things.

So, Aly had faced off against one of the true legends of the UMMA, and she had literally beaten her into unconciousness. And, what did she get for her trouble? Another matchup with the beautiful and deadly Erin Heatherton. These two have met up numerous times in the past, and Aly has come up short far more often than she'd like. But, the title she craved was right there, and there was no way she was letting it go. The women slugged it out as usual, but Aly was far more accurate, punishing Erin both at distance and in the clinch. Apparently growing bored with the high kick approach, Aly finished this one with a huge overhand right that landed flush on Erin's cheek. Erin collapsed in a heap, and, at long last, Alyson Michalka had a championship to her name.

When it was all over, the usually stoic Michalka struggled to contain her emotions. "You keep telling yourself you can do it. That you're so close. But, I was tired of being close. I was... I just...," said Michalka, her voice cracking as she fought back tears. After taking a moment to compose herself, she continued. "No matter how good you are. No matter how close you come. You start to wonder if you can do it. And, I really started to think that maybe I couldn't." Asked about what it's like to compete in the FAC light heavyweight division, Aly was brutally honest. "Oh, it sucks," she said, a laugh breaking through as she wiped her cheek with her hand. "To have to compete with Brooklyn and Erin all the time? To have younger fighters like India and Jodie nipping at my heels? Would YOU want to be caught between the ILF and the RWB Group?" A rare smile played across her face as she finished her thought. "I mean, I never back away from a challenge, but it would be nice if there weren't so many of them."

Indeed. I don't normally tell her this, because I don't want her going soft. But, I have a ton of respect for Aly. She never has an easy night. Every time she fights, she's facing a dangerous opponent. Most of the time, she finds herself in there with the very best. But, guess what Ms. Michalka. Here in Cologne, YOU were the best. This championship is well deserved, and well earned. That said, Heather is not going to take her loss in the final lying down. And, no doubt, Brooklyn is going to be pissed that she didn't even make the final. So, enjoy your moment. Because, tomorrow, you need to prepare for war.

Congratulations, Aly.

Can someone please explain to me what in the world has happened to Julianne Hough? Wasn't it just a few months ago that she was struggling just to compete? And, now, she wins her second tournament championship in a row. Not only that, she does it in less than five full rounds of work. That's right. Not one of her matches in this tournament lasted to the end of the first round. And this was no fluke performance. Much like Aly, Julianne faced off against some of the best fighters in her weight class, including FAC champion Vikki Blows and DEF champion Kristin Kreuk. Seriously, who is this woman? Last time out, I mentioned that Julianne's recent performances were going to force me to come up with a whole new list of adjectives for use in these press releases. Well, Julianne clearly took that as a challenge, so here goes: why don't we try incredible, thrilling and devastating on for size? Honestly, she has simply become a joy to watch, and her newfound confidence has taken her normally sunny personality and made it absolutely effervescent. I have more words I can use, but I think I'm going to save them. Something tells me I'll be needing them down the line.

While I may have a lot to say about Julianne's performance, the fighter, herself, was exceptionally humble after her win here. Having now won two tourneys in a row, complete with victories over Blows, Kreuk and Keira Knightley, Julianne was asked if she felt that she was now the one to beat in the bantamweight division. "Not really, no. I feel as though I can compete with anyone. And, I think having my name in the mix with those women... that feels right. I think I've earned it. But, to say this clearly makes me the best... I don't think you can say that. There's too much talent out there. I'm just having a good run, that's all." Humility is probably a wise choice, as there's no question that the competition will now be gunning for RSI's diminutive powerhouse. I won't add any fuel to that fire either, except to say this: get used to seeing your name prominently featured in these press releases, Julianne. Clearly, we'll be hearing a lot from you in the years to come. Congratulations on a fantastic performance and another great win.

So, here's the thing about expectations. There comes a point when the best you can possibly do is meet them. If you slip up, you're seen as a failure. But, if you do everything right, you've simply done what what you're supposed to do. Such is the life of top seed Danneel Harris. After facing down an onslaught of upstart rookies and top contenders, Danni emerged as the welterweight division champion here. And her name doesn't even appear until somewhere "below the fold" of her own stable's press release. Just doesn't seem fair, does it?

When I started RSI, my goal was to build our success. I didn't want to sign a bunch of champions and top contenders and then make claims about how great we were. I wanted to earn it. But, I knew I couldn't do it alone. If RSI was going to succeed I would need experienced and charismatic fighters on the roster. Women who could be the face of the company while we worked to recruit and mold talented rookies. Women with the abilities and the maturity to make the company strong. Alyson Michalka. Britney Spears. Elisha Cuthbert. Stephanie McMahon. I recruited these women in the hopes that they would be the foundation upon which RSI would be built. And, not one of them has disappointed me. Ever. But, at the very base of that foundation... the cornerstone, if you will... is Danneel Harris. None of what we've accomplished would have happened without you, Danni. Your steady presence, both in the gym and in the cage, is the rudder that steers this ship. You're always willing to do your part... training, recruitment... whatever. You are the unquestioned leader of this team. So, congratulations on your win, champ. And, thank you. For everything.

Yet another chapter in the adventures of Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert is in the books. And, to be honest, it looks a lot like the ones that came before it. Both women fought beautifully in the early rounds until they inevitably found one another and faced off once more. Once again, it was Elisha who advanced, and that's a plot point that's really starting to get to Britney. "I'm always happy for Elisha when she moves on," said a visibly frustrated Spears. "And, I like to think I'm still confident in my skills. But, losing like this starts to make you wonder." Seeing the pain her friend was in, Elisha could commiserate. "We've all been there, at some point. You hit a rough patch, and you just can't seem to snap out of it." First hand experience, though, made words of encouragement come easy. "I know she's questioning whether or not she's still got it. But, I know for a fact that she does. And, I've got the bruises to prove it." For my part, I don't mind the competition between these two. In fact, I'm actually starting to enjoy watching them work, both in and out of the cage. But, what's bothering me is how each chapter in this story seems to end. Once they're done with each other, the winner never seems to have anything left for her next fight. And, when your next opponent is Doutzen Kroes, you can't come in with anything less than your best. If you do, she'll just make you look silly. And, that's exactly what happened to Elisha, as Doutzen needed less than two minutes to completely crush her in their fourth round matchup. So, I'm proud of both of these women. But, we need to get this figured out.

Younger, inexperienced fighters don't usually stand much of a chance in these tournaments. For fighters such as Brittany Tacy, Jennifer Lawrence, Jennifer Stano and Krystal Forscutt, just making the tournament is an accomplishment. They know that their low seeds mean they'll be facing the best of the best right out of the gate. They hope to make a good showing and gain a few fans. They may even entertain thoughts of stealing a win from a veteran who underestimates them. But, they don't have any realistic expectations of taking home the championship. And for these RSI fighters, it was a tournament of surprises, both good and bad.

Krystal has easily been RSI's greatest success story. We have documented her unbelievable turn around numerous times in the past. Certainly, her recent successes had her thinking of stealing an early upset. But, when your first round draw is a fighter with the skill and experience of Melissa Joan Hart... well, let's just say that would be a pretty big upset. Krystal made a little noise early, but Melissa was in control throughout. "She left no doubt that I'm not in her league," said a badly bruised and battered Forscutt afterward. "I have a lot of respect for Melissa. Hopefully, I'll keep improving. Maybe next time I can give her a decent challenge." Krystal's saying all the right things, and I have no doubt that's truly how she feels. Now, let me tell you how I feel. Melissa's performance in this match was, quite simply, an abomination. It was obvious to everyone that Melissa was the better fighter. There was no reason to spend nearly three full rounds brutalizing Krystal to prove her point. I don't think toying with her opponent the way she did gained her any fans. More likely, it lost her a few. And, it certainly cost her my respect.

Speaking of people who lost my respect, what was with the illegal headbutts Kirsten Dunst used in her match against Jennifer Stano? I'll skip past the questions about what Nell McAndrew was doing, or not doing, in this case. But, what in the world was Kirsten doing? She's always been a class act and a top draw here in the UMMA. In fact, the only thing that kept us from approaching Kirsten about joining the RSI family is that Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert are in the same division. Behaving the way she did in the cage tonight is just not Kirsten's style. I understand losing yourself in the heat of the moment. But, to do it not once, but TWICE? I'm willing to give Ms. Dunst a pass on this, given her history. I just hope this isn't a sign of a change in attitude from her.

So, if you're stepping into the cage for only the third time in your professional career, and you look across and see that your opponent is the SCQ lightweight champion, what do you do? Well, if you're Brittany Tacy, you stand your ground and take your beating like a big girl. Brittany had a few bright spots in this one, but champion Ali Larter left no doubt who was in command. Indeed, Ali made short work of the Floridian beauty, dropping her with a little more than 15 seconds left in the first round. For her part, Brittany took the loss in stride. "I never expected I was going to win every time," she said as she adjusted the cold compress on her eye. "I've said before that I still have a lot of work to do. And, Ali gave me a painful reminder of that, tonight."

I've made no secret of the fact that we at RSI are expecting big things from Jennifer Lawrence, and we got a nice taste of that here in this tournament. On paper, it doesn't look like Jen should have stood much of a chance against the far more experienced Halle Berry. But, in the cage, she gave the older fighter everything she could handle. Scoring with precision strikes, Jen rocked Halle again and again. For her part, Halle weathered the redhead's repeated attacks, working steadily from the clinch to do some damage of her own. It was a great battle, and it was an awesome show for the fans who got to see it. It could be argued that Jennifer actually should have won this fight. Maybe I'm biased, but I did have it scored in Jen's favor. But, there'll be no crying over how this one turned out. This fight was as close as they come, and I don't envy the judges that had to make the decision. In the end, they gave the nod to the seasoned veteran. And, maybe that's how it should be. But, Jennifer certainly opened some eyes with this performance. And, with so much of her career still ahead of her... I think it's safe to say Jennifer Lawrence is going to be one to watch for a long time to come.

On top of all the tournament news, RSI has a couple of roster moves to announce. It is my distinct pleasure to welcome Catherine Bell and Tiffany Mulheron as the newest members of the RSI family. Each of these women gives us an experienced competitor in a weight class where we had none before. And, each of these women is more than capable of serving as a role model to the young talent that is already on the RSI roster. "When Richelle approached me about coming on board, she didn't have to sell me on what RSI could do for me," said a clearly excited Catherine. "I've had my eye on them for a while, and I've seen the success they're having." So, what was the deciding factor? "When she told me she wanted me to serve as mentor to Marzia Prince and Holly Bricken. Not only am I excited about getting to work with these bright young stars, but I also like the trust Richelle is putting in me."

In the featherweight arena, however, the situation was not so clear cut. How do you make sure your newly minted champion doesn't revolt at the idea of having another fighter serve as her mentor? Simple. Just ask the champ.

"Are you kidding me?" said an absolutely thrilled Emma Watson. "Championship or no, I'm not daft. I know I still have a lot to learn. Having the chance to work with Tiffany is awesome. Believe me when I tell you, I'm looking forward to it."

We believe you, young lady. But, how does the veteran feel about it?

"I love it. I came to RSI because I want them to push me," said new recruit Tiffany Mulheron. "Training with someone of Emma's talent is exactly the push I'm looking for. I'm happy to help her any way I can. And, I have no doubt I'll learn from her, as well."

We're happy to have both of these women on board with us. Both have had a great deal of success on their own, and I commend them for it. But, I'm confident that both can achieve more. Even here in this tournament... a loss to Demi Moore is certainly nothing for Tiffany to be ashamed of. But, it shouldn't be enough for her. Both of these women should want and expect more. They're capable of it, and we're going to help them get there.

Geez... what the hell is going on around here anyway? Championships. New recruits. Mentoring programs. It's starting to look like I know what I'm doing. And, if that thought scares you, trust me... it terrifies me.

Well, that wraps up another edition of As RSI Turns. No. That title sucks. It was rushed. It was lame. And, it shows a total lack of effort on my part. I'll work on it, and get back to you. In the meantime, we'll see you in Berlin, where the women of SCQ will take center stage. Take care.
