TKO Issue: #102
Date: March 2014
Franchise: Steel Cage Queens
Event: Final Conflict
Location: Civic Centre (Ottawa, Ontario, Canada)

Frustrations Boil Over

There's been a lot of talk within RSI about Stephanie McMahon's recent struggles. Having established such dominance early in her career, it has been shocking to Steph's teammates (not to mention me) to see her drop 5 of her last 7 fights. Throughout this period, Steph has been visibly frustrated, but largely silent. After tonight's loss to Abi Titmuss, however, she finally spoke out. And, I didn't like anything she had to say.

First, let's take a look at the facts. At 34-10, Steph is still one of the most successful fighters in the UMMA. That record has put her near the very top of the SCQ super heavyweight division. And, that division, more than any other, is loaded with experienced talent. Looking at the top 7 fighters in that group, Steph's 44 total fights makes her the only one who has less than 60. When surrounded by that kind of talent, struggles are not surprising. Also, though she keeps coming up short, she has not necessarily been dominated. Aside from a bad loss to Deborah Ann Woll at the most recent team event, there's nothing truly terrible about her recent performances. Even tonight, though the fight was short, Steph was giving as good as she got. But Abi's punching power is well known. Get caught flat-footed just once, and that may be all it takes. And that's what happened to Steph in this one.

I expected Steph would take this loss hard, and I offered to cover her press conference for her. Though I was a little surprised when she turned me down, I figured she just wanted to stand up and face the music. Unfortunately, the tune she heard was darker than I'd realized. "I don't know what to do," began a calm, but dejected, McMahon, speaking without even taking a question. "I feel like I've reinvented my approach a hundred times the last few months. Nothing seems to be working." Naturally, this wasn't the opening I was hoping for. Both what Steph was saying and how she was saying it sent a shiver down my spine. I had a very bad feeling about where this was headed. "I really don't..." she continued, her voice catching as a tear rolled down her face. "I don't know if I can keep doing this." As my stomach tied itself in knots, McMahon took things a step further. "I don't have any desire to go to the gym tomorrow. I'm tired of trying to figure this out." She sat very still, staring off into the distance as if lost in thought. Then, she practically tore my heart right out of my chest. "I think I'm done," she said, her voice soft but firm. Suddenly, the questions came rapid fire. "Did you just decide this now, or have you been thinking about it?" "Are you just leaving RSI, or are you leaving the UMMA all together?" "Was your age a factor in your decision?" But, Steph waved them away. "I'm sorry. That's all," she mumbled as she rose from the table and walked out of the press conference, the unanswered questions echoing down the hallway behind her.

I stood for a moment, trying to process what I'd just heard. Stephanie McMahon, former DEF super heavyweight champion, didn't just decide to quit, did she? I pulled myself together and headed to the locker room. As I approached, the door banged open as Stephanie stormed out, her bag slung over her shoulder. She hadn't even bothered to change. "Steph, we need to talk," I said, literally blocking her path to force her to stop. "I know you're pissed, but you need to calm down and think about this." She avoided looking at me, choosing instead to stare off over my shoulder, as if someone better might arrive at any moment. "I have thought about it," she said finally. "I just don't want it any more. I'm sorry, Richelle." And with that, she stepped around me and left the arena.

Hours after the event, when I should have been working on this release, I was instead fielding questions in the locker room from Stephanie's team leader, Britney Spears, and her counterpart, Elisha Cuthbert. "Do you think she's serious?" asked Britney. I shrugged. "I think she's pissed and frustrated. Beyond that, I don't know." "She just needs to cool off," chirped Elisha. "She'll come around." I thought a moment, then decided this would all look better in the morning. "I'll talk to her tomorrow," I said. "I'll get it straightened out."

Suddenly, there was a quick knock. The door popped open, and there stood Stephanie. "Thank God," I thought to myself. "She's come to her senses." But my sigh of relief caught in my throat when I noticed the envelope in her hand. Realizing the gravity of the situation, Britney and Elisha faded away into the locker room. "They said you were still here," Stephanie said as she strode over to where I sat. She held the envelope out to me. "I didn't want to waste any time getting this to you." I shook my head. "I won't accept that," I said sternly. "You're over-reacting." She stood a moment, then set the envelope down on the bench. "I want you to know that I appreciate everything," she said, turning away. "Stephanie, you're not thinking straight," I said softly. "We can get this turned around." "I don't think so," she whispered over her shoulder. And then, she was gone.

Almost magically, the blonde duo reappeared. "Way to get it straightened out," Britney grumbled, an undertone of anger in her voice. "Yeah," added Elisha. "I'm sorry I didn't have a pen and paper, so I could take notes on your management technique." I realize now that both of them were just upset about what had transpired, and they were taking it out on me. But, I was having my own emotional reaction, so I wasn't feeling very understanding at that moment. "Shut the fuck up, both of you," I spat. "Not another fucking word."

As we sat, brooding in silence, Danni came through the door. "I just saw Stephanie passing through the lobby," she said. "Did she stop in here?" Not bothering to answer, I simply held up the envelope for her to see. Suddenly, a low, guttural sound filled the locker room. And I'm pretty sure it was coming from Danni. Before I knew it, she reached out and snatched the envelope away from me, then turned and stormed out of the locker room. "Did Danni just growl?" asked Elisha. "I'm pretty sure she did," I stammered, unable to keep the shock out of my voice. "Holy shit," Britney said. "Where do you think she's going?" I spread my hands in front of me. "I guess she's going to find Steph."

I haven't seen Danni or Stephanie since that moment. Since Danni took the letter, I never got to read it. So, as far as I'm concerned, Stephanie McMahon is still a member of RSI. But, I'm not optimistic about where this is heading. I want Steph here. Danni... Brit... Elisha... we all want her here. But, in the end, there's nothing any of us can do to force her to stay.

Anyway, not all the news was bad for RSI at this event. Though Marzia Prince wasn't able to follow up her fine performance at the team event with another win, she looked great out there. I mentioned last time that Trish Stratus was going to be a big step up in class for Marzia. But she stood her ground and showed a lot of poise, lasting the full three rounds against her far more experienced opponent. Though the decision didn't go her way, she had her moments in this one, and I think she learned quite a bit. I'm extremely pleased with the way she's developing, and I expect more of the same in the months to come.

Another win for Roselyn Sanchez here tonight, and she really let her nasty side out in this one. Foregoing more traditional tactics, she latched into Kim Raver's long, wavy locks and just threw the Grey's Anatomy actress around like a ragdoll. I'm not sure what it was that had Sanchez feeling so catty, but I'd prefer she stick to her training in the future. I'm always happy with a win, but there are better ways to get one.

Meanwhile, Catherine Bell seems to have recovered quite nicely from her disappointing performance in the final round of the Hit the Beach tournament. Utilizing her relentless striking game, she made quick work of opponent Moon Bloodgood. The race is extremely tight at the top of the SCQ heavyweight division. Every point is crucial, and Mac did a fine job of staying focused and getting the job done. I knew when we signed her that she was capable of competing for a title. But, I'm still stunned at how quickly she's advancing.

Last, but not least, we revisit the super heavyweight division. When we signed her, Torrie Wilson was struggling near the bottom the rankings. I actually didn't have much interest in her, but it was Stephanie who convinced me to give her a shot. And Torrie hasn't looked back, since. Her win tonight over Mariah Carey makes it 5 out of 6 for Torrie since joining RSI. She wasn't able to enjoy her win, though, as she was devastated by the developments regarding her friend and mentor. I understand how she feels, but I need her to pull herself together. If we're going to have any chance at convincing Steph to stay, we're going to need Torrie's help.

One last note. There was a historic moment at Hit the Beach that I'm ashamed to say I hadn't been tuned in to. It's no secret that there's been no love lost between RSI and RWB, but there's no particular ill-will, either. The fact is that both organizations work hard to stay at the top of the UMMA game, which means our athletes often find themselves pitted against one another in big spots. So, while there is an almost necessary rivalry, there is also a mutual respect. Both know what it takes to win championships, and we know it's not easy. Which is what makes what Louise Glover has done so remarkable. For a fighter, winning a single title is a great achievement. Winning every title she has available to her is exceptional. In the past, I've used phrases such "one of the greatest" and "among the top" in describing a number of different UMMA fighters. For Louise, we can definitely drop the qualifiers. She is currently the greatest heavyweight fighter in the UMMA, without exception. Congratulations on your title sweep, Ms. Glover. I'd wish you luck in defending those titles, but that would be carrying the whole respect thing just a little too far.

Well, another event behind us. The DEF is up next, and, as usual, there will be plenty of action. Injury has forced welterweight champion Kristanna Loken to the sidelines, which means Danni Harris won't get the chance to reclaim her title. The news is doubly bad for Danni, as she will now be forced to face the always dangerous Jennifer Lamiraqui. Jennifer comes into this one having won 8 of her last 10. No doubt, this will not be an easy fight for Danni. And I'm tempted to stop this release right here, if only out of sheer spite. But, truth is, I'm just not that evil. Though she and her partner in crime have made a hobby out of annoying the crap out of me, I can't leave out the fact that Britney Spears is FINALLY getting a shot at the DEF middleweight crown. Britney has handled champion Malin Akerman pretty easily the last two times they've faced each other. Can she do it again with the hardware on the line? Tune in next time to find out.
