TKO Issue: #117
Date: November 2014
Franchise: Elite Starlet League
Event: Raging Rumble
Location: ARCO Arena (Sacramento, CA)

Humiliated, No More

Only a few short months ago, Tiffany Mulheron was completely destroyed by champion Nadine Coyle. It was the second time in a little over a year that Nadine had beaten her that way. Those two facts alone don't sound too Earth-shattering. But it was what came in-between that makes the story so heartbreaking. After the first beat down, Tiffany found her way to RSI, and she trained like a demon. She found new friends who got behind her and helped her develop. She faced off against Nadine again, coming up short but proving she had the talent and heart she needed to keep up with the vaunted champion. More work, more sweat, more determination... and the next time out she did what few thought she could. She took Nadine's belt away. It appeared the UMMA had a fresh, intense new rivalry from a place no one ever would have expected.

And then, the perfect story somehow twisted and melted it's way back to where it had started. The crushing and total loss to Nadine this past July left the RSI fighter adrift in her doubts. It was baffling. It was infuriating. Most of all, it was humiliating. But Tiffany has some dedicated friends watching out for her, and they pulled her back to shore and helped her get right. Just as they did in the beginning.

Coming into tonight's fight, I was expecting a brutal, emotional war, and my biggest fear was that Tiffany would lose her mind and beat herself before Nadine got the chance. What I got instead was a vision of calm, calculated perfection. Not that it started out that way.

The bell rang, and Nadine moved to clinch immediately. Ten seconds later, she hammered Tiff's body with a brutal combination that made me wince all the way up in the RSI box. Less than thirty seconds later, she tried to break Mulheron's ribs with a flurry of punches that took my breath away. She followed that with a hook to the midsection that had Tiffany doubled over in pain and made me put my hands over my face. The fight was quickly turning into another disaster, and I couldn't bear to watch. This was just the kind of start I feared would crush Tiffany's spirit and make her cover up and endure yet another slaughter.

The crowd roared again, and I peeked out from between my fingers. Only this time, it was Tiffany doing the damage, hammering away with knee strikes to Coyle's body. More clinching. More knees to Nadine's face and body. I drew my hands away and started to believe. Tiff swung and missed. Nadine countered and the blood started to flow. Tiff responded with a straight right that slipped past the champion's guard. Nadine countered, but Tiff blocked and clinched. Bang! Bang! Bang! More knees from the challenger. I looked to my right and stared, wide-eyed at RSI team leader, Danneel Harris. Danni looked back at me with the same expression. Tiffany unloaded with a series of uppercuts as Nadine struggled to escape. It did her no good as Mulheron scored with another series of punches. Nadine tried to counter, but Tiff smothered the attempt. Again the punches rained down on the champion! The crowd was on its feet, sensing the end was near. But Nadine finally broke free and got the distance she wanted. Tiff's big chance had passed her by, as the champion was now free to employ her fearsome boxing skills.

Tiffany tried to throw the champion off her game with a quick kick. Nadine blocked and countered with a straight right... that missed. Another kick from Mulheron, this one finding it's mark. Coyle reset and launched a crisp combo. Mulheron blocked and smashed a hook into the champion's chin. I looked at Danni again, both of us with our mouths hanging open. Tiff was beating Nadine at her own game! Another straight right from the challenger landed unchecked, and she chased after the champion and tied her up. More knees from the clinch, followed by an uppercut that knocked Nadine off her feet. The crowd was going wild, but one voice could be heard clearly above the roar, as Emma Watson was screaming at Tiffany from cage side to finish Coyle off. Unfortunately, Tiffany's ground game is her biggest weakness, so she wisely waited Coyle out. Nadine, on the other hand, was in no rush to get up, and she ate up nearly a half-minute of the round by stalling. Clearly, she didn't become such a dominant champion by being stupid. Tiff tried a few kicks to force the action, but they were no trouble for Nadine. By the time Coyle retook her feet, there were only twenty seconds left in the round. Tiffany hurried to try and finish, but she took a reckless approach that only allowed Nadine to score with a few clinch strikes before the round ended. Coyle had survived to fight on.

I could see both Emma and Steph McMahon giving instructions to Tiffany between rounds. Though I'm sure they were disappointed that Tiffany wasn't able to finish what she'd started in the first round, no one looked flustered. Just cool and calm. There was more fight to come, and the only concern was making sure Tiff was ready. As the second round got underway, the fighters exchanged punches at the center of the octagon. Mulheron slipped to her right and threw a straight right over the top of Nadine's guard. The punch caught her flush and down she went. Just like that, the title was Tiffany's, once more.

As soon as it was over, Danni was gone like a shot. She has a lot of personal investment in Tiffany, and I knew she was headed to the locker room to greet the newly crowned champion. Meanwhile, the happiest person in the arena appeared to be Emma, as she burst into the cage and threw her arms around her lover. Though I'm sure Tiffany was dealing with a powerful mix of emotions, it was Emma who was doing the crying as they celebrated together. Steph, on the other hand, looked plainly relieved.

Everyone was pretty well spent by the time we got to the post-event press conference, but Tiffany still seemed happy to answer whatever questions were sent her way. When asked how she felt after her big win, I was surprised when ambivalence seemed to rule the day. "I really don't know," she said. "I'm excited to be the champion again, and I'm very happy with my performance tonight. I just can't understand why it's not always this way." A follow-up asked if she was saying she felt she was clearly better than Nadine. Tiffany shook her head. "I hate Nadine. I've made no secret of that. But, I have to respect what she can do in the cage. She's one of the best there is, and she's proven that multiple times." She paused to consider how she wanted to continue. "The thing is, I know I can compete with her. I KNOW it. So, when I come out and fail like I did last time... I just don't understand how I let that happen. Losing is one thing, but sometimes it's like I'm never even in the fight. That just can't happen."

There were a lot of factors that went into Tiffany's last performance against Nadine, but, in the end, none of that matters. What she said tonight, is correct. She is now the reigning Red Carpet, Lingerie Invitational and divisional champion. She's needs to fight like a champion every time out... win or lose. She did it tonight, and that's what I'm proud of. The title is nice, but it's the way she performed and the way she carried herself that really impressed me.

Ah, yes... it seems like old times for Emily VanCamp. Her epic battles with rival Irina Sheik are well documented. The two have traded the ESL lightweight back and forth for over a year now. In between title fights, they've met up in various tournaments. For the most part, the fights have been even and exciting. However, their last two meetings have been fairly one-sided victories for Emily. Had she found some weakness in the legendary champion's game? Or, was Sheik simply losing her touch? Turns out the answer is neither.

All eyes were on Irina tonight to see if she could get back to her winning ways. And the former and once-again champion did not disappoint. Irina attacked immediately, and Emily spent the whole first round on the defensive. She tried to fight back in the second, and managed to do some damage. But it was clear that losing two in a row to Emily was not sitting well with Sheik, and Irina simply would not be denied. She just kept coming, hammering VanCamp with tremendous body blows and scoring with looping shots to the head. Em survived the onslaught as best she could, but try as she might, she just couldn't get anything going in response. A little more than three minutes into the round, Irina landed another looping right hand to Emily's chin, and the champion could take no more. RSI's fearless warrior collapsed to the canvas, and Irina had reclaimed the title once more.

Emily was understandably disappointed in the post fight press conference, but she wasn't making excuses. When asked whether the two one-sided wins had made her a little complacent, Em said that was never a factor. "If anything I was more prepared than ever," she said. "Irina had been having a tough time recently. I've seen the comments from her. It was obviously getting to her. So, I expected her to come out with a vengeance, and she did. I was prepared for it, but I just couldn't do anything to stop it." When asked her opinion on Sheik's sudden resurgence, Em couldn't resist throwing in a quick I told you so. "You guys were the ones who wanted to write her off," she said with a knowing smile. "I told you were crazy then, and now you see it. I may not know Irina well personally, but I know her as a competitor. If anything, you guys helped her beat me, tonight. All the talk about her being finished was insane, and she wasn't going to put up with that shit for long." Oh, Miss Emily... such language! When asked how she felt about having to again contend with Irina for the belt, Em sang a familiar tune. "I feel like I've answered this question a million times, but I'll be happy to say it again: I don't have any desire to collect championships. I'm here to compete. When I do win a title, I want to feel like I've earned it. She has the belt now, and I want to take it from her. When I have it, I know she wants it. This thing we have going is never going to be finished. Right now, it's just my turn to want."

Emily isn't the only one with the right to claim I told you so in this situation. With both Irina and Doutzen Kroes struggling a little recently, there was a lot of talk about the demise of the ILF. From the word go, I said it was ridiculous, and I was proven right, tonight. Not only did Irina win back the lightweight title, but Doutzen reclaimed the middleweight crown she'd held for so long. So, even though Catrinel Menghia dropped her title defense against Cheryl Cole, having Sheik and Kroes restored to the championship ranks somehow makes the ILF feel restored, as well. Like Emily, RSI thrives on competition. So, we wouldn't have it any other way.

Light Heavyweight champion Daniela Hantuchova looked untouchable once again, as she made quick work of opponent Olivia Wilde. That's not to say Olivia made it easy. She scored early with a couple of brutal combos, and it looked like she was set up to take Daniela to the mat. But the Slovakian foiled the takedown attempt, and made Olivia pay with a crushing kick to the head that could be heard throughout the arena. Wilde somehow kept her feet, but she was never the same after that. With Daniela on the offensive and plenty of time left in the round, it was only a matter of time before the young actress was overcome.

Though Daniela has been getting the better of this rivalry, she made it clear she never takes Olivia for granted. "Olivia is... she is excellent fighter. It does not matter how many times I win. I know she is dangerous." When questioned about Olivia's fateful takedown move, Daniela was honest about how lucky she was. "Olivia is much better ground fighter. She took chance, and it did not work. Was big risk, but also big chance. If I am little bit slower... who knows? Maybe different story."

Daniela's right, of course. She's been around a long time, and she's among the UMMA's most experienced fighters. If opponent's are going to have a chance against her, they need to take their shots when they can. This may not have worked out for Olivia, but she deserves credit for going for it. That's the kind of gutsy move it'll take to beat the tennis star. Though the pack is closing in, Olivia's going to get another shot or two at Daniela's crown before then. Don't be surprised if she finds a way to make one work for her.

Speaking of championship shots, Amanda Seyfried is certainly making a case for getting one soon. After pulling out a gutsy, clutch win over Megan Fox in their last bout, Seyfried was much more dominant tonight. Fox, as always, wanted to take this fight to the mat. Unlike last time, Amanda did an excellent job of keeping her feet... succumbing only once to the dangerous submission specialist. Even that takedown came to nothing, as Amanda was able to tie Megan up and get the fight back to standing. Once both fighters were upright, it was all Seyfried... wailing away with precision strikes until her opponent could take no more.

Despite the dominant performance she gave tonight, Amanda knows how dangerous Fox can be. "Anyone who's seen her in action knows how easy it is for Megan to turn the tables in a fight. One minute, you're in control. The next, you're tapping out. I knew I had to stay on her." Amanda would do well to keep her skills sharp, too. She still has a quite a ways to go before she'll catch Catrinel Menghia and new champion Cheryl Cole. In the meantime, it's likely she and Megan will be seeing a lot of each other.

I don't know exactly what to say about what happened to Allira Cohrs tonight. On the one hand, I thought she gave a great performance against a more experienced fighter in Lindsey Vonn. On the other hand, I felt she got robbed. By my count, Allira was the clear winner in rounds one and three. I had her winning round two, as well, but I admit that one was closer. One of the judges agreed with me, giving all three rounds to Cohrs. However, the other two judges inexplicably gave both of the opening frames to Lindsey, ultimately leading to the bout being declared a draw.

Allira was gracious about the loss, giving the usual speech about Lindsey being a quality opponent. Though the statement was true, it was obvious she was disappointed, and I don't blame her. She did enough to get the win, and I'm really not sure how the judges saw it differently. But, experience can be measured in a number of ways, and tonight, Allira experienced the fact that judging a fight can be a mysterious and mystical process. Sometimes, the stars just line up against you, I guess.

It takes a long time to truly get good at submission defense. No matter how good your offensive weapons are, an opponent can end things instantly if she locks on a crippling hold. Krystal Forscutt learned that lesson the hard way tonight, as she fell victim to a tough and determined Karolina Kurkova. For the first two minutes, the two traded punches in the center of the octagon, and Krystal was clearly out-fighting the blonde supermodel. Karolina was bloodied and literally knocked to the canvas. But she's been doing this a long time, and she knows it's never over until it's over. She surprised Krystal with a quick takedown and slapped on a leg lock before Forscutt even knew what hit her.

"I let my guard down," admitted a disappointed Krystal. "I thought I had her beat. She looked like she could barely stand." Then, she shook her head and gave a small smile. "I don't know if she was messing with me, or what, but I have to give her credit. She sure fooled me." For my part, I saw what Krystal saw. I thought Kurkova was finished. I even wondered if the ref was going to step in and put a stop to things. Krystal had fought a great fight up until that point, and she definitely looked like the better fighter. But, Karolina knows it's not about how you look. It's about winning.

Speaking of winning, it's time for rookie Michelle Lewin to start doing some. In her second professional fight, she looked exactly like she did the first time: good, but not good enough. She did some good things out there, but opponent Genevieve Morton did more. That was exactly the way the judges scored it, and so Lewin dropped her second decision in a row. Am I worried? No. Michelle put forth a good effort in both contests, and it's clear she's capable of competing. But it would be nice to see her break into the win column sometime soon.

Once last piece of business before I finish up. In the last issue of TKO, Hellfire fighter Kim Kardashian issued an Open Fight Night challenge against RSI's Torrie Wilson. When I asked Torrie if she wanted to accept the challenge, she said simply, "Sure." I don't know if she was bored, or if she was just trying to keep from being caught up in Hellfire's continuing war of words against us, but whatever. The only thing I'll add is that, unlike the approach her stable owner has employed against us, I found Ms. Kardashian to be pretty professional... saying her reason was that she viewed Torrie as a challenge for her. Though Torrie's been on the rise since joining RSI, Kim's record clearly shows she's more than just a pretty face. This should be quite a fight, and I'm looking forward to it.

Coming up next time, it's the women of the FAC. Unfortunately, even though some of RSI's best will be in action, injuries all over the place will mean no title fights. With top challenger Christobelle Grierson-Ryrie sidelined, featherweight champion Emma Watson will face a tough non-title challenge against the ILF's Xenia Deli. Injury also will keep bantamweight champion Vikki Blows out of action, so Julianne Hough will compete against Lyndsy Fonseca. With Hellfire using the idea of beating RSI at it's motivation, no doubt Lyndsy will have some extra incentive. Meanwhile, with light heavyweight champion Erin Heatherton unable to go, Aly Michalka will have to settle for another showdown against old foe Brooklyn Decker. Aly is always looking for a challenge, and she certainly knows she'll find that in Brooklyn. But she's worked her ass off to make it into the top contender's slot, and the disappointment of not getting her title opportunity is apparent. But, don't expect that to mean a letdown is coming. She's worked too hard to get here. She's not about to give it up without a fight.
