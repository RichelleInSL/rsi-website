Sticking To It

It wasn't always like this for Emily VanCamp. She's always been good. Good enough to fight her way to the top of her division a number of times. But each time, just as it seemed she was primed to make a run at the belt, the tide would turn and she'd drift back down the rankings. She'd shown she had the skills, but she couldn't seem to get over that final hurdle and get her hands on some hardware. She needed help, and RSI needed someone to lead our lightweight group.

I have no reservations about saying it was a match made in heaven.

Emily quickly discovered how much she could learn by teaching, and we quickly learned she was the real deal. Not only did she surge her way to the title, but there was no slow fade afterward. Now, she sticks like glue.

Or, more accurately, like a thorn in her competitors' sides.

Emily is not a contender anymore, nor is she just another champion... she is one of the most elite fighters in the UMMA. She is sweet and fun, but she's far from soft. She has a confidence to her... a steel spine that comes from constantly competing against the very best. Irina Sheik. Ali Larter. Having held her own against opponents like that, Em knows now that she can rise up and face whatever challenge lies before her.

Tonight, a new chapter opened for Emily. Apparently, the forces of fate didn't feel her vicious and repetitive battles with Irina were enough of a challenge for the Revenge star, so the UMMA franchise shift brought Ashley Greene to the ESL... making it a three-way battle for the belt. Though Greene was new to the division, this was not the first time these two have met. In fact, they faced off twice before, both resulting in landslide victories for Greene. The first is easy to write-off, as it comes from a time when Emily was still struggling to find herself. But, the latter battle happened only last year. So clearly, Ashley had history on her side.

Luckily, Emily's memory doesn't seem to be very good.

Showing no sign that she had the slightest worry about handling Greene, Emily was absolute perfection as she turned the tables on her new rival. Ashley aggressively tried to put the pressure on early, but Em ate it up with her defense and seemed to score with every counter she threw. Greene grew increasingly frustrated with each failed attack, and Emily grew progressively bold. By the two minute mark, VanCamp was in command, and Ashley was just trying to hang on. That was too much time, and, after amazingly withstanding two straight kicks that caught her flush on the cheek, Greene finally succumbed when Em blasted her for a third time.

This fight was a powerful demonstration of all that Emily can do, and she admitted afterward that was exactly what it felt like.

"Everything was clicking," said the woman who is now the top ranked lightweight in the UMMA. "I felt like I couldn't do anything wrong. It was a good feeling, and I wish I could make that happen more often."

Fact is, Em didn't do anything wrong. Her defense was rock solid, and her accuracy was insane. It's hard to lose when your opponent can't touch you and you're hitting over 80% of your strikes.

Speaking of rock solid... Ashley's lack of offensive success didn't stop Emily from being impressed with her toughness.

"I don't how she withstood those kicks," Em said. "The first one, maybe. But I land two in a row, and she's still standing there? It's like she's made of granite."

With this victory, Emily has clearly cemented her place with the best lightweights in the sport. Sharing a division with two of those elite fighters may make her life difficult, but Em's up for the challenge. With all the work she's put in to get here... believe me, she's not going anywhere.

Julianne Hough's meteoric rise started with a handshake. When I approached her about joining my new stable, she had only ten wins in her twenty-six career fights. She was a decent fighter who wanted to prove to herself and to everyone that she could be more. I was a new manager, whose leadership experience was precisely zero. So, you can say I took a chance on her, but I think she took a much bigger chance on me.

I don't think either of us has any regrets.

Jules won four of her first five fights for RSI, with the lone loss coming in a terrible mismatch against Keira Knightley. She said at the time that being part of a stable made her feel stronger and more confident than ever. That sounds reasonable, given what RSI is today. But back then, we didn't have a clue what we were doing. The leadership group consisted of... well... me. Danneel Harris was on board, but our relationship was nothing like it is today. Steph McMahon was also with us, but she was simply one of the fighters in the stable. If someone had told me then what she would become, I'd have laughed at them.

In other words, I don't really know what made Jules so confident. But I don't doubt that she meant what she said, because what she did next astounds me to this day.

Julianne Hough, owner of a career record of fourteen wins and seventeen losses, went into the 2013 Red Carpet tournament seeded twenty-fourth out of thirty-two fighters.

She came out as champion.

They highlight of the tournament for Ms. Hough? A third round matchup that resulted in a stunning, end-to-end, no doubt about it crushing of... Keira Knightley.

Trust me. A party was had after that event, the likes of which had never been seen before.

The win rocketed Jules to second in the rankings. So, while the rest of us were delirious about RSI's first big success, Julianne was very busy training. Less than a month later, she faced divisional champion Vikki Blows in a non-title bout.

And, hot damn... she won.

Next stop for Julianne? The Lingerie Invitational tournament.

Seeding? Seventh.

Result? Champion... with a run that included victories over Blows, Carrie Underwood, and Kristin Kreuk.

Now, things were just getting ridiculous.

The win over Blows established the two as rivals, meaning the title would be on the line the next time they met. It was a chance to secure the crown jewel to top off one of the greatest turnarounds ever.

Meanwhile, Vikki realized things were slipping away from her, so she gave up her independent status and signed on with the top stable of the day in RWB.

The move didn't surprise me, and it didn't give me much pause. How much difference could RWB really make?

Good thing Danni was there to save my ass... and Jules.

Danni saw the signing for what it was. Vikki's game was going to be totally revamped, and Jules needed to adapt or get destroyed.

I told Danni she was crazy. She told me I had my head up ass.

That was the first time she'd ever said anything like that to me. The first of many, as it turns out.

Danni wasn't taking no for an answer, so I relented and turned her loose on an unsuspecting Julianne. Weeks of intense and brutal training sessions between the two followed. Jules would later say she thought Danni was trying to kill her.

She didn't die. But she did win.

Danni had been right on the money, and the prep work allowed Jules to sail to a dominant win and secured her the divisional crown she was after.

What the hell does all this have to do with tonight? Well, since then, Julianne has gone on to establish herself among the top bantamweights in the game. Her title wars with Vikki have been legendary, and the two are fan favorites, for sure.

But tonight was to be the dawning of a new era, for Jules. The wave of insanity created by the franchise changes had swept both she and Blows into the ESL. There, they found the one fighter Jules has yet to solve reliably... the one and only Keira Knightley. With Julianne having to surrender the FAC crown due to the move, she would get first crack at taking the ESL strap away from the Pirates of the Caribbean star.

These two don't get to see each other often. They've met once since 2013, with Knightley avenging that loss at last year's Lingerie tourney. So, the rivalry was close to even, with Keira possessing a 2-1 advantage.

Without question, the hype for tonight's matchup was huge. Too often, a highly touted matchup between two top-tier fighters ends up being a one-sided route. It's no one's fault when that happens. The women on that level have the skills and the power to end a fight in seconds. One mistake, and it's all over for someone.

Watching one fighter destroy another makes for great headlines and visuals, and it's the kind of thing most fans want to see. But there are a few of us who appreciate a real struggle. A fight decided on pure skill, rather than power and chance. And we got one of those, tonight.

For three rounds, the two went at it in an epic chess match. Both showed strong defense and superb ring awareness, as well as an astounding ability to recover from bad situations. The anticipation was immense, as the crowd waited for one or the other to take control. But each time it looked like one was taking command, the other would fight her way back into it. It was a beautiful and entertaining battle for anyone who understands what goes into making a fighter great.

The only disappointment was that it couldn't go on forever.

In the end, the judges decided Jules had done enough to take the win. As the owner of Julianne's stable, I'm very happy with the decision. As a fan... I've never seen a fight that so desperately screamed to be called a draw.

If you make me choose, I think Jules was just a little bit better. But I don't think it was enough for Keira to lose her title. Of course, it's not my decision to make, and I don't envy the judges who had to make the call.

When it was over, an exhausted Julianne talked about what it was like in the cage with the UMMA's top bantamweight.

"She gives you nothing," the Safe Haven actress said. "Never an angle to work, or an opening to exploit. Every point I got was excruciating."

For those who thought the lack of effective offense in the bout was boring, Jules politely disagrees.

"The whole fight had this feeling to it... like any second the tide could turn. Keira is that dangerous, and I'm proud to have held her off the way I did."

And how does she feel about the way it ended?

"Unfulfilling," she said. "I'm happy with to have the title, but it doesn't feel quite right. It was like every second was a struggle and we just ran out of seconds, so they had to pick. Neither of us really accomplished what we wanted in that cage."

There's only one solution when a fight ends this way, and Julianne knows exactly what it is.

"We need to do it, again," she said.

Soon enough, Jules. Soon enough.

Things did not go quite so well for RSI's other bantamweight contender here in Orlando, but it's hard to blame Ashley Tisdale. After all, opponent Lyndsey Fonseca is an excellent fighter, who's known for many things. But matwork and submission skills are not among them. So, when Lyndsey shot in for a surprise takedown, well... that just wasn't the fighter Tiz had prepared for. If there's anything I can find fault with it's that Ashley's ground game was sloppy once the takedown happened. That's something she's been working on, and she should have been better than that. But Tiz had a game plan, and the takedown threw it all out of whack. It took her too long to regroup, and Fonseca was quick to take advantage and secure the submission.

Ash was still visibly stunned at her post-fight news conference.

"I really don't know where that came from," she said of her opponents grappling prowess. "Lyndsey is a dangerous opponent. I knew I had to be at my best, and I was expecting a tough battle. I had studied her past fights... trying to be as prepared as I could be. But there was nothing there that would have prepared me for this."

Surprise or no, Ashley made clear that Fonseca deserves full credit for the victory.

"Her job is to win. It doesn't matter how. She saw a chance and did something different. It's on me to deal with it, and I didn't. So, it comes out as a great move by her, and I have to accept that I screwed up."

That's just one more thing to love about Tiz... she's got no room for excuses in her life.

Everybody loves a good underdog story, and Jennifer Stano's has been one of the best the UMMA has had recently. When she beat Doutzen Kroes at last year's Hit the Beach tournament, it was like she'd done the impossible. People were so stunned, they didn't know what to say. When she did it again at the regular ESL event a few months later, people simply couldn't stop talking.

It was great fun, and it didn't stop there. Stano ran deep into the next two tournaments, succumbing to Kroes and Britney Spears in the respective championship matches. This was a true rookie, competing with the legends of her weight class. It suddenly seemed like Jen was a once-in-a-lifetime talent, who was going to take the UMMA by storm.

Then, reality came to visit.

Jen's success had propelled her up the rankings much faster than she was ready to deal with. While she had shown clear flashes of brilliance, competing with the elites on a constant basis was not something she was prepared to do. The losses mounted and her ranking dropped quickly. I can't say I'm surprised about the way she's fallen back to Earth. I also can't say I'm disappointed.

If Jen is truly going to be one of the sport's elite, she needs to do it right. That means continuing to prove herself against opponents who have experience similar to hers. She needs to be tested against fighters with different skill sets and styles, and she needs to build her ranking up the right way.

Tonight's opponent, Kitty Lea, is exactly the kind of challenge I'm talking about. In her young career, Kitty has shown a balanced attack, blending good striking skills with aggressive submission moves. If Stano had any question about how dangerous Lea can be, stablemate Jennifer Lawrence can tell her all about it. After all, Kitty has forced J-Law to tap out twice recently, with both wins coming in under two minutes.

So, Kitty may be relatively new, but she knows how to bring the hurt.

Stano proved up to the challenge, though... using her striking game to keep Lea off-balance, and prevent her from getting to those brutal submissions that have worked for her in the past. It was a good fight and a good win for Jen. But, next comes the bad news...

These two only got together due to a rash of injuries in the division. Jen's ranking hasn't fallen quite as far as I'd thought, so she now finds herself back in the top contender slot... meaning another date with Kroes is on the horizon.

Well, there are quite a few special events coming up before then, so we'll see if the rankings hold. Meanwhile, no slacking of for you, Jen. It's obvious your life isn't going to get any easier anytime soon.

MMA is a cruel sport. One minute, you feel like you can do no wrong. Then, a single, stupid mistake has you feeling like a fool.

Such was the case for Amanda Seyfried, tonight. Her game looked sharp early, and she seemed to be getting the better of Rosie Huntington-Whiteley. Amanda had already knocked Rosie to the mat once, and Huntington-Whitely had just missed badly with a desperate kick, causing her to fall on her own. Amanda moved in to finish, and Rosie launched an upkick to fend her off. The kick landed perfectly on Amanda's chin, and, just like that, what felt like a sure-fire victory turned into an embarrassing defeat.

It shouldn't have happened this way. Leaving herself open the way she did goes against everything Amanda is trained to do. And no one knows it better than she does.

"I was stupid," the Ted 2 actress said. "I can't believe I let that happen, and I can't believe I let the win get away from me."

If you think Seyfried was feeling a little deflated after her error, then try this analogy on for size.

"It was like losing to a stranded turtle."

I don't think she meant that exactly the way it sounded, Rosie. Mandy's just pissed off, is all.

Shake it off, Amanda. Team Warfare is coming, and you have a very difficult opponent on tap.

Anna Kendrick came up a little short tonight, but her performance is nothing to hang her head over. This was her second divisional matchup with Emmy Rossum. And, thought end result wasn't any better, Anna was.

In the last fight, Emmy seemed to be in control throughout. Kendrick hung on bravely, but it really felt like she was just waiting for the end. As you may recall, that end finally came in the form of a painful submission hold that elicited some off-color remarks from the Pitch Perfect star after the fight.

Tonight wasn't like that, at all. Anna gave as good as she got, and I don't think the decision was as clear cut as the judges made it seem. Though Emmy threw more strikes, Anna was far more effective with hers. Anna also had more submission chances, though she was never able to sink one in and go for the finish. But when a fight is this close, it's usually the more active fighter that gets the nod, which explains how the decision went Rossum's way.

Though anyone with eyes could see Anna was much improved tonight, she may need a little time to realize it for herself.

"Slapped around again," she said of Rossum's repeat victory. "At least she didn't make me beg her to stop, this time."

Her self-deprecating humor may serve Anna well in the near future. If she really wants to make it big, she's going to have to follow the same route Jen Stano is being forced to take. Work your way up, learning as you go and brushing off the setbacks that are bound to go with that.

With more than triple the number of fights under her belt, Rossum has a lot more experience than Anna does. She's supposed to win a fight like this. But Anna made her earn it, tonight. It's clear she's improved a lot in just a few months. I have no doubt she'll continue to improve as time goes on.

Speaking of the power of experience, Joss Stone reminded Brittany Tacy that they are alike in ranking only. In a repeat of the one-sided destruction the fitness star suffered in their last fight, Joss was in control from beginning to end... simply pummeling Tacy throughout the round and a half the beating lasted. If anything, this was worse than last time, as Brittany wasn't able to get anything going, offensively.

This is simply a situation where the rankings aren't reflective of the talent involved. Brittany isn't ready for a fighter of Joss's experience, and I really don't understand how it is that they're ranked so closely. Hopefully, they'll get a little separation after tonight, giving Brittany the opportunity to compete with fighters on her own level.

Krystal Forscutt continued her habit of knocking off divisional rivals with a win over Ciara, tonight. Since signing with RSI, Krystal is 11-2 in divisional matchups. She's suffered a few setbacks in the special events and PPVs, and that's held her ranking down. But, there's nothing wrong that. She's winning the fights she's supposed to win. That's all that matters.

It's nice to see Allira Cohrs back in the win column, but I wish it didn't have to happen like this. Opponent Emma Glover suffered a serious rib injury less than a minute into their match. The damage will force Glover to the sideline for a while, and, unfortunately, there's nothing left to do but wish her a speedy recovery.

And finally, Michelle Lewin has worked her record back to even with a commanding victory over Jenna Pietersen. After a tough start to her career, it's good to see Lewin having some success. She came into the league with some heavy expectations, but she's been able to bear the weight so far. Winning will only make that easier.

Well, that's all for this event. The FAC will be in action next, and that means it's time for RSI's "placeholder" champions to prove themselves. Alyson Michalka and Jennifer Lawrence were both elevated to their respective title spots when the champions of their divisions were pushed up to the ESL. Neither has been willing to call themselves a champion, and both refused to accept the belts when they arrived. Aly was actually angry, saying Erin Heatherton should have been made champion, as she's held the title in the past and she has the edge in their head-to-head matchups. Meanwhile, I'm under strict orders not to display the stable's copy of either belt in RSI's trophy case. I'm not usually very good at taking orders, but I was willing to go along with this one. I know what it means to earn a title in the cage, and I know there isn't a fighter out there who would consider herself a champion doing it any other way. Well, Aly and J-Law will both have the chance to prove themselves when we get to Miami.

Meanwhile, I'm off to... fill in for Dragon and put a release together for the Den. You mean to tell to me I'm this close to the Mouse, but I can't go yet? There oughta be a law against this sort of thing.
