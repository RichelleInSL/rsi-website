FRESH FACES COMING TO RSI

Although RSI had no one in the cage at the Team Warfare event, that doesn't mean we haven't been busy. Seeking to fill out our already impressive roster, RSI has added five fighters who are brand new to the UMMA.

As a female MMA management organization, we are often forced to seek out women with the skills and dedication to succeed in the MMA arena, and then basically convince them it's something they want to do. Even the most athletic of women often shy away from the fight game, and convincing them otherwise can be difficult. On the other hand, sometimes the right woman simply falls in your lap. Tucked away in the Feburuary 2013 edition of FIGHT! Magazine, we found Allira Cohrs. Though Allira was clearly featured as a centerfold rather than a fighter, we couldn't help noticing the list of her favorite things. Nestled in there with dark chocolate and blueberries were weight training, boxing and UFC. We wondered just how serious her love of MMA was, so we figured we'd make a simple phone call. The next day, Allira was on a flight up from Australia's Gold Coast. She arrived at our facility a few days later, gear bag in hand. After the recent departures of Rhian Sugden and Danielle Lloyd, RSI was left without a competitor in the I Hate You super heavyweight division. So, Allira comes along at just the right time. We're excited, and so is she. "When I got the phone call from Richelle, I really thought somebody was messing with me," she said. "I mean, an MMA organization totally devoted to women? I never imagined there could be a place where women's MMA is taken so seriously." Yeah... we didn't tell her about the breast smother or stripping submissions, yet.

Another fighter who basically fell out of the sky and landed in the RSI offices is Floridian Brittany Tacy. And, this one didn't even require a phone call, as Brittany found us. A naturally gifted athlete, Brittany knew early on that she was not going to be satisfied with "girly" things. Middle school activities such as baton twirling and cheerleading quickly gave way to varsity weightlifting and flag football in high school. Soon she realized she was a workout nut, and she decided to make a career out of it. "I have always been obsessed with working out," she says. "I wanted a 6-pack since I can remember." That obsession led her to the world of competitive fitness competitions. It was a clear match for her, and she's never looked back. "I like the diet, and I like the discipline," she says, noting that her weight doesn't fluctuate more than a meager five pounds between off-season and competitions. So, why MMA? "Well, I've got this body. I've worked hard to get it. Now, it's time to find out what I can do with it. The truth is, I'm a naturally competitive person. The fitness competition lifestyle is fine, but you're mostly competing with yourself. The UMMA will give me the chance to really compete with the other women and prove myself." Any questions on what we see in Brittany? I thought not.

Jennifer Stano's background is certainly not what you'd expect of an MMA fighter. A life-long Californian, her early career as a model and fitness athlete certainly fits the bill. But, since then, her life has come together in ways most people can only dream about. She caught the eye of a man who is not only heir to the fortunes of the 2nd largest Coca-Cola bottler in the world, but is also a highly successful businessman in his own right. However, though she has traded on her "trophy wife" status for TV purposes, it's not really a term that applies to her, at all. Not only has she continued her modeling career, but she is also the CEO and chief designer of her own high-end swimwear company. So, why would a woman with a life like that risk pain and injury in the cage? "I need to be challenged," said a clearly excited Stano. "Things have gone very well, for me. And, success is great. Believe me. But, I had to face challenges and risk a lot to get here. And now, the challenge and risk are basically gone. The UMMA brings those elements back, for me." Not exactly the type of inspiration most MMA fighters posesss, but Jennifer has shown us she has the skills and drive to succeed in the octagon. Simply put, she doesn't like to lose. We don't either, so we expect this to be a good match for all involved.

The first of our Texas-based rookies, Holly Bricken certainly falls into the "needed convincing" category we discussed earlier. Though she was a naturally active person, Holly didn't realize the importance of staying fit and eating healthy until she went off to college. While most new college students have to deal with unwanted weight gain, Holly referrs to her "Freshman 15" as the "Freshman 25." "I was so busy studying and adjusting to college life, I really didn't notice how bad it got," she said. "Then, I looked in the mirror one day and thought, 'No. This needs to stop.'" Holly not only got herself back in shape, but she discovered a love of working out. Still, though, she didn't immediately see it as a career. After graduation, she went to work as a kindergarten teacher, and the backward slide started again. Not only did she start gaining weight, but she also realized she simply missed working out. So, she decided she didn't want to just hit the gym after work. She wanted it to BE her work. Now, as a competitive fitness athlete and marathon runner, she's seeking out a new challenge in the UMMA. Though she lacks power, she certainly has the conditioning and work ethic required to succeed, and her RSI team is excited to have the opportunity to make it happen.

Last, we have Texan Marzia Prince. While Marzia is certainly older than most UMMA rookies, she doesn't let that sort of thing bother her. "I get a ton of email on what I do to look good 'for my age'.", she said. "For those that are wondering how old I am, I am 37 years old and damn proud of it!" Well, let me just say that, as a person who is definitely older than a spring chicken myself, Marzia looks damn good for any age. Most would think her age would scare her away from entering into the fierce world of competitive fighting. But, Marzia has a unique and positive view on aging. "I used to think aging was a bad thing, but as I live life every day, I love growing older. I actually love the life experience and knowledge I have right now. I love how confident I am at this age, and that wasn’t the case in my twenties." Does she worry about competing at this level? "Sure, a little bit. Clearly, the women here know how to handle themselves, and they've been doing it a lot longer than I have," says Prince. "I know it's going to be difficult to succeed. But, I wasn't looking for easy. I want this, and I'll do whatever it takes to succeed here." Now, THAT'S and attitude we can work with.

Well, that's all, for now, but rumor has it that RSI will have more roster additions in the near future. Stay tuned.
