My parents stepped out in the hall with the doctor, leaving me alone with the Three Amigos.

Apparently, they'd been waiting for a moment like this.

"Did you really do what they say you did?" Frank asked.

There wasn't anything remotely related to that question that I wanted to talk about, so I lied.

"I don't know what I did," I said. "The whole thing was crazy."

Turned out, the lie wasn't worth the three seconds I spent coming up with it.

Frank was not exactly a member of the Honor Roll. He'd be a solid C student if he could spell "C," and he would honest-to-god forget his ass if it wasn't attached. But he'd apparently spent the last few days compiling and memorizing a complete catalog of the damage I'd done.

"They said you broke one girl's face. Smashed her into a wall and then used her head to smash one of the others."

I didn't say anything.

"They said you broke both their skulls."

"Broke?"

"Well one, anyway. Said you..."

He turned and looked at the other two.

"What was the word?"

"Fractured," Jim said.

"Yeah, fractured," Frank agreed. "Said you fractured one of their skulls and broke the bone around other one's eye."

Again, I was silent, but he just kept going.

"Said you caved one of their chests in and tore another's arm apart. Crushed one of their throats. Said you broke her coccyx."

I couldn't possibly stay silent after that.

"Christ, Frank... you're a coccyx," I said.

"Well, that's what they said!" he protested.

"I guarantee they didn't because that's the bone in your ass, you dipstick."

"Well, it was something with an X!"

"The larynx?"

"Yeah, that's it!" he exclaimed like he'd just discovered penicillin.

"You're such a tool," I said. "Those aren't even close to the same thing."

"Well, it's what I heard," he mumbled.

I shook my head at him some more.

We couldn't be related. It just wasn't possible. Somebody made a mistake.

My mind started doing a comparison between what he'd said and what I remembered. The scenes played through like a VHS tape on fast-forward as I checked off the boxes on his list.

And when I got to the end, I realized something was missing.

"What about the arm?" I stupidly said out loud.

"What arm?"

I hadn't meant to say it, but since I had...

"The one with the throat," I said. "I broke her arm."

"Oh, yeah," he said. Then, "Wait, I thought you didn't remember."

"Yeah, Frank," I said as I narrowed my eyes at him. "I crippled three people and then I forgot about it."

Something about the way I'd worded it brought the conversation to a halt, and we were all quiet for a minute or so.

I wondered what they were thinking.

"There's no way you did all that," he said.

Did he that because he didn't believe it, or because he didn't want to?

Instead, I just sighed.

"Cops say I did it?"

He gave a little nod.

"Doctors say I did it?"

Another nod.

"Then I guess I did it, right?"

We were all quiet again.

"No way," Frank eventually mumbled.

Despite the fact that a large part of me was ashamed of what I'd done, I suddenly sensed that I was missing an opportunity to make it work for me.

"Yes way, Frank, and you might want to remember that the next time you think about putting your hands on me."

He just sort of looked at me. Maybe searching for a sign that I was kidding.

I decided it was best to let them wonder.

Tom suddenly spoke up.

"I heard a couple of doctors talking in the hall," he began.

Oh, good. My laundry list of destruction was going to get an addendum.

"They said you probably saved her life."

"Who's life?" I asked in bewilderment.

"I don't know her name," he said. "The lunatic."

This was a term that could have correctly been applied to everyone who was in the courtyard that day, including me. But I took an educated guess at who he was talking about.

"Christine?"

"Yeah," he said. "They said she probably would have died if you hadn't knocked her out."

"How the hell...?"

"When you broke her throat, it made it real hard for her to breathe. They figured she was probably freaking out, which would have made it worse. But everything slowed down after you booted her in the face. It was like calming her down. It made her breathing go easier, and she didn't need as much air anymore 'cause she wasn't flopping around all crazy."

This was not exactly a positive development. It didn't make anything I'd done better, and I didn't know how I felt about being Christine's accidental savior. I hadn't tried to kill her, but I don't think I'd have been too broken up if she died, either.

At least then I wouldn't have to testify.

The silence returned.

Frank looked like he had something to say, but didn't. Tom looked at his big brother and decided to go along.

And then Jim spoke out from behind them.

"Dad said they messed you up bad," he said.

The look on his face said things I don't think any of them would have said to me out loud. Like he was mourning his dead sister who just happened not to be dead.

It made me wonder exactly what they knew... and just how bad things had gotten while I was out.

Hell, I didn't really know how bad things were now.
