Today was not really a great day.

I mean, I don't often have great days. Or even good ones. But this one had been measurably worse than average.

It started off like all the others. I got out of the house on time. Caught the bus without issue. Rode in stony silence. Sighed as I trudged past the sign outside the main entrance that read, "Oceanview High — Dedicated to building respectful and responsible citizens and empowering all learners!"

Yay.

Homeroom was fine. It only lasts eleven minutes. Not much time for anything to go wrong. But as I picked up my way-too-heavy bag, my shoulder yelled at me a little. A less than subtle reminder that my brothers were assholes.

I've asked my parents how things worked out this way. How they managed to have four kids but only one girl. My father's answer was mostly mathematical. By pure average, four should have meant two boys and two girls, but averages don't work that way. He had only one sibling himself - a brother. That's just how these things go. Theoretically, there was a family out there somewhere with three girls and a boy to balance out this torture to which I was being subjected.

I didn't care much about the math, but I often experienced a strong desire to be a part of that theoretical family instead of this one.

My mother, on the other hand, was also one of four. Like me, she was the oldest. And then came her three brothers. All at once. Triplets. So, she pretty much just told me to shut the fuck up and stop whining.

Which, you know... was fair enough.

My brothers weren't triplets, but they did seem to share a brain. And as near as I could tell, every thought they had was stupid.

The injury had happened when I walked in the door yesterday. I had barely taken more than a few steps when Frank appeared in front of me, smiling in a way I found instantly suspicious.

I let my school bag go immediately. My instincts automatically telling me to free up my hands while my mind considered the evidence.

He looks like he's going to do something stupid. Which means...

Tom and Jimmy appeared from behind the door and grabbed my arms — Tom on the left and Jimmy on the right. I had known they'd be there because of the whole shared brain thing. If one was going to do something stupid, they were all going to do something stupid. I called this The Law of the Morons, and the law was immutable. Which made my current situation partially my fault. It took me at least three seconds too long to realize what was coming.

"Get the fuck off me, idiots!"

I started to struggle but they held fast. Despite being younger, all three were bigger than me. A product of human evolution. The males of the species did the hard work, so they were naturally larger. I had a lot of issues with this reality, starting with the fact that fucking with me was not the reason nature had gifted them this advantage.

"Get OFF!"

I surged as I yelled - an impulse that was innate to me. Getting pissed off and letting my rage fly gave me strength noticeably greater than my size would indicate, but it was not enough for this. There were two of them, both appreciably bigger and both knowing full well the move was coming.

Like I said... it was innate.

This was all a scene that had played out many times before, and we were proceeding quickly through the stages. Avoidance was first, and I had blown that one. Rage surge had come up short. So, now it was time for the most useless one of all: negotiation.

Which really wasn't the right word for how this always goes.

I settled down and stopped struggling. Frank squared around in front of me.

"What the fuck do you want, asshole?"

"C'mon Erin," he said. "You know what we want."

Which wasn't a hundred percent true. They were stupid, and the motivations of stupid people can be unpredictable. But I had a strong suspicion.

"You can't have the ball, assholes! He gave it to me!"

The ball in question was a baseball, signed by a significant portion of the New York Yankees roster.

Don Mattingly, Dave Winfield, Dave Righetti, Ron Guidry, and the newest addition — Rickey Henderson. There were other names, but those were the ones we cared about. Henderson's arrival had given us the complete package. We had the pitching. We had the hitting. And we finally had the speed. This team was finally going to start winning.

They didn't. By the time the Yankees' World Series drought — which lasted all throughout our childhood — ended, all five of those players would be gone. But as far as we were concerned, they were the greatest players in baseball back then.

The ball had come from our father, who had gotten it from somebody at work. That was as far as we could trace its authenticity, and it was actually much further than we cared about.

Dad had given the ball to me. Because before the three idiots had finally learned not to pee in their pants, it had just been Dad and me. I was the one who had suffered through the team's brief yet abominable period the longest. Dad would tell me they were the greatest team in baseball. That they had won countless titles. More than twice as many as any other team in history.

I never really saw evidence of any of that during the 80s, but I sat through the games with him anyway.

Mo, Larry and Curly had started joining us at some point, but they wouldn't even have been Yankee fans had I not been one first. And, to be honest, their arrival ruined everything. So, they weren't getting the ball. End of story.

Why they wanted it wasn't exactly clear. Maybe they thought it was going to be valuable someday. Or, more likely, there wasn't any specific reason. Dad had chosen me and not them. I had the ball and they didn't. Stuff like that would be more than enough to get the shared brain thinking dumb thoughts that it then could not let go of.

"You don't deserve it," Frank said. "You'll just draw little hearts all over it or color it pink."

Because this is what you do, apparently. When it's three to one in your favor, you play the gender card.

I don't know how they learned it — certainly not from Dad — but I guess it's good that they did, right? It's important to know how the world works and "men are superior" was still the principle by which it did so, even if you weren't allowed to say it out loud anymore.

Of course, it was also specifically a stupid thing to say to me, on a couple of different levels.

First of all, I was never like that. Yes, I was a girl, but I was not girly.

I played sports: soccer, basketball, track and, yes... baseball.

Some of these sports had gone away when I got to high school. Basketball hadn't worked out, partially because I didn't quite fit the criteria anymore. Oceanview was a big school, and the pool of athletes they had to pull from was enormous. So, even at nearly six feet tall, I would have been on the short side. Also, it may have been possible that I just wasn't good enough. I had been good at the rec level — I was good at all the sports I tried — but I'd never been anything special

Baseball went away for more annoying yet practical reasons. Girls couldn't play on the baseball team in high school, and I didn't want to play softball.

Which just proves my point.

I wasn't girly, and I was rarely drawn to anything defined as such. I didn't have a stuffed animal menagerie, I didn't draw hearts and I didn't even like pink.

Meanwhile, on the other side... did I mention my brothers were morons?

If they got hold of the ball, it would never again be recognized as a ball. In less than a month, the cover would be torn off, the names would be half erased, there'd be a pile of weird string on the floor and a little rubber sphere rolling around that looked like something that fell out of a dog's toy.

Because THAT'S the kind of shit boys are actually good for.

"You're not getting the ball, now get the fuck off me!"

He took a step forward.

"Frank, I swear to god..."

But he kept coming. And then he reached out toward me.

Why?

Who knows? He had done all kinds of things to me in the past. He'd grabbed individual fingers and bent my knuckles. Sometimes, he grabbed a whole hand and squeezed, rolling the knuckles against each other.

Sometimes, he'd just tickle me.

Which I fucking hated.

So, fuck him.

I waited until he got close — leaned out and committed. One foot fully in front of the other, with his weight out a little bit in front of both. His eyes carefully watching my right foot.

Then I kicked him in the nuts with my left.

I couldn't generate the same force with that foot, but it didn't seem to matter. Though I did not have testicles myself, I'd taken health class. My understanding was that the threshold for incapacitation was low, and all of my experiments had borne this out.

He didn't yell or even cry out. He just kind of moaned and fell over.

The other two watched him fall with their mouths hanging open. I tried to take advantage. Getting my feet back on the floor, I tried again to rip myself free. But they knew what would happen if I got loose, and they came out of their stupor pretty quickly when they realized what I was doing. Tom tightened his grip but Jim wasn't quite as quick. He realized he didn't have me and tried to regrip just as I surged forward. That's how the injury happened. I pulled myself forward with all the force I could muster while he did the same in the other direction, and the net sum of those forces seemed to make my shoulder very unhappy. But I honestly didn't feel it right then. With my adrenaline and fury both set to max, I was definitely in "hit now, hurt later" mode.

Meanwhile, they hadn't saved themselves. What they had coming was still going to come. I just had to find another way, and "go with the flow" seemed like the best approach.

I got my feet back under me and started pushing. If they didn't want to let me go forward, we'd all just go backward. Stumbling and bumbling the whole way.

The truth was, this was as likely to hurt me as it was them. Which was nothing new. "Reckless abandon" was another technique I used frequently.

Tom — being the "smart" one — let go of me and got himself righted. Which worked out well because it let me put my focus on Jim.

One step, two steps...

I rotated a little, so I was driving straight into him, leading with the same shoulder I'd just injured.

Three, four, five... contact.

We hit the front door with a full head of steam. All of my weight went straight through my shoulder and into his chest. Our combined weight then went into the door, which had no capacity to absorb it so it reflected it right back into him.

Two down, one to go.

Despite the size difference, the odds were now in my favor. Because I give no quarter. And when you come at me three to one, you shouldn't expect any.

It ends with me either victorious or dead, and what happens to you as a result is not my problem.

Tom knew this, so he went backward instead of forward. Which really didn't help him because there was no place to go. Behind him was the living room wall. He could try to run left, but the crippled Frank was lying in his path.

He backed up to the wall. I pulled my right arm back like I was going to slug him in the face and took a half step forward. He put his arms up to cover himself. I brought my arm back down, turned my shoulder forward and accelerated through the two or three feet between us.

The impact was similar to what I'd done to Jim. There was risk involved because the wall was not the door. Walls were thinner, and if I put Tom through it, I was going to have to answer to Dad. But either the sheetrock held or we found a beam because all the force ended up in Tom, who understandably ended up on the floor.

And that made three.

And yes, I had in fact used the same shoulder again. So, maybe we didn't know exactly when or how the injury happened, but it was still their fault.

The twin impacts had shaken the house, which caused Mom to FINALLY realize there was a problem.

"What the hell is going on in there?"

Tom was the first to answer.

"Erin is hitting us!"

THIS, from the smart one.

I kicked him in the stomach, using the right foot this time.

Meanwhile, Mom — she of the triplet brothers — asked, "Were you fucking with her?"

I didn't answer and no one else could. Mom took the silence as a sign that whatever was going on was over, and that was good enough for her.

I went over to Frank and pressed him into the floor with my right knee as I knelt down next to him.

"Ask me for the ball again," I said through my teeth, "and see how far up your ass I shove it."

I gathered my bag onto the good shoulder and left them all laying there.

I had homework to do.

And a souvenir baseball to stare at.

Which I could do because it belonged to me.

And all of that nonsense from yesterday explains why it hurts to lift my bag today.

I put it back down, hoisted it up onto the other shoulder then sprinted off to history class.

All I was trying to do was get to class.

Come to think of it, that's all I really ever want to do here at Oceanside Regional. Get from class to class. Get my work done - with the emphasis on efficiency rather than quality. Then get the hell out of here.

I have a few people I talk to. Not necessarily friends, but we manage to tolerate each other. That's kind of rare for me. I don't care for people all that much. Despite the just-enough-and-no-more amount of effort I put in, my grades are fairly good. I seem to understand things better than most. That makes me useful to others, but most of them are of no use to me. And I'm not big on charity.

So when the bell rang to signal the end of Language Arts, my only goal was to get to Physics. Down the front hall to the commons area. Stop in the bathroom. Hover while I peed. Then down the back hall and up the stairs. Same thing I do every day at this time. No chit-chat. No loitering. No drama. No problem.

Only today, it didn't go as planned.

The gym is a problem. In fact, it's not called the gym. It's officially called The Field House. Which sounds pretentious. Mostly because it is, but it's also appropriate. Inside there are three full-size basketball courts. The main court is actually farthest to the left, with a huge twelve foot high collapsible bleacher along the side and smaller bleachers on each end that could also be hidden away. When all of the bleachers were stowed, there was plenty of room on each end to set up a volleyball area or whatever else the gym teachers wanted to throw at us. And the whole thing was encircled by an indoor track.

There was no way to tuck something that big in among the classrooms. It could only exist in a space by itself. Way out past the Drama department - with its similarly enormous theater that doubled as the study hall. Past the administrative offices, Home Ec and a handful of other rooms I didn't even know the purpose of. It was out in the boonies. Teetering in the edge of civilization itself.

And I had three minutes to get there.
