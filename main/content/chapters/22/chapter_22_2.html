Things moved fast at Oceanside. Three minutes between periods was not a lot, and the school was huge. So, there was often a ton of ground to cover in that tiny span of time.

As a regional school, the student body came from everywhere. Wealthy districts, poor districts and all the ones in-between — and there were a lot of us. The average graduating class was three to four hundred students. That makes well over a thousand people coursing through the halls every time the bell rang. Like trying to exit a stadium after a game or concert. A visual cacophony of people that magically managed to get where they were going.

Part of the magic was our natural instinct to split up like cars on the highway, producing a torrent of students going your way on the right side of the hall and an identical wall of humanity traveling the opposite way on the other side. The key was to stay focused and keep up with the flow — and god help you if you needed to go left across the other stream.

Nameless faces would swim by at break neck speed, but sporadically mixed in with the van Gogh-esque blurs of color were people you recognized.

I spotted the esteemed Mike Andersen — the Student Council President who would not let you forget for a second that he was the Student Council President — floating around in the sea of humanity in front of me.

I exchanged nods with Megan Watkins — captain of the soccer team and member of the tiny fraction of people here I actually liked — as she whipped by in the other direction.

The flow turned the corner and proceeded down the hall beside the commons area — the exclusive turf of the much feared, Christine Peltz.

Christine was the self-appointed bully of a ruler every high school had and no high school needed. Selfish, mean and vindictive — with a healthy helping of implied violence to keep the masses in check. She certainly had the look down.

Torn up jeans? Check.

Studded leather jacket and self-decorated Chuck Taylor sneakers? Got 'em.

Terminally bored, I could give a flying fuck expression etched on her face? So perfect, she must have practiced it at home.

Everything I knew about Christine came in the form of terrified whispers and crazy innuendo. People said she'd ruin your life over absolutely nothing. That she'd dragged people out behind the school and beaten the shit out of them, and she'd never faced a single consequence for anything she'd done. Rumor had it that she'd seduced the principal, and had the pictures to prove it. A similar rumor held that he was her uncle, and he was terrified of having to deal with his sister. Another still claimed that Christine had been at the school for eight years, and had only just become a Junior.

Was any of it true?

On the one hand, I'd never personally witnessed any of this, nor did I know of anyone who had.

On the other, I didn't know anyone who'd ever seen her in class, either. So, something was clearly off about the whole thing.

At any rate... a proper needs servants, and Christine's circle of lackeys were a motley group that looked like they came straight out of Central Casting.

Most referred to as Christine's Crew.

Those who preferred a darker cult vibe called them The Devoted.

Personally, I went with The Minions.

I'm sure they all had names, but I didn't know or care what they were. Instead, I just called them how I saw them.

The Follower was the right-hand woman. The chief sycophant. If Christine turned around too quickly, she would become one with The Follower. That's how closely she followed behind her.

Chuckles provided the laugh track. Her guffaw was so loud, you would hear her at least twice as often as you'd see her every day.

Blondie was... well... blonde. With hair teased to the moon.

In case it's not apparent, I didn't spend a lot of time on these.

Shifty looked like the FBI was hot on her trail. Always looking around. Shifting back and forth from foot to foot. Ready to run at all times.

Hoodie lived inside the hood of her sweatshirt. I assumed she had a face, but I'd never seen it.

The last Minion had literally no noteworthy characteristics that I could discern. She just sat there. Her face was as plain as a potato. Her hair was... hair. She never smiled, laughed, spoke or — as far as I could tell — breathed.

With nothing to go on, I just called her Number Six.

Many spoke of this group in nervous whispers. The fear of Christine was palpable in the halls. People said she would ruin your life over absolutely nothing, and never faced any consequences. Rumor had it that she'd seduced the principal, and had the pictures to prove it. A similar rumor held that he was her uncle, and he was terrified of having to deal with his sister. Another still claimed that Christine had been at the school for eight years, and had only just become a Junior.

If you went to high school, I'm sure this all probably sounds pretty familiar.

As for me, I didn't really care what the deal was or if the rumors were true or false. I just kept my distance and had no problems. I had enough of those already, and I didn't need to add to the pile.

It really wasn't hard to steer clear. The school was huge, with many areas served by parallel hallways, and alternate routes available to everywhere. I could always get where I was going without having to get within ten feet of that crew.

I took one of those alternates routes now, and still made to History with a whopping seven and a half seconds to spare.

History should not be taught at eight o'clock in the morning. It should literally be a crime.

Actually, I sometimes wondered why it was taught, at all.

Hadn't we gotten the rhythm of it all, by now?

This is Group A. Next to them lives Group B.

One day, the wealthy aristocrats from Group A decided they wanted what Group B had. Land, riches, resources, etc. So they raised an army of mostly dispensable peasants and attacked.

Group B retaliated with its own dispensable peasants. A whole shitload of people died. Eventually, Group A won — killing most of Group B's people and enslaving the rest. The common people of the new kingdom lived the same shitty lives they'd lived before, while the wealthy lived lives of renewed abundance and prosperity. Peace reigned supreme across the land.

Uh, oh. Here comes Group C...

By the end of class, yet another civilization had met its end. As expected, lots of people were dead and the Roman Empire was much bigger than it had been forty minutes ago.

Off to English...

Yeah, I know. It's Language Arts, not English. But English was the language we were learning to read and write and digest.

The content, on the other hand, came from everywhere.

Our current focus was based in Central and Eastern Europe at a time when that was not a terrific place to live.

Night by Elie Wiesel.

A horror story completely based in real life.

The teacher did her best to lighten the content. Wiesel — to his much-deserved credit — tried to help. The book was funnier than anyone would ever have guessed the subject matter would allow. But there's only so much you can do when you a Jewish man telling a story about that time when you were a kid and the Nazis swung by and murdered most of your family along with millions of your fellow Jews.

I mean, really... it's just not that funny.

Even as know-nothing teenagers, we all seemed to grasp the incredible resilience Wiesel showed in even telling the story. But how he managed to also make it palatable is something we never understood.

You know what else we never understood? The how and why of that horrible time. The what was drilled into us over and over, but nobody ever tried to explain the reasons behind it.

Maybe that was because — even now — nobody really knows?

Anyway... when the bell rang, we carried the weight of the world out into the hallway and rejoined masses.

Next for me was Computer Science, and I would politely ask you to please shut the fuck about it, thank you. I'm having enough problems with this already.

The reason I was here was simple. My town isn't actually from this district. We're supposed to go to high school in the town next to us — and yet nearly half of my middle school classmates were here at Oceanview with me. The catch is that we have to study a subject that neighboring high school doesn't offer. Computer Science was one of those subjects.

Now, the reason I like it — that's a whole different thing.

My best guess is that the logic of it appeals to me. I like things that make sense, and an entire course based on if this, then that certainly fits the bill.

The problem is that I'm good at it.

Like... really good at it.

We filed into the classroom and I took my seat. Since it was a programming class, there wasn't much in terms of lecture time. The teacher explained a few things, then turned us loose on the computers.

Today's subject was sorting lists, and it was more complicated than it sounds. If there was one thing the teacher made clear to us — repeatedly — it was that code had to written in a way that conserved resources. Objects in memory had to be destroyed once they'd served their purpose. Communications clients and serves had to be minimized as much as possible. And sorts had to be optimized.

Optimizing meant knowing the data. Which led to all kinds of questions.

What kind of data were you sorting?

How big was the list?

How randomized was it?

There were algorithms for all of that. Some simple. Others less so.

Then came the sorting itself.

Bubble sort... Shaker sort... Selection sort...

A variation on the Bubble sort that I would later discover was called the Cocktail Shaker sort but they apparently couldn't say that in high school...

Every one of these algorithms had their benefits, drawbacks and complexities.
