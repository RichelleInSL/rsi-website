Rumors, propaganda, conspiracy theories... they all spring from the same basic formula: strange facts and unfiltered gossip, woven together by a connective tissue of not quite insane but awfully close to it conjecture.

Students were vanishing. That much was fact. Female students, to be specific. Not by the thousands or even hundreds. Not even by the dozens. Over a period of two years and a few months, it had happened three times. The first was [victim1]. At the time, she was a fellow freshmen. She came from one of the wealthy districts. I didn't know who she was, and I didn't care. I didn't even know she existed until the rumors started, and she was gone a long time before I heard about it. It's a big school, with a lot of students. Three to four hundred per class made it twelve to sixteen hundred overall. Plenty of room to disappear into the woodwork. You could easily pick a hallway, walk from one end to the other while traffic was in full swing and not see anyone you knew along the way. That was how I operated. Keep quiet, make no waves and you could walk past hundreds of people a day without any of them realizing you were there.

[victim1] was different. Many of the other students knew her. Not most of them. In a school of well-over a thousand students, only a very select few were know universally. But [victim1] was not invisible. She had friends. Friends who noticed when she didn't show up to school. Friends who started to talk among themselves when she missed a second day, and then a third. Then they started to talk to others once a week had passed. Some of the members of group A - the common group of [victim1] friends - started to talk to their other friends in group B. Those friends then talked to people they knew in group C. The circle of gossip widened, but the answers remained elusive.

Direct contact was a challenge. Try to imagine a world without TikTok, Instagram or Snapchat. A world where - god forbid - there were no cell phones. I know it's hard. I lived through it, and even I have trouble remembering. But trust me, it was real. If your friend had her own landline in her room - which [victim1] did - you could call her there. If she didn't, you were pretty much out of luck. What were you gonna do? Knock on her door and risk talking to her parents? Ridiculous. Even back in ye olden days, it was almost unthinkable.

[victim1]'s friends had tried to call her, but there had been no answers. Either [victim1] was ignoring them, or she wasn't there. Which was a problem with the communication methods of yesteryear. Having your own phone was special and cool, but you couldn't take it with you. You had to be where it was for it to be useful. There was no way for people to easily find out where you were. They could only determine where you weren't.

A second week went by, and that conjecture engine was running at full throttle. By then, the rumors were so loud that even I had heard them.

[victim1] had moved away suddenly. Whisked away under cover of darkness in the middle of the night. Because her mother was a spy. Or her father was a wanted fugitive who had committed some massive financial fraud. These theories were far-fetched, but made some sense given the fact that [victim1] had actually vanished so weirdly. But they were also easily disproven by the fact that people who lived near her said her family was still there.

Her parents had sent her away to boarding school. Was that really a thing? We had all heard of it, but no one knew anyone it had actually happened to.

She had been killed or seriously injured in a car wreck. This was a horrifying but sensible theory, but it also seemed like something we would have heard about at that point.

There were, of course, numerous tales of her grisly murder. Each possible on a scale from plausible to are you fucking smoking something, but there was still the same sticking point. We would have heard something. Her death would have been confirmed. Or at least, her disappearance would be making headlines. While her friends might not be able to contact her, we were going on a month now. Cell phones didn't exist then, but daily attendance records had been a thing since forever. By now, people with some authority must have noticed she was missing. Someone had to be asking questions.

So how could that be? How could we have arrived at the point where the administration and maybe even the police had to know what had happened, but no one was telling us anything?

The answer was obvious: they didn't want us to know.

Which made the reality even more obvious: [victim1] had killed herself.

No one wanted to believe it. Her friends outright rejected it. She had been fine. She had been happy. She had no reason whatsoever to kill herself. Suicide fell into that same black hole of things we heard about but never experienced, but the difference was that we knew for a fact this happened. The hows and whys escaped us - partially because we were still young and partially because even the experts didn't fully understand it - but people did kill themselves. And the things [victim1]'s friends were saying sounded exactly like what people always said when something like this happened.

And then, exactly seventeen days after anyone at school had last seen her, [victim1] came back.

Officially, there was no change. No commotion whatsoever. She wasn't there on Friday. On Monday she was. Mystery resolved.

Among the students, there was immediate pandemonium. [victim1] provided no answers. Her friends closed ranks, and they weren't talking - either out of respect or because they didn't know anything either. Mostly, they held the crowds at a distance. In the weeks that followed, [victim1] never walked alone.

Meanwhile, the rumor mill immediately spun up to full speed, then spun itself out just as quickly. After all the insane theories, there wasn't any credible story that could match that intensity but also end with [victim1] still alive. It wasn't really a morbid fascination thing. As far as I could tell, everyone was glad the terrible rumors weren't true. But they were also sick of it. Days of fearing the worst had left them with no taste to continue the investigation. She was alive and she was back. We had all lost our minds for no reason.

There was one detail that seemed to survive past the rumor stage, though: some who had seen her said she looked banged up. Like she'd been in a crash or some kind of accident. Her arm was in a sling, and she was badly bruised. I never saw her for myself, so I couldn't verify. And honestly I agreed with the general sentiment: I no longer cared. But I remember that as the weeks and months went by, that was the one detail that persisted. Something bad had happened, and she had the marks and scars to prove it.

Then, just a few months later, it happened again.

[victim2] hadn't been quite as popular as [victim1], but enough people knew her to notice she was gone. The similarity also likely had something to do with it. After the first escapade, people were much more alert when it happened this time. But the rest happened just the same. Here one day, gone the next. Nobody had a clue, and no one could get in touch. This time, she was gone a full three weeks. Unlike last time, she apparently lacked the support group necessary to insulate her from the gawkers. I actually passed her in the hallway shortly after her return... and she looked like hell. Blackened eyes. Broken arm. Just a mess. I had never scene a car accident victim, but she looked the part as far as I was concerned.

The school year ended with no further incidents, and no real mysteries left unresolved. Two people who didn't know each other had been injured in accidents. Unsurprisingly, neither had wanted to talk about it afterward. The similarities were just a terrible coincidence. Thank god both were alright.

Then in the fall, it happened again...
