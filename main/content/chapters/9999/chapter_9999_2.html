According to the league rules, each stable needed to have something called a "Stable Leader." A member of the roster who would serve as a sort of leader of the pack.

The concept struck me as superfluos. I owned the stable. I was going to run it as I saw fit, and I didn't need anyone else to help me do it.

That didn't mean my search wasn't thorough. After all, if I had to have someone in the role, I wanted it to be the right person.

Satisfying that simple requirement turned out to be harder than expected.

I interviewed a number of applicants. Some had experience in the same position elsewhere. Some were new to the role, but eager to prove their worth. Many would have made good choices, and some have flourished in other places since. But none struck me as the right fit for me. All had the necessary management skills, but it felt too false. Like the kind of knowledge you get from a book or leadership seminar. I wanted someone more natural. I didn't have any idea how I would know that I'd found the right person. I just assumed I would.

Time went on, and pickings got pretty thin. I was starting to think I was just going to have to settle and move on.

My last interview happened on a particularly busy day. The contractor was finishing up construction on the RSI facility, and I spent my time chasing him around and harassing him about final details. Because I was paying him a ton of money, and, god-dammit, I wanted things done right.

We were in the lobby haggling about exactly what flooring choice would give me the modern-yet-workmanlike vibe I was looking for when I saw her coming.

Something about her struck me immediately, though I couldn't tell you exactly what it was. Her look was casual. Simple brown flats below a pair of black jeans. A soft, brown t-shirt showed along the open zipper track of her leather jacket. Her absolutely fabulous leather jacket, to be precise. Worn and creased, yet clearly well maintained. Snug where it should be and loose where it shouldn't. Accentuating her broad, square shoulders. Like it had simply been made to fit her.

I didn't know who she was or why she was here, but damn... I wanted that jacket.

I remembered the interview as I watched her approach, which caused me to reassess a little.

Jeans and a t-shirt for an interview? Hmmm.

Still, I was quietly intrigued. I liked the way she moved. The confidence she radiated. There was no hesitancy. No moment to adjust her look or settle any nerves. She just headed straight for the door like she was going to the gym for a workout rather than an important job interview. Looking all cool and composed.

Of course, maybe it was all just the jacket.

I met her at the door, stuck out a hand and said, "It's Harris, right?"

"You don't remember my name?" she asked as she completed the handshake.

It came out so plainly, it kind of went by me before I backed up and realized what she'd said.

Well, son of a bitch.

Clearly, Ms. Smartass wasn't going to be here very long.

"You're here for an interview, and the first thing you do is argue with me?"

"You brought me in for an interview, and you don't know my name?"

"Is it Harris?"

"Yeah."

"Then I know your name."

And the rest, as they say, is history.
