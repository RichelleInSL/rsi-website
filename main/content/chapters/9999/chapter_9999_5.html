It wasn't a total surprise when the phone started to ring. It was Thursday, after all.

Danni paused the show we were watching. An older show we'd just started digging into about a pair of brothers who apparently spent their days battling all manner of terrifying paranormal beings. Vampires, werewolves, witches, ghosts. You name the dark, murderous creature, and these two knew how to hunt and kill it. It was a mission and purpose passed down to them by their father. The job, they called it. The family business. Weird.

Even weirder, the phone that was ringing was sitting in a charging cradle on the end table. One that not only had a power cord, but also a second wire that snaked down behind the table and into a funny little box attached to the floor molding.

If you know what that little box is, I have bad news: you're old.

If you don't know what it is... well, then I hate you.

Indeed, the phone was actually hard-wired right from the base all the way to the poles out on the street. The receiver itself was wireless, so it wasn't quite as ancient as the phones we'd used when I was a child. But in an age where just about everyone walks around with a phone in their pocket that they can use pretty much wherever they happen to be standing, the concept of a "home phone" was teetering right on the edge of drifting into antiquity. Yet Danni had kept hers. I still had one at my house, as well.

Read into that what you will.

Danni checked the caller ID out of habit, then answered with a simple "Hey" as she stood and casually made her way out into the dining room where I couldn't overhear.

By now, it was a familiar routine. One that I had never taken any offense to. Danni has lots of phone conversations I'm not privy to, and there's no reason I should be. It's just that the timing on these calls makes it awkward because I'm almost always curled up next to her on the couch when they arrive.

The real problem, though, has to do with the second half of the process. The conversations are normally very short, lasting only a few minutes. But Danni is not the same when she returns. Her mood is always just a shade darker. Her presence just a few degrees colder. It's a difference so slight, most probably wouldn't notice. But it's obvious to me.

Tonight was no different. She slipped the phone back into the cradle, sat back down on the couch and restarted the show

I have the ability to solve this mystery.

It started simply enough.

It was months ago, on a night just like this
