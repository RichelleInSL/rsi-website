Sweat ran down my neck in torrents and my lungs burned like they were on fire. My body ached and my head was fuzzy from the way I'd already been knocked around.

Danni jabbed me back, lining me up for a savage cross. I went with the momentum of the jab, slipped outside and felt the breeze as her right hand whipped past my nose. She tried to reset, but I was on her... hammering my left into her unprotected side. It was a solid shot, and I was already moving to capitalize with a right hook upstairs. She gathered herself quickly and interrupted me with her jab again. I watched for the cross, but she took a half step back. I pursued immediately, realizing too late that she had baited me... using the brief retreat to reload her left hand and launch a brutal hook that rattled my skull. Instinct told me the right hand was coming, so I stepped in suddenly and tied her up before she could throw it. Then I pushed her away just as abruptly, buying a little space and time.

Guards up, we circled... watching each other closely. There were no smiles or witty banter. We could do that later. Right now, we owed each other every ounce of attention and effort we could muster. That was our unspoken agreement.

Suddenly, she made her move... flashing the jab as she advanced on me. I slipped it and fired my own jab to ward her off, but she came in under it. Her angle told me she was looking to hook off the jab again, going to the body this time. There was nothing I could do to stop it, so I let fly with a big left of my own. She was going to get me good, but I was going to make her pay for it. Then, at the last moment, she changed levels and went head-hunting. My right had dropped in an instinctive attempt to lessen the damage from the expected body shot, so I was wide-open upstairs. Her hook crashed into the end of my jaw at nearly the same instant that she stood up into the path of my left hand. The simultaneous impacts combined into a hollow, wet thunk. Like the sound of someone hitting a watermelon with a baseball bat.

Both of us staggered away drunkenly, and the room actually spun for a moment. Once I got my bearings, I brought both hands up to my head.

"Fuuuuuuck me," I moaned.

I glanced up and saw Danni in a similar recuperative pose.

"That'll clear out the sinuses," she mumbled.

We stood like that for a while, each of us trying in vain to shake off the effects of what we'd done to each other.

"We calling this one a draw?" I asked eventually.

"Call it whatever you want," she said. "I'm done."

In case you haven't already figured it out, the chains are off.

After we got back from the event, we expanded our nightly sessions to include a little sparring. As the nights progressed, the skirmishes got more intense... as these things are wont to do between us. Both of us continued to hold up our end, so we agreed tonight would be all systems go.

The end result is that all systems were now in recovery mode. Fortunately, today was Friday. We would have the weekend to heal before we tried to kill each other again.

I wobbled my way over to the fence and collapsed, letting gravity take me to the floor. Danni eventually joined me. We fished our water bottles and towels through the fence, then just sat there in silence for a while. Mostly spending the time wondering if we would ever have the strength to move again.

That's not to say we didn't like the feeling. In fact, the opposite was true. Being totally spent. Feeling like we had left everything we had in the cage. That was what we yearned for, and only after feeling it again recently did I realize how much I'd missed it.

Among other things, the feeling carried away the stresses and aggravations of the day. Mine were small. Mixing it up with RSI's best had proven to be more entertaining than I'd ever expected. Still painful, but entertaining.

Danni, meanwhile, was dealing with a more aggravating set of challenges.

Apparently kicking my ass wasn't enough for the RSI group. The veterans wanted to take a more hands-on approach to helping Danni, and even the less experienced fighters were willing to sacrifice themselves and get in the cage with her. Though she politely declined those offers, the younger women still made it known that they were ready to do whatever was needed.

Publicly, she did as I'd suggested and just rolled with it. She accepted the offers were going to come, and she was receptive and friendly - smiling all the way. And, deep down, I know she was truly thankful. But the constant attention was also getting under her skin.

"This is driving me crazy," she said as we toweled off. "I feel like I'm the pet project for the whole stable."

"You knew it was going to go this way."

"I know," she said, "and, overall, it hasn't bothered me the way I expected. They've been kinda..."

"... casual about it," I said, filling in the words she was hunting for.

She nodded.

"Yeah," she said. "It doesn't really feel that out of the norm. It's almost like we're just mixing things up a little. Trying out new training combinations."

I understood what she meant. In fact, I've been surprised - and slightly awed - at the way the her teammates have been handling things. Somehow, they'd managed to keep the support constant, yet not overwhelming. A roster of over thirty fighters, all eager to be of assistance. Yet they'd managed to give Danni her space, too. How were they doing that?

Danni suspected the answer was collusion.

"They're organizing it, somehow," she said.

It came out as part statement, and part complaint. Because organization would mean less upheaval, but it also took the pet project  concept to a different level.

I shrugged.

"You want me to tell you again how this is all your fault?" I asked. "I mean, how do you think they learned to be this way?"

She clicked her tongue in mild annoyance, and let that stand as her answer.

"Yeah, well... I'm just saying it needs to stop soon. They've been good about it, but I still feel like I'm distracting all of them from their own training."

"Steph's got an eye on that," I said. "If someone's falling off, she'll get on them. And we agreed she'd do it sooner rather than later."

With that statement, I revealed that I'd been part of the collusion, to a degree. I doubt that fact came as a surprise to Danni, but it earned me a hard look and a little growling, just the same.

"Don't look at me that way," I said flatly. "I was the one who told you this was going to happen. So yeah... I talked to Steph, and we decided how we would monitor the situation."

"Without me."

"You wanted me to invite you into the discussion so you could protest the whole thing? Yeah... like that was going to change anything."

That was met with more grumbling. In a concerted effort to give her what she expects from me, I ignored her for a while before changing the subject.

"Steph came to me today," I said.

"About the rookies," she said.

I nodded. Apparently, our new rookie training program wasn't going quite as smoothly as we'd hoped, and Steph was talking about killing people again.

When that happens, it's usually a sign that Danni and I need to get involved.

"She wants us to come by Monday morning and see what's going on for ourselves."

"I can hardly wait," she drolled.

After a few more minutes, we scraped ourselves off the canvas and got cleaned up. Then we parted ways... for barely more than an hour.

* * *

Tramp and Winnie were poised in separate corners of Danni's kitchen, just waiting for me to drop something. The layout seemed almost strategic. Both the left and right flanks were covered, and if I tried to escape up the middle they would no doubt converge on me before I could get away.

I suspected the underlying reason for the arrangement was far simpler, though. The duel islands in Danni's kitchen are magnificently useful, but they also divide the room into somewhat narrow channels. There's still plenty of room to operate, but not enough for the canines to fit side-by-side. And since neither wants to be further from the drop zone than the other, the triangle approach is the equitable solution.

Tonight's meal was quick and simple, which left Danni with little to do other than watch me.

On one burner, a mixure of ground beef and diced onion was browning in a large skillet. On another, water was nearing the boiling point in an oversized pot. A large mixing bowl rested on the right island, with the sauce ingredients arrayed around it. I started by pouring in the tomato sauce, then the Worcestershire sauce, and finally a cup of ketchup.

"Ketchup?" Danni asked.

"That's right. Ketchup. Don't you judge me."

"Pardon me, Chef Boyardee."

"Watch yourself, girl."

A thin smile appeared on her face, but she didn't take it any further. Not until she'd had the chance to taste it, anyway.

I coated the mixture with oregano, pepper and garlic powder, stirred the seasonings through, then repeated the process.

The water was boiling when I finished, so I added the pasta, then drained the ground beef. After a little more browning, I added the sauce mixture and let it heat.

It did not escape my attention that both canines had inched closer as I worked.

"It smells good," Danni said.

"The hounds seem to agree."

Once the pasta was ready and drained, I combined it with the meat mixture in the pot. I intentionally dropped a spoonful to my left and another to my right, then we escaped to the dining room while the dogs were distracted.

We sat down and spooned out our servings. Danni forked some into her mouth, and looked impressed.

"Ketchup," she said with a note of admiration.

"You should know by now not to doubt me."

"Not when it comes to food, anyway," she clarified.

I decided to take that as a compliment.

After we'd eaten and cleaned up, we retired to Danni's inner sanctum. It's casually decorated with furniture she likes and carpet she likes and colors she likes. Nothing truly matches. Comfort and her personal satisfaction are the only common threads. One wall features a huge TV and a pair of shelving units filled in photos and memories arranged in a way that made sense to her without regard to anyone else. This was her safe, calming refuge from everything. I call it Danni's room, and every time I see it I'm reminded of just how much it means that she's shared it with me.

Tonight's schedule called for some catching up on our Agents of SHIELD  viewing, on which we were only about a half-season behind. We sat down beside each other on the couch, and she queued up the next episode. Then we settled in, with her in the corner and me leaning against her.

This is the way we've been for a while now. Close. Cozy. Loving.

It felt right and good and safe and warm... yet, somehow, incomplete.

There was no mystery regarding what was missing. The way I was nestled into the curve of her body. The heat of her hand resting against my side. These were the physical touches of a normal romantic relationship. I had experienced them many times, and I had willingly let them lead me in the past.

But here with Danni, those cues felt weird and different, and I wasn't sure what to do with them. So, I just enjoyed the show and pretended they weren't there, while quietly stressing over the fact that things couldn't stay this way forever.
