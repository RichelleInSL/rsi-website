"Did you get to meet Robert Downey, Jr.?" an excited Olivia Holt asked.

"Sure," Emily answered. "He and I didn't share a lot of scenes, but we certainly spent time together on set."

"Is he as cool as he seems?" Dove asked.

"He's awesome. And completely hysterical, all the time."

"And then there's Chris Evaaaaaaans," Olivia sing-songed.

"Girl, that Captain America is fine!" Zendaya Coleman commented.

"Ohmygodyeah," Dove said. "You kissed him. You have to tell us what that was like!"

"Uhm... no she doesn't," I interrupted.

All four sets of eyes snapped to where I stood beside the cage, flanked by Danni and Steph. The women had been so busy yakking, they hadn't heard us approach.

"She's here to help you three figure out how not to get your ass kicked," I said sharply. "From what I've seen, you need a lot of that kind of help. And since all three of you have fights coming up, I'd shut up and get to work if I were you."

The younger trio looked a little annoyed. Like teenagers who'd just been told to put their phone away at the dinner table. But they made their way to the center of the cage, just the same.

Emily threw me a quick look of apology before joining them, but I motioned for her not to worry about it. It's not like she was the first to fail at getting the children to stay focused.

"They're like this all the time," Steph said.

She paused as though that was all she had to say, but I could see something more was building inside her.

When she spoke again, she started to highlight certain keywords - saying them clearly and loudly enough to be heard three blocks away.

"With their event so close, you'd think they'd be more motivated. Maybe they'd want to spend their time LEARNING HOW TO WIN. But, no... apparently they don't give a shit."

"So, it's not working?" Danni asked.

"It is," Steph said. "Sort of. The different voices and different approaches seem to be more effective, but they're too easily distracted. They spend less than a third of their time on what they're actually supposed to be doing. The rest is them... TWITTERING LIKE BIRDS."

All three rookies glanced at her, then looked away just as quickly.

Steph brought the volume back down as she shook her head and added, "It's like their starstruck!"

That was all the information we needed to understand the situation. But Steph carried on in a flighty, high-pitched tone.

"Ohmygod, Emily kissed Captain America!"

"Jules, you're wedding dress was SO beautiful!"

"Emma, I can't believe how sweet your singing voice is!"

Steph McMahon, doing schoolgirl impressions. The world was obviously coming to an end.

She kept her attention on the cage, so she didn't notice me squinting at her in disbelief.

I turned the squint to Danni and said, "Uhhhh..."

"Yeah," she nodded. "We definitely need to do something."

As the quartet in the cage got down to business, it quickly became clear that Steph was right about the approach being effective. As Emily gave instruction, the younger fighters were hanging on her every word in much the same way they'd been enthralled over Captain America. The question was, how could we get them to stay on task?

"Maybe a go-between?" Danni suggested.

I turned to look at her. Steph remained focused on the cage, but we both knew she was listening.

"You want to have a liason to help them talk with their teammates?" I asked.

"Well, no. Not like that. More like someone to push them past all the nonsense stuff."

"So, basically a babysitter?"

"I guess," Danni answered. "I don't think I'd say it that way, though."

"Doesn't matter how you say it. This is a mixed martial arts stable, not a nursery school. They have a responsibility to get themselves prepared to fight. We give them all the tools they need, and I'm actually getting a little sick of trying to figure out ways to make them use them. I mean, do they WANT to get their asses kicked? That alone ought to be enough to get them motivated."

"Well, it's not."

"Well, then maybe they should find some other way to spend their time."

"And maybe you oughta think about what you're saying."

Remember that time when Danni folded like a house of cards at the slightest notion that I disagreed with her? Yeah... apparently we're not doing that anymore.

"All you need to do is take ten seconds and think about what you were like, at their age," she said. "How indestructible did you think you were, back then?"

My expression flattened instantly as I realized I was being manipulated.

Of the three, Olivia was the youngest at twenty years old. Dove and Zendaya were both twenty-one.

At that age, I was just starting out in the underground fight circuit. A place where there were no refs, no rules and no sympathy for the loser.

You have to feel pretty invincible to think you can survive in a world like that, and Danni's remark had been more than a casual statement. She knew exactly what she was saying and who she was talking to when she said it.

"Cute," I deadpanned.

"Just sayin'," she shrugged.

I sighed as I realized I had no choice but to hear her out.

"You have a specific babysitter in mind?"

"Tiz," she said. "They already know her, and they respect her. She comes from a very similar background, so they relate to her. And she gets along great with all the veterans."

That was a bit of an understatement. The truth is, Tiz gets along with everyone.

"That might work for now," Steph said, "but it brings us back to the problem we talked about originally. Rotating someone different in to work with these idiots for a day is one thing. But pulling Tiz - or anyone - out of her training every single day isn't going to work. Tiz is just coming off an event, so she's got the down time. But the team event isn't that far out. And no one is going to have time, then."

In the team competition, the women fight as groups. Each group consists of seven fighters - one from each weight class - who will compete against a group from another stable. RSI has three teams, which means all of our top talent is involved.

"I say we take our problems one at a time," Danni said. "Let's try this now, and see if it has any impact for these three in their next event. Team will be after that, so it will have to stop. But let's just see what happens."

It sounded pretty reasonable to me. I looked to Steph, who gave a little can't hurt to try  shrug.

"Alright," I said to Danni. "Talk to Tiz. If she's good, then go ahead and set it up."

"And do it quick," Steph said through gritted teeth.

Danni and I turned to see what was causing the latest flash of anger. Sure enough, while we'd been busy with the discussion, the activity in the cage had faded back to silly banter, with the three rookies giggling like fools.

I angled my pinkies into my mouth, and blew out a shrill, piercing whistle.

The sound grabbed the attention of everyone on the training floor.

"Do NOT  make me come in there," I snarled.

My three targets quickly looked away and got back to work. The rest of the room slowly followed suit.

"Babysitter," I said firmly.

"Yeah," Danni sighed. "You're right."
