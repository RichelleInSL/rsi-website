After some brief post-takeoff banter, Captain Moore settled into the routine of keeping the plane in the sky. Not to be rude, but that's sort of where I want my pilot's attention to be. Also, we were nearly halfway through the flight before it occurred to me that Moore was the only one on board capable of flying the plane. An understandable financial necessity on Isa's part, but still... Hey Devonte, don't die or anything.

With nothing to distract me, I had a lot of time to think about Danni and how the two of us had gotten to this point. Somewhere over the western tip of Virigina, I began to think I was an idiot. By the time we landed, I was sure of it.

Time, as it turns out, was not even close to the concern I'd expected it to be. Moore had gotten us here exactly as scheduled, putting me on the ground nearly an hour before Danni's flight was due to arrive. On top of that, she had experienced the typical delays of commercial air travel, which put her behind by another hour.

That's not to say everything went perfectly, mind you. Once I was on the ground, it took me a while to get my bearings. Seems they do things different here in Louisiana. In most airports, the main concourse connects the different terminals together. Here, according to the map, the main terminal connects the various concourses.

Sure. Whatever.

Once I had a handle on the local terminology, the rest was easy. So, when Delta Airlines Flight 1577 completed its journey from Los Angeles to New Orleans, I was sitting on a sleek, curved and phenominally uncomfortable bench just outside the security checkpoint for Concourse D.

In fact, I even had time to sample the local cuisine. On the floor between my feet was a plain, white paper bag from which I'd extracted the beignet I was now eating. Honestly, I was having a little trouble figuring out what all the fuss was about. It seemed to me an okay way to get your fried dough fix, but not overly impressive. Not as good as a zeppole from the Wildwood boardwalk, that's for damn sure.

I had chosen this spot because Danni would have to walk right past me in order to get out of the airport. Foot traffic had been fairly light during the time I'd been waiting, and it seemed impossible that I would miss her.

But now, as her flight emptied out, the crowd around me thickened. I popped the remains of the snack into my mouth, then dusted my hands as I stood - craning my neck to see if I could spot her. Harried passengers scattered left and right as the terminal opened up into the main building, and, for the first time, it occurred to me that the walk right past  idea might turn out more literally than I'd anticipated. Panic edged in, giving me the feeling that I was drowning in a sea of faces. I turned and spun - studying each as they passed - but none of them were the one I was looking for. Then the sea parted and there she was, absently drifting along with her wheeled duffle behind her. She froze when she saw me, and the look of total disbelief on her face probably made a nice counterpoint to the instant relief that must have registered on mine.

We eyed each other for a moment, and I wondered if she might try to play things tough. To let me know she was still angry. But, once her mind accepted what she was seeing, her eyes softened and the corners of her mouth turned up. The sight filled me with a total sense of happiness. Racing to the airport to spend three hours on a plane. Spending another hour sitting on a bench in a different airport thirteen hundred miles away. I had done it all just to see that smile, and it had been worth it.

She gathered herself and walked over, shaking her head steadily.

"How are you here?" she asked with a hint of amusement in her voice.

I shrugged.

"Once you master the power of teleportation, anything is possible."

She didn't inquire any further about how I'd done it because she didn't really seem to care. She also didn't bother with the obvious question about why I had come because she already knew. She just kept smiling, and she wasn't at all shy about it. That unreserved happiness at seeing me erased any lingering questions about what made her run down here in the first place. Because I didn't care, either.

I don't know how to explain it. It was like everything that had gone wrong had instantly disappeared. The distance that had slowly grown between us over these past few months closed in an instant. There was no guesswork or complication involved in how we were feeling. She was happy. I was happy. Nothing else mattered.

Emma and Tiffany had been right. Though we'd barely spoken a word, I already knew whatever had been broken in Melbourne could be fixed. Knew it beyond a shadow of a doubt.

For a long moment, we just stood there smiling stupidly. Then, I put my hand out, and she put hers in it. I turned my wrist over, gently rubbed my thumb along the back of her hand and just enjoyed the feeling as the physical connection fed the emotional.

After a while, I gestured her toward the bench, and we both sat down.

"I'm sorry," she began.

"Me, too," I said. "We sure made a mess of this, didn't we?"

She nodded steadily, which told me I wasn't the only one who'd been spending her lonely time in the air thinking things through. And much of my thinking revolved around my behavior in Australia.

"I know I made it worse with what I said after the fight," I said. "I was upset and mad and..."

I paused. All that thinking time had allowed me to realize - and accept - exactly what had happen to me that night. And it all boiled down to three words I almost never say.

"I was scared," I said. "Seeing you like that... you scared me."

"I know," she said softly.

"But it wasn't you I was mad at," I continued.

"I wasn't ready," she said simply. "You had a right to be angry."

"But I wasn't. Not at you, anyway. I was just... mad. It shouldn't have happened, and I had as much to do with that as..."

"Yeah, I'm gonna have to stop you there," she said firmly. "Say whatever else you want, it was my fault. My fight, my training, my responsibility. You don't get to take any of the blame for that."

I thought about arguing, then hesitated as I remembered this was where things started to go south the last time.

My brief pause gave her enough time to fill the silence before I had the chance to repeat my mistake.

"Speaking of making things worse," she continued, "I shouldn't have done this."

She made a broad gesture with her hands to indicate that this  meant our surroundings. The airport. New Orleans. That she shouldn't have come here.

"I didn't want to come back, yet. I didn't think I was ready to deal with it. I didn't know how I felt or what I was going to say. But as I was flying down here... it felt like running away."

But all week long, I'd been trying to figure out what I was going to say when you got back. These past few months have been such a mess, and I had no clue how to untangle it. But when I found out you were gone, I lost it. I just wanted to find you and talk to you and fix it. All the other stuff was gone. It was like..."

"...none of it mattered, anymore," she said.

A warm rush surged through me as she finished my sentence. For the first time in months, it felt like we were locked into each other. It was a feeling that had come so naturally to us for years, and I hadn't realized how much I'd missed it until I suddenly felt it again.

"While we're on the subject of making me chase you... today might have gone a little easier if I'd been able to talk to you. What the hell is going on with your phone?"

"Uhm... it's not really functional right now," she said sheepishly. "It suffered a little damage during the trip."

I almost laughed out loud.

"During your prolonged stop in L.A., I'm guessing?"

"Yeah, that was such a... hey! How did you know about that? And how the hell did you know I'd be here, now?"

I borrowed a page from Emma's book.

"I have my ways."

"The travel agent," she said as the answer became obvious. "You called the travel agent."

"I did, and let me tell you... over twelve hours waiting at the airport? After the marathon flight from Australia? I'm pretty happy I met you here and not in California. At least you had some time to cool off."

"Yeah, that was not a good time to know me."

We were quiet for a minute, then I cocked my head and looked at her a little sideways.

"Did you throw it?"

"What?"

"The phone. Did you throw it?"

She surprised me with a flat denial.

"No. I had it safely tucked away in the side pocket of my bag."

Now, obviously, the phone was broken. And the way she was looking at me said I had been given me all the details I needed to figure out how it happened.

I took a moment to parse her words before it clicked.

"You threw the whole bag."

"Like a discus," she nodded. "Just spun around and heaved it. It landed halfway across the terminal. The airline people were not amused."

"Screw 'em," I said. "They should know better than to fuck with someone who'd just spent fifteen hours on a plane."

"The security people didn't find it funny, either."

"Yeah, that... that probably wasn't good."

"Eh... I doubt it's the first time someone did something like that."

"That's probably true," I agreed.

The little exchange made me smile, but it also gave me a hollow feeling in the center of my chest. Finishing each others' thoughts. Communicating with nothing more than looks and shrugs. It all felt so automatic and familiar, but where the hell had it been all this time?

I put my hand on her knee. She immediately put hers on top of it.

"I've missed this," I said.

"Me, too," she said. "My god, what happened to us?"

"I don't know," I admitted. "Everything we did just felt so... gummed up."

"Like running underwater," she said.

I nodded my agreement.

"So, how do we do better?" she asked.

"I don't know," I shrugged. "Maybe we could start by figuring out what happened a few weeks ago."

"What do you mean?"

"When we were talking about Liv and Dove. You had a plan, then I asked one question and you decided we should just trash the whole thing."

I nodded. Off  was definitely the word for it.

"And the way we stopped our training sessions..."

"That was you," I protested.

"What?" she blurted.

"Don't look at me like that. The training just... stopped. And you never said a word about it."

"Me?  Danni, I loved those times with you. And you have no idea how hard I've been kicking myself these past few days. If we had kept at it, I could have picked up on what was going on. Maybe I could have done something."

I sighed. I wanted to argue, but something stopped me. The way she kept pushing me back... vehemently insisting she own what happened that night... it tickled a concern lurking at the back of my mind. A reminder that our fight in the training room was not the only traumatic experience she'd suffered in Melbourne.

The aftermath of her fight with Cole ran much deeper than just a bad loss. Losing that way can be crippling for a fighter. Especially one of Danni's caliber. I was going to help her deal with the struggle I knew was coming - whether she liked it or not - but now didn't seem like the time to wade into that battle.

Caught between the desire to fight on and the need to handle things with at least a tiny bit of intelligence, I settled for a half-hearted protest.

"For the record, I think that's bullshit. I can think of at least half a dozen ways I dropped the ball - before, during and after the fight. I deserve some blame. But, you know what? I have enough screw-ups to atone for. You want that one? It's yours."

She nodded, then we both seemed to drift away. Thinking larger thoughts. Coming to grips with the fact that this complicated emotional mess we'd made might not be that complicated, after all.

Planes landed and departed. Travelers raced this way and that. And we just sat there - each of us turning the facts over and over. Slowly recognizing the true - and truly stupid - way we'd let things get away from us.

All the while, emotions played visibly over Danni's face. Mostly, she looked angry. For a brief moment, she even looked like she wanted to throw something. Instead, she blew out a long, frustrated sigh, then jammed her palms into her eyes and rubbed like she was trying to erase them from her face. Then she brought her hands together in front of her face and pressed the edges to the bridge of her nose. She looked like she was praying to some god somewhere for the self-control required to not strangle someone. I knew the person she was most annoyed with was herself.

I knew because I had that same feeling of self-directed loathing.

All of our problems had stemmed from the same ridiculous mistake. We had gotten caught up in trying to read each other. Each of us trying to passively see and do and be what the other wanted. Guessing... and clearly getting it wrong much of the time. And it had all happened because we had stopped doing the one thing we did best. The thing that made our relationship what it was.

We had stopped talking to each other.

"Have we been chasing each other down the rabbit hole?" I asked.

"I think so," she said. "I thought this and you thought that and..."

"...neither of us thought about just talking to the other instead of playing guessing games," I finished.

It was an aggravating conclusion to come to, but just saying it out loud tranformed the problem. What had only a short time ago seemed vague and complex was now real and understandable. Better yet, it was manageable.

It was like waking up from some long, debilitating nightmare only to feel the relief that came from knowing it was just a dream.

This was something we could fix, and the repairs had started the moment we'd laid eyes on each other as she was coming out of the terminal. Our gears were beginning to mesh the way they used to, but it was more than just reading each other's thoughts and finishing each other's sentences. It was a feeling of completeness. Things had fallen apart but the pieces were still intact. Like a puzzle that had been knocked on the floor. We just had to pick it up and put it back together.

There were still things that were nagging at me, though. Questions and worries that needed to be voiced.

"There is something that's been bothering me," I said.

She turned her attention toward me and waited.

"I'm fine."

Remember what I said about Danni's temper being a lot like mine? Well, the difference is that she makes a greater effort to hold it back. She's pretty good at it, which is what makes her the more reasonable and mature of the two of us. But everyone has a limit, and I'd just found hers.

Like he'd been in a hurry just a moment ago, and now he didn't know whether he should keep going or turn around and run the other way. He shook himself out of his trance and moved on. The rest of the world seemed to restart along with him.

I smiled at Danni.

"You seem upset," I said. "Wanna talk about it?"

"I'm gonna let you in on a little secret," she said.

"I kinda liked what happened."

"Are you insane?"

"I hang out with you, don't I?"

"Don't get me wrong," she continued. "It was terrifying. But there was something about it. This feeling like it was you and me against the world."

"But you do," she said definitively. "You felt it, didn't you?"

I didn't answer.

Because I didn't want to.

Because she was right.

I hated what had happened. I hated that I had to go through it. I was livid that Danni had been so viciously dragged into it. But neither of us had been given a choice. Once I accepted that, then I had to admit that there were any number of moments where I had been glad to have her there. Moments when she lightened the mood and helped me keep it together. Moments when she stood at my side and gave me strength. And moments when she stood directly in my path and saved me from myself.

Despite the extraordinary events we'd been through, there was nothing extraordinary about Danni's role in it. No matter the subject or activity, I am better with her than I am alone.

Meanwhile, my silence told her all she needed to know.

"I told you that night," she said. "I want more of that feeling. I want it all the time."

"You enjoyed being shot at?"

"Not particularly," she said, "but you know that's not what I mean."

You and me against the world.  I let that idea carry my thoughts for a while and found it hard to deny the appeal. It was also hard to dispute how cleanly the phrase cut to the heart of who we were and why I flew all the way out here in the first place.

I had spent a lot of time living life in solitary. I didn't want to do it that way anymore, but I also didn't want to change myself to make things fit. If I was ever to going to share an existence with someone else, I wanted it to be someone who understood what made me who I was. Danni had shown time and again that she had that understanding and that she was comfortable with it.

More comfortable, apparently, than I was.

This kind of thinking was clearly too much, too soon. Only a few hours ago, this relationship had been a wreck. Now, I was sitting in an airport thinking forever thoughts. But there was something about Danni that made it easy to think that way. I didn't understand it, but there it was.

Still, something was holding me back. Some deep-seated fear that was blocking my path. I tried to push it aside and accept that Danni was right. We were never going to get anywhere if I spent all my time worrying. But the fear fought back, and my mind wandered back to what was, for me, the worst moment of the Powers affair.

"Do you remember that fiasco at your house?" I asked. "The night Powers' man kept setting off your security system just to fuck with you?"

"Kinda hard to forget."

"You remember the day after?"

"I'll have to send him a thank you  card."

You see what I mean about me and my funny friends?

It was too late to keep you out of it. Once I accepted that, it was easy for me to see that it was unfair not to let you stand and face the problem."

"And I appreciated that."

She sounded hopeful. Like maybe I was starting to see her point. But that wasn't where I was headed.

"But that was then," I said. "It's not too late for me to shield you from whatever else is out there."

She sat back, obviously disappointed at the way I'd twisted the conversation back to where we'd started.

Disappointed, but not defeated.

"You're right," she said. "You can keep all that stuff from me. But that means cutting me out. It means we can't take this thing between us any further."

It was a statement that pierced straight through to what had been bothering me from the beginning. The reason I had awoken that morning on Danni's sofa and immediately started to doubt things I had been so sure of the night before. Somewhere, deep in my soul, I understood that it would come to this. The choice between what I felt in my heart and what I knew in my head.

I wanted to go with my heart. After all, we had already been forced to face danger together, and we had prevailed. Danni was smart and strong and capable. And she had made her choice. Why couldn't I just accept it?

A memory crystalized in my mind. The horrifying moment that had concluded that terrible day. It was as clearly and vivid as it had been the first time. Powers gesturing to Danni, saying he'd been looking for an excuse to get rid of her. An excuse I had inadvertently given him.

Kill her.

Those were the words he'd said to the goon standing beside him. Words that had turned my blood to ice.

As long as I live, that moment will haunt me.

I relayed none of what I was thinking to Danni. I'm not sure I could have, if I wanted to. The memory was too real. The emotions too overwhelming for words to squeeze past them.

Their job done, I was able to set the memories aside and focus on what made them so important at that moment. They were a reminder. Pure, unequivocal proof that I couldn't do what Danni was asking. Not ever.

"I'm never going to stop worrying," I said.

Turns out, she knew it, too.

"Maybe that was wrong," she said. "I think worrying isn't really the problem. I would worry about you if the roles were reversed. I do  worry about you now."

Something in my brain changed at that moment. Her words had untangled a little knot, releasing some detail I needed to deal with. I didn't know quite what it was yet, but I had this feeling it was important. Like it might be the reason I couldn't just let go and trust that she knew what she was getting into.

I pressed on, hoping the conversation would eventually bring it into focus.

"Why would you worry about me?"

She turned a little and looked at me sideways.

"You can be an idiot sometimes. You know that?"

"Well, yeah... I do," I said. "I'm just wondering what particular brand of idiocy we're talking about, this time."

"You talk about me being shot at and how my life was in danger," she said. "You remember you were standing right next to me during all of that, right?"

"Of course."

"Then you know  what I worry about. And how can you do that all the time? How can you be so worried about everyone else, but never yourself?"

I tilted my head in a little are you kidding me?  gesture.

"I think we can look back on some of my life choices and agree I don't worry much about my physical safety."

She shook her head emphatically.

"No. It's because you always feel like you can handle it."

The statement was undeniably accurate. I may be delusional, but I always believe I can handle what's in front of me. Because, if I don't believe, then I won't be able to handle it.

Again, my silence became a form of admission.

"I'm not asking you to change who you are," she continued, "but this is never going to work if you're going to be afraid to let me be involved in your life. You need to learn to live with the fact that we're in this together and trust that I can handle it, too."

I lifted my eyes to hers.

"I don't understand why you would want to," I said. "When I look back... there are so many things I wish I hadn't done. But I did them, and I have to live with what comes with that. But you don't. Why would you want to?"

She stared straight back at me, and the answer to my question was right there in her eyes.

"I don't really know, either," she said, "but I do, and there's nothing either of us can do about it."

The three words neither of us was ready to say hung in the air between us, but we didn't have to speak them aloud to make them true. They were the reason we were here, and we both knew it.

"Truth is, no matter what happens from here, I'm going to worry," she said, "but, if we can figure this out, at least I'll be able to do something about it."

Suddenly, the thought that kicked loose before broke through to the surface. And I was right. It was the thing that was holding me back.

Danni hadn't just been in danger because of me. She had willingly put  herself in danger to protect me.

Emma's words came back to haunt me. As the vice was tightening and the danger of the Powers situation was becoming all too real, she had been the one to warn me of what Danni might do.

Danni always puts herself last. ALWAYS. She will do something stupid if you let her.

In the final moments, I had deliberately tried to provoke Powers. I wanted him to lash out at me. Because I didn't just want him beaten. He had been beaten in Boston, but somehow managed to come back and terrorize me again. This time, I wanted him dead. So, I pushed and pushed until he reached for a gun to kill me with.

You couldn't ask for clearer evidence of Danni's point. Despite the fact that she was standing right next to me at the time, I had acted completely alone. Willingly putting myself in jeopardy to get the solution I wanted.

But I wasn't the only one who'd acted independently. As Powers moved to kill me, Danni stepped between us. Putting her life on the line to save mine.

Danni always puts herself last.

Spenser and Hawk killed Powers before he got his weapon out, but that didn't change the risk Danni had taken for me. Or the helpless feeling I'd had as it all went down.

"No," I said flatly, "you can't  do anything about it."

She looked at me in shock.

I worked to calm myself. To push the memory away and focus on the present.

"What you said earlier," I began. "About me and you against the world? I like the sound of that. But that's the way it has to be. You and me, shoulder to shoulder. Not you in front of me."

"I don't..."

"You tried to take a bullet for me, Danni."

The completely stunned look that took over her face was actually pretty funny. Without meaning to, I had turned her entire argument back on her. Sure, I was careless with my own life. But hadn't she done the same thing with hers?

Her expression passed through the stages quickly. Stunned went to denial, then realization and, finally, acceptance.

"I guess you have a point," she admitted.

She sat up straight and turned her head slightly away. Staring absently across the terminal as she reformed her thoughts. I turned a bit the other way and did the same.

For me, it didn't take long because I realized Danni's selfless act had been more than just a terrorizing moment for me. It had been a taste of my own medicine. How many times had I taken risks and caused her to worry? More times than I had fingers to count them with, for sure.

And I knew those moment stressed her out. She had told me so in the past. As a result, I've tried to make little changes to the way I operate. To think things through a little more and to try and include her in things so she wouldn't be surprised.

But I had slipped into old habits that day with Powers and took an enormous risk without so much as a second thought. Then Danni took that risk away and put it on herself. The memory of that moment will terrorize me for as long as I live.

Is that  what I did to her? Did I make her feel that same terror with the risks I took? If so, then little changes weren't enough.

Could I give up my solitary - and slightly insane - approach to solving the problems I was confronted with? Could I let her in and swallow the risk that came with that?

I would have to. Because this thing between us wasn't going to go away. She had been willing to lay her life down for mine, and I would absolutely sacrifice myself for her. And, as twisted as that might sound, it was love. No doubt about it.

I reached over and slipped my hand into hers. She gripped it firmly as she turned back to me.

"You and me against the world," I said.

"Shoulder to shoulder," she replied with a smile.

"God damn," I marveled at the thought.

"World's not gonna know what hit it," she said.

I leaned over and rested my head on her shoulder. We had a long way to go and any number of obstacles to overcome, and we had already traveled a long way just to get this far. Literally. But this felt real. Like it had a future. Not the glowing, rosy future I'd bought into that first night. This future was grounded in the understanding that the fear of losing each other is what drove us apart. I felt pretty confident that emotions that strong gave us something solid to build on. We just had to get them under control. No simple task, but it had to be easier than this past week had been.

Easier, and definitely more rewarding.

We just needed to keep our eye on the goal and take things one step at a time. And step one was getting the hell out of Louisiana.

"Let's go home," I said.

Her head dropped instantly.

"Oh, god... not another fucking plane."

"This one will be better," I said.

"Oh, yeah? Why's that?"

"You'll have me to keep you company," I smiled.

She made a face and said, "Maybe I'll walk."

"I don't think so," I said. "You made me fly all the way out here. You're gettin' on the damn plane."

"Fine," she sighed sarcastically.

"Besides," I continued, "if I show up back at RSI without you, I'll be killed."

She nodded understandingly.

"Emma?" she asked.

"To start with, yeah."

"Alright, I'll go," she said, "but, just so you know, my family is expecting me."

"They'll get over it."

She shook her head.

"You don't know my mother. I'm gonna get an earful over this."

I shrugged.

"Tell her you work for a heartless bitch who made you give up your vacation."

She thought about it for barely a second before she started to nod.

"Yeah, you know... I might just do that."

Is it just me, or did she agree to that a little too easily?

She dug a playful elbow into my side and I gave her one back. Then she stood and grabbed her duffel, and I picked up my bag of so-so snacks from the floor.

"What's that?" she asked.

"Beignets," I answered. "Want one?"

She stopped dead and eyed the bag as if it contained a biological weapon.

"Where did you get them?"

Her stern tone made me pause for a moment. Then, I slowly raised my eyes to the giant, black and white West Beignet  sign that was no more than fifty feet away.

"Gee... I can't remember."

She followed my gaze to the sign and made a noise like she'd just discovered doggie doo on the bottom of her shoe. Then she snatched the bag out of my hand, spun and deposited it in the nearest garbage can.

I looked at her for several beats, then frowned and said, "You owe me seven bucks."

"You should be thanking me for saving you."

"From what?"

"Did you eat one?"

"Of course."

"Worth seven bucks?" she asked defiantly.

I considered my earlier reaction to the pastry.

"No."

"Of course not," she said. Then, "Who the hell buys beignets at the airport?"

"What's the big deal?"

"Name a food from Jersey."

I shied away from saying zeppolis  because I didn't want to accidentally get in a fight with the entire state of Louisiana.

"Jersey corn," I said.

"And do you buy that at the airport?"

"Fuck no," I said. "And, despite what the signs say, you don't buy it at the supermarket, either. You buy it at a roadside stand from a guy in a t-shirt, work boots and overalls."

She held her hands out in front of her.

"I see your point," I said with a nod.

"You want a beignet? I'll take you down to the French Quarter and get you the real thing."

I did a slow turn of my wrist and checked my watch.

"I don't think we have time for that," I drolled.

"Next time," she said. "I'll take you to Cafe Du Monde, we'll stand on line for an hour and get you the real thing."

"You paying?"

"Sure."

"Good, because you still owe me seven bucks."

We started walking toward the center of the concourse. Or terminal. Or whatever the hell it was.

"An hour, huh?" I said with a hint of amusement.

"Have to," she said. "It's the waiting that makes them taste so good."

I smiled wider.

"It's a date," I said.

"Like a real date?" she asked.

"Yeah," I said. "We should do that more often, too."

"Eat beignets?"

"Go on real dates."

Even in profile, her smile was impossible to miss.

Then, something else occurred to me.

"I can't sleep in your guest room anymore, either," I said.

She studied me out of the corner of her eye.

"When and if the time comes, we'll spend the night together," I explained. "Until then, no more of this sleepover stuff."

She had no trouble understanding what spend the night  meant, and she made a little whistling sound in response.

"Dates? Spending the night together? I feel like this relationship just moved a mile in less than a minute."

I leaned into her as we walked, bumping my shoulder into hers.

"Making up for lost time," I said.

We walked along the wide corridor side-by-side for a short distance before she turned to head for the ticket counters. She spun around when she realized I hadn't followed her.

"I thought we were flying out."

"We are," I said, gesturing toward the main exit.

She frowned, but I didn't wait for her to ask questions.

Once outside, I led us right as I dug out my phone and called Devonte.

"Ready whenever you are," I said.

Danni looked puzzled as I listened to Moore tell me the shuttle for the private terminal was on its way.

"What the hell is going on?" she asked when I disconnected.

I didn't say anything, and I gave her a look that told her I wasn't going to. She remained confused as we boarded the unmarked shuttle, but it all cleared up for her as we neared our destination.

"You hired a plane to chase after me?" she said with a gentle seriousness.

Her wording gave me pause. Up until that moment, I hadn't given much thought to how I'd gotten here. I needed to catch her, and I'd done what I had to do to make that happen. Certainly, I hadn't thought about how the gesture might look.

"When I know what I want, I spare no expense," I said.

She shook her head lightly - like I shouldn't have gone to so much trouble - but the smile was back.

I put my hand out on the seat next to me. She took it - intertwining her fingers with mine - and we didn't say another word for the remainder of the ride.

When we got to the terminal, I introduced Danni to our pilot. He, in turn, introduced her to the plane.

She was impressed.

"The flight from Australia might not have been so bad in something like this," she observed.

Hmm.

We climbed aboard and got settled.

"This is niiiice," she cooed as she sat back into the plush seat and ran her hands along the armrests. "It must have cost you a fortune."

"I know people," I shrugged. "I got a discount."

Moore wrinkled his brow.

"I thought you said..."

"Could you just fly the plane and not help, please?" I interrupted.

He gave me a few seconds of flat staring, then went back to whatever he'd been doing as if the conversation had never happened.

The takeoff procedure on this end was a bit more complicated than at the small airport in Jersey, but it took less than half an hour for us to be cleared for departure.

As the relatively tiny plane hurtled along the runway, I saw Danni's face light up like a five year old on Christmas morning. She turned the smile on me, and something told me there was more going on than just the joy of traveling like big shots.

"What?" I asked.

She held up a finger, then sat back to ride out our ascent. Throughout the climb, the smile never left her face. By the time we leveled off, I was very sure I was in big trouble.

"What?" I asked again as the smile spread onto my face.

She held the finger up again, then reached into her bag and produced her phone. Whole and intact.

"I thought that was broken," I said.

"I had a lot of time in LA," she shrugged. "I stopped and got a new one, then spent some time setting it up during the flight."

Oh, I thought to myself. Then that would mean...

On cue, Danni frowned at the phone and said, "I have voice mails."

Uh, oh.

Though neither of the messages I'd left her had been particularly nasty, neither had been particularly friendly, either. To be safe, I preferred that they just go away.

"Yeah... you can just delete those," I said.

Confused, she turned the frown on me.

"They might be important."

"How many are there?" I asked.

"Two."

"They're not important."

She frowned a little more before she caught on to what I was saying. Then she gave me a wry smile that told me she was probably going to listen to them eventually, but I was forgiven in advance for whatever I'd said.

That worked for me.

With the little mini-drama concluded, she went back to her original intent with the phone. A few taps later, a slightly tinny version of Michael Bublé's Take You Away  filled the cabin.

She looked at me expectantly and waited for me to get the message. It didn't take long.

"Danni..." I began.

"What? We've got this beautiful plane all to ourselves. We could go anywhere."

"No, we can't."

"The Bahamas... Barbados..."

"I don't have my passport."

She shrugged and just kept smiling.

"Florida, then. The Keys must be beautiful, right now. Warm, but not hot. No crowds."

"We can't just run off," I said. "We need to get back to work."

There was a tightness in my voice. A note of forced determination. Forced because I really wanted to give in. Determined because the small-yet-assertive portion of my brain assigned to making me act like an adult wouldn't go for it.

"Steph can handle things for a few more days," she coaxed.

"Steph has already done enough."

The smile just stayed where it was, defiantly announcing that I was having no impact on her.

I tried going practical.

"We can't just take the plane."

"I'll bet you can figure it out."

"We can't do it," I said. "I mean, I'm sure Devonte has things to do. We can't just steal him."

"As long as you're paying," Moore offered, "I'm available."

"Shut up," I said. "What did I just say about helping?"

He went back to the business of flying the plane, nodding casually to himself. Like he was used to being snapped at and had long ago learned to let the moments pass right by.

A necessary skill for living with Isa.

I turned back to Danni and resumed trying to disabuse her of her notion.

"I have no clothes," I protested.

"I'll buy you whatever you need."

"We have no reservations."

She waggled the phone and said, "You pick a place. I can make it happen."

"What about the dogs?"

I scored a hit with that one but she recovered quickly.

"I've got a service taking care of Winnie. I'm sure we can find a way to get Tramp over there."

I shook my head. With gates, cameras and a centrally monitored alarm system, Danni's house had more security than my bank.

"Nice try," I said dubiously. "No one's getting into Château Harris  without you letting them in."

Even turning her home into a French winery didn't get her to flinch.

Another wave of the phone and she said, "I can open it all from here."

I sighed.

"You just have all the answers, don't you?"

"Mmm-hmm," she smiled confidently.

I sank back into the seat and stared at the ceiling, still thinking there was no way this could work. Meanwhile, Buble's smooth baritone glided into the second verse.

We don't need to go far, to stare at the stars and change our point of view.

My eyes flicked to the phone, then up to Danni. Once again, I could instantly tell we were having the same thought.

If we went somewhere closer to home, a fair number of my objections would be satisfied. I was pretty sure I could convince myself that was an adult-like compromise.

She shrugged and said, "It's not as magical as whisking off to the Caribbean, but I'll take it."

I smiled at her, then pulled my own phone out.

"I'll make the arrangements," I said as I scrolled through my contacts list. "You call Steph."

"Hey!" she said. "You're the one in charge. You should call."

"Fuck that," I said. "This was your idea. She can yell at you, for a change."

She frowned at her phone again.

"The magic of this is running out fast," she said.

I held my free hand palm up and quoted Rumpelstiltskin from Once Upon a Time.

"All magic comes with a price, dearie."

"Ohhh," she groaned. "I can't believe you did that."

I lifted an eyebrow at her and said, "Sure you can. We've known each other for years. You know what you're signing up for."

She rolled her eyes and looked out the window. Like maybe she was thinking this relationship wasn't such a great idea, after all.
