Rod Laver Arena, Melbourne, Australia

As soon as my feet touched the arena floor, Steph grabbed my arm and pulled me aside.

"You can't go back in that cage," she said sternly.

It was an absolutely shocking moment, both for its suddenness and it's rarity. I could probably count on one hand the number of times Steph and I have physically touched in the years we've known each other. Never had she grabbed hold of me that way.

"What do you mean?" I stammered.

"When you go in that cage, you're there to help," Steph said. "All you did was let her know how bad things are going."

"What the hell are you talking about?" I protested. "I didn't say a fucking thing!"

"No, you didn't," she snapped. "In a moment when she need you more than ever, you had nothing to say."

"What did you want me to do?" I choked. "She's getting the shit beat out of her, and it's killing me. I figured it was better to keep my mouth shut than let that show."

"Yeah, well you should see the look on your face," she said, "because you're not hiding anything."

I was pissed. Danni was getting her ass kicked. I couldn't stop thinking about my part in that and the numerous warnings I ignored along the way. And now Steph was giving me shit about... what? Not being a good cheerleader? What the fuck was there to be cheery about?

For a moment, Steph almost became the focal point for all of that anger. In a different setting, I might not have been able to control myself. Would I have thrown a punch at her? You don't have to know me very well to know the most likely answer is yes. I'm not saying that would have been a smart thing to do. Nor am I saying that such a thing would have worked out well for me. I'm just saying that at that moment, with Danni being so thoroughly beaten and humiliated the way she was, my shit was absolutely not together. So I'd be lying if I said the thought of balling up my right hand and smashing it into Steph's face hadn't occurred to me.

But, as fierce and uncontrolled as my temper can be, there has always been some counterweight within me. An unconscious situational awareness that checks my rage when I cannot. Lord knows it's not foolproof, but it's usually effective. Here - in front of a packed house - that part of me took control. I settled for wrenching my arm free and turning my back to her as I returned to my position at cageside. She followed and wordlessly resumed her responsibilities.

In the cage, things weren't looking any better than they had before. If anything, they were worse. Marcy had done a great job of stopping the blood flow, but the improvement was mostly cosmetic. The beating Danni had already taken had been bad, and the effort she'd put in to keep it from being worse had sapped most of her energy. Hurt and exhausted, she was no longer in any condition to handle Cheryl, let alone mount a threat. Still, she was game. Her gloves were up, and she was doing what she could. But she was past the point where she'd had enough. You could see it in everything she did. Slow to move, slow to attack and slow to defend... and Cheryl was just eating her alive. Before long, all of Marcy's handiwork had been undone, and Danni was once again a bloody mess.

After another half-minute of picking her opponent apart, Cole decided to turn up the pressure. Coming in behind her jab, she launched into a perfect combination.

Jab, jab, cross, hook, uppercut.

Her movements were crisp and clean. Her footwork pristine as she flowed back and forth. It was a standard combination, and Cheryl threw it effortlessly - like she was working the pads back at the gym. But, standard or not, Danni had no answers. The double-jab seemed to surprise her, and her defense never recovered. The best she could do was backpedal to gain space, but Cole expected it and stayed with her step for step. Every punch landed with authority until Danni finally managed to get out of the way of the uppercut.

If you were simply focused on that moment, then you would think the last second recovery was a brilliant, veteran move that saved her from a devastating knockout. But the reality was nothing like that.

Danni's retreat had used up every inch of room she had. Her instincts kicked in - experience telling her the edge of the octagon was coming. But Cheryl was quick to head her off when she tried to slide out, tying her up and pressing her into the mesh directly in front of me.

So, the missed uppercut was nothing to Cheryl. She had gotten what she wanted - a beaten and fading Danni trapped on the fence, with most of the round still left to work with. Missing the knockout blow had simply prolonged the inevitable. And what happened from that point forward had to be as painful for Danni to endure as it was excruciating for me to watch.

Cole pecked away up high to bring Danni's guard up, then drove a knee into her side. Danni tried to turn her body to limit the damage, but she was late seeing what was coming and the twisting only opened up her right side for the short left hook that followed. The pain of that impact brought her guard down, and Cheryl's right sailed cleanly over the top and drilled into the champion's cheek.

To a fight purist, watching Cheryl work was like watching a Master Potter deftly shape an amorphous blob of clay into an exquisitely ornate vase. A blow to Danni's body would not only do damage, but also cause her guard to sink in order to protect the damaged area. Cole would respond by smashing her elbow into Danni's now unprotected face. Sequences like that repeated over and over, with Cheryl constantly a step ahead of her prey. She would see an opening in Danni's defense and attack. The strike would land and Danni would automatically adjust to close the hole. But Cole knew each adjustment would create a weakness somewhere else. She would pounce instantly, striking again - even before Danni finished reacting to the previous blow. In essence, she was dictating where Danni's guard would go, then attacking where she knew it wouldn't be.

The beginning of the end came when a crisp, straight right hand landed square in the center of Danni's face, slamming her head back into the mesh. Up came her guard, and Cole hooked her hands behind her victim's head and pulled her forward as she slammed her right knee up into Danni's gut. That's when the champion's body finally surrendered. I heard Danni groan as the knee sunk in deep, folding her nearly in half.

Using her whole body, Cheryl pressed her opponent back into the mesh. Then, her right fist swung out wide before hooking back and crashing into the champion's bloodied face. The blow spun Danni's head right - positioning it perfectly for the left hook that followed. That blow landed with a nauseating crunch right in the center of her unprotected face, and the effect it had was immediate. The muscles in Danni's body all seemed to contract at once, and her left arm twitched upward for no explainable reason. It was a response I had seen many times before, and I knew that - among other things - it meant Danni was unconscious.

Referee Natasha Yi moved in to put a stop to things, but there was no way she could get in-between the fighters quickly enough to stop Cheryl. The punch had driven Danni into the cage, and the natural rebound of the fencing caused her to pitch forward. But Cole was already moving - flowing into the next part of her assault. Even if she had noticed Danni's distress, it's doubtful she could have stopped herself. She finished the job with a smooth shift back to the right - unloading an uppercut with every ounce of her power. The punch landed with such force that I have no idea how it didn't break bones.

Video would show that the entire combination landed in mere seconds.

In real time, it felt like hours to me.

Yi finally got in the middle and stopped the onslaught. Cheryl dropped her guard and took a half-step back. Danni toppled backward and fell stiffly against the cage. Natasha tried to grab onto her and stop her from falling, but, again, she was a step too late. Just as the concerned ref reached out, Danni's muscles went flaccid and gravity took full effect. Her arm dropped back to her side and then she collapsed, her lower limbs becoming entangled beneath her. As her legs tried to bend in ways her bones would not allow, her upper body was forced back into the cage. For a moment, it appeared she would slide down and land in a seated position. But her right leg was too far under her, causing her torso to list to the left on the way down. Once that happened, her head had a clear path to the mat, and it slammed to the canvas right in front of me - bouncing like the head of a discarded toy doll.

Instantly, there was action and movement all around me.

Steph and Marcy rushing to Danni, with the on-site medical team right behind them.

Cheryl and her team celebrating the dominating victory that had just secured her the welterweight crown.

Fans of both fighters reacting in the crowd behind me.

But for me, the world moved in slow motion.

Danni's body was completely limp and motionless. There was blood all over her face and upper body - seemingly coming from everywhere. Her right leg had folded at the knee, leaving her foot trapped in a very unnatural position beneath her. The left leg had somehow straightened itself, jutting out at an odd angle toward the center of the octagon.

Part of me knew I should move. That I should get in there and help her. But I simply couldn't. I just stood there with my fingers gripping the mesh as if I was going to tear the cage down with my bare hands - crippled by a mix of fear and sorrow and anger. I could not take my eyes off of Danni, and I watched as a rivulet of blood and sweat ran down her nose, then snaked across her cheek before it finally cascaded to the canvas beneath her.

That was the last sight I saw before help arrived. Marcy, Steph and the team surrounded Danni and went to work - justifiably oblivious to my state.

I swallowed hard and searched for something else to focus on, and my eyes came to rest on Cheryl. She was standing across the cage, her fist in the air as she continued to celebrate with her group. Occasionally, she would glance in Danni's direction with a subtle, contemptuous sneer.

It was a dismissive look, meant to drive home how easily she'd dethroned the legendary champion. To display for the world that Danni wasn't on her level. It was a look that, had I seen it from one of my fighters, would have earned her quite an earful. But, overall, it was not an uncommon occurrence. It was also not hard to understand the mentality behind it.

After months and months of talk and questions and doubts and intrigue, there was no doubt Cheryl had been keyed up for this fight. Despite what she might have told herself, there was no way she could be sure of how things would turn out. She had to have been feeling the pressure. Now that it was over, all of that tension had evaporated at once. She had not only won, but done so in dominating fashion. It was hard not to feel a little full of yourself in a situation like that.

All of the hype and the hoopla of the past few months had culminated in this cage here tonight. This is where the talk ends, and the cage door closes and there's nothing left to do but prove yourself the better fighter. Cheryl's job was to destroy her opponent, and she had done a masterful job of it. There was no question who the better fighter was tonight. On this night, she had proven that Danni was not on her level. In doing so, she had earned the right to look at her beaten foe the way she was now.

But I still hated her for it.

Before I knew it, Danni was sitting up. Steph was talking to her, along with one of the medics - trying to get a quick read on how bad the damage was. Meanwhile, Marcy set to work once more on getting the bleeding under control. She worked as much magic as she could - getting Danni mopped up and the blood flow staunched within just a couple of minutes - but the swelling and other damage would have to wait. So, it was still obvious that RSI's stable leader had gotten the shit beat out of her, but at least she no longer looked like the victim in a horror movie.

The team helped her back to her feet, but she was still a bit woozy. Enough so that it would have been perfectly understandable if she had just let the medics lead her out of there so she could be tended to further. But she refused, just as I knew she would. Instead, she joined Yi and Cole at the center of the cage, for the official announcement.

I didn't hear a thing the ring announcer said, and I was unaffected when Yi raised Cheryl's arm in triumph. Instead, the only thing I could see was the fighters themselves.

Cole, happy and smiling. Her body healthy and unmarked. Looking like she was enjoying the adrenaline surge that follows a brisk workout at the gym.

Danni, somber and silent. Staring down at the canvas. Her face swollen to the point where she was nearly unrecognizable. Waiting for the ring announcer to formally end what was easily the worst fight she'd had in the time I've known her.

As I watched her, sadness and anger overtook me. I felt my eyes moisten, but I fought back the tears - adamant that I would not make things worse for her by getting emotional at cageside.

Through it all, only one question kept spinning around in my mind.

How the fuck did I let this happen?
