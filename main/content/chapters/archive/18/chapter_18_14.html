The parade started the instant we got back. Danni and I were both at the facility early Monday morning, as usual. Not long after, the women of RSI began to trickle in.

Many of them stopped in to see me.

All of them stopped in to see Danni.

One at a time. Two at a time. Five at a time.

They visited her office.

They stopped her in the hallway.

She was granted an hour or so of relative peace when Trish pulled her aside to run some tests. The results were all satisfactory, and she was cleared to return to full-contact workouts.

Then she was surrounded as soon as she stepped on the training floor.

At one point, there was a small crowd ringed around the cage where she was sparring with training partner Amanda Seyfried. Like they were checking out the lion cage at the zoo.

"Alright guys," I said as I moved in to break up the gathering. "Yes, yes... Danni's back. Now everybody get the hell back to work."

The group broke up and drifted away, and Danni flashed me a somebody help me  look that made me laugh.

I hung around for a while to discourage loitering, and I used the time to take my first assessment of where Danni was at, skill-wise.

What I saw early on was disappointing, mostly because it was boring. Mandy had come to see me earlier, concerned about how hard she should push, given Danni's injury. I gave her the same response I give whenever a fighter is coming back off medical suspension. If Trish says you're good, then you're good. But Amanda still seemed tentative - jabbing and moving and keeping Danni busy, but never really going on the attack.

Danni seemed to sense what was happening, and she eventually took the initiative herself. Pressing forward behind her jab, she bullied the blonde into the fence. She tangled up Amanda's hands, then thrust her knee toward her partner's side. Seyfried angled her body away and minimized the impact, but Danni suddenly pulled free of the grapple and threw the one, two.

Bap, bap!

Mandy took both punches full in the face, and it clearly rattled her.

In a competitive fight, that's the moment the aggressor goes nuts - throwing bombs from every angle. Forcing her disoriented opponent to gather herself in the face of intense pressure. That's what Amanda expected, and she instinctively tucked herself behind her guard. But Danni laid off, pulling back to the center of the cage.

Amanda uncurled from her defensive posture and stared at her sparring partner. Danni stared right back then lifted a hand and pointed at her.

The message was obvious.

You'd better hit me because I'm definitely going to hit you.

A warning, but also a form of permission. A tacit confirmation that she was OK, and a silent challenge for her training partner to bring it.

Amanda smiled as her eyes narrowed. Then the smile disappeared behind her guard, and she turned up the aggression as the spar resumed. Nothing was boring after that.

As I watched the back and forth, two thoughts occurred to me. The first was that Danni definitely still had it. She may not have been at her best against Cole, but her skills were still top shelf. In fact, she was looking extremely good for someone who had just followed a bad training camp with a terrible loss and a forced two-week layoff.

And that led to the other thought. When our evening training sessions stopped, Danni's regular training had continued. Day in and day out, she had been working hard to prepare for the fight with Cheryl.

I, on the other hand, had been basically inactive.

If I was going to be the stone that sharpened her skills, then I was going to need some hardening of my own. And it dawned on me that the tools I needed to make that happen were all around me at that very moment.

With my mind made up, I walked off to my office.

Fifteen minutes later, I was back - having traded my khakis and blouse for a pair of black compression shorts and a red sports bra.

I sought out Steph, and she saw me coming.

"Looks like you're ready to get back to work," she said.

"Yep," I nodded, "and I need your help."

I laid out the basics of my plan. She mostly just listened, and, at the end, she nodded steadily.

"I can set it up," she said. "Get some sort of rotation going."

"Alright," I said. "You let me know who to see and when. Meanwhile, I'm going to go stick my nose in somewhere. Find a place to get started."

As I turned to go, she gave me a friendly warning.

"They're not going to go easy on you," she said.

I thought back over all the previous times I had skirmished with the women of Rampage Sports.

"I know they won't."

I looked around the room again. Counted the faces that were silently turned in Danni's direction. Taking their own measure of where her skills were and thinking about how they could help her rebound.

"In fact," I said to Steph, "tell them why I'm coming."

"Now you're just begging for it."

I moved away, searching for trouble to get into. Emily VanCamp would be an interesting choice. Emma, of course, would be fit to explode over the thought of laying into me. But some strange gravity pulled me in the direction of Julianne Hough.

"You want to fight me?" she asked after I told her what I was after.

There was an excited tone to her voice that led me to suspect we were getting off on the wrong foot.

"Spar, Jules. The word we're using here is spar. Let's try to keep that in mind, OK?"

"Oh," she said disappointedly.

It was then that I really began to appreciate what I was opening myself up to. If the friendly and bubbly Julianne was so eager to kick my head in, what were the others going to be like?

I reminded her that the idea was to get me ready so that I could work with Danni, and that I would be of no help if I was dead.

She found that funny. Which didn't make me feel any better.

To my relief, RSI's first home-grown champion was merciful - handling the session more like a workout than a competition. Instead of trying to knock me flat, she focused on making me work. Using footwork to move me around, and pressuring me with fast combinations.

It was a good approach, given that it was my first real workout in a long time. Before long, I was drenched in sweat, but it felt good to let the heat sink into my joints. Jules seemed to sense that I was starting to feel more like myself, so she turned it up a notch. Coming at me harder. Not just chipping the rust off, but blasting it away with pure power.

When it was over, I was proud of myself for keeping up. Then I compared the two of us. Jules looked primed and ready for more. I was one step away from needing medical attention.

Pride was quickly replaced by the feeling that I had a lot of work to do.

"How's she doing?" Julianne asked after I'd recovered a little.

We both looked over to where Danni stood with her fellow welterweights.

"She's alright," I said.

With their morning spar completed, Danni and Amanda had moved into mentor mode - coaching Cobie Smulders and Anna Kendrick through some defensive techniques. Any lack of confidence Danni was feeling definitely wasn't showing at that moment. She was focused directly on Anna as the two rehearsed the art of recovering your guard after an exchange. As I watched her walk her younger teammate through a sequence I was struck by how comfortable she looked. This was Danni in her element, and she was taking to it the same way she had before Melbourne.

But she wasn't the same, and I wasn't the only one who knew it.

"This has to be hard on her," Hough said.

I turned and studied the blonde champion for a moment. Julianne had certainly had some setbacks, but she'd never suffered the kind of letdown Danni had. Yet, there on her face, I saw that she somehow understood. That she knew the pressure that comes with constantly battling the best. The fear that you would come out flat one night and end up humiliated. Danni's fears had now come true, and, on some level, Jules grasped what that was like.

I nodded my agreement, then we watched the welterweight group for a bit longer.

Anna was gloved up and ready go to work with Cobie. Danni was standing beside her, verbally walking through the plan one more time. Highlighting the nuances of the technique before turning the two of them loose.

As I watched Danni work, I realized her faith wasn't as shaken as I thought it was. When she was busy and involved, there were no traces of doubt in her behavior. She still had a natural belief in herself. A deep-seated layer of confidence that Cole hadn't been able to touch.

Her teammates and I could work with that and help her get the rest back. And the woman standing next to me was ready and willing to report for duty.

"I owe her a lot," Jules said.

My mind drifted as I nodded absently, taking me back to when Rampage Sports  was almost completely unknown and making me think of the sequence of events that had changed that.

Three years ago, a steadily improving Julianne came out of nowhere to win one of the league's biggest tournaments, overcoming many of the best fighters in her weight class to make it happen. Later that year, she did it again, conquering many of those same fighters, along with a pair of titans the seeding had allowed her to avoid the first time.

In next to no time, Jules had gone from relative obscurity to super stardom. Two tournament belts in one year. Numerous victories over the best fighters in the league. It was insane, and her divisional ranking had shot up just as quickly as her popularity. So, when it came time for her to put the icing on the cake and nail down the divisional crown, she was the odds-on favorite.

Then, with less than a month to go, current champion Vikki Blows gave up her independent status and signed on with Savage Wolf MMA - the top stable in the league. The same stable that manages Cheryl Cole, as a matter of fact.

The group's success was owed to a common mentality shared by the entire roster.

Win at all costs.

Attack your opponent relentlessly.

Destroy her before she even knows what happened.

Jules and Vikki had already met a few times, and recent history was heavily in Julianne's favor. So, I didn't think much of the signing. With less than a month to go, how much difference could the new stable make?

Danni felt differently. She was in my office the next day, telling me that we needed to revamp Julianne's training. That the version of Vikki she was about to face would be vastly different from the one she'd seen in the past.

I resisted. I worried that we would end up retraining Jules for a Vikki that didn't exist.

Danni was adamant - refusing to back down from her assessment.

Until that point, discussions like this had mostly been a waste of time for us. I was somewhat opinionated - Danni would likely substitute a word that was less polite there - and she had consistenly been too quick to retreat. Unsure of herself and, therefore, unsure of how hard she should push.

But this one was different. Whether it was her past experience with Cole or just some gut instinct she wouldn't let go of, she made it clear that she wasn't leaving my office until I relented.

I remember thinking at the time that I had little choice but to give in. Not because I believed her. I was still certain I was right. But this was what I'd wanted to see from her from the beginning. I didn't need someone who would yes me to death. I needed someone who would challenge me and get in my face. And here she was, doing exactly that. Standing up to me, definitively and ferociously.

Despite my reservations, I owed her the chance to see it through.

Jules spent all of her remaining training time working directly with Danni. The experience was nothing short of hell. Danni was vicious and unrelenting, bringing Julianne to the point of tears a number of times. Knowing her as I do now, I imagine that had to be hard on her. To continuously attack her own teammate, and treat her so mercilessly.

But, when fight night came, Jules quickly found it in her heart to forgive her stable leader. Because Danni had been absolutely right.

Vikki came out with a whole new attitude and a new game plan that was pure offense. If not for Danni, Jules would have been blown right out of the cage. Instead, she was completely prepared... weathering Vikki's early attacks, then responding with an all-out assault of her own.

It was a savage contest, with both fighters spilling a lot of blood in a very short period of time. But it ended with the ref holding Julianne's fist in the air, and she hasn't looked back since.

I knew immediately that Julianne wasn't the only winner that night. I went into that event with a rag-tag collection of fighters who were slowly discovering themselves and learning to win. I came out with a solid new champion to inspire them and a strong, capable stable leader to show them the way. And none of it would have happened if Danni hadn't chosen that moment to plant her stake in the ground.

There is no way to quantify what that night meant for Rampage Sports. All I can say is, just look at us now. When we started, only Danni and Steph held divisional titles. Now, we can boast of a roster that holds thirteen such champions. And our stature in the league has grown right along with that number. Then, we were barely a blip on the radar. Now, when people talk about the best stables, there is no debate to be had over which group is number one. It's us. By a mile.

"We all owe her," I said as my mind came back to the present.

Then I focused hard on Danni and said, "And we're going to pay her back."

"You bet your ass we are," Jules added.
