Daya had proven up to the task so far, but, with twenty seconds left in the round, Grace finally found a way.

The two were circling left with Grace staying just out of range. Then the blonde suddenly switched directions, stepping back to her right. Z did likewise and raised her foot for the teep. Grace changed directions again, stepping left as she surged forward. Daya popped the teep but - because of Grace's lateral movement - she only got a small piece of the blonde's side. Jillian, meanwhile, finally had a clear lane.

Left hook, uppercut.

Daya was badly out position and Grace's aim was true. Both punches hit home and it was immediately clear that Coleman was hurt. She retreated and pull her head down behind her guard. Grace went to the body... first the left hook, then the right. Daya's guard came down - just as Jillian knew it would - and now the same two punches sailed in head-high. Z's head whipped left, then right. She stumbled and backpedaled and Grace practically ran after her to finish her off. Coleman blundered into the fence, and you could almost see Jillian salivating.

DING!

Reeser jumped in to separate the two as the bell sounded to end the round.

I exhaled loudly as I looked up into the rafters again.

"Ohhhh... my god," Danni moaned as she ran a hand through her hair.

Though the round had been completely different, it had ended exactly the same way the first one had. With Z lucky to still be standing upright.

* * *

Two rounds down, one to go, and I didn't like the look of things at all. Though the judges had probably leaned Z's way in the second, the near disaster at the end meant there was no guarantee. And Grace had clearly taken the first in a landslide. Even if Daya could control the upcoming final round, there was no way to say for sure how a decision might go. She would need a decisive performance - or an even more decisive finish - if she was going to win this thing.

There was no wasted time for the RSI team this time around. Steph went over and nonchalantly helped Daya to the corner, where Marcy and Emily were already waiting. With no new open wounds to tend to, the Nordic magician went right to work on the swelling - knowing she had a much better chance of controlling it this time since Grace hadn't done any real damage until the end of the round.

I was so busy watching Marcy work, that it took me nearly ten seconds to realize that it was Emily standing in front of Coleman this time while Steph had taken on the role of assisting RSI's cut specialist.

Danni and I looked at each other.

"How is it that, no matter how much credit I give her, I constantly underestimate Steph?" I asked.

Danni just shook her head and gave a what can I tell you?  shrug.

The connection between Emily and Zendaya was now blatantly obvious. Rather than trying to become part of that, Steph had gotten completely out of the way. Some might see that as her admitting failure. I saw it as her doing her job better than ever. Daya needed Em right now, and Steph wasn't going to let some arbitrary pecking order interfere with that. Instead, she did what she always does. Focus on what was in the best interest of her fighter. And so she had handed the reins over to Em and then gotten down on one knee and started handling water bottles and washing spit and blood off of mouthpieces.

Not only was it the right thing to do, but I was absolutely certain it had been entirely Steph's idea.

Emily, meanwhile, was right in Z's face. Not yelling. Coaching. Throwing imaginary punches. Slapping her elbows to remind her pupil that she could use those too. Mostly though, she was pointing down. Telling Daya to remember that her feet were her greatest weapon.

There were a lot of ways to look at this situation. I think many might feel that Z had already scored an enormous victory. After all, no one really believed she even stood a prayer when this had started, but she had lasted two full rounds and had definitely done much more than merely survive. She had stood nose-to-nose with Grace for the entirety of the second, and had owned most of the round. It was a performance to be proud of, but every look Em gave and every move she made delivered a much different message. A message that said, I don't want to hear about moral victories. I believe in you and I know you can WIN this thing.

As the break wound down, she grabbed Z's chin in much the same way Steph had earlier. She didn't say a word. Just gave a firm, resolute nod that Daya immediately returned.

All I could do was shake my head. Steph had been absolutely right earlier. While I was busy being encouraged by the way the rookies were getting along with their veteran counterparts, I had failed to see that something truly amazing was happening within the walls of Rampage Sports. They weren't just getting along. They were getting together. Teaming up and getting involved in each other. Nowhere was that more apparent than in the scene unfolding in that cage right now. Prior to Zendaya being forced to haul Emily's bag a few months ago, I couldn't recall seeing the two so much as speak to each other. Now they looked like life-long comrades, marching into battle together.

Maybe that was the deal. Carry my bags rookie, and I'll carry YOU.

Regardless, I now found myself wanting this for both of them. It would be the fitting result. The one they both deserved. But in the cage, you don't get what you deserve.

You get what you go out there and take.

* * *

The most aggravating thing about the way the previous round ended was what it did for Grace's mental state. For nearly the entire round, she had been frustrated, angry and confused. Then, with only seconds left, she had come within inches of putting Daya out of her misery. Another ten seconds, and this one would have been all over.

The confidence boost that had given her was obvious as the final round got underway. Once again, she was dancing just out of Daya's range, but there was nothing tentative or uncertain about the way she was moving. Every move she made seemed deliberate. She would surge and feint, then back away. Quick sudden movements designed to get Z to show the teep. And it was working. Daya's foot was bouncing up and down like a yo-yo, and she actually threw the kick several times without hitting anything. All of this was data for Jillian to compute. A way for her to get comfortable with Z's movement and her timing on the kick.

Once she had the feel, she made her move.

Again, it started with a quick step to her right. Z turned to square and popped the teep, but Grace had immediately slipped back to the left. The kick missed right and Jillian moved in, hands up high. Daya stuck her jab out, but Grace guarded it aside and hit her with a big left hook.

Daya was already sliding away to her left, which helped to lessen the impact. Still it was a solid punch that did real damage.

Once she was safely out of range, Zendaya stole a look at her mentor. I noticed the broadcast feed seemed to continually find Em between exchanges now, as well. Apparently, someone with a brain had clued the TV people into the peculiar dynamic between the young rookie and RSI's most decorated lightweight, and they were watching as closely as we were.

Right now, it showed her miming an aggressive sweeping punch across her body with her left hand.

"She wants her to counter into the gap," Danni translated.

It was a basic law of fight physics. If your opponent's movement opens a window for her to punch into, it also gives you a place to put your counter. If Z could keep her positioning and react quickly enough, she could get there first.

Of course, the downside of that is what could happen if the counter didn't land. It was a lesson Daya was about to be reminded of in a very painful way.

The next exchange started with Grace moving right again. Z raised the teep, but wasn't as quick to bite. Focused on maintaining crucial balance and positioning that left her better prepared when Grace changed direction. Jillian moved in and Z tried to counter with the left hand, but the punch was slow and tentative. Worse than that, the miss gave away that balance she'd been so careful to protect and Grace caught her flat-footed with another big left hook. There was no lessening the impact of this one. It caught her flush on the cheek and it was followed immediately by a wicked right hand that drove Daya straight back. The younger fighter retreated, stumbling and tripping on the way out, but managing to get clear before more damage could be done.

Daya looked more than a little frazzled. Emily was gesturing mightily with the left again, imploring her to throw the counter more confidently.

Grace came forward again and offered a few feints that Daya refused to bite on. Then she did the right-left slip and in she came. Instead of being more emphatic, Z's counter was a disastrous, half-hearted mess because she was already in full retreat before she threw it. The early fall back worked in that she got out of danger before Grace could get to her, but it gave off the terrible impression of running away. The kind of thing that was guaranteed to lose her this fight.

At cageside, Emily - perhaps realizing her fighter was coming unglued - was going with a whole new approach. Quieting her movements, save for a steady palms-down gesture with both hands.

This one was universally recognizable.

Alright, settle down.

Amazingly, the gesture appeared to work. Only seconds ago, Daya looked so unnerved as to be on the verge of panic. But now she settled back into her stance and seemed to quickly find a rhythm.

I'd never seen anything like it. It looked like Z was actually feeding off her mentor's emotional state.

"OK, what in the hell is happening here?" I asked.

Danni looked equally mystified, but took a shot at explaining anyway.

"She's a smart kid," she said of Zendaya. "She obviously trusts Emily, and it's gotten her this far. Why stop now?"

Emily's gesturing became slower, but more emphatic. Soothing, but also staunchly supportive.

You know what you need to do, and we both know you can.

In truth, there was no choice now. No time to mix in some new tactic or idea. Daya was already behind and Grace was getting the better of her again in this round. She needed to turn things around, and the first thing she needed to do was put a stop to the way her opponent was outmaneuvering her.

To do it, she was going to have to do more than trust Emily. She was going to have to trust herself.

And now would be a good time to figure it out because Jillian was coming again.

The two fighters circled near the center of the ring, both looking for angles. Grace was looking supremely confident for all the reasons I just mentioned. Despite suffering her biggest scare to date, she was now controlling the cage once more. Instead of a letdown, this would be a feather in her cap. After so many quick and easy wins, she had faced a true challenge and still come out on top.

Now, if she could only get her younger opponent to stand and fight her so she could go for the big finish.

She stepped right again - a move Daya still had to honor despite knowing it was likely a fake - then slid immediately left and went on the attack.

BOOM!

Rather than giving ground, Z had planted and fired as soon as she was sure Grace was coming. Exactly what Emily had wanted. Not a defensive jab. A heavy, confident, perfectly-timed left hand thrown across her body and into the lane Grace was trying to come through - catching her just as she was surging forward.

The resulting impact was spectacular.

A halo of sweat sprayed from Grace's head as the punch crushed her face. Her left knee momentarily buckled, then she staggered in that direction with her head leading the rest of her body. It was hard to tell just how much of her faltering was the simple shock of getting caught with the counter and how much was her being truly hurt.

Two seconds later it didn't matter.

Daya - who clearly knew that it was now or never - had stayed in her stance and walked the reeling blonde down. Ready to pounce on any opportunity she saw. That opportunity came when Jillian got her feet under her and started to gather her defense. Z never gave her the chance. With one last step she slid left, planted her lead leg and spun... bringing her right foot off the canvas. Her momentum merged with the power generated by her leg. The kick sailed cleanly over Grace's meager guard and exploded just below the blonde's left ear.

The classic high hick. The move we'd seen Emily end so many fights with, now executed to perfection by her young pupil. Simple, yet effective... there is no way to overestimate the damage it can do. As in, if it hits you squarely, you are done.

Such was the case for the previously undefeated Jillian Grace.

The blonde fell over in a heap, and Reeser was there immediately. Covering the prone fighter with her body while shouting for Z to back off.

Daya recovered her stance like a veteran. Her leg retracted and the foot touched back down to the canvas... perfectly spread from its mate. Hands up and ready for more.

She stayed like that for a few seconds. Frozen in her combative pose. Like an ancient warrior, serenely overlooking her fallen rival after a great battle.

Then she slowly realized what she'd done and turned to Emily and Steph with a look that asked, Are you fucking kidding me?

Both veterans gave her a smile and a little be cool  gesture as they worked their way into the cage behind Grace's team. Once through the door, Emily went immediately to Z. The two touched foreheads as the teacher quietly praised her young pupil in a genuinely heartwarming scene.

Then Marcy broke it up as she abruptly shouldered her way in to check Daya's condition.

I guess you can add nor touching moment of victory  to that list of things that don't slow RSI's wound specialist down.

Up in the box, Danni and I were both smiling broadly.

"Three-for-three," I said. "They all won."

True, a couple of them had done it by the skin of their teeth. But a win is a win, and it was a damn fine improvement over their past performances.

"Once again, RSI shows what it's made of," she said with an authentic note of superiority.

That kind of prideful boasting didn't fit at all with her personality or the personality of the stable she leads. But given the circumstances, it was more than warranted. This wasn't a win for a single fighter or even three. It was a victory for the entire organization.

The medics had just gotten Grace to her feet when the announcer's voice started in on the official announcement, but we didn't stay around to hear it.

We had someplace we needed to be.

* * *

By the time we reached the RSI locker room, every member of the stable was gathered in the back. Zendaya was holed up in one of the training rooms, being assessed by the medical staff. I didn't see her fellow rookies anywhere, so I assumed they were in there with her. I didn't really understand how that happened, but I wasn't worried. If they got in the way, Trish would calmly open the door and unceremoniously boot them into the hall.

I guess everyone behaved themselves because no such corrective action was required. All three emerged together when the door opened a few minutes later, and they were immediately swarmed by their teammates as a deafening roar erupted in the locker room. Britney and Elisha wasted no time getting to Z, with Brit catching the youngest member of the middleweight group in a headlock while Leesh gave her what I believe is technically known as a noogie. Emily was there too, standing a discreet distance from the fray while shaking her head in mock disapproval. Jules threw her arms around Dove, with Jen Morrison standing by waiting for her turn. Tiz slung an arm over Liv's shoulder while Emma reached out and tousled the younger fighter's blonde locks.

Danni and I paused to check in with Trish and Marcy. All good. Liv and Zendaya would need to go through the full concussion protocol as a precaution, but neither were showing any significant signs of trouble.

With that established, Danni took a few steps toward Liv, who saw her coming and practically launched herself into her stable leader's arms.

For the time being, I sort of hung off to the side. I wanted to run over and pull all three young fighters into a big hug. To congratulate them and tell them how proud I was. Then I wanted to drag them into a room and show them some video. To take each one and run tonight's performance side-by-side with her last fight. Let them see just how far they'd come in such a short time.

But all of that would have to wait because I couldn't even get close to them. Their teammates had them now, and there would be no prying them loose. The party was on.

Welcome to the family, kids.
