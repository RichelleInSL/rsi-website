Rod Laver Arena, Melbourne, Australia

After the bludgeoning on the fence, the action in the cage slowed considerably. Despite the fact that Danni looked as vulnerable as I'd ever seen her, Cole continued to take her time. She had the champion hurt, bloodied and on the ropes. With more than two rounds to go, there was no reason to take chances and risk a stupid mistake. Instead, she happily began a torturous cycle that slowly eroded whatever fight Danni had left. Cole would carefully pursue... methodically chipping away while maintaining her own defense. Danni would protect herself as best she could, but she was already so worn down... she just couldn't keep up. Finally, she would get fed up with Cole's relentless pecking and try to actively fight back. But Cheryl would easily ward off the attack while letting the champion get herself out of position, then launch a counter while Danni was defenseless.

Wash, rinse, repeat.

Cole faithfully continued the pattern until the bell finally rang and mercifully interrupted the game. There was no mistaking the wicked smile on her face as she turned to head back to her corner. She wasn't just winning the fight. She was making a mockery out of the champion. That fact would be apparent even to someone who walked into the arena at that exact moment. All you had to do was compare the two fighters. Cole looked like she'd just finished a light warmup before her workout - her skin glowing with a thin sheen of sweat. Danni looked like a ragdoll that had been carelessly discarded on the highway, only to be recovered months later in the middle of a thunderstorm. She was covered in blood and sweat, and strands of hair that had escaped her braid flew wildly all around her face.

The seconds poured into the cage, with Steph heading straight for Danni. She kept things cool, talking and gesturing as though the reason she'd rushed over was that she simply couldn't wait to start giving her fighter advice for the next round. In reality, I knew she was trying to minimize the appearance that the RSI team leader was disoriented by the beating she'd taken. She got Danni turned in the right direction and nonchalantly guided her to where I was waiting with the stool and corner gear. She got her down on the stool I'd set up, then kept right on talking as Danni unconsciously spit her mouthpiece into my hand. Meanwhile, the cutman went to work on her face.

Oh, pardon me. I should have said cutwoman.

The function of the cutman is two-fold. First, that person needs to prepare the fighter before the fight to minimize the damage she suffers during the melee. I won't get too deep into the ways in which that's done, partially because some of the techniques are slightly icky. But suffice it to say that the prep work can be handled by just about anyone with the right knowledge. The real key to the job is being good when the fighters need you most - during that preciously tiny window they get to collect themselves between rounds.

For years, we've made do with a merry-go-round of different people stepping in to handle this role. Some have been good. Some have left a little something to be desired. But the women of RSI have finally found what they're looking for in Marcelina Bukowsi.

After so many swings and misses, I finally turned the task of finding a cutman over to Dr. Patricia Leone, head of the RSI medical staff. As with so many things, Trish came through with flying colors... even though initial reaction was somewhat skeptical.

The first thing people notice about Marcy is that she is an effortlessly beautiful woman. Tall, blonde and blue-eyed - with fine, sharp facial features that look like they were carved from the ice her ancestral Norwegian home was known for - she absolutely commands the attention of every eyeball in any room she enters. She had the kind of looks advertisers kill for, and she seemed predestined by nature for supermodel stardom.

And if by model  you mean the very model of a modern major-general, then that's exactly what she became.

There are many things the fighters like about Marcy. They certainly respect her knowledge and professionalism, and I think they enjoy her accessible, self-effacing nature. But, most of all, I think they appreciate the way she sheds that nature and turns on the intensity when they need her most. Especially during that oh-so-important one-minute period between rounds. The girls have taken to calling it Marcy's Minute  because, when a fighter is injured, Marcy takes command. And nothing will prevent her from getting the job done. Neither snow, nor rain, nor Steph McMahon.

You heard me right.

The two tangled not long after Marcy joined us. Dianna Dahlgren was doing battle with the far more experienced Emma Kuziara, and she was doing a great job of holding her own. But a particularly nasty blow had opened up a gash above the blonde's right eye.

Blood is part of the deal for a fighter, but if it's running into your eyes, you're gonna have problems. So, when Marcy entered the cage, she knew Dianna was counting on her.

The Norwegian went straight to one knee, and assessed the damage. Steph - who was accustomed to running the show during the breaks - moved in on Dianna's other side to start giving the fighter advice. Just as she did so, Marcy stood and switched sides... walking right through Steph and knocking her on her ass. Steph sat up, looking ready to kill. Marcy didn't notice. In fact, she never acknowledged anything had even happened. She just found the position she wanted, grabbed what she needed from her caddy and set to work. Toweling, poking, smearing... she was like a tiny whirlwind. Steph, too, quickly shook off the collision and refocused on giving Dianna the advice she needed.

Once they were back outside, Steph found Marcy at cageside - her caddy up on the apron as she reorganized to be ready for the next break.

I wasn't there for the conversation, but the reports I got from those who witnessed it were all basically identical.

Steph only got the word "Hey" out of her mouth before Marcy cut her off.

"I had a lot to do, and only a minute to do it in," the Nordic beauty said matter-of-factly. "These women need me as much as they need you, and I need space to work. I'm sorry about what happened, but you can't be in my way."

Then she turned back to her reorganizing, leaving Steph to stare at the back of her head.

The matter wasn't discussed again that night. Steph gave Marcy a wider berth for the rest of the event and there were no further problems.

The next day, I saw them together in one of the RSI training rooms, involved in an intense but seemingly level-headed discussion. I have no idea what they said to each other, and I likely never will. I only know that, since then, the two of them operate like a well-oiled machine. Maybe they worked out a system of subtle signs or maybe they've just learned to anticipate each other, but watching them work is akin to watching the inner workings of a ticking clock.

The first thing they established was that Marcy gets first preference on positioning. She's the one who needs physical access to the fighter, so Steph let's her get in, assess the damage and decide where she wants to settle in. Whenever possible, she picks a spot that will let her do everything she needs to. When Marcy does need to move, Steph seems to sense it coming and adjusts accordingly.

How they manage to do this without slamming into each other, I have no idea. The best answer I can give is that, when you have two women who take their jobs as seriously as these two do, shit just gets worked out.

I will say this much: It's highly doubtful that Steph yielded because Marcy demanded it. I find it much more likely that she granted our new trainer the additional latitude out of respect.

It takes a lot to earn that type of deference from Steph, which should tell you all you need to know about Marcelina Bukowski.

Always one to happily learn from the mistakes of others, I knew enough to stay the hell out of Marcy's way, despite the fact that this was my first time performing as Danni's second. I agreed to take on the role during the period of excitement that came after we'd decided to explore a more romantic angle to our relationship. The mixture of fear and anticipation that existed right at that moment is impossible to describe. I knew that it could all end in disaster, but I was focused on all the possibilities that existed if things went well. I wanted to believe in those possibilities, and agreeing to stand behind Danni when she fought seemed like the right way to show that.

As I stood now and watched Marcy mop the blood off of my friend's face, it occurred to me that I should have thought things through a little more.

I pushed the thought aside and focused on my job. After a quick assessment, Marcy picked her spot - moving left to right from Danni's perspective - and set up shop on that side. I moved into the space she'd vacated, and set the two buckets I was carrying down on the canvas about four feet from Danni - where I could easily reach them while allowing room in case Marcy had to come back. One bucket was loaded with ice, while the other was empty.

I pulled a water bottle from the ice bucket. Danni never looked my way but seemed to sense the bottle was near. She angled her head slightly in my direction, opened her mouth and took a long pull. She faced full-forward for a moment - with her focus on Steph - as she worked the water around in her mouth, then angled back toward me and spit into the empty bucket, which I had raised up from the floor.

Working the corner is not an attractive activity.

Danni gave me a quick glance and a nod, indicating she wanted another mouthful of water. We repeated the procedure as she kept her attention on Steph, who was busy giving pointers on what was going wrong and how Danni could make it right.

Or, more accurately, she was barking orders at her.

"Keep your feet moving! You're making this way too easy for her!"

Danni spit again and said, "She's too fast!"

In the time we've known each other, Danni has faced every single one of the top fighters in her weight class. She's had great performances, and she's had tough times. But, before that moment, I had never heard her admit an opponent was better than she was in any way.

"Bullshit!" Steph replied. "You're faster than she is! You know you are!"

Marcy produced a medium-sized glass bottle and an overlong cotton swab. In a series of steady, fluid movements, she pulled the rubber stopper off the bottle, dipped the swab in its contents, then dabbed it along a gash over Danni's right eye. The injured fighter winced sharply at the contact, but quickly shook it off.

"I can't get ahead of her," she said to Steph.

"That's because you're letting her dictate. You have to be more aggressive. Challenge her to respond instead of waiting for her to attack."

Danni nodded passively - thinking about what Steph had said. She was desperate to believe, but looked totally unconvinced that she could turn things around.

Marcy tossed the used swab into my empty bucket and reached down for another. She dipped a second swab, then went to work on a patch of broken skin under the other eye. She tossed that one, then nodded at me. Down went the spit/trash bucket and up came the other. Marcy grabbed a tool called an enswell from the ice and pressed it against the mouse that was growing under Danni's right eye.

The enswell is basically a chunk of metal with a handle attached, and it looks a lot like a miniature iron. Remember when your mother put ice on your boo-boo to prevent it from swelling? That's what an enswell does, only without the need for the little plastic baggie.

With my job basically done, I turned my full attention to Danni. Again I was struck by the ridiculousness of what I was seeing. Nothing about the woman in front of me looked like the Danni I knew. To see her like this was jarring and scary, and - when her desolate gaze drifted over to me - I struggled to even form a sentence.

"Maybe stop letting her punch you in the face so much," I said finally.

Maybe if I'd felt stronger - more confident about Danni's chances - I could have sold the wiseass remark better. Instead, the weak tone of my voice seemed to reveal what I was truly feeling and only added to the dark urgency that hung over our little group.

Danni needed to get her shit together, and she needed to do it now. And I wasn't helping.

"Listen to me," Steph snapped, pulling Danni's attention back to her. "None of us are stupid. We all know this isn't going well. But she hasn't seen your best yet. Not tonight. So, go out there and show her what you can do."

It wasn't the world's greatest pep talk, but, under the circumstances, I thought it was pretty good. Danni seemed to agree, nodding solidly as she stared across the cage at her tormentor. Then she looked at me, and I nodded, too.

Referee Natasha Yi called for the seconds to exit the cage. Grabbing the water bottle, I quickly rinsed Danni's mouthpiece, then helped her get it back in her mouth. After that, I was careful to keep my focus on collecting my supplies and avoiding eye contact with Danni. Not because I thought I would make a mistake and leave something behind, but because I was worried that she would see something in my face. I wanted to leave her with Steph's final words echoing in her ears and that confident nod I had managed. What I didn't want was for her to perceive the doubt I felt.

No, not doubt. Certainty. Certainty that Cheryl had seen the best she had to offer on this night, and that it was nowhere near enough.
