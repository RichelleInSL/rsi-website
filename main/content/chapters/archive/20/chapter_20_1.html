Steph McMahon looked a little disgruntled. Which, to be honest, was a pretty happy state, for her.

She and RSI Stable Leader Danneel Harris were gathered in my office to discuss the upcoming event for the Youth Gone Wild franchise, which featured the league's youngest fighters.

"Where are we at?" I asked.

"Behind, as usual," Steph grumbled. "The rookies are improving, but still clueless. Gomez is coming along, but she still needs time. And Alex Morgan is no joke, so that's likely to be a disaster. Emma's pretty good, but that's not always enough when someone is gunning for your title. J-Law is status quo, and I give Dahlgren a decent shot to win."

She said it all very calmly. Simply presenting the facts. But Steph's facts come by way of a very dark filter, so they all had to be run through a thorough reality check.

Our current crop of rookies had not shown the greatest potential when we first brought them on, but a major attitude adjustment - both for them and for the stable as a whole - has really kick-started their development. They were still far from seasoned veterans, so Steph wasn't totally wrong. But when compared against other fighters of similar experience, they were showing signs of being able to pull away from the pack.

Selena Gomez was one of our most recent signings. A veteran fighter with plenty of experience, and - by our measure - plenty of untapped potential. Again, I'm sure Steph had a point. Sel had only been with us for a couple of months, and I'm sure she wasn't as squared away as our Development Coordinator would have liked. But she was still a damn-good fighter, and she'd beaten Morgan in the past. It would be a tough fight, for sure, but probably not the calamity Steph was hinting at.

As for Emma Watson, we all knew her to be well-beyond pretty good. She was, in fact, one of the most elite fighters in the league. Tournaments and special events have brought her toe-to-toe with just about every top fighter at her weight, and she's always proven up to the challenge. But her divisional competition was a clear step down. Upcoming opponent Victoria Justice was a solid fighter who was getting better all the time, but it would be a considerable upset if she took the title away from Emma. Of course, once again, Miss McMahon had a point. When an opponent has her eye on your belt, no amount of preparation is enough to guarantee victory.

Meanwhile, status quo was a Steph-style, full-throated compliment for Jennifer Lawrence, who has been on a steady march to the top since the day we signed her. A title-run at the last tournament ended in a decision loss to Josie Maran, but not before Jen amassed a load of ranking points from her preliminary round victories. That moved her up to third in her division, with the path to the belt laid out for her as plainly as a yellow-brick road. With champion Debby Ryan on the shelf due to injury, Jen was due to face top contender Gracie Carvalho. A victory for Lawrence would see the two swap places, putting Jen in position to face Ryan for the title upon her return. Champions are made in many different ways. Emma had the benefit of being a woman among girls, so to speak. Using her experience advantage to stay ahead of a rotating cast of challengers. Julianne Hough had blasted her way to the top in a single, crazy year that saw her win not only the divisional belt, but two tournament titles along the way. Emily VanCamp's career had been a roller-coaster before she finally established herself with the elite on a permanent basis. But J-Law's ascent had featured a much more traditional approach. Stay focused. Stay patient. Get better. And, most importantly, win more than you lose. Now, all that patience and effort was on the verge of paying off. She had come to us as a good fighter with a lot of potential. Now, she was a legitimate threat to anyone in the league, and she routinely displayed the calm confidence that comes from knowing that fact. Carvalho was a dangerous opponent, but not one a status quo J-Law couldn't handle.

Lastly, Dianna Dahlgren... who had put on quite a show of her own at the last tournament. One that reminded everyone that not all Cinderella runs are created equal. While J-Law vaulting to the final despite her low seed was a surprise, it was not a total shock. It was more like watching the expectations surrounding her translate into results at long last. On paper, Dianna's run looked nearly identical, yet it had much more of an earthquake feel to it. She'd shown flashes of talent in the past, but she'd never been able to put it all together consistently. Now, here she was, suddenly knocking down contenders like dominoes. After a couple of solid victories over similarly ranked opponents, her journey seemed sure to end against last year's Cinderella, Samantha Hoopes. But the fight was all Dahlgren, from start to finish. A statement win, if ever there was one. Then came Traci Bingham, a bona fide title contender and frequent nemesis of our own Catherine Bell. A fighter totally out of Dahlgren's league... yet Dianna went right ahead and wiped the canvas with her in a fight that was never even a contest. Suddenly, she was on the verge of actually bringing home the belt... until she ran into a bit of bad luck in the form of Cat, herself. As frequent training partners, Bell knew every move the younger fighter had in her growing arsenal, and sheer will just wasn't enough to see Dianna through. But the astounding run she'd put together had been more than enough to get tongues wagging, and the opinions were just about evenly split. Many felt it was her true potential finally showing through, while others said it was merely a mirage. One of those crazy things that happen every so often, but can never be fully explained. This upcoming fight was Dianna's chance to prove to everyone - including herself - that she was for real. Knowing what was on the line, Steph giving her a decent shot told me she was gonna make it count.

All in all, things were far from as grim as Ms. McMahon made them seem, but I've gotten used to the pessimistic view of everything that is her norm. In fact, if she ever told me we were totally prepared for an event, I'd probably have her checked for a fever.

"Anything else?" I asked.

"Got Aly coming up," Danni offered.

Unh.

Talk about a mood killer.

Alyson Michalka's plight had become something of an ongoing joke. One I didn't find the slightest bit funny. Make no mistake... she's a good fighter. She's even got a pair of tournament titles to her name, and they don't give those things away for free. In fact, having seen her fight, I'd say she's as good a fighter as Emma. But unlike Em, Aly's division has always been loaded with high-level talent. Think of it as... well... the pit of hell. For years, it was ruled by the vaunted Brooklyn Decker, while the battle for second place was a never-ending scrum between Aly, Erin Heatherton and India Reynolds. Alyson has beaten all of these fighters numerous times. She's also lost to all of them... numerous times. In all, it's fair to say she's held her own. But while Heatherton and Reynolds would occasionally swipe the belt from Decker, Aly's never been able to get the timing right. She just couldn't seem to pull out a big win when the belt was on the line.

Then she got her big break. The same league reorganization that brought Knightley into Julianne's division swept Decker up into the next franchise. Aly was the top contender at that moment, which made her the nominal champion. True to form, she roundly rejected the title because she hadn't earned it, but it was still a huge opportunity. If she could "defend" the belt against second-ranked Heatherton, that would legitimize her status and finally make her the champion so many had long believed she was. Alas, it was not to be. Erin won the fight and has since cemented herself as the rightful heir to Decker's throne. Meanwhile, Reynolds has fallen away, leaving Aly as the persistent number one challenger. But time after time, she's simply never been able to get over the hump. Heatherton vs Michalka is always a headline attraction, and the two never fail to put on a terrific fight. The only problem is that Michalka never comes out on top.

"And?" I asked.

Another shrug.

"She's as good as always," Steph said.

"No telling what that'll mean, though," Danni added.

Good as always was pretty high praise. As with Jules and J-Law, it meant she was ready. That she was the Aly we knew. The one who'd shredded the competition in those title-winning tournament runs. But Danni was on point, too. This frustration had gone on so long that readiness no longer meant anything.

I hemmed. I hawed. I sighed. And then we moved on because there was nothing more to be said.

"What's the final call on McNamara?" I asked.

"We kept her off the card," Danni answered.

Katherine McNamara joined RSI on the same day as Selena Gomez, but, where Sel was an experienced fighter we snatched away from a rival stable, Kat was a true rookie. Her natural talent left the lot of us with our mouths hanging open at her initial workout, but that was a far cry from being prepared for a real opponent. So, I was eager to get her out there and see what she could do, but not before my resident experts agreed it was the right move.

I looked to Steph.

"Next time?"

"She'll be ready," she nodded.

That settled the last of our topics for this event. Time for a peek forward.

"What event do we have after this?" I asked.

Steph sat forward and gave me a sort of apologetic look.

"Open Fight Night," she said.

My demeanor turned instantly dark.

"I hate that event," I seethed.

"We all do," Danni agreed.

Open Fight Night was the kind of pandering, low-brow, made-for-TV idea that could only come from a celebrity fight league like this one. It was largely fan-driven, meaning that if enough people wanted to see a particular fight happen, the league would make it so. Weight-classes, experience level and common sense be damned. If the people wanted Bella Swan vs Hermione Granger, then sure enough... Kristen Stewart and Emma Watson would find themselves together in the cage. It didn't matter that Kristen was two weight-classes heavier. Or hey... let's have a sisters battle. Big sis Britney Spears versus little sis Jamie Lynn. Who cares that Brit had a full weight class and literally years worth of experience over her sibling? It'll be fun.

At the end of the day, I think it's stupid and more than a little dangerous. And it aggravates me to no end.

"What do we have on the card?" I asked.

"All quiet, so far," Danni answered, "but fan voting only just opened, so..."

"And nothing on the peer-to-peer challenges?"

"They don't open that up until the fans have had their say," Steph said.

"I know, but you two always seem to hear the whispers."

Danni shrugged.

"There's been a little noise."

The peer-to-peer challenges are the one theoretical upside to OFN. It's a chance for the fighters to issue their own challenges to each other. And I call the benefit theoretical because many of the matchups aren't any better than the fan-generated stuff. Most challenges are issued by unheralded fighters looking to get a big win and move up the rankings. The result is another batch of dangerous mismatches. But there is also a chance to execute some real strategy. To maybe pick off a higher-ranked fighter you match up well with. To make a move that helps you move up into contention for a belt. It's even possible to call for a title shot... provided you meet the qualifications.

One way is to have more ranking points than the current champion. A rare condition that generally only happens when a top-notch fighter shifts to a new division and brings a mountain of points with her.

The second way to qualify is to establish what the league officially considers to be a rivalry with the sitting champion. It's a status that has only one requirement - the two of you simply need to have faced off at least three previous times. Doesn't matter when the fights happened, or who won or lost. You just need three. Which sounds a lot easier than it is. Because each fight will separate you, essentially. The winner moves up the rankings while the loser moves down. Then things have to break the right way for the two of you to get back together.

Again, it's a situation that usually only happens at the higher end of the rankings. When two or more fighters separate themselves from the field. Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik are a prime example. The two seem to fight only each other as they pass the title back and forth. Meanwhile, they've left the rest of their division in the dust.

For these fighters, calling for a title shot serves no real purpose. For example, Emily is the current champion, but Irina remains the top contender by a mile. So there's no point to her challenging Em now when she'll get her shot at the next regular event.

However, there are always some exceptions.

"Anything I should know about?" I asked.

"We think Veronica may take a shot at Cat," Danni said.

Veronica Varekova was third behind champion Catherine Bell in a tightly contested division. They had already faced one another quite a few times. More than enough to meet the rivalry qualification. And such a move would allow Varekova to jump over second-ranked Traci Bingham... who also had rivalry status with Cat and could block the move by issuing her own challenge. Either way, it seemed certain Mac was going to find herself invited to a brawl.

"Zemanova or Thurman will probably come after Torrie, too," Steph added.

Veronika Zemanova and Uma Thurman were both past champions who had fallen off over the past year or so, and were at risk of losing touch with the division leaders. Though neither had fared too well against reigning champion Torrie Wilson recently, both had rivalry status with her and both would be hungry for an all-or-nothing shot at reclaiming their former glory.

"Anything else?" I asked.

"Nothing that sticks out," Danni said, "but you just know something crazy is going to come up between now and then."

I grunted in disgust and turned to Steph.

"You see any problem for Cat or Torrie?"

She shook her head.

"They've both been tearing it up, recently," she said. "Anything can happen, but I really don't think anyone can touch either of them, right now."

Which was the Steph equivalent of saying both were as bullet-proof as they could be.

"So, we're done?"

Danni started to nod, but Steph had more.

"We do have some opportunities we could take ourselves."

She gave Danni a knowing look that caused the RSI Stable Leader to roll her eyes.

"Oh, that's right," I said to her. "You can take a crack at Cole, if you want."

This past year or so has been something of a roller coaster for Danni, starting with one of the worst losses she'd ever suffered. An eagerly anticipated title bout against nemesis Cheryl Cole had turned into a terrible disaster when she entered the cage distracted and unprepared. Two words that had never applied to her before and haven't since, either. She took full responsibility for the failure - even though it was not entirely her own. She also took the heat that followed without a word of complaint, as experts and pundits who had long celebrated her dominance were suddenly talking like her career was on life-support.

Not fun times, but the ship has since been righted. A league-wide tournament held a few months ago gave her the chance to remind everyone of what she was capable of, and she didn't let the opportunity go to waste. Four rounds of near-perfection left a pile of broken opponents behind her and only the loud-mouthed Cheryl Cole standing in her way. The final was no contest. Danni took Cole apart and silenced every doubter in the process. But the result left one of those strange situations I mentioned before. Though Cole still held the divisional title, Danni actually outranked her based on the points. In fact, she was so far ahead that she was close to leaving Cheryl and the entire field behind. She also had rivalry status with Cole... which meant she qualified on both counts and absolutely had the right to challenge Cheryl for the chance to bring the belt back to RSI at OFN.

There was no urgent need to issue the challenge. Passing it up just meant she'd have to wait for her shot at the next regular event. But I had to admit it was tempting to put Cole in her place just that much sooner. And the sooner the better, as far as the women of RSI were concerned.

Danni dismissed the idea entirely.

"I'll just wait my turn," she said.

"Really?" I asked. "You're gonna make your teammates wait?"

She waved me away.

"We all hate this event, and we've made no secret of that. It seems kind of hypocritical to abuse it, now. I'll get my chance soon enough."

Though I saw her point, I took a different view. Yes, we hated this event. But if we had to repeatedly suffer through this idiocy, then there was nothing hypocritical about making use of it when it made sense. And even Steph agreed this was one of those times.

"I think you should go for it," she said. "That beating you gave her at the tournament must have put some serious doubts in Cole's head. The sooner you kick her ass again, the sooner she'll understand those doubts are facts."

Steph may be curt and grumpy, but there is no one better for telling it like it is.

Meanwhile, the three of us have been working together for a long time now, and it's rare for one to hold out when the other two are on the same page. But this was completely Danni's call, and she didn't seem interested in having her mind changed.

"I'm gonna wait," she said firmly. "If I can beat her now, then I can beat her then."

It was a curious decision, but one that was obviously made. My only option was to spark a full-on war that would likely end in a stalemate, at best. So I just shrugged at Steph and let it go.

"Anything else?" I asked.

We batted around a few more Open Fight Night possibilities, but it was all theoretical until we saw what the voting and challenges would bring us. The meeting broke up shortly thereafter, but I caught up with Steph in the training room later in the day.

"Any idea why Danni wants to wait on Cole?"

"I'm sure she has her reasons."

"Thanks for the info, but I already assumed that much," I said. "I'm asking if you have any idea what the reasons are."

"She hasn't said anything to me."

Something about the way she said it was off. It was very specifically worded. Like she was trying to get off on a technicality.

"Do you have a theory?"

She shook her head.

"You're gonna have to ask her."

Which, once again, avoided giving me a direct answer.

"Steph?"

"I got nothing for you," she snapped. "She's your girlfriend. Go ask her. I've got work to do."

With that, she walked away and left me to wonder just what the hell was going on THIS time.
