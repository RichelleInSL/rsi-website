Everything about the preamble to Sophia's fight with Cheryl Cole made one thing very clear: Bush was being viewed as the scrappy underdog, at best. That likely would have been the majority opinion regardless, but the way Cole had wiped the mat with her at the last tournament made it impossible to argue otherwise. There was plenty of talk about how much Sophia had improved over the years, and just about everyone agreed she had the ability to upset the champion. But no one was picking her to win. This was a bigger spot than she'd ever been in before. The brighter lights, the longer rounds... it can be a lot for someone who's never experienced it. Bush would likely need some time to adjust, and that was something she was highly unlikely to get.

Cole was known for two things: power and aggression. Her ability to end a fight in brutal and sudden fashion is what got her where she is, and the conventional wisdom was that she was going to obliterate Sophia before she could even get out of the gate. Oddsmakers had Cole as the heavy favorite, with the over/under on the length of the fight set at three minutes. At last check, the majority of bettors were taking Cole and the under.

Even the league itself seemed to be against the challenger. Of the seven title fights on tonight's schedule, this was the one that had received the least attention. The only reason it wasn't lower on the card was because of Cole's involvement, and that's what the PR people focused on. Every comment and release the press got hold of seemed to subtly push the idea that Cheryl was likely to put on a vicious demonstration, and wouldn't the bright-eyed Sophia look just fantastic getting her head beat in?

Obviously, I didn't like that message at all. I was growing kind of fond of Sophia. More importantly, she was Danni's friend. Plus, hey... I really, really hated Cheryl Cole. But I also had to admit I would have done the exact same thing if I were in charge of hyping this one. It really was the only narrative you could sell.

Both fighters entered the arena without a lot of pomp, though both got a huge reaction from the crowd. Bush's fan club was still relatively small but it was also dedicated, and they made themselves heard loud and clear as Sophia made her way to the cage. Fact is, she's the kind of person most people want to root for. Selfless and fiercely protective of her friends, she reminds me a lot of Danni. But Bush is much more vocal. More willing to put herself out front. Where Danni prefers to work behind the scenes, Sophia is more likely to get in someone's face. But she does it with the kind of charisma that makes her easy to like. Matter-of-fact, but not nasty. And now that she was getting some winning results to go with that charm, her audience of supporters was growing rapidly.

Cole, on the other hand, was a thoroughly divisive personality. You either loved her or you hated her. There was no in-between. The latter group - of which I am a card-carrying member - would call her selfish, loud-mouthed and arrogant. In short, her totally dismissive attitude toward her opponents pisses me off to no end. But where I saw contempt, her supporters would say they saw confidence. A confidence that was backed up by a very impressive record. But I think the key to her popularity is simply that this wasn't a contest to determine who was the better person. It was MMA, and the fans came here with a hunger for violence above all else. That was an area where a brutal knockout artist like Cole regularly left them satisfied, and they were letting her know they were ready for the carnage to begin.

After the walkouts came the introductions, during which neither fighter showed much. Both seemed preoccupied with their respective teams. I watched Sophia for a deer-in-the-headlights look, but nothing really stood out. She looked appropriately tense, but no more. Meanwhile, Danni - who had hemmed and hawed extensively before finally deciding to watch the fight with me from RSI's reserved box - was literally radiating stress.

"She looks good," I said of Sophia.

"Uh-huh."

I gave her a sideways look, but she didn't even notice.

It was weird to see her like this. After all, pressure and expectations were not exactly new to her. Every event she's a part of has her name at the top of the card. Yet only once - just before her big comeback fight with Cole - did I see even the the tiniest hint of prefight jitters from her. Jitters she then quickly wiped away on her way to a resounding knockout victory. So it's safe to say this is a woman with a considerable amount of ice water in her veins.

On the other hand, her behavior now made perfect sense. She and I are alike in many mays, and both of us are much happier when we've got hold of the reins. When she fights, Danni's in control. Win or lose, she knows it's all in her hands. But tonight there was nothing she could do but sit and watch. That's not the kind of situation either of us are comfortable with.

Referee Nell McAndrew called the fighters to the center for final instructions. Cole tried to get in Sophia's head with her trademark withering glare, but Bush continued to play things cool. Overall, I liked what I saw from her, but the real proof would come after the fists started flying.

McAndrew sent the fighters back to their respective corners and quickly signaled for the fight to begin. As always, Cole came out fast. Punches and kicks fired off in a variety of combinations as she applied a steady pressure to the challenger. But Sophia responded nicely. She quickly found her range with the jab and put it to good use in slowing the attack. More importantly, her footwork was excellent. Retreating and turning. Letting Cheryl surge forward while keeping herself squared up to the action. The real danger that comes from Cole's high-volume style is that it will make you lose your shape. One bad step or slow reaction, and suddenly your assailant is coming at you from angles you can't defend. That's the challenge. To weather the barrage while keeping the fight in front of you. That's what had won Torrie the fight earlier. It also just happens to be the way Danni wins almost all of her fights.

The round closed without much in the way of damage done. Cole's persistence had paid off a few times, but Sophia was always quick to gather herself before things got out of control. And she managed to do some scoring of her own off the counter along the way. In the end, I think the casual fan had Cole way ahead because of how active she was. Though they were more discerning, the judges probably gave Cole the edge, too. But I wasn't thinking about the scoring, yet. Not with four rounds to go. Instead, I found myself thinking Bush had just scored a major victory. Her lack of title fight experience hadn't cost her a thing. Her performance so far had been calm and calculating. Conventional wisdom could suck it.

"What do you think?" I asked Danni.

She shrugged.

"Cole held nothing back," I continued, "but Sophia seemed to handle it OK."

"She's not here to prove she's worthy," she said stiffly. "She's here to win."

Apparently, this version of Danni was a little snippy.

I decided it was best to shut up. The two of us can easily get into an argument under normal circumstances, so no good could come of trying to deal with her like this.

The second round started much the same. Cole would turn on the pressure, and some of her strikes found pay dirt. But Sophia would handle most of it, then look for a hole she could exploit and do some damage of her own. Cole's style made it exciting. Like something big might happen at any moment. But time kept ticking by and it never did. Neither of them was going to win the fight this way. Something had to give, but I was a little surprised when it was Sophia that started to break the pattern. Her defense seemed to get stronger. Her counters got crisper and found their mark more often. She was learning. Identifying Cheryl's tendencies and using that knowledge to her advantage. Just like her buddy Danni. It was pretty impressive, especially under the circumstances. Cole's constant pressure didn't leave a lot of opportunity for analysis, but Bush was showing she was pretty good at thinking on her feet.

Unfortunately, it didn't last.

When you're a vicious predator who's been devouring opponents for as long as Cheryl has, you develop a lot of different ways to bring the pain. You also learn how to properly play with your food. Sophia was finding more success, but she was also getting more comfortable. Maybe a little less alert. Thinking ahead to the counter and not respecting her opponent quite the way she should. Cole knew this was happening. She was probably even letting it happen, a little bit. Sophia was landing more, but she wasn't doing much damage so far. So, Cheryl let her have her fun. Lulling her into a false sense of security while she waited for the right time to snap the trap.

That time came with just over a minute and a half to go. By then, the round was Sophia's to lose. Her defense was thwarting just about everything Cole threw, and she was scoring regularly off the counter. Cole's next attack seemed to play into that theme. She seemed tentative. Still throwing her trademark bombs, but the assault was shorter and she was quicker to back out. Like she was afraid of getting hit with the counter. It seemed to work, too. By hurrying out of range, she left Sophia with nothing to hit. But her reluctance to commit meant she hadn't done any real damage either. The fighters reset and Cole did it again, with the same result. Her attack failed, but she also made it impossible for Sophia to counter.

I saw Danni out of the corner of my eye. Slowly shaking her head as she mumbled something I couldn't hear.

Again, Cole surged and withdrew. No damage done. No chance for the counter.

Danni was still shaking her head.

Cole attacked and retreated again. This time, Bush followed. Taking a huge stride to close the distance. Lunging after her opponent with a big right hand.

Danni let out a big sigh as her head fell and her shoulders sagged.

MMA is - by definition - a mix of all kinds of different fighting styles. But regardless of the flavor of destruction you prefer, there are certain common fundamentals you have to get down if you ever want to win. And rule number one is that you have to stay small. Balanced and compact. Making yourself harder to hit by staying behind your defense and staying nimble.

Bush had just taken that rule and smashed it into little bits. Yes, the punch had a lot behind it. Had it connected, Cole probably would have been counting the little birdies circling her head. But it didn't land. Her opponent was almost out of range and retreating. Sophia had to stretch to her maximum length to even have a chance. Cheryl slid a half step to her left. The punch missed by a mile and Bush was totally fucked.

Balanced and compact? She looked like a life-sized Stretch Armstrong doll.

Nimble? Her feet were so far apart, she was practically glued to the canvas.

Cole went straight to work as Bush tried to recover her stance. Sophia drew her hands back but not before Cheryl stabbed a left into her exposed flank. The pain drew Bush's already shoddy defense lower, creating plenty of room for Cole's right to find it's way to Sophia's chin. She fought through it and tried to square up, but Cole still had the angle. A monstrous left looped around and cracked into the side of Bush's head. Sophia wobbled and quickly scrambled backward to get away. Cole pursued, landing another right. Bush continued to back away as she brought her hands up. Cole kept up the chase, turning her attention low and landing with both hands to Sophia's midsection. Bush kept retreating, desperate to get out of harm's way. Cole kept chasing. Intent on keeping her wounded prey in range.

Danni leaned forward in a very unnatural way. Sliding forward in her seat like she was pushing herself into some obstacle I couldn't see.

Bush kept backing up, but she was running out of room. God help her if Cole got her on the fence. But then, just as disaster seemed certain, she surprised me by sliding left and circling away. She still had her wits, and she obviously knew where she was in the cage. Both positive signs, but the danger was still enormously high. The challenger was living on borrowed time and there was zero chance her luck would hold out for long. This had suddenly become Cole's kind of fight. She had her opponent hurt and on the run. It could all end at any second. All it took was one good punch.

Danni pushed forward in her seat again. Leaning so far out that her head was almost pressed against the glass that fronted the box.

Bush was starting to get it together. She got her guard up and managed to gain a step or two of separation, putting her right on the edge of Cole's range. Her footwork was good, and she kept herself square. Cheryl didn't care. She just kept coming behind a never-ending barrage of punches. Right, left, right, left. Leaving Sophia no room to operate or even catch her breath. Every time she peeked around her guard, another fist was headed for her face.

There were only two ways this was going to end. Either Bush was going to find a way to slow Cole down, or she was going to end up taking a sudden and involuntary nap.

Danni gave another urgent forward lean, this time raising her fist in front of her. Balling it up and squeezing hard enough to make her arm shake.

Sophia was still staying ahead of the assault, but the fence was coming again. Cole quickened her pace. Bush would have to make an adjustment, and Cheryl wanted to rush her into making the wrong one. Sophia slid left and got clear. Cole turned to stay with her. Bush stopped retreating and surged forward, tying Cheryl up and pushing her to the fence.

Danni blew out a sigh and went slack. Releasing the tension that had strained every muscle in her body.

This is what she'd wanted. The reason for all that squirming - silently urging Bush to step forward and engage. Because running away from Cheryl Cole doesn't work. She's not the type to reset and try again. If she's got you off-balance, she's coming for you. A relentless tornado of brutal hands and feet. And she's most dangerous at distance, where she can really let the fists fly. Which is why you have to ignore your instincts and go forward instead of back. Into the storm instead of away. You may have to take a few hits for your trouble, but the payoff is worth it. Because if you can get in there and get your hands on her, then you can make it all stop.

I suddenly felt awful for Danni. She wasn't simply on the outside looking in. She was sitting here with all the answers. No one could defang Cheryl the way she could. She had practically turned it into an art form. But what good was that knowledge now? Whatever help she'd given Sophia in training was either going to stick or it wasn't. Bush was on her own in there. All Danni could do was writhe in her seat and will her friend to make the right moves. Not the most effective method of communication... though it did seem like Sophia had finally received her sympathetic vibrations.

With twenty seconds to go, Bush decided to try and ride out the round. I wasn't sure how I felt about that. It was certainly better than getting flat out whacked in the mouth, but it wasn't going to be easy either. Cole is pretty good inside. Not an uncontrollable beast, but strong enough to push her way around some, with the dirty boxing skills to dig in some good shots despite the close proximity. But Sophia proved up to the task, making up for whatever power she lacked with solid fundamentals. Constantly playing the angles and maintaining her balance at all times. And it seemed she liked her clinches with a Muay Thai flair. Jamming sharp knees into Cole's sides as they continued their vicious dance.

The bell sounded to end a round that was definitely going to land on Cole's side of the ledger. The same round Sophia had been winning only ninety seconds ago. But if any of that bothered Bush, she didn't show it. She was still here, and the fight was still on. She wiped her face with the back of her hand on the way to her corner. Gave a cursory check for blood. Found none and moved on. She had work to do.

Unfortunately, I was starting to think it was more work than she could handle.
