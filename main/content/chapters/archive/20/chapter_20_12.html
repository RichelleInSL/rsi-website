The prefight theatrics for the battle between Danni and Sophia were mostly notable because they were distinctly un-theatrical. The crowd was definitely into it, and the roar was deafening during the walkouts and introductions. But there were no histrionics in either corner or cold stares exchanged while referee Autumn Reeser was reviewing the rules. It just wasn't that kind of fight. Instead of animosity, there was an almost palpable excitement between the two. There was smiling and nodding and some words exchanged between friends. The customary glove tap felt more like something between a handshake and a hug. It all gave off a feeling of joy that was both refreshing and utterly ridiculous. There was a title on the line. Careers could be made or lost in a fight of this caliber. Not to mention that they were going to be smashing each other in the face in a few minutes, which isn't really that friendly of an activity. But none of that seemed to matter. They had wanted this moment, and they had worked to make it happen. From Danni's side of things, I know she had done all she could to be at her best for this fight, and I had no doubt Sophia had done the same. Now they were here, and you had to give them credit. They certainly seemed to be enjoying themselves.

Then the bell rang, and it all changed in an instant.

The two closed on each other quickly, and Danni opened up the action with a quick one-two. Not until it happened did I realize I'd never seen it happen before. In all the times I'd watched her fight, the ever patient and defensive Danni had never swung first. And it wasn't any kind of feeling out thing, either. The initial strikes were followed immediately by a feint with the jab and a right hand designed to take Sophia's head off.

And it almost worked.

Bush spotted the feint easily, and she seemed to anticipate the right. What she didn't expect was the speed and ferocity. By the time she realized what was coming, she was just about out of options as far as dealing with it. No way to guard and too late to retreat or counter. Down was the only place to go as she ducked under the punch in a panic. The quick dodge caused her to lose her footing a bit, but that turned out to be a blessing. Scrabbling to recover her balance carried her away from the left hand Danni had locked and loaded.

Bush righted the ship as Danni turned to square up, and the two exchanged looks. Sophia arched her eyebrows, as if to ask, What was up with that, girlfriend?

Danni responded with a tiny little smile and a barely perceptible nod. A warning to expect the unexpected.

It quickly became the theme of the round, too. Danni staying on the front foot. Sophia playing defense, and doing pretty well with it. Despite a lot of movement and pressure from Danni, Bush's footwork mostly kept her out of trouble. But she was slow to adapt when Danni changed her rhythm, and she didn't always make the best choices when things got a little out of hand. Taking the hits as she jabbed her way out, when a little clinching and grappling probably would have gotten her back on track with less damage.

Overall, a highly-anticipated fight that had started with some promising fireworks ended up with a snoozer of an opening round. But it was a snoozer I knew the judges would score Danni's way, and that was alright by me.

* * *

The round break showed me little other than the status quo. Steph likely telling Danni she stole that one, which gave them a little breathing room. On the other side, Briana would be saying there was no reason to panic, but they'd let the opener slip away from them. Making suggestions on how Sophia could shake things up and make sure the next one went in their favor.

I spent most of the time studying our group. Looking for a clue. Because I had to admit I was mystified. Why was Danni coming out of her usual strategy like this? Were they expecting to catch Bush off-guard? That made sense early, but not for the whole five minutes.

The more I wondered, the more I realized there was only one logical goal. A concerted effort to make sure round one went in Danni's proverbial pocket. Which told me they thought this was going to be close. Close enough that every point would be valuable.

On paper, it made sense. In reality, it made none. Danni's ranking as the pound-for-pound best welterweight in the league was not a distinction she'd gained by accident. She'd earned it by stepping in the cage against whoever they threw at her and doing her thing. Because her thing is more than even her greatest opponents can handle. And now we were deviating from the tried-and-true approach in a quest for precious points against Sophia Bush?

I shook my head, certain I was missing something.

* * *

The seconds cleared out, and Reeser signaled the start of round two. The fighters paused for another quick glove tap, then the action picked up right where it left off. Danni applying a steady pressure and finding little moments of success. Sophia defending and countering, but still taking too many hits. Waiting in vain for the pace and style of the fight to match her expectations. But after another full minute or so, she finally accepted reality and tried to force a change.

She stepped things up in an attempt to meet fire with fire. The first results weren't great because her approach was too tentative. Danni started to score more often and more easily. But then Sophia surprised me. Where most fighters might have reverted back to the defensive, she redoubled the offense instead. The effect was immediate. Punches started to connect, and her confidence grew. All of the sudden, the battle was tilting her way.

A sliver of panic jammed itself into my thoughts as Sophia's shots started to land. Probably because what I was seeing looked a lot like the moment I'd been fearing since this fight had been announced. The one where the clearly inferior Bush inexplicably took control against one of the greatest fighters I'd ever known.

Then the moment disappeared.

Sophia's next volley was the one-two, thrown hot and heavy in an effort to be the first to land. Trouble was, she was trying to win a race Danni suddenly wasn't running anymore. Instead she guarded the jab aside, slipped under the cross and put two punches into Bush's gut.

Sophia backpedaled, but Danni put a long, crisp left on her chin before she could get out of range.

Bush circled for a moment as she shook it off, then surged back in. Again, she went for broke. Leading with the cross, followed by the left hook. Conventional wisdom says another cross was planned, but Danni didn't let it get that far. She faded away from the cross, slipped under and outside the hook, then snapped back like a rubber band and hammered a right hand into Bush's face. I'm not sure Sophia even had a chance to feel the pain before Danni hooked a left into her rib cage. The right hand was loaded up to deliver more, but Bush somehow had the presence of mind to slide out of danger.

It happened again on the next go-round. Another exchange initiated by Bush ended in a big win for Danni.

Suddenly, I understood what I'd been missing. The real reason Danni had been fighting a style that was so out character.

Sophia had a weapon at her disposal. Important information than none of Danni's other opponents ever had access to. The kind of intricate, detailed knowledge you can't get from watching film. All along, that knowledge had been my greatest concern. That Sophia's intimate familiarity with Danni would give her too much of an edge. But all that worrying never really got me thinking about what could be done about it. How do you tackle a situation where your opponent knows exactly how you like to play your game, and is experienced in dealing with every little move you use to make it happen? More importantly, how do you ever get her to play the game in the first place?

The answer the RSI team came up with was apparently to let her think it was her idea.

Despite all early appearances, what Danni really wanted was to settle into into her counter game. So why come out on full attack? To show Sophia that she could. Yes, it was slow and boring as hell. Definitely not Danni at her best. But she showed she had the fundamental skills to pull it off, and there was no debating that it was winning her the fight.

That left the ball in Sophia's court. She could stay the course and let Danni nick her to death, or she could force a change. But how?

The solution to almost every problem you face in the cage is to go back to basics. Use your essential tools to take back the initiative, and stop letting your opponent lead. Use that jab. Beat her to the punch. Regain control.

It was solid, simple strategy at a time when simplicity was needed, but it overlooked an obvious flaw. Sophia was now trying to win at a game Danni never really wanted to play. By going on the attack, she was playing directly into Danni's very capable defensive hands. Essentially turning the fight into exactly the kind of fight she'd wanted in the first place.

Bush came again. Jab, jab, hook. All with the left hand. Following another tried and true approach by showing an uncommon look. Danni was unfazed - dismissing the entire assault with small, precise head movements, then having her way on the counter. Another right-left to the body, then an uppercut that missed by millimeters as Bush slid out the way. This time Danni gave chase. Sophia flashed the jab to back her off, but it did no good. A quick slip of the head and the punch sailed by, leaving the door wide open for Danni to get in close. A straight left/right hook combo to the body brought more pain. And Bush's troubles were only just beginning.

The fence came quickly, taking her by surprise. She tried to clinch, but Danni fought her off and scored with a right hand to the chin. Sophia tightened her defense to protect her head, so Danni landed two more shots to the body.

Another desperate clinch attempt succeeded in slowing the assault, but Danni knows how to work from that position, too. Bush dodged a short right hand, only to get clobbered by the left that followed. When Sophia tried to tie up her hands, Danni put a knee into her belly. More tangling and hand grabbing followed, but there was no denying Danni. When she couldn't find room to throw punches, she simply started to batter Sophia with knees and vicious elbows. A loose pattern emerged. Danni digging in wherever she could find space to work. Sophia doing her damnedest to tangle things up and take that space away, but always seeming to be a step behind. Danni seemed to be landing almost at will, and the noise in the arena picked up as the crowd realized the trouble Bush was in. Suddenly, a fight that had been plodding along only a few minutes earlier looked like it might end at any second.

I couldn't help but feel a little bad for Sophia. After all, I had been in her position on countless evenings in the RSI training room. Being trapped on the fence while Danni beats the crap out of you is a truly terrible experience. She knows all the little tricks you'd use to get free, and she also knows exactly how to thwart them.

The only way to make it stop is to...

Sophia suddenly went ballistic. It started with a quick shove to buy space as she came out of the clinch. Then the fists started flying. Wild swings with both hands that Danni was easily able to avoid, but only by backing out beyond Bush's reach.

Yeah, that... that's what you have to do. Just go insane. Bring everything and the kitchen sink, too. You may not land a damn thing, but you have to give her no choice but to retreat.

The insanity has to be temporary though, and the transformation back has to be immediate. From reckless savage to composed and controlled as soon as you get free. Go wild on Danni for too long, and she will eat you alive.

Sure enough, Sophia slid off the fence, circled out of danger and brought herself right back under control. Stance regained. Guard reset. Ready for whatever came next. As though the mauling she'd just received never happened.

The way she seemed to be reading my mind bothered me. These were my secrets to dealing with Danni, not hers. Which was a silly thought. Sophia spent quite a few hours each week in the cage with Danni. She would have learned the same hard lessons I had along the way.

Still ticked me off, though.

The crowd was still buzzing as the two fighters circled. The action they'd been waiting for had finally arrived, and they were hungry for more. Which made the clang of the bell a very unpopular sound when it came.

* * *

As I got my first clean look at Sophia, it was apparent her team had some work to do. A thin rivulet of blood was trickling from a cut under her right eye, and there was some visible swelling to go with it. Not a great situation to be in less than halfway through the fight, but honestly a small price to pay for letting Danni unload on you against the fence.

She should probably consider herself damn lucky to still be in the fight, at all. Not many could say they'd survived an ordeal like that with Danni.

Beyond the damage Bush had suffered, the rest of the break was mostly uneventful. These were two veteran fighters, surrounded by teams that new the score. For Team Rampage, life was pretty simple. Everything was working so far, so the conversation was just a review of things that had already been discussed in detail. Establishing where things stood, what to do next and what to expect as far a response from their opponent.

On the Dragon's Den side, things were more serious. Plan A had failed and plan B had gone awry. All was far from lost, but there was a lot of work to be done. Wounds had to be tended to, and strategy had to be changed. No energy was wasted on worrying about what had already happened. It was all about giving Sophia a path to getting things back on track. It would be a simple plan, relying heavily on her greatest skills. Because the solitary minute between rounds was not the time to try and reinvent the wheel.

* * *

Reeser ordered the seconds out. Again the fighters touched gloves. A gesture of respect most fans would pay little mind to, and ordinarily I'd be right there with them. Once at the start of the fight is fine, but doing it over and over usually rings hollow. Some do it every time as part of their routine, but then is it really genuine? The only way to truly respect your opponent was to bring your A game. Give her everything you've got, and come in expecting her to do the same. Treat her like the worthy adversary she is.

Between these two it was different. Right now, they were both friends and foes. This was their way of letting the world see both sides, and I loved that they kept doing it.

The tone of round three was different from the start because it had to be. Danni's gameplan had gotten her the lead she'd been hoping for, but it had relied on a kind of wily deceit that wasn't likely to work again now that Bush knew the score. Sophia, on the other hand, was still searching for answers, which made a change in strategy a must.

In essence, it put things back to the way the fight had started, with both prancing and pawing early because neither was quite sure what the other would do. But there was an urgency to it now, especially for Sophia. She had no more time to waste and little room for error. If she didn't establish herself in this round, she would have little hope of staying in the fight.

She took her first shot at it barely thirty seconds into the round, with a move that should have been obvious in hindsight. She couldn't just try harder or do better. She needed an approach that would change the whole dynamic of the fight. So, if trading strikes with Danni wasn't working, the logical alternative was to take things to the canvas.

A looping right hand that had no hope of landing served as the distraction as Bush surged in behind it. Danni saw the takedown coming, but Sophia timed it just right. Getting there before Danni had a chance to set her base, and following through with almost reckless abandon. Putting a shoulder into Danni's midsection, she wrapped up the legs and slammed her opponent to the ground like a linebacker hitting the quarterback. Realizing she was going for a ride no matter what, Danni put her energy into getting her defenses in place. Wrapping her legs around Bush in mid-flight and readying herself for whatever came next. Making sure Sophia's impressive takedown didn't also result in easy access to a dominant position or submission chance.

After that, the real fun got started.

In case you were wondering, ground fighting makes me crazy. Don't get me wrong. It's extremely exciting to be a part of. An intricate, violent chess match fought at the closest of quarters. But watching is excruciating in a variety of ways. The tension is always insane because everyone in the arena knows it could all be over at any second. That an impossible tangle of bodies and limbs could suddenly roll out into a vicious submission or someone being strangled unconsciousness. Would Danni gain the upper hand and bring the fight to an abrupt conclusion? Or would Sophia suddenly secure the win and ruin the RSI leader's hopes... not to mention mine?

But then there was the third option. That this would drag on forever and amount to a whole lot of nothing. That I'd spend minutes hovering on the edge of my seat with no satisfaction.

That's the most common result by far, and it was the one I got in this instance, as well. A long, intense battle on the canvas resulted in little of consequence other than the loss of over half the round. It was no one's fault. Both fighters did all they could do, and they did it incredibly well. Every hard-fought advance was met with a brilliant counter move. Every time one fighter got close to making something happen, the other would slam the door shut. To a true student of the game, I'm sure it was a strategic delight. But I was too heavily invested to take the academic approach, and I was too busy squirming in my seat to enjoy any of it. My emotions seesawed back and forth from one extreme to the other. Holding my breath one second because I thought Danni was about to put it away, then holding it again the next when it looked like Sophia might steal the victory. That didn't leave a lot of time in-between for actual breathing, and I was ready for a Valium and a long winter's nap by the time the action finally ground to a halt and Reeser was forced to intervene.

But my condition was nothing compared to the fighters in the cage. Because despite all the hand-wringing uncertainty, there is one aspect of ground fighting that is absolute: it will leave you thoroughly exhausted. I often think that's the real secret. It's not just about defending yourself on the canvas. It's about being able to conserve your energy while draining it out of your opponent. Weakening her now to make your life easier later.

I couldn't spot the winner in this instance, though. As they regained their feet and assumed their stances, both looked equally haggard yet eager for more.

There was just over a minute left in the round when Reeser signaled for the action to resume... and boy, did it ever. Both fighters pushed forward immediately, with Sophia leading things off with the one-two. Danni guarded the jab and slipped the cross, then responded with another left to the body and a tight little right hook aimed at Bush's jaw line. Sophia took the body shot, neatly slipped under the hook, then responded with a pair of body shots of her own.

The two separated briefly then came together again near the center of the cage. Sophia led off the same with the one-two. This time Danni backed off slightly. Because if Bush was so eager, then the RSI fighter was happy to let prey her come to her. Bush kept coming, launching a pair of strikes to Danni's midsection. Harris lowered her guard and caught both on her elbows. Then it was feeding time, with Bush's head right there, unguarded, and Danni hungry to sink her fists into something. She led the assault with a quick right hand, followed by a big left hook. Bush somehow got her guard up in time to knock the right aside, then slipped under the hook and put two more punches into Danni's midsection. She followed that with a powerful uppercut that Danni managed to lean away from as she backed out of the fray.

The way things were unfolding seemed very familiar to me because god-dammit... Bush was doing it again. Reaching into my bag of tricks and using my techniques to try and deal with the league's top-ranked welterweight. She was baiting Danni the way I had back in the RSI practice cage. Leaving her head in harm's way a second too long in the hopes that she could draw out the counter she wanted and was ready for.

I had to give her credit. It takes a lot of faith - and just a touch of crazy - to leave yourself open in front of Danni like that. But Bush was making it work... and that was pissing me off.

The women faced off twice more before the round ended, and it was the same story both times. Both got their shots in, but Bush was always able to make Danni miss late, then finish with a flurry of her own.

As the fighters retreated to their corners, I was forced to confront a very unwelcome fact: Sophia had done exactly what she needed to do. The successful takedown coupled with the way she got just a bit more on the final exchanges had been enough to win her the round.

The seemingly overmatched Bush was creeping her way back into the fight. Now where had I seen this movie before?
