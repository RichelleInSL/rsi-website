RSI's adventures at Open Fight Night went pretty much by the books. Our Anna came out on top of the Barden Bella battle, as Ms. Kendrick made short work of the far less experienced Anna Camp. Jen Lawrence's fight with Willow Shields was also one-sided... so much so that it got ugly. To the point that Jen all but demanded referee Fearne Cotton stop the fight late in the second before the bloodied and overmatched Willow suffered lasting damage. But Cotton, who has a reputation for digging in her heels when her judgment is questioned, paid her no heed. With Shields no longer showing any desire to engage, Jen coasted into the break - where Steph made it clear the best thing she could do was keep her mouth shut and not antagonize the stubborn ref any further. Sure enough, after only a couple more relatively harmless exchanges, Cotton stepped in and ended Willow's torture.

Nothing like a ref letting her ego get in the way of doing the job.

Meanwhile, the Kacey Musgraves-Ashley Tisdale fight went pretty much exactly the way Steph said it would. Kacey came out hard early, but Ash handled it in her typically unorthodox manner. Sometimes she did it with defense. Sometimes she used movement. Mostly, she subscribed to the I-can-take-more-pain-than-you-can approach. Kacey got her licks in, but Tisdale never let her get away unscathed. Boom-boom. Every strike landed was met with a return salvo. Without even realizing it, Musgraves was letting Ash turn the fight into a flat out battle of wills. Just the kind of thing she excels at. And sure enough, the tide started to turn as the second round drew to a close. While the blistering pace of the battle was obviously taking a toll on the challenger, Tisdale seemed to have plenty to give. How Ash does this is never apparent. She just seems to have an unfathomable ability to push the pain away. To kick ass now, and hurt later.

The final frame saw Tisdale take over. Not because she did anything different, but because Kacey simply didn't have it anymore. If Musgraves tried to stand and trade, Ash would beat her to the punch. If she tried to clinch, Tiz would easily overpower her and grind her down a little more. Kacey quickly became desperate to get something to go her way. She no longer had the energy to do the things she wanted, so she simplified and went with a straight up trip. Nothing slick or controlled about it. She just stuck her leg out and sort of shoved Ashley over it. I give her credit. It was better than doing nothing... but not by much. Tiz instinctively reached out and grabbed onto her opponent, dragging her down as well. They hit the mat and Ash rolled right up and into mount position. Just like that. One second they were falling, the next Tiz was firmly planted and beating the crap out of her. First it was the punches. Right, left then right again before Kacey managed to get hold of Tisdale's wrist. Ash kept right on going - folding her arm and pounding home a couple of elbows. Musgraves let go and tried to roll into a better position. But Ash quickly snuffed out any remaining hope her opponent might have had. As Kacey rolled onto her left side, Ashley used her left hand to grab Musgrave's right and pull it back under her head. The result was that Kacey's arm ended up wrapped around her own throat. Worse still, her left shoulder was pinned down underneath her, making that arm useless, as well. Musgraves was totally defenseless while Tisdale's right hand was completely free.

And that's why they call it the gift wrap position.

Tiz slammed her fist into Kacey's unprotected face twice before the ref got the message and put a stop to the beating. The queen of the scrappers had left another worn out victim in her wake.

The news was not as good for Amanda Seyfried, who suffered an extremely tough loss to Lilly Allen. Ranking-wise, it was an embarrassment. Allen was six positions behind Amanda, and that dictates an easy win. Talent-wise, it wasn't much better. Seyfried was the more skilled of the two by almost every measure. But the most annoying part? It went down just the way Danni hinted it would. Defense, ring awareness, movement, striking accuracy... Amanda was clearly better in all of those categories. But she kept letting Allen tangle her up, and it was a completely different fight every time the grappling got started. Didn't matter if it was in the clinch or on the canvas. Lilly got her licks in every time, and Seyfried had a helluva time getting the situation under control. Even when she did, it was only a matter of time before Allen got her hooks in again.

In the end, the decision went Allen's way. Was it the right call? Technically-speaking, it's hard to say. Though the grappling was a problem, Amanda did a lot of good things, too. But judges aren't robots. They are often affected by outside influences, and a ranking difference like this one is not something they can easily overlook. When the tenth-ranked fighter takes the number four contender to a razor-close decision, she's going to get a little extra credit. Fair or not, that's the way it goes.

Meanwhile, I anticipate a lot of intense grappling drills in Amanda's immediate future. And seeing as how she spends most of her training time in the cage with Danni, I expect it's going to hurt quite a bit.

Similar to what happened with Tiz, another war of attrition broke out in Torrie Wilson's title defense against Uma Thurman. More accurately, defense was the key, and attrition was the result. Thurman came out flying and she put on quite a show, with lots of movement and punches coming in bunches. That's what they teach you. Don't make it easy on your opponent. Throw combos. Change angles. Make her work for it. What they don't tell you is what to do when none of that is working. When you're bringing everything you've got and it isn't getting you anywhere. That's what Thurman needed to know. For two full rounds, she went full throttle, while Torrie managed to repel her with little more than the basics. Wilson kept her defense tight, used her footwork to keep the fight in front of her and put her jab to work to keep Uma at bay.

The eye test put Thurman way ahead by the time those rounds were over. She seemed to be driving the action every step of the way, and she owned the strikes thrown stat by a mile. But looks can be deceiving and strikes landed told a different story. The law of averages still had the challenger ahead, but Torrie had been far more accurate in making her fewer strikes count. As the two headed to their corners, I'd say they were about even as far as damage done, but Thurman had expended a lot more energy getting there. That made it easy for Torrie to slide into the driver's seat in the third.

Uma was noticeably slower as the round got started, and every second that ticked by seemed to shift the balance further to the champion. Thurman became less willing to attack, a little sloppier with her defense and easier for Torrie to corral. She slowed further still as Torrie's strikes found their mark more frequently. By the end of the round, the writing was on the wall.

Thurman opened the fourth with a crazy flurry. Pouring out everything she had left in a frenzy that went nowhere. After that, it was all Wilson, all the time. Steadily pressing the action and waiting to exploit the mistakes she knew would come. The finish came with just under thirty seconds left in the round, and it was yet another example of the kind of well-rounded fighter Torrie has become. A flurry of punches brought Thurman's guard up, which opened a window for Torrie to shoot in low for a takedown. She moved like lightning. One second she was punching. The next she had Uma's legs wrapped up. There was nothing Thurman could do. Torrie put a shoulder into her midsection and lifted her up, then slammed her back down and easily moved straight to mount. Instinct told Thurman that hell was about to be unleashed, so she brought her arms up to defend. Torrie swept them aside with her left and pounded her right fist into the center of her opponent's face. Uma tried to recover her hands. Torrie reloaded and dropped the hammer again. Thurman's limbs went rigid and her entire body seem to lock up. A tell-tale sign that the lights had just gone out for her. Torrie raised the right again, but referee Autumn Reeser threw herself in-between to protect the unconscious challenger.

Torrie's only celebration was to stand and put a fist in the air as she slowly walked to other side of the cage and waited for her team to arrive. Mission accomplished. Title thoroughly defended.

That left only Cat Bell, and I have to be honest... I don't really understand what's going on with her anymore. I mean, I see what she's doing, but I don't know how she's doing it. I've heard of being in the zone. I've even been there myself a time or two. But Mac pretty much lives there nowadays. Her fights are not fights. They're clinics. Every move her opponent makes, she has an answer for. Every tiny misstep, Cat is there to punish. It's like she has somehow reduced the entire sport to an algebraic equation. Just plug in the value for X, and the solution pops out instantly.

Traci Bingham came with all kinds of tricks up her sleeve. Striking, grappling, in-fighting... Bingham is good at all of them, and she tried all sorts of sequences and combinations in the quest to find just the right monkey wrench to throw into Cat's works. Over more than seventeen minutes of action, she never came close to finding it. Bell was completely dominant in a way you almost never see at this level. It had nothing to do with strategy or any kind of style mismatch. She was simply invincible. She didn't just beat Bingham. She wore her the fuck out, then threw her away like an old dish rag. When Cat went for the finish two minutes into the fourth, she encountered zero resistance. She grappled Bingham to the fence with ease, then ground her down the mesh and took her back. As the champion sank in the choke, the challenger looked almost too tired to be bothered tapping out. But I guess some inner voice convinced Bingham that being choked unconscious would only make everything worse, so tap she did.

The big winner? Veronica Varekova, who did nothing yet now found herself in position to face Cat for the belt at the next regular event. Hard to view the prospect of getting in the cage with seemingly unbeatable Catherine Bell as a win, though.

Cat's victory brought RSI's involvement in the event to a very successful close. Of course, thanks to Danni's little scheme, it did not conclude our interest in the evening's festivities.
