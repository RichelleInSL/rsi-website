I hustled through the hallways to the RSI locker room, and received the report I expected when I got there. Danni was banged up but mostly OK. Tests would continue for a while to monitor her cognitive state. Physical wounds would need time to heal. But no major problems had been found and the expectation was that all would eventually be well.

Despite knowing this, I opened the door to the trainer's room slowly. A little afraid of what I was going to find. Haunted by memories of terrible moments from the not-so-distant past.

The memory that tormented me more than any other took place in a darkened treatment room halfway around the world. It was mostly made up of sadness and confusion and the crushing weight of disastrous failure on almost every front. But the scene I found here was markedly different. The lights were on, and Danni was sitting up. True, she was quite a mess, but her improvement mirrored Sophia's. Face cleaned, wounds treated, swelling reduced... it seemed to me that she was already well on the road to recovery.

Some of the difference between the terrible memory and the present could also be chalked up to mentality. She had taken a lot of damage in both fights, and she would probably take just as long to heal this time around. But pain and suffering look much different when viewed through the prism of victory.

"Where the hell have you been?" she asked me.

Without a word, I stepped across the room, gingerly took her face in my hands and gave her a long, gentle kiss.

"Well," she said as we separated, "hello to you, too."

"You look terrible," I said.

"I feel terrible."

"But winning makes it easier."

"Sure."

I think she wanted it to sound earnest, but the response fell predictably flat. Because again, this was a victory unlike any other she'd had in her long and successful career.

"So, where the hell have you been?" she asked again. "And have you heard anything about Sophs?"

"She's fine," I said. "I just came from there."

Her eyes widened a little.

"You... what?"

"I figured I'd check in on her since I knew you'd be asking."

She stared at the floor tiles as she thought that one over.

"You must have been a big hit," she said.

"Things started off a little choppy, I admit."

"But you were able to smooth the waters," she smiled.

"Of course. I am a veritable mistress of public relations, after all."

She nodded and studied the floor some more and said nothing because what do you say when someone like me says something like that?

After a while, emotions started to play across her face in a way that let me follow along with her thoughts. The idea of me being in the opposing locker room reminded her of why I was there. That led to mental images of Sophia and the shape she must be in. And how she got to be that way.

These were not the things a fighter usually thinks about after winning a title.

"Not enjoying your win as much as usual?" I asked.

"Not really."

"Would it help if I said, 'I told you so?'"

"Only if you're trying to piss me off."

"Don't tempt me," I warned. "You know that's one of my favorite hobbies."

She cracked a little smile. The type that means little for the present, but lets you know it's all going to work out in the long run.

"You can't tell me you didn't know it would be like this," I said.

She shrugged, then seemed to disappear into her thoughts again for a few seconds.

"I knew it would be different," she admitted. "I expected there to be a price to pay, but this just feels..."

"Hollow."

"Yeah," she said. "Like there's no way to enjoy it. Every good thing for me is a bad thing for her."

"Neither of you seemed to mind it much when you were punching each other's lights out."

Another little smile. Enjoying the memory of what the fight itself felt like. Maybe playing back those little, silent exchanges in round four.

"That was different," she said. "That was part of the thrill."

That earned a smile from me.

"And now that you've come out of it with the spoils of war?" I asked, gesturing toward the title belt on the table beside her.

"I feel a little like a bully."

I raised an eyebrow.

"A bully?"

"How long have I had this belt for?"

"Do you want me to count up the days, or...?"

"Exactly," she said. "It's a long time. And we both know that whenever I lose it, I can always get it back."

"That's mighty condescending of you."

"Well, since it's just the two of us..."

"Of course."

"Anyway... my point is Sophia had to fight tooth and nail for a long time for a chance to get her hands on this thing. And when she finally got it, she made good. She was finally the champion every one of us dreams of being. And now..."

"Now she's right back where she was a few months ago."

"Exactly."

"Except that she's not."

She just looked at me.

"I know it's too soon to appreciate it," I said, "but you got everything you wanted tonight. You and Sophia went toe to toe, and - though I don't think it ever went quite the way anyone thought it would - you both put on quite a show."

She gave a little shrug.

"And whether you realize it or not," I continued, "you helped elevate your friend's stature in a way no one else could."

"By taking her title away?"

I narrowed my eyes, doubtful that she could be this oblivious to the bigger picture. Her expression remained unchanged, so I offered a clue.

"Since it's just the two of us, I'm going to ask you a very blunt question."

"I can hardly wait."

"Do you really think most people expected Sophia to have a prayer tonight?"

"I told you before. She's a lot better than everyone gives her credit for."

"Yeah, and now I know it. I still don't understand how she's doing it, but I can't deny her anymore after what I just saw. Neither can anyone else."

She didn't say anything to that, so I just kept going.

"You don't like to say these things, so I'll do it for you: there is no one better than you for Sophia to measure herself against. You gave her the chance, and she came out much better than I think anyone expected. She may have lost the title, but that felt fluky to begin with. What she did in there tonight... the way she stood up to the great Danneel Harris and even took her to the brink a time or two... that's the kind of thing that will earn her some respect. There will still be doubters and people looking for reasons to knock her down, but she's on the radar now. She's going to get chances she wouldn't have gotten before this. Because she more than held her own against the best there is."

"OK, I know I started it, but let's calm down."

"This is not the time for your humility, my dear. This is the time for you to accept reality and realize what happened in there. People are going to be talking about what she did for a long time."

"Yeah, but how long is long?"

"Oh, c'mon. You know how this works. You can't perform miracles for her. She's going to have to do her part. This is going to carry her to her next fight, where she's going to have a ton of expectations piled on her. She's got to respond to that. If she gets her ass handed to her, then she'll be back where she was. If she backs up what people saw tonight, then she'll be able to build on it. All I'm saying is that tonight put her in a very good place."

"Not as good as it could have been."

"Yeah, well... as good as I'm willing to tolerate."

She smiled and said, "Me, too."

Which brought up an interesting question. Because mixed in with that pride and awe of Danni's abilities was a new emotion I wasn't nearly as comfortable with.

"How close was this one, really?" I asked.

"I had it the whole way."

"Why don't I believe you?"

"Because I'm lying," she said. "Playing possum at the end was pretty much plan Z. I was behind, and I knew I needed the finish. But if that hadn't worked..."

She'd only confirmed what I already suspected, but I was still stunned.

"Wow," was all I managed as a response.

"You ain't kidding," she said.

I left shortly after that. Because, as usual, Danni's teammates were waiting for her. And there were other things to celebrate, too. A title long-sought had finally been won. A new champion had roared out of seemingly nowhere. And dominance had been reinforced almost universally.

I worked my way around the room, and celebrated along. Because despite how often I deny it, these really are my girls, and few things make me happier than enjoying their success. But in the back of my mind, the wheels were turning. Because I had work of my own to do. Training to push through and new skills to hone.

Sophia was out of contention for now, but it wouldn't stay that way forever. In the meantime, others would be coming. Right now, Cheryl Cole was back on the menu, and I was going to do everything I could to make sure Danni was ready to eat her for lunch.
