Despite the way the complexion of the fight had changed, the story in the corners was the same old, same old. Everybody went about their business just the way they had before. The exchanges near the end of the round had reopened the wound beneath Sophia's eye, but the swelling was still minor and the situation was manageable. Her team would get it cleaned up, and there was to immediate concern that it would impact Bush's ability to defend herself.

The final skirmishes had also marked Danni up a bit, but it was nothing serious and Marcy had it all under control.

Danni sat on the stool and did a lot of listening and nodding. Which looked harmonious but also made me wonder what Steph could even be saying. The game had changed completely in the closing minute of round three. Strategy-wise... well, I was pretty sure that kind of stuff was all about to go right out the window. The fight was on now, and all the cards were on the table. Where it went from here was up to the fighters themselves, and there wasn't a lot either Steph or Bri could do that would make much of a difference.

* * *

Round four started with the same routine. Reeser signaled for the bell and the fighters touched gloves.

That was the last second of the round that felt even slightly familiar.

As soon as the two were set, Bush pressed forward behind a quick jab and a right hand to the body. Danni guarded the jab, then slid just out of range of the right. She moved right back in to take advantage of the miss, with a right hand aimed squarely at Bush's unprotected head. Swing and a miss. Bush ducked under again, then put a pair of punches into Danni's torso. She stayed low and tried to slip back out of range, but Danni was waiting and hit her with a right hand as a parting gift.

The two circled for about a quarter turn, then stepped into each other again. Bush launched the right hand. Danni slipped inside it and replied with a right of her own. Bush, in turn, slipped that and came back with another right to the body. That punch landed, but Danni stayed in the fray and flashed the jab. Bush slipped outside of the blow, but as she weaved back in, Danni's right was waiting. Bush realized her mistake a split second before impact and tried to tilt her head out of the way. She avoided the worst of it, but Danni's fist still caught a nice chunk of the side of her head. Bush stepped in to clinch. The two exchanged a few knees. Short punches and sharp elbows went back and forth. Every strike taking a little something out of the opponent, but no killer blows landed. The firefight ended with the two seeming to mutually push each other away. Like they'd both decided they'd had enough at the same time.

It was a respite that did not last long.

The two surged together again, with fists flying all over the place. Danni led off with the jab. Sophia brushed it aside and responded with her own left. Danni slipped outside. Bush fired off the right, trying to catch Harris coming back in. Danni foiled her by going low. Two more punches thudded into Sophia's body, but she refused to give any ground. She reached out to clinch. Danni pushed free immediately, but not before Bush squeezed in a solid knee to the body. With no good way to respond, Danni retreated out of range.

I stole a quick glance at the clock, then did a double take. Somebody was screwing up somewhere because that time had to be wrong. There was no way all of this had happened in just 45 seconds.

The two met again near the center of the cage for another furious exchange. Jabs and crosses flew back and forth. Both changed levels continuously, taking whatever openings the other left. Defense was strong on both sides. An area in which I would never have guessed Bush could keep up with Danni.

It became a question of who would crack first. A question that would be answered only seconds later.

Danni took a right on her shoulder, then turned into a right hand of her own. Bush slipped under it and landed another pair of body shots, then moved to regain her full posture. She did it the way you're supposed to. Popping up quickly as she recovered her guard. But Danni had the right hand already moving. Timing it to arrive just as Bush's head came up. Like she knew in advance that it would be there. Probably because she did. Because the intimate knowledge street goes both ways. Working together with the same fighter day after day lets you develop a sense for everything they do. Attacks, movement, rhythm. I was willing to bet Danni had seen this exact set of strikes and movements from Sophia thousands of times in training. A familiarity that had just paid off big.

The impact was like something out of a movie. Bush's head spun right as the punch drove it sideways, her neck stretching and flexing to the limits of its mobility. Her body followed behind. Wobbling for a step or two before she fell.

I thought she was out. Danni likely thought she was out. The roar of the crowd told me they thought she was out. Reeser was hovering, trying to assess the situation.

And the broadcast team was losing their minds.

She's down! Bush is down!

Danni sprinted forward. Throwing caution to the wind as she raced to finish the job.

She paid for it, too.

Just as Danni reached her opponent, Sophia turned on her back and kicked up. Having committed completely to her attack, Danni took a solid shot as both feet slammed into her chest.

She landed on her back then scrambled immediately to her feet. By the time she was standing, Bush was too. I don't know how she did it. Durability may be her single greatest asset, but this was ridiculous. Two seconds ago, the whole arena thought she was unconscious.

The broadcasters were shouting things I couldn't make out and the crowd had gone insane. A knockout finish would have been great, but instead they were going to be treated to more? Outstanding.

To make it more exciting, the warriors were really showing the scars of battle now. The wound beneath Sophia's right eye had reopened and was bleeding enough to warrant a little concern. A new cut was also visible over her left eye. Minor for now, but it could become a serious problem if it got any worse. On the other side, Danni had some visible swelling under her left eye, and what I was pretty sure was a small cut to go with it.

Oh, gee, I thought. Isn't this fun?

Not for me, no. But I caught a quick glimpse of something down in the cage. As the fighters retook their stances and made ready to battle on, Danni gave a sly little look that seemed to ask, Still here, are you?

Sophia answered with just a tiny smirk that said, You bet your ass, sweetie.

I guess it was good that someone was having a good time.

The clash resumed with no change in intensity. Each exchange was like a tiny war, with both giving and taking a ton of punishment, yet neither giving an inch. It was like they'd made some sort of handshake agreement beforehand that the fight wouldn't go past round four. And so they beat on each other like well trained savages. Back and forth until someone finally fell.

Only no one did. I saw at least three blows that looked like they should have finished the job, but the battle just kept going each time. Twice more, Danni was able to stagger Sophia, yet the Dragon leader somehow kept her feet.

As the insanity raged on, the damage got much worse. Soon it became impossible to tell exactly who was bleeding from where. None of that seemed to give either fighter any pause, though.

Then came the moment that stopped my heart. A furious exchange ended with Sophia landing a right hand that sent Danni reeling. Though she managed to stay upright, she stumbled briefly. A little wobble that turned her slightly and let Sophia get in behind her.

The right arm came over Danni's shoulder and curled around her throat. Bush brought the left up to close the circle and secure the choke... just like she'd done to Cheryl Cole.

With a shrug of her shoulders and a quick drop of her head, Danni slipped out of the loop and shuffled away.

There was another exchange of glances as both acknowledged the close call. Then the lunacy resumed.

And because one heart-stopping moment isn't enough, I was treated to a second only a few skirmishes later.

The exchange started off with the same kind of wild frenzy as all the others. Some strikes found their mark. Others missed by millimeters. And it all happened much too quickly to keep track off. But just as the storm seemed to be subsiding, both seemed to have the same idea. The same sense that there was an opportunity to land the knockout blow. And unfortunately, Sophia's got there first.

Bush's right hand came up and around just a split second after Danni had opened up to launch a right of her own. Sophia's fist sailed straight through the defensive gap and into Danni's face. The impact was brutal, and the result made me physically ill. Danni's knees buckled and her whole body canted off to the right. Her eyes were glassy as she wobbled a few steps with her arms up and forming a feeble-looking guard. I'd been through it enough times to know exactly what she was experiencing. That dazed, half-conscious feeling where your eyes are open and your brain is kind of working, but not really. Your thoughts are fuzzy and your not really sure what's happening or what to do. Absent commands from mission control, instinct and training take over, only the body isn't quite willing to do what's needed to make it all work.

Sophia scrambled forward to finish her off, and ended up missing badly as Danni's legs finally gave out and she literally fell out of the impact zone.

Bush followed her to the canvas and pounded her with another right hand. Then came another. Danni offered no answer and still seemed to have little idea what was happening. Reeser was right there, ready to call a halt to the bludgeoning.

Then something changed. Danni still seemed confused, but she got hold of Bush's arm. Sophia tried the left, but Danni grabbed that too, then somehow seemed to knot the two of them together. Tying things up and making it difficult for Bush to throw punches. A form of control that didn't last long, but bought Danni precious seconds to regain some mental awareness.

Unfortunately, she probably would have preferred to remain oblivious given what came next.

A vicious cycle began. Sophia would gain a good position, then hammer down some strikes. Danni would eat a few, then somehow squirm and twist her way to temporary safety. The two would wrestle a little, then Sophia would get loose, posture up and bring the hammer down again until Danni could get things back under control. Occasionally, Bush would try to worm her way into position for a possible submission. Danni would fight it off, but the effort would only leave her open to more strikes. It went on like that for a full, excruciating minute, and I honestly don't how Danni survived. I don't know how I survived, either. I saw at least five heavy shots land square in her face, and each time I thought it might be the end. After the third or fourth one, I started to think maybe that was what I wanted. For it to just be over.

Honest to god, I thought I was going to be sick. The sound of the bell that ended the round was like a miracle that came two long minutes too late.

As Reeser pulled the fighters apart, my thoughts were a complete wreck. And it wasn't just Danni. In a single round, this fight had transformed into my worst nightmare. The vicious back and forth pounding had opened a host of wounds on both fighters, and all the close-quarter grappling had turned the whole scene into a bloody mess. To the point where I doubt I could have told you who was who if they weren't wearing their respective stable colors.

But my nightmare was a dream realized for the crowd, and under any other circumstances I'd probably be inclined to agree with them. A brutal, bloody war was exactly the kind of action fans came to see. A primal struggle with both fighters needing every ounce of their skill and experience to survive.

Yeah. If it had been any other two fighters down there beating each others brains in, I probably would have been thrilled, too.
