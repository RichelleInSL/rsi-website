I stayed quiet during the break - mostly because I was afraid Danni would literally snap. But I got that same feeling again. Like just surviving this far meant something for Bush. Something beyond moral victories. Two rounds in and she was losing. The last round had been an unqualified failure that had come within scant centimeters of ending in disaster. So why did it feel like she was winning? She had found her way back from that edge and regained control. How many other fighters could say they'd done that? Danni's success against Cole stems from the way she's able to tame the beast. And the one time she let the lion out of the cage... well, it didn't end very well. So, really... how many had faced an unbridled Cheryl Cole and somehow gotten the bit back in her mouth?

Unfortunately, this time my feeling was tempered by a growing sense of dread. I liked the way Sophia was handling herself. She was doing things that most didn't know she could do. But the situation had changed dramatically. Cole hadn't eked the last round out. She had won it in a landslide, which put Bush in a sizable hole. Keeping a handle on things wasn't enough, anymore. Sophia needed to turn it around and do some damage of her own. That was something she still hadn't shown any indication she could do.

Down in the cage, there was no sign anything was amiss in the challenger's corner. The Den's cutman was busy tending to whatever wounds or swelling Bush had suffered. The Den also has a steady second who handles the scut work for all their fighters. Washing mouthpieces, managing the spit bucket... fun stuff like that. But the floor belonged to Sophia's close friend, Briana Evigan. Through circumstances beyond her control, Bri somehow ended up with a job she never wanted. In general terms, she's the Den version of RSI's Steph McMahon, but the truth is the two have very little in common.

Steph is our guru. An experienced pro with a knack for breaking things down in a way that makes it all easy to understand. No matter the issue, Steph has the sage advice needed to set things right.

Bri, on the other hand, is a little short on sagey-ness. She's smart and she knows the game. Her advice is always solid. But she doesn't have the next-level insight Steph provides. No matter. What she lacks in strategy, she makes up for with spirit and charisma. She knows her people, and she knows how to get through to them. If you're mailing it in, she'll light a fire under your ass. Opponent crushing your resolve? Bri will have you believing you can run through walls by the time the break is over.

For RSI, Steph is the rock. For the Den, Bri is the heart. And despite their differences, both are somehow exactly what their fighters need.

Bri was working her magic now, punctuating every other sentence with a little, c'mon, you got this fist pump. Sophia was nodding along. Things weren't going her way so far, but that was all going to change next round. For no other reason than because Bri just said so.

I found myself hoping there was some technique being discussed along with the rah-rah. A shot in the arm can make all the difference, but only if your strategy was solid. And so far... not so great.

Round three started off the same as the prior two. If anything, Cole came out even harder than before. Making Sophia run had paid all kinds of great dividends in the last round. Could she get her to do it again? The answer was yes. As Cole raced out with her fist up high, instinct overcame training again. Sophia backed away.

I swear I thought Danni was going to go straight through the glass.

She held on for a while but Sophia's defense couldn't keep up with the pace of the assault, and Cole soon found the seam she wanted. The right hand came up from below and exploded on Sophia's chin like a bomb. For a moment, it looked like it was over. Bush's head went right without the rest of her. She was going down. Probably unconscious. Then came signs that there was still someone home. Her leg lurched out and stopped the fall. She wobbled to her right, her arms down as she fought to regain her balance. She was collecting herself, but there was no time. Her defenses were nowhere to be found, and Cole was right on top of her.

Just as Cole surged in, Sophia surged back at her. Cheryl was caught short. Like almost everyone else, she thought she was about to deliver the finishing touch to her helpless opponent. Sophia got her hands on her and pushed. Hands gripping Cole like vises, her legs started to churn. Walking forward like a steam roller. Cole was slow to react. Their legs got tangled and down they both went, with Bush on top. It was a takedown so sloppy you couldn't really call it a takedown, but it got the job done. Cole suddenly found herself in the place she least liked to be. On the mat. A fearsome destroyer in almost every other position, Cheryl was a barely passable ground fighter. Good enough to keep herself out of trouble most of the time, but that was about it.

Cole bucked and squirmed. She tried throwing some punches, but there was no room to generate any kind of power. Sophia, meanwhile, just hung on for dear life. Smothering everything Cheryl tried and keeping her tangled up. Buying as much time as she could get to recover from Cole's uppercut.

Cole did her best to help her out, too. If she had just gone still, McAndrew probably would have intervened and gotten the fighters back to their feet. But Cheryl wasn't thinking that way. In fact, I'm not sure she was thinking, at all. Pissed off to find herself here after coming so close to ending it, she thrashed like a caged animal. Twisting and jerking as Sophia worked to counter her. Satisfied with the level of activity, the ref chose to stay out of it.

Cole eventually got herself under control, but I couldn't help but marvel at what Bush had accomplished. Cheryl's whole game is to swarm her opponent until something big connects, and most opponents don't last long once that happens. But it had now happened twice in this fight, and yet Sophia was still here. Not only that, but this mess of a recovery she'd just managed had caused Cole to use up a ton of energy as she seethed on the ground.

In a weird way, I was massively impressed. Bush wasn't winning this fight. At this point, I was starting to wonder if she even could. But I also didn't see any reasonable way she should still be in conscious after all that had happened. And yet, here she was.

If there was an award for best sheer survivor, she'd have my vote, hands down.

As McAndrew stood the fighters up, I could see Evigan yelling from cageside. Though I couldn't hear her, I could take a pretty good guess at what she was saying.

Hey, moron... remember what we talked about during the break? Well, none of what I just saw looked anything like that.

Sophia gave no visible reaction.

McAndrew restarted the action. Cole closed quickly, looking to pick up where she'd left off. Bush locked in her guard and stayed put. Cole led with the jab. Bush shunted it aside and stepped forward.

I swear Danni stopped breathing for a second. Here was Sophia, finally executing what they'd worked on in training. Now it was time for the big the question to be answered: would the plan work? I agreed with the overall thinking. Staying close was better than running. But the danger was still high, and I really didn't know if Bush had the ability to pull this off. Danni was widely recognized as a masterful defensive fighter. An accolade even she would admit sounds horribly boring, but trust me, it's not. Not if you're her opponent, anyway. As the poor sucker who trains with her most often, I am an authority on that. For her, being defensive means a lot more than just being able to protect herself. She has a way of turning almost everything you do back on you, and she's got a full box of razor-sharp tools at her disposal to make it happen. Cagey guard positioning lets her goad you into attacking where she wants you to instead of the other way around. Dizzying head movement makes her extremely hard to hit. Pristine footwork keeps her in the perfect position to strike when the opportunity presents itself. To put it plainly, she's as slippery as an eel. One second, you've got her. Her head's right there, her guard's too low and your right hand is on the way and on target. Then you whiff. Danni jinks her head left, you miss by a centimeter and now she's got you, instead. She can do it all day, too. That's why the no retreat strategy works so well for her. Because she can stand right in front of you - basically setting up camp inside your kill zone - yet somehow come out unscathed. And all the while, she's beating the stuffing out of you with her counter game.

Could Sophia do all of that? Doubtful. Could she do any of it? Probably... and we were about to find out if that would be enough.

Cole followed up with the cross. Bush slipped under it, buried a right in Cheryl's gut and kept moving forward. Cole finished her combo with the left hook, but it was too late. Sophia was too close. Already inside the arc of the punch. She shot her left hand into Cole's nose, then launched a tight little uppercut with the right. She missed the sweet spot, but got a nice chunk of Cheryl's cheek.

Normally, that was enough. A counter like that would make the average opponent step back and rethink her choices. But Cole doesn't rethink. She redoubles. If at first you don't succeed, smash the shit out of something.

The right hand came again, but now Sophia was practically occupying the same space as her opponent. There was no room for Cole to squeeze the punch in. But something wasn't right. There was also no room for Sophia to throw the counter. So what was the plan then? Another messy takedown?

Then something happened. Something I didn't catch. Separation was gained, but it was Cole who seemed to back out first. Bush eased away as well. Guard up and eyes wary as she passed through Cheryl's ideal range once again. Nothing came of it. Cole never attacked.

Danni blew out a sigh of relief. Whatever had happened, it was part of the plan.

Had Cole retreated? I really wasn't sure. And why did she just let her go? There'd been at least an instant where the distance was perfect. Why hadn't the queen of the go-for-broke style taken a shot when it was there for her?

The sequence repeated in similar fashion. Most of the mechanics were simple. Bush would watch carefully. Waiting for Cole to trigger everything else with her opener. Once she spotted the attack, Sophia would deflect it while moving in. Her reaction time was great. She repeatedly closed the distance before Cheryl could follow-up, then found an opening to do some damage. Then it would all just mysteriously evaporate. The two would separate without another punch being thrown.

It happened four more times before I figured it out. Which was pretty good, to be quite honest. After all, each exchange lasted less than three seconds, and there was a whole lot going on each time. But the key to it all was so subtle it was almost invisible.

One constant of fighting in close is that everything is always a mess. Punches tangled in guards. Dodges in tight quarters led to inadvertent collisions. Counters landed awkwardly because there was no room to maneuver. Contact was happening everywhere. So how has Cole to notice the way Sophia was pushing her around? How was she to understand that each time Bush drew close - practically close enough to kiss - she was also quietly determining how the two would separate?

It was Cole's style that made it all possible. Because Sophia was right there. Close enough to grab, every time. A lot of damage can be done in the clinch, if you do it right. But Cole doesn't like to grapple. She likes to hit people. That's where her mind was. Focused on getting some distance so she could put her knuckles on Bush's jaw. Unfortunately for her, Sophia was looking for distance too - but only on her own terms. Which is why each separation started with Bush giving Cole a little shove. Nothing major. Just a little guidance. The specifics seemed to vary with the situation. Each time, Sophia would gauge their positioning and then do her thing. A little push or nudge with the forearm. Something to get Cheryl moving in a particular direction while Sophia went exactly the opposite way. That was all she needed to make a clean getaway.

I nodded approvingly.

"I like it," I said.

I saw a smile play at the corners of Danni's mouth, but she didn't say anything.

I was getting the answers to my questions. Could Sophia do what Danni could do? As expected, she could not. She simply did not possess the skills to live inside the pocket. And there was no shame in that. Very few could... especially under a withering, Cheryl Cole-style assault. But with enough care, she could make brief visits.

I didn't know where the idea had come from, but I had to admit it was brilliant. Sophia was not Danni. She was Sophia. And right now, she was down in the cage, beating Cheryl Cole in her own way.

The round played out without further incident, and without much more in the way of damage done. As the bell sounded, one thing was clear to all. Sophia had taken control. She was taming the beast.

Unfortunately, I was still pretty sure it was too late.

Despite all Bush had done, I had Cole winning all three rounds so far. At minimum, she was up two with two to go. Sophia would have to win them both handily just to get the draw. It was an almost impossible task. No one - not even Danni - could contain Cole all the time. She was going to have her moments. Could Bush put together enough of her own to overcome that? It seemed highly unlikely.

It was a shame, really. So far, she'd impressed me at every turn, and I found myself thinking she could have pulled this off. But now it seemed like her inexperience had bitten her, after all. She had taken too long to get going, and she'd made a couple of big mistakes. Now she needed a huge comeback... and I just didn't think she had it in her.
