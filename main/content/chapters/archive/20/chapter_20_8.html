The waters were quiet for a while. The event had gone well for both RSI and the Den, and just about everyone went home happy. The regular day-to-day resumed, with the exception of those who were active at OFN. That's standard procedure after every event. Gotta run the tests. Cross the t's and dot the i's. Make sure nobody's eggs are scrambled before you start letting people punch them again.

The trouble started once Sophia was cleared to get back to training. Because we've got that whole inter-squad training program going. A program that I'd just recently put a few very strict limits on.

This was our week to host, and the Den crew had been in the building for about thirty-seven seconds before Sophia stormed into my office.

"Do you really hate me this much?"

Danni trailed in behind her, shaking her head. Because their differences were shining through. I was certain both were mad at me. Both for the same reason. No doubt, Danni had plans to try and persuade me to change my mind in a quiet moment. Working behind the scenes, as she prefers. But no such luck. Because here was Sophia, getting right in my face.

"What are you whining about, Bush?"

"I think you already know."

I shrugged my shoulders and played dumb.

"The training," she said. "You know we've been looking forward to this. We were excited about it. And now I find out you've forbidden us from training together?"

I found it interesting that I was getting all the blame, seeing as how I'd discussed the new limitations with the Dragon's Den owner and we had fully agreed before they were put in place. But it was OK. I have no problem being the bad guy.

"I didn't forbid you so much as I applied a little common sense."

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?"

"It means you don't train with your opponent, Sophia."

"That's ridiculous. We've been training together for years, now."

"Yes, I know. I've heard the story. You know each other inside and out. You're gonna be a real challenge for each other. Very exciting stuff."

Danni's eyes narrowed a little as she realized she'd heard these words before.

"What's the problem, then?" Sophia asked.

"The problem is that this isn't fun and games. You two are getting ready to fight for real. You need to prepare just as you would for any other fight. Which means this is the time to develop strategy. To think about what you need to do to prepare for the specific adversary you're about to face. And it makes no sense to then turn around and reveal that strategy directly to your opponent in real time."

"You're being ridiculous."

"No. The two of you are being thick-headed. If you think about what I'm saying for two seconds, you'll know I'm right."

"But we were looking forward to this."

"You can't have it both ways. You guys like training together. I see that, and I totally get it. You like competing with each other. I think you both know I understand the appeal of that, too. But now you want to take that competition to a new level. And that's fine, but you have to do it right. If you're going to meet as opponents, then you have to prepare for each other as opponents."

"So, I'm just the enemy now?"

"I don't like that word so much, but yeah... for the next few months, you're the enemy."

"And we're not allowed to talk to each other?"

"Of course, you are. And what you talk about is your business. You guys want to meet for drinks once in a while and divulge your gameplans to each other, you be my guest. But that would be a pretty stupid thing to do, and all I'm saying is I'm not going to help you do it."

She let out a frustrated sigh, then looked to Danni for support. Danni tilted her head toward the door. Silently asking Bush to head back to the training room and leave the two of us alone for a few minutes.

"For the record," I said to Sophia, "I don't hate you. I never did."

Her only response was a little nod of her head that didn't really tell me anything. Then she walked out.

"I think you told a little white lie there at the end," Danni said.

"What are you talking about?"

"About not hating her."

"I don't hate her."

"Maybe not, but you said you never did. That's definitely not true."

"Can't we just let that bygone be bygone?"

"I don't think you can use that word that way."

"Oh, shut up."

She smiled as she settled into one of my guest chairs.

"Thing is, I'm not really sure she believes you."

"So, what do you want me to do about that?"

"Well, you could try acting like you don't hate her."

"I just told her I don't hate her."

"I don't think you know what acting means."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"I'm talking about the fact that you say you don't hate her, but then everything you do seems like maybe you do."

"That's not true."

"What are you whining about, Bush?" she quoted. "You didn't even say hello."

"Neither did she."

"She was pissed. Not the first time someone's come through your door that way, but you don't usually try to make it worse."

"But she doesn't work for me."

"You're only nice to people that work for you?"

"I mean that she works for a competitor."

"A competitor whose owner you get along fine with."

"I suppose."

"And you don't seem to have a problem with any of the other Den fighters."

"No, I guess I don't."

"So?"

"So, what?"

"So, I need you to this for me."

I didn't say anything for a long while.

Here I thought I was actually beginning to gain a little fondness for Sophia, yet Danni was telling me I basically acted like a bitch whenever she was around. And I had to admit she had a point. Because I do think of Sophia as the enemy. Whenever she's nearby, I feel myself become more aware. More defensive. Which was fine, on the surface. She was the leader of a competing stable. But I didn't have that reaction when I was around her teammates. Only her. Interesting.

"She thinks I hate her?"

"Pretty much."

"Well, I don't. And if we all manage to survive this little fiasco you two came up with, I'll see what I can do about straightening that out. But there's no way we're gonna kiss and make up right now."

"I'd prefer there be no kissing at all, if you don't mind."

"I doubt that will be a problem."

She nodded. Then we sat quietly for a while. Too long a while. As far as I could tell, the conversation was over. So...

"You're still here," I noted.

"Indeed, I am."

"And why might that be?"

"I can't just take a moment alone with the woman I love?"

"Something tells me that's not it."

She just smiled some more.

"You have a problem with the limits I put on the training?" I asked.

"Not particularly. Would have been nice if we'd discussed it first."

"There was nothing to talk about. This is how it's gotta be."

She gave a tiny little nod of agreement, then left the conversation for me to pick up again.

"I can't help but notice that you're still still here."

"What did you mean before when you said it was fine?"

"When I said what?"

"When you were talking about sparring, you said you totally get why we enjoy it. But when you were talking about the fight, you just said it was fine."

"And that's a problem?"

"That's what I'm trying to figure out."

"Figure out?"

"If you have a problem."

"With?"

"This whole thing. Me fighting Sophia."

"Didn't we already talk about this? And besides, what difference does it make? At this point, it's happening. Whether I like it or not."

"It makes a difference to me. I need to know."

I hesitated. Because I didn't have a problem so much as I saw one coming. But Danni was so excited. She'd given me that whole speech about it. I didn't want to rain on her parade.

"No. No problem."

"I feel like you're lying."

"There's no problem. It's just... I think this is going to be more complex than you realize."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning I'm not sure you've really thought it through."

"I have."

"You sure?"

"I can't really speak for Sophia..."

"I don't really give a shit about Sophia."

"That's nice."

"You know what I mean."

I got another little nod.

"I've thought it through," she said. "I know what I'm getting into."

I wasn't sure I believed that, but I was sure she believed it. That was the best I could do.

"Alright then, I have no problem. I hope it turns out like you want."

"But no training."

"No. No training."

She shrugged as she stood to go.

"I had to be able to tell her I asked."
