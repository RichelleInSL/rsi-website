Danni feinted with the jab, and sold it well. Plenty of body movement and speed, but I picked up on it, anyway. I couldn't tell you how I knew. It's just one of those senses you develop when you do battle with each other as often as we do. I turned my attention to the right hand. Because there was no doubt that one was coming, and coming fast. Even as she drew the left back, she had the right moving. Rolling her body weight from one side to the other like some kind of hellish teeter-totter. I watched it come. Like a baseball player keeping his eye on the ball. I jinked my head back and left at the last second. Her fist flew by the tip of my nose.

The miss gave me an advantage, but only a tiny one. Because Danni isn't one to lose her form for long, and she was already recovering. Stepping right to gain space as she drew her hands back to guard. But there was a window, and I snuck a quick left through it, making solid contact before she got out of range.

We closed again. I waited. Getting aggressive with her is a bad idea. Patience is better. Forcing the action plays into her defensive prowess, and that only gets me pain. So, I've learned to watch and move and probe. To put the work into opening up her defenses before launching any attacks.

She's not keen on letting me do this, of course. Though she prefers to lean on that defensive expertise of hers, she's not willing to give me all day to plot my strategy. Which is why she chose to make the first move again. Another jab that didn't seem to have enough on it. Another feint, but this time the right that followed went to the body. Where there is more of me to hit and a slick little move wouldn't be enough to get out of the way. So I didn't even bother to try. I stayed where I was and launched my own jab. Success. Not much damage done, but it interrupted her and caused her to lose a little something on her punch. Better yet, it set up my own right hand, which I emphatically placed on her chin.

Betcha that one rattled a few teeth.

Contact like that should have led to more. A chance for me to press my attack while my opponent shook off the cobwebs. No such luck with Danni. Her ability to cover up when hurt is second to none, and her shell-like defense is nearly impossible to crack. Still, I had to try. Knowing I had only seconds to capitalize, I quickly popped the jab again with the right hook right behind. Neither punch made so much as a dent. All right, new plan. I switched my target to her unprotected midsection and readied my right hand, but the clock ran out before I could get the punch off. She got her bearings, then wedged a leg up in-between us and defensively kicked me away.

I'd just landed my best shot of the spar and ended up getting almost nothing for it. Great.

As we reset our stances, she peeked around her guard and gave me a little wink because she knew she'd stolen one from me.

I just smiled and shook my head.

That's the attitude you have to have against her. She makes you work harder for less, so you have to be satisfied with whatever tiny successes you get. You also have to accept that she's never going to go toe-to-toe with you, and there's no way to make her. She's too slippery, and she's an expert at making you miss in exactly the way that leaves you most vulnerable. If you want to win, you have to learn to play her chess game. If she's thinking two moves ahead, you have to go three.

That's how I approached the next exchange. I fired first with the one-two, and I put a lot into it. Looking to give the impression I was getting frustrated. Or maybe letting a little bit of my actual frustration show through for strategic purposes. Danni guarded the jab and slipped outside the cross, putting her in prime position for the counter. I was slow getting my head out of danger. Deliberately leaving it too high, and tempting her to take a swing. She took the bait and launched a big left hook. I slipped under the punch and now I was the one with the chance to counter. Left hand to the gut, followed immediately by the right. Now, the uppercut. Sonofabitch. She jerked her head out and the punch whistled by the point of her chin.

Another win that paid me pennies instead of the quarters I would have gotten against just about anyone else.

Still, it was a win. Now I just had to dig in and go get another.

This is not the kind of fighter I was during my so-called career. Patience and little victories were not my thing. I was more the you-hit-me-and-I'll-just-hit-you-harder type. Danni likes to call me a "pit fighter." She does it purely to annoy me, but she also happens to be on to something. Not only does the term describe my style, it tells you all you need to know about the venues I fought in. Abandoned warehouses. Empty swimming pools. Dirt floor rings that looked more like animal pens. The "fans" that came to see these fights were not exactly connoisseurs of the sport. They had no appreciation for strategy and nuance. What they wanted was uncomplicated violence and mayhem, and they could always count on me to deliver. In the beginning, I was the piñata - shedding blood like candy as I got the crap kicked out of me night after night. Thought the masses were delighted, I got tired of that pretty quick. The smartest way to make it stop was to quit, but something in me wouldn't let it go. It wouldn't have been enough for me to stop losing. I wanted to win. I needed it. So I got hooked up with the right people, and learned how to do the little things that make you the one giving the beatings instead of taking them. And I was damned good at it. I wasn't a tactician. I was a brawler of the highest order. Stronger. Faster. Tougher. And I could put on a show like nobody else.

But now, thanks to Danni, I'm so much more than I ever was before.

When we first started donning the gloves, I think I surprised her. Because I might not be the most cerebral, but I can definitely be overwhelming. And I have a few more brain cells than Cheryl Cole. I can do more than simply charge ahead like a bull. But Danni figured me out pretty fast, and quickly learned how to control me. I'll never admit it to her, but the fact is she was better than me. That realization came much too late, though. I'd gotten a taste of what moments like this could be like with her, and there was zero chance I was giving it up. There was also no way I was going to let her kick my ass every day, so I learned the hard way and taught myself how to beat her. Then she learned to beat me, so I learned how to beat her again.

And so it goes. Round and round on this crazy carousel that neither of us have any intention of getting off of.

I went back to the wait and see approach on the next exchange, mostly because giving Danni the same look twice in a row is a real good way to lose. She tried a few jabs to draw me out. I returned the favor and nothing much came of it. Then she made her move. Her left hand moved just an inch or so. Not so much a feint as a flick. Something to make me think for just a split second while she launched the right. I thought faster than she expected and angled my head out of her path, but the dodge was hurried and sloppy. My stance was suddenly less than ideal. My defense was out of position, and I was perfectly lined up for the hook that followed. Not good. But instinct told me it was coming. I ducked low, and the punch sailed over my head. Great, but now what? Danni had the position, and standing back up would only make my situation worse. The only move was to take what was in front of me. I sprang forward and reached for her legs.

Takedowns and ground work also used to be high on the not my thing list, but the times they are a-changin'. Being the best means being good at everything, and Danni has had to continuously evolve every facet of her game to stay on top. So if I'm going to keep climbing in here with the champ, it means I have to evolve, too.

Apparently my evolution is incomplete because the move didn't work. Danni sprawled, pushing me low as she scooted her legs back out of range. I put a hand down to keep from going face down on the canvas, then reached again for her leg. I had to at least tie her up. No luck. She spun over the top and took my back. Her arm slipped around my neck, and I was suddenly a split-second from disaster. Instinct took over. I grabbed the arm and quickly stood up, then swept my foot back and levered her body over my hip. The good old hip toss. She went down like a sack of cement.

Take that bitch.

Then she grabbed my foot and snatched it out from under me.

Uh, oh.

I went down right next to her, and she was on me before I could roll up. I kicked my legs up to keep her from getting the mount, so she shouldered under them and moved to my side. She went after my arm. I snatched it away. I tried to roll up into her and deny her the space she needed to operate. She pushed me back and forced her way over me. Gaining the mount via the side door. We made a quick game of grabbing at each other's hands, but I was playing catch-up right from the start. She eventually got what she wanted, sweeping my arms aside with her left as she brought the hammer down with the right. Her fist stopped about an inch from my face. Then she opened her hand and lightly slapped my cheek. I went slack and sighed in defeat.

"I hate you," I said.

"Not all the time."

She took her hand away as she leaned down and gave me a quick kiss in the same spot.

"No, not all the time," I admitted, "but definitely right now."

"That's perfectly understandable, given the circumstances."

"Uh-huh. Get off me."

She rolled off, and we both sat up. She clambered over and retrieved our water bottles, then we both sat in the middle of the cage for a while. We were in no particular hurry. It was late and we had the place to ourselves.

"So, what do you think?" she asked eventually. "Am I ready?"

"As ready as I can make you."

"Guess I must be ready then."

"I'm sorry. Was that a compliment?"

"Yeah, but don't get used to it."

"I wouldn't dream of it."

"Besides, I still kicked your ass."

I gave her some flat side-eye.

"Sorry," she said. "Just wanted to give you proper balance."

"Much appreciated."

"My pleasure."

Truth was, she was more than ready. I still have a whole bunch of concerns over this stunt, but any questions I might have had about her taking Sophia too lightly were answered weeks ago. Her prep for this one has been relentless. Hitting the weights, running the strategy and seeking out the best fighters in the RSI fold to spar with. She clearly wasn't kidding when she said she was looking forward to the challenge Sophia represented, and she's making damn sure she brings her A-game to the party.

"How's Sophia making out?" I asked.

"How would I know? I'm forbidden to speak to her."

"You're forbidden to train with her. You talk to her all the time. So, cut the crap and answer the question."

"There's no reason to be snippy."

"No reason to be disobliging, either."

That brought a grin.

"This gonna be a thing with us now?"

"What's that?"

"The big words."

I shrugged.

"I can't see why not. We're both intelligent women. Strong vocabularies."

"'Disobliging,' though... kind of obvious, no?"

"And yet, shockingly uncommon. Also, don't think I haven't noticed that you're changing the topic instead of answering my question."

"Well, then I shall do so forthwith, for fear of being labeled contumacious."

I paused for a smile, then said, "Little late for that, I think."

She chose not to offer a denial we both knew was pointless.

"Sophs is good," she answered. "She's as into this as I am, and she really seems to enjoy teasing me daily about whatever new trick she's got up her sleeve. She certainly thinks she's got a plan to win."

Those were pretty much the exact words I didn't want to hear.

"Do you think she can?"

"Of course."

"Excuse me?"

My words came out sharp, and maybe a little strangled. A conversation that had started with an innocent throwaway question was suddenly going very badly.

Danni caught the tone.

"Did you really think I wanted this so I could just hop in the the cage and beat the crap out of my friend?"

"Well, no. You said it was because you wanted the challenge, but..."

"So, when you asked how she was making out, you were hoping I'd say the idea of facing me had her curled up in a ball and sucking her thumb?"

"Uhm... yes."

She gave me a look.

"Okay, no. But I gotta tell you, she freaks me out a little. I've been doing this a long time, and I'd like to think I know a little about what it takes to be a champion. And I'm sorry, but I don't see those things in her. Champions have weapons at their disposal that are better than everyone else's, and I can't name one skill that sets Sophia apart from the pack. Most champions are masters of strategy, too. Bush looked like she was making everything up as she went against Cole. The truth is, I don't know how she won. I keep telling myself it was almost an accident. That she just took advantage of a chance that suddenly came her way. But that's not something you see too often, which makes me wonder if maybe I missed something. Then you say stuff like this, and now..."

"So, you're finally starting to give her a little respect."

"I don't think calling her a freak and an accidental champion qualifies as respect."

She shrugged.

"Better than before."

I shook my head.

"Didn't we do this already? Why do you keep harping on it? Am I really that mean to her? Have I been cursing her out in my sleep or something?"

"No."

"And I already told you I'm going to do better. Are you going to even give me the chance?"

"Sure," she said, "but what does that have to do with you respecting her as a fighter?"

I started to answer, then stopped because I wasn't sure what I wanted to say.

"I understand you don't know her the way I do personally," she continued, "but you know fighters. When was the last time you saw someone turn it around the way she has? What she's accomplished this last year or so... you can't deny it's pretty damn incredible."

"No, I can't."

"So, why are you trying to? You've seen what she's done. Why don't you think she has a chance to beat me?"

"Because I don't want to. Because I don't get her. I don't like unknowns, and she's one of the biggest unknowns I've ever come across. This little engine that could thing she's got going makes a great story, but not knowing how she's doing it leaves me no way to help you gameplan for her."

"So, you're worried about me?"

"No, not worried. I just want..."

I left the sentence unfinished. Because it was stupid. She was Danneel Harris. One of the top pound-for-pound fighters in the league. A fact she had emphatically reaffirmed not very long ago. She had nothing left to prove to anyone, which made the thought running through my head sound selfish and whiny...

"You want me to have the belt back," she said.

...and obvious, apparently.

I gave her a playful shove with my elbow.

"If you already knew what I was thinking, why did we have to play this little game?"

"Purely for my own entertainment," she said with a smile.

"Well, I'm so fucking happy I could help."

She just kept smiling her stupid smile and let me stew for a moment. Then she gave me a little push back with her own elbow.

"I'm ready for this," she said. "To be honest, I don't have much of a choice. I've got more help than I know what to do with around here. Plus I've got you. I've got Steph. Not to mention that I've been doing this for a while, and it turns out I'm pretty good at it."

"So I've heard."

"And I'll let you in on a little secret: Sophia's not as big a mystery to me as she is to you."

I gave her an incredulous look. I'm not the type to just sit and worry. I've been going through film on Sophia since the day after she beat Cole. What could Danni possibly know that I didn't?

"Have a little faith in me," she said. "This isn't going to be as easy as you want it to be, but I'm not going to underestimate her the way others have."

I leveled my gaze with hers.

"I have nothing but faith in you," I said.

And thus, my problem was solved, simply because there was no problem. Danni was ready. Nothing was ever certain. I was being a control freak. Everything was as it should be.

"Now," she said, "about dinner..."

Literally everything, exactly as it should be.
