It took the whole flight home plus a couple of days for me to get over Aly's loss. The disappointment was so bitter, it cast a shadow over everything else. I was just so sick of her coming so close, and even more sick thinking about how she must be feeling. It was really starting to seem like fate had it in for her, and she was quickly running out of chances. Though she still had enough separation from the field to remain the top contender for now, it couldn't go on like this forever. Someone would eventually emerge from the pack and make a run... and Aly's shot might be gone forever.

Eventually, though, I started to come around. Overall, it had been another great night for us. Our champions had held serve. Our up-and-comers were up and coming. The rookie trio were rounding into shape, and all three looked like they were going to continue to be competitive as they rose through the ranks.

Between disappointment on one hand and elation on the other, I completely forgot about the whole OFN debacle... until the fan-voting results came out.

Only three RSI-related matchups had garnered enough votes to make the card. The first was apparently meant to be a battle of the Barden Bellas, featuring Anna Kendrick and Anna Camp. It was stupid, as usual. The two women were nowhere near each other in either experience or size. But in a rare development, both advantages fell on our Anna's side of the ledger, as Ms. Kendrick was slightly heavier and had considerably more cage time under her belt. Of course, that also meant she had more to lose, but at least there was no safety concern for our fighter.

The second selection was also centered on pop-culture, as fans wanted a Hunger Games showdown between J-Law and Willow Shields. I couldn't even make sense of this one. Shields was only nineteen years old. She had literally been a child when the films were made, and there had never been any publicly known tension between either the actresses or the characters they played. What was the appeal of pitting the two of them against each other? Just because Willow had grown into a beautiful young woman, and people loved to see pretty girls fight?

I didn't get it. Or maybe I did, and I just hated it. Either way, the mismatch went our way. Jen had the advantage in both size and experience. She had nothing to gain and everything to lose, but again there was no elevated risk our fighter would suffer some heinous injury.

The final selection had me on the phone about fifteen seconds after the announcement came out. Despite the fact that she had yet to step into the cage against an actual opponent, it seemed Katherine McNamara was already attracting some attention from the masses. Which is fine, I suppose. The fans are free to want whatever they want. As a result, stuff like this is to be expected. But the league should have been smart enough to pull the plug on it, right away. We'd just held Kat out of an event to give her more time to prepare for her debut. Now they were letting her get dragged into this nonsense? And against the vastly more experienced Scarlett Johansson, no less.

I called the head of the league's competition committee, and asked just what the hell they were thinking allowing the request to go through. Then I called the VP of Operations and told her she needed to call the competition committee herself and get this shit straightened out. My next call was to the advertising VP, telling him to hold up on whatever hoopla he had planned to publicize the card because a grievous error had been made. Finally, I called the league commissioner directly and told him point blank that there was no way my fighter was going to make her debut at his stupid, nonsensical event, and if he insisted on going forward with the idea, I was going to raise all kinds of hell.

A half hour later, a revised version of the card was published, and Kat's name was nowhere to be found.

See? I have my uses.

The change was welcome news, but it didn't make me feel much better. Kat made her name playing tough, capable heroines on screen, and the fans were going to keep pushing for that fantasy to be made real. They would want to see her pitted against the numerous female action stars that were already on the roster. Scarlett, Milla Jovovich, Kate Beckinsale, Charlize Theron... the list was endless and loaded with many of the top fighters in the league.

I suddenly realized we had a lot of work to do, and not nearly as much time as we thought to do it.

But that was a problem for the future. For the time being, we were in much better shape than usual when it comes to OFN. Only two fights so far, with no real concerns.

And it didn't get any worse when Danni, Steph and I gathered again to review the peer-to-peer challenges that came out a week later.

As they'd predicted, our two most important entries on the card involved Cat Bell and Torrie Wilson, and both were just a little bit more than meets the eye. Torrie had been challenged first by sixth-ranked Uma Thurman, but this was not a first-come, first-served thing. If a fighter is challenged by more than one possible opponent, order of precedence is decided by ranking position. So when fifth-ranked Veronika Zemanova decided to also challenge the champion, Thurman was effectively shut out.

The situation with Cat was even more interesting. Third-ranked Veronica Varekova started the chain of events by issuing the expected challenge for Cat's title, but that only led to second-ranked Traci Bingham issuing a challenge of her own. From our perspective, the end result was that Cat's impending matchup with Bingham had simply been moved up a few months, as they would now face each other at OFN instead of the next regular event. But the weird part was that Varekova could still potentially come out as the big winner. If Bingham managed to beat Cat, then the two would swap places while Veronica's position remained unchanged. But if Mac hung on to the belt, the loss would likely drop Traci to third in the rankings, allowing Varekova to move up without even having to fight.

Interesting, but it made no real difference to us.

"This looks about like we expected," I said.

Danni nodded her agreement.

"Just a few surprises, and none of them bad," she said.

I nodded back, then checked Steph. Her expression was as unreadable as usual, so I turned my attention back to the list.

"You don't think Allen challenging Amanda is an issue?"

Lilly Allen was buried in the middle of the pack in the Battle Tested Brawlers welterweight division, while our own Amanda Seyfried was only just beginning to emerge as a title threat. Apparently, Lily decided the quickest and easiest way for her to rise above the mediocrity was to beat Amanda, and Danni agreed it was sound thinking.

"Anything is a potential issue, and I have to admit that I don't love the matchup from Amanda's point-of-view. Allen's strength is her grappling, and Mandy hasn't always fared well against those types. But if she wants to compete for the title, she's going to have to learn to deal with whatever comes her way, so..."

"True," I said. "What about Kacey Musgraves going after Tiz?"

"Musgraves is out of her tree," Steph scoffed. "She sees Ashley as low-hanging fruit, but she's picking on the wrong fighter and she's going to get trashed for it."

Strangely enough, I found myself seeing the wisdom of Kacey's challenge while simultaneously agreeing with Steph. Musgraves was a fairly inexperienced fighter whose recent performances hinted that she might be a little better than everyone had first assumed. Tiz, on the other hand, was a veteran fighter who was just a bit short of being one of the elite. A slow, lengthy climb up the rankings had her currently sitting sixth, more than ten positions higher than Kacey. So, it wasn't hard to follow Musgraves's thought process. She and her team had looked at the rankings and decided Tiz was the top-ranked fighter they thought she could pick off.

The thing is, they were wrong.

Tiz wasn't in the sixth slot because that's where her talent maxed out. She was there because she'd already blown past where convention dictated her skills could take her. Above her sat some of the best bantamweights in the league. Vikki Blows, Keira Knightley, Julianne Hough... and there was Ash, right behind them. On paper, the facts said that simply shouldn't be. That she wasn't anywhere near as good as they were. But Tiz doesn't worry about what the paper says. Her style is not polished or elegant. She's a fighter's fighter. A real grinder, who wins just as many fights on heart as she does on talent. Maybe even more.

Musgraves was trying to make a big move by beating a top-ten fighter. She's not the first to try it, and she certainly won't be the last. To make it work, she would have to dig deep. To claw and grind and scrap her way to a victory. And when scrapping it out is your only route to a win, challenging the reigning queen of the scrappers is probably not the wisest plan.

I couldn't deny there was some risk for Tiz. Maybe Kacey's team knew something we didn't. Maybe they thought they had some intricate plan that improved her odds. But as far as I was concerned, Steph was right. Musgraves was biting off far more than she could chew.

I scanned the list of RSI fights again.

"Looks like we're all clear after that."

Neither one said anything. No yes or no. Just silence. Like they were waiting for the other shoe to drop.

That's when I realized I had been so caught up in what was on the card, I'd forgotten about the fight that wasn't. And now I was suspicious. No other challenges had come Danni's way, and she hadn't issued any either. So, was there some secret reason she'd refused to challenge Cole, or had she simply wanted to wait like she said?

I scanned the list again, this time finding the clue I was missing listed under Cole's name.

TITLE CHALLENGE
Division: PTP Welterweight
Champion: Cheryl Cole (Savage Wolf)
Challenger: Sophia Bush (Dragon's Den)

"Are you fucking kidding me?" I snapped.

"That's my cue to exit," Steph said as she rose to leave. "You ladies have fun."

I kept my eyes on Danni as I tapped my finger on the page in front of me.

"You passed up a chance to reclaim the title to make room for your girl Sophia?"

"That's one way to look at it."

"You have another way to suggest?"

"Not until you calm down."

The words fuck you stopped just behind my teeth.

I had a right to be pissed, but I almost immediately starting working to rein it in. This was not the first time Danni's friendship with Sophia had caused me grief. Last time it happened, I may have overreacted just a tiny bit and made things a whole lot worse before they got better. I didn't really want to do that again.

That didn't mean I was just going to let it go, though.

"You know you work here, right?"

"Of course."

"So, then what's this about? I know Sophia is your friend, but your job is to do what's in your best interest - and therefore RSI's best interest."

"I'm not sure those two things are the same, in this case."

"What the hell does that mean?"

"It means this might not be in the stable's best interest, but I think it is in mine."

I didn't say anything, but I'm sure my expression made it clear that I didn't get it.

She sat forward, resting her elbows on her knees and clasping her hands in front of her. Like she was getting ready to make a presentation, but wasn't sure how to start.

"I didn't join this league just to win," she said.

"Would you prefer to lose?"

"No, but I want to have a little fun, too."

"Winning isn't fun?"

"Sure it is, but I do it all the time. It gets a little boring."

A thin smile spread across her face, and a second later I was wearing one just like it.

"Wow... where's that legendary Harris humility I'm so accustomed to?"

"That's just my public persona. You know better, so there's no reason to pretend."

I smiled again. Both at the remark and the way she'd so quickly disarmed me.

"I know it's a little stupid," she continued, "and I know there's no guarantee the pieces will fall into place. But facing off with Sophia... I want that fight."

I raised an eyebrow.

"Let me get this straight... you're hoping she'll win so you can battle her for the belt instead of Cole?"

"Just think about it," she said. "She knows me - almost as well as you do."

"Don't patronize me, Harris."

"I'm not... but you know exactly what I'm talking about."

I did, but admitting it would put me in a bad position to continue this argument. I decided to try a different tack.

"How is she even able to make the challenge? I've only seen her fight Cheryl once."

"She fought Cole a couple of times years ago. When they were both starting out. When they met up again at the last tournament, that gave them rivalry status."

"So she's trying to slip in the back door."

"Now you're just being a pain. She's not doing anything that hasn't been done a thousand times."

I knew she was right. Both about me and about Sophia's tactics. The rule says you have to fight three times, it doesn't matter when, and Bush was far from the first to rely on ancient history to meet the criteria.

That made twice in less than a minute that my argument had gone nowhere. I was running out of ways to protest.

"You really think she can handle Cole?" I asked.

She hesitated as scenes from Sophia's recent tournament fight with Cheryl played through her head. It had not been a pretty thing from Ms. Bush's perspective.

"She can do it," she said eventually.

"She got her ass kicked last time."

"No saying that won't happen again," she admitted. "She's gonna need to make some changes. That's for sure."

"Gee, I wonder who's going to help her with that?"

She just looked at me and waited.

I rolled my eyes as reality set in.

"How silly of me. This is news to me, but you've had it all planned out. So, you've already been helping her."

She shrugged and said, "It's possible I may have given her a few pointers that might prove effective."

"Pointers... right," I scoffed. "Seems to me you've done that for her before."

Not too long ago, there was no chance Sophia would have been viewed as any kind of title threat. Though she had almost as much experience as Cheryl and Danni, her results were in no way comparable. She was an average fighter who'd had a few shining moments and more than a few poor performances. The most enthusiastic praise that could be showered upon her was that she was capable. She knew her way around a cage and she could put up a decent fight, but she simply didn't have the skills to rise above the middle of the pack. All signs said she was destined to finish out her career as a forgettable also-ran.

But that's all changed over the past couple of years or so.

In a sense, it all started when Sophia took on the stable leader role at the newly-formed Dragon's Den. Let's just say theirs was not an overnight success story. A year went by, and though the group did have a few bright spots, the losses were starting to mount and the pressure was building right along with it. Add to that the fact that Sophia wasn't one of those bright spots, and it's easy to see how desperate her situation was. A middle-of-the-road fighter leading a middle-of the-road stable, with a whole lot of people starting to point fingers in her direction. She knew she needed help to turn it around, so she decided to seek out an old friend. Of course, it didn't hurt that friend happened to be one of the best fighters in the league. One who not only knew how to win, but had also proven quite adept at leading a stable of her own.

Sound like anybody we know?

I don't know all the details or even how it got started, but somehow, some way, Sophia convinced Danni to ride to the rescue, and the two came up with a plan that was just half-assed enough to work. Danni led RSI by day, then slipped away to train Sophia at night. Because if you want to be a leader people can believe in, you first need the confidence to believe in yourself. And the only way Danni could give her friend that confidence was to teach her how to win.

The situation apparently went on for months, and the two made steady progress while also managing to keep their collusion a secret... until Danni was injured one day during a sparring session.

The injury was relatively minor, but it was bad enough that the cat got out of the bag. And make no mistake... when I found out what was happening, I lost my shit entirely. Did a few things I'm not too proud of, too. Tore into Sophia at the hospital. Fired Danni. Stuff like that. But I eventually came to my senses and remembered who I was dealing with. You have to be willing to trust at least a few people in this world, and Danni had long been high on my list. And the truth is, none of what happened should have surprised me. Faced with needing to stay true to RSI while also helping a friend in need, she had tried to squeeze herself between a rock and a hard place. That's vintage Danni, all the way.

As for Sophia... she and I are not exactly best buddies, but I think we've developed a sort of mutual respect. Thinking about what she did still ticks me off, but I have no problem understanding why she did it. And when you peel back the layers, you start to see just how far out on a limb she'd been willing to climb. The whole thing became quite the soap opera as it was, and that was with a limited number of people involved. If word had gotten out that she'd gone crying for help to another stable... well, it wouldn't have surprised me if she'd been laughed out of the league. She had basically risked everything to better herself and save her stable. It's hard not to admire her a little bit for that.

Anyway... with Danni's help, Sophia has tapped into skills no one knew she had. Those skills have brought her success, which has led to a gain in confidence. Which, in turn, has brought her even more success. She has reinvented herself as a totally different fighter. One with more talent than she'd ever shown before. Though Danni deserves some credit, Bush has really done most of the work herself. She stands on her own two feet now, both as a fighter and a stable leader. And the stable she leads has been on the rise ever since.

RSI has gotten something out of all this, too. Once Danni's little secret got out, it put an idea in Steph's head. Dragon's Den had talent in the form of both budding superstars and established champions... many of whom could fill in holes where our own training program was lacking. Our younger athletes needed as much experience as they could get, and the Den had all types of young talent for them to train against. More accomplished fighters such as Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert needed to shake things up if they wanted to continue to improve, and a veteran like Josie Maran could certainly do that for them. Even premier fighters like Catherine Bell, Petra Cubonova, Emma Watson and Tiffany Mulheron could stand to learn a thing or two from long-time champions like Caroline Wozniacki and Rachel McAdams.

And so, instead of ending the covert little inter-stable training program, we moved it into the open and expanded it. The two stables now train together two days a week, and there's no denying it's been a success for both sides.

"Clearly Steph already knows what you did," I said. "She have any thoughts?"

"You know Steph," Danni shrugged. "She's all about training and game plans and finding the shortest path from here to a belt."

"So she's on my side of the argument."

"Oh, are we still arguing?"

"Danni, hon... we're always arguing."

She smiled and nodded and said nothing. Because arguing is the one thing we have no trouble agreeing on.

Sensing she had softened me up enough, she nudged me toward the finish line.

"C'mon," she said. "Is it so bad that I'm looking for a real test?"

I narrowed my eyes.

"Trying to get to me via my competitive side, are you?"

"If it works, sure. I'm also betting you'll understand the allure of meeting an opponent you truly respect."

"Oooh, allure. Nice word.

"Thank you."

"What about Cole?" I asked. "She's not enough of a challenge for you?"

"Cheryl's a good fighter, and she deserves another shot after all that's happened. But she doesn't really fall into that respect category. Sophia, on the other hand... that's a match I want to be a part of. And now may be our only chance to make it happen."

I studied her for a moment and realized I was seeing something I hadn't seen in a long time. Maybe something I'd never seen from her before. When the reorg landed Cole in Danni's division, it was sort of like the circus coming to town. It was new and exciting - with lots of back and forth between their respective fanbases and a media only too happy to fan the flames - and Danni seemed eager to be a part of it. But all of that was more about drama than fighting. True, Danni made it more interesting than it should have been with that disastrous second fight, but the thorough whipping she gave Cole at the recent tournament had pretty much put all the questions to rest. That fight came after they'd both just spent weeks beating some of the best fighters in the league, so there was no question each woman was at the top of her game. Then Cheryl showed up with a gameplan designed to steal a win by employing all sorts of cheap delaying tactics. A tacit yet stark admission that she didn't think she was good enough to face RSI's stable leader head on. And yet Danni still won the fight handily.

So while the hype machine was warming up for a fourth matchup between the two, the truth was that interest had waned. No one was writing Cole off entirely, but only her most devoted fans still saw the rivalry as a battle of equals.

Now, Danni was searching for the challenge she thought she was getting the last time, and the look on her face said she expected to find it in Sophia.

That's a match I want to be a part of.

She would know. After all, one of the most notable impacts of our program with the Den is that it lets Sophia and Danni continue to train together several times a week. And what greater challenge is there than a talented fighter who can get inside your head and knows your every move?

I had to admit I could see the appeal, but I could also think of about a dozen ways this could end in disaster.

"For the record, I think the whole thing is a stupid idea."

"I already admitted that."

"So, that makes it OK?"

She stood and came around to lean against my side of the desk.

"Richelle..."

Unless we're about to have sex, hearing her use my first name is almost never good. And since sex in my office would be both unwise and uncomfortable, I assumed I was about to get the final sell.

"I really want this," she continued. "I don't know if Sophs can beat Cheryl, but I want to give her the chance. And just think about what would happen if she does. Me and her? Brooke vs Rachel in the battle of One Tree Hill? The fans would eat that up, and I think we can give them a fight to remember."

"You work for the league's PR department now, do you?"

"Richelle..."

That's one, two Richelles... ha, ha, ha!

By now she knew she had me, so she went in for the kill. Her features softened and her smile broadened as she reached down and gently guided my chin up so I was looking directly into those beautiful brown eyes.

I sighed in defeat.

"You know I can't say no when you look at me like that."

"That's why I look at you like that."

"That the only reason?"

She smiled and pulled her lower lip a little with her teeth as she leaned in for a kiss, which soon became two and then three until I put a palm on her chest and eased her back. Then I stood up and went to lock the door.

Danni raised an eyebrow.

"This is not a good idea," she said.

"It's funny, I was just thinking the same thing myself a second ago."

I went back to her. The kissing resumed, and it didn't take long for one thing to lead to another.

Turned out it wasn't nearly as uncomfortable as I thought it would be.
