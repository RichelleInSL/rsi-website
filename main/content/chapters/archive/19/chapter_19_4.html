When Danni and I arrived, Steph was standing near the line of heavy bags on the left side of the training room. I'd given a lot of thought to what might be coming, and I gotta tell you... finding her standing there with a bonnie young Irish lass was not on the list.

Did I know for certain she was Irish? Nope. But let's just say she had all the qualifications I require. Fair skin, a perfect smattering of freckles, and a positively gorgeous head of fiery red hair.

Even her name was right on the money.

"Ladies," Steph said as we approached, "allow me to introduce..."

"Katherine McNamara," I finished as I offered a handshake that was readily accepted. "Welcome to RSI."

"It's a pleasure to be here. And please, call me Kat."

"You already know each other?" Steph asked.

"Not personally," I said, "but neither the totally badass Mia Queen nor the infinitely capable Clary Fray require any introduction for me."

"Me neither," Danni added as she also greeted our guest.

"Ah, I should have known," Steph said.

I gave her a knowing look that said, Don't play stupid with me.

McNamara wore a sheepish little smile throughout the exchange. Like she'd become used to her notoriety, but still not totally comfortable with it.

It struck me as odd. I'm not exactly a maven of social media, but I'd run across a post or two from her and sheepish didn't seem like her style.

"So," I said to her, "thinking of stepping in the cage, for real?"

"Why not?" she said with an indifferent little shrug. "Figured I should find out what I can really do."

I nodded and turned to Steph.

"What do you have in mind?"

"This is Kat's initial run-through, so we're just going to start off hitting the bag, a little."

She looked to Katherine.

"Sound good?"

"Sure," the redhead answered.

Steph pointed to the door on the near wall.

"Locker room's through there," she said. "Suit up, and then we'll get started."

I smiled, thinking of the multiple meanings suit up might have for the woman who'd only recently become the new Green Arrow.

"OK, McMahon," I said once McNamara was gone, "I have to admit, your surprise was a good one. And despite your lies, I know you expected us to recognize her."

She made no attempt to deny it.

"But I'm not supposed to be here for a first run-through. She should have the chance to get comfortable first. That's why we do these things the way we do."

"Why don't you just relax and trust me?"

I gave her a look that amounted to a slightly more mature version of pllllbbbttt! and left it at that. She and Danni both know they can play the trust me card at any time to get me to back down.

Kat was back in short order, and she got the suit just right. Black compression shorts and a cream-colored sports bra. Simple and action ready. She had all the right equipment, too. Plain black boxing gloves slung over her shoulder for use on the bag, and a pair of black, fingerless MMA gloves in her hands for mitt and cage work.

"I guess I shouldn't be surprised that Mia Queen came totally prepared to throw down," I said.

She gave me a weak smile that was totally wrong. She said she wanted this, and she'd come ready to play. So why was every response she gave so tight and forced?

Trust or no trust, I thought Steph was off the mark. The poor kid was nervous as hell... and I had a pretty good idea as to why.

I stepped closer and lowered my voice.

"Just so you know... the Mia/Clary stuff... that's why Danni and I are here," I said. "Steph knew we'd recognize you, and that we'd get a kick out of it. But we all know this isn't a TV show. No one expects you to put the gloves on and blow us away."

I watched the weight come off of her immediately. Her expression brightened and the smile turned genuine. She even seemed to grow a little taller.

"I'm glad to hear that," she said, "because Mia is like a one-woman wrecking crew."

"Oh my god, she's awesome," I agreed, "but not the kind of reputation you want to have to live up to in real life."

She smiled again, looking a little embarrassed.

"It was the one thing I was worried about."

"And then Danni and I walk out here and it's the first thing we're talking about."

"Yeah..."

"Well, don't sweat it," I said. "We want the same thing you do. To find out what you can really do."

With another appreciative smile, she turned and put the gloves on... and then went right ahead and blew us away.

Steph positioned herself behind the bag to hold it steady, and our first clue as to what was coming was the way Kat slipped right into her stance. Smooth and easy, with not one word of correction needed.

Interesting, but I didn't think much of it. After all, she must have had quite a bit of training for those roles of hers, and the basic stance is probably the most important element of looking the part.

Steph started with the basics, calling for the 1-2. The response was crisp and immediate.

Tap, thump!

I freely admit I wasn't ready. I was looking for form. The way she held her stance. The way she shifted her weight. They were the most basic elements, and I expected them to come out rough and messy because that's how it usually is with new fighters. These things were basic because they were essential, not because they were simple. Fighters often struggle with them well into their careers. But Kat was so fast and smooth... both in her reaction and the pure speed of the punches. What I picked up looked good, but mostly I just found myself thinking, holy shit.

I needed to see it again. Steph read my mind and called the same combo.

Tap, thump!

Jesus...

Again, it was too quick to see everything. But those basics were all there. Good zip on the jab. Excellent footwork on the turn, which allowed to get her weight behind a strong cross that creased the bag nicely.

Show it to me, again.

Once again, Steph obliged.

Tap, thump!

I looked for more and found it, this time focusing on what came after. How well did she reset herself? How fast did she recover her hands back to guard?

Again, I was impressed. After the cross landed, she melted right back into her stance. Feet set perfectly. Guard high and tight. All with no visible thought or effort. It was automatic.

By my estimation, she had already completed about a month's worth of training. Probably more than that. Her basic technique was textbook.

Steph stepped it up a notch.

"One, two, three!"

Tap, thump, thump!

The added hook made no difference. Her form remained perfect, and her footwork was sensational. New fighters have such a strong temptation to try too hard. To make every punch a killer blow. That leads to all kinds of position and balance problems as they try to generate maximum power. But Kat didn't let herself be seduced. She didn't even appear to be fighting it. Her movements were smooth, precise and, most importantly, minimal. And, once again, she finished in the exact same position she had started in. Square to the bag with her defense in place.

Steph leveled things up again.

"One, three, two!"

Tap, thump, thump!

A combo known as hooking off the jab. Instead of the normal left-right flow, this one kept both opening punches on the left side. It's not a thing that's easy to do. You have to pull the arm back, change the angle of attack and get the second punch off quickly. Then finish with the cross.

The new girl got it all, and snapped right back into her stance.

I was starting to suspect that Kat's mere presence was not the real surprise Steph had in mind.

What happened next confirmed it for me.

"One, two, nine-b!"

She was getting the feet involved, which introduced a whole new list of possible problems.

McNamara never even hesitated.

Tap, thump, BOOM!

Perfect. A solid kick to the body ended the assault, and her reset was spot on.

Danni bumped me with her elbow. As if to say, are you seeing this shit?

Hell, yes I was... and I was far from alone because Kat was starting to draw a crowd. Having a new recruit hit the bag a little was fairly common and generally not noteworthy. But having a true rookie pound the leather with this kind power and technique was something of an event. And our three existing rookies - Olivia Holt, Dove Cameron and Zendaya Coleman - had all gotten themselves a front row seat for the show.

Meanwhile, Steph decided it was time for the advanced class.

"One, six, five, two!"

A pair of uppercuts in the middle to bring the opponent's guard all the way down and open up a window for the right. Power was needed all the way through, and your positioning had to stay perfect if you were going to make that happen.

Tap, bang, bang, BOOM!

She was a little sloppy through the middle, but got all of the closing cross. Like a gymnast experiencing a slight wobble on the uneven bars just before sticking the landing. Maybe not perfect, but she'd ended it on a high note. And the judges were impressed.

"Daaaaaaamn," Daya marveled. Beside her, Olivia was slowly shaking her head while holding her hand over her mouth, and it seemed to me the whole gym had stopped what they were doing and drifted our way to get a look at the new girl.

Kat bounced out of her stance. She took note of the audience she'd drawn, then looked at me.

"Did I do something wrong?"

"No, maam," I said with a definitive shake of my head.

She turned back, but it was immediately clear she was unsettled. This was just a little workout. Why were all these people watching her?

Steph took a hand off the bag, and tried to smooth the waters.

"Forget about them," she said. "Just stay on me and keep it up. You're doing fine."

The redhead nodded and resumed her movement. Steph gave her a couple of one-two's to help her get back in rhythm, but she didn't keep it simple for long.

"Two, three, two, nine-b!"

Cross, hook, cross, BOOM!

It wasn't perfect, but she got through it. And geez... the way she hit the bag on that kick...

"One, one, four, nine-b, nine-b!"

Even calling it out was hard, with Steph pronouncing the first kick as, "Nine-buh." But Kat got the message.

Tap, tap, thump, boom, BOOM!

A collective Ooooooh! went up from the crowd when the second kick landed.

I saw Kat turn a little, then she brought her attention straight back to Steph as a huge smile split her face. Apparently, she'd figured out why the crowd was there.

Steph saw the smile, too.

"Easy does it, superstar," she said. "Just try to stay with me."

Kat brought her gloves up high and got ready. Taking the statement as the challenge it was.

"One, two, three, ten-L!"

The L takes the kick downstairs. A Muay Thai style shin kick.

Tap, thump, thump, thump!

And now I had questions. Like how did Kat know what all the numbers meant? And how did Steph know she knew?

"One, two, three, ten-b!"

Almost the same combo, but now the kick would be aimed at the body.

Tap, thump, thump, BOOM!

Ohhh, my lord...

Another cheer went up as the bag folded from the final impact. What the girl's left foot could do, her right could do even better.

"Eight, eight!" Steph barked.

Teep kicks? How did she...?

Thump, thump!

McNamara drove her foot straight into the bag, recovered her stance then did it again. Just as before, the technique was beautiful. Quick, powerful kicks, with the impacts coming as her foot was rising.

I searched the crowd and found our resident Muay Thai queen, Emily VanCamp. She was nodding and chatting with Emma and Tiffany, and everybody looked impressed.

"Nine-L, two, three, ten-b!"

It was a combo that said, Bring the pain, and Kat delivered.

Thump, thump, thump, BOOM!

Another chorus of ooohs rose up as the quick kick was followed by a trio of heavy blows. I found myself feeling bad for the bag.

By now, Kat was starting to flag a little. Which meant her conditioning needed work, but big deal. That was something we could handle easily. As for the rest... she wasn't perfect, but she positively oozed potential. I had seen more than enough.

But Steph had one more.

"One, two, three, ten, nine!"

No way, I thought.

Kat snapped the jab. Pivoted into the cross, then back for the hook. As soon as that punch landed, she reset her balance and up came the right foot.

BOOM!

The high kick took her theoretical opponent's head clean off.

She followed through with the motion of the kick, and wound up facing backward when her foot hit the floor. Not normally a good position to be in, but she rolled right through. Her left foot came up as she continued to rotate. A twist of her hips and the foot whipped through the air.

Her heel struck the exact same spot on the bag with another thunderous BOOM!

Any opponent - no matter how big - would have been on the canvas. No doubt about it.

Steph let the bag go and signaled they were done. The crowd went nuts, and Kat played right into it. Giving a deep, exaggerated bow and a flourish of her hand. The women of RSI ate it up. Liv stepped forward, threw an arm around the redhead and said, "Holy shit, girl!" in a way that made me think the two knew each other.

I crooked a finger at Steph in a get your ass over here gesture.

"Quit jerking me around, McMahon," I said. "How did you know she could do that?"

She gave me a no big deal shrug.

"I heard some whispers. Friend of a friend kind of thing. Apparently, Kat had said something about wanting to do this for real, and people who would know thought she'd be good at it. So, I had someone put me in touch with the guy who trains her for the TV work. He said she takes it dead serious. That she actually seems disappointed when they get too theatrical. And they've been together a long time. He was convinced she could do it. We compared notes on the number system so I would know how to work with her, and here we are."

"Yeah, but that was crazy," Danni said. "How did you..."

"I didn't. With everything I'd heard, I admit I was planning to push her. But I had no idea she'd be that good. You saw how it went. I started with the basics, then leveled up from there. And every time, she was up to it. She's got a lot of work to do. Hitting the bag is nothing like being in the cage with a real opponent. But she's got the best raw skills I've ever seen. I don't say this often, but I'm impressed."

Not often translated to never. I had never heard her say anything like that about a rookie before. Which told me exactly where she stood as far as bringing Kat on board.

So, I was in. Steph was in.

I turned to Danni.

"Speak now, or forever hold your peace."

She shook her head.

"I'm in," she said. "No doubt about it."

"And we all agree that we're looking at an honest-to-god prodigy, here?" I asked.

Danni gave a solid nod. Steph hesitated, but didn't say no. That was good enough for me.

I stepped out so Kat could see me.

"Hey, McNamara," I called.

She turned to face me.

"How'd you like to fight for RSI?"

The women around her let out a cheer that said they would like that, very much.

"But I haven't even been anywhere else!" she protested.

"Do you really need to?" I asked as I walked out to where she was.

My implication was clear. There were a lot of good stables in the league, but we were the best. That's not bravado. It's a fact. I don't gloat about it often, but these circumstances called for it. I wasn't about to let Kat get out the door and let someone else snap her up. This one was mine.

The smile on her face told me my ploy had landed perfectly.

"I guess not," she said.

"Good," I said, "then we have a deal."

I offered my hand. She took it without hesitation. Another cheer followed.

"I see you already know some of these idiots, so I'll let them tell you what it's like here. Go ahead and ask whatever questions you have. If one of them gives you an answer you don't like, tell me and I'll bounce her out of here so fast it'll make you dizzy."

She laughed.

I hooked a thumb over my shoulder.

"Danni here will show you around when you're ready. Any problems, let me know. Otherwise, I'll see you later and we'll make it all official."

"Sounds good," she answered.

She headed straight back to the rookie trio, who absorbed her right into the group. Yet another excellent sign.

I went back to Steph and Danni.

"Hot damn, McMahon," I said quietly. "You brought me the real deal. A superstar in the making."

"And she's a natural redhead, too," Danni chimed in. "Who'da thought that combo was even possible?"

I shook my head and sighed deeply.

"Why do you always make me want to slap you?" I asked.

She shrugged.

"It's a gift."
