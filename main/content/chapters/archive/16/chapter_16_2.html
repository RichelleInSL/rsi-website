"How would you know?"

"C'mon, Liv," I said. "I've seen you do interviews. You come across as capable, level-headed and intelligent. Not the type of person who mouths off to her parents. Also, not the type of young woman who was raised by parents who would let her."

"So, there you go," she said. "I already have all the mothering I need. I don't need you to help out."

I was banking on being right about what I'd seen from her this past month. As far as I could tell, she was happy here. I was hoping she'd want that to continue.

She tried not to smile, but couldn't quite hide it.

"I assume that's not the reason for your interest."

"No," I said, "but you also showed how bad you wanted it. That's an attitude I can work with."

She kept her eyes on me as she considered what I was saying.

"We both want the same thing, Liv," I said. "We don't have to be enemies."

The smile started to get away from her, but she reigned it in.

"I still don't like the way you went about it," she said.

"We get this right, and I'm betting you won't care about that, anymore."

She sat quietly for a minute, rolling the knuckles of her right hand as she let her gaze drift off over my shoulder.

"So, what do you think?" I asked. "You wanna keep doing battle with me, or would you maybe prefer to put this behind us and focus on getting what you need?"

"I'm not happy with what you did," she answered, "but I'm more interested in winning than anything else."

"Fair enough," I said as I put my hand out to her.

She thought about it for another second, then reached up to shake my hand.

It wasn't exactly the peace accord I was hoping for, but at least we'd reached an agreement on a cease-fire. I was pretty confident that winning would smooth the rest over.

"So, how are you finding things?" I said as I made my way back to my chair.

"It's been pretty good," she said. "You guys really seem to know what you're doing."

"That's because I let people who are smarter than I am run the show," I said. "And if you ever tell them I said that, I'll kill you with my bare hands."

"What?" she barked.

No further information or details were provided. We had a problem, and the proposed solution had been tested by Stephanie McMahon and deemed acceptable.
