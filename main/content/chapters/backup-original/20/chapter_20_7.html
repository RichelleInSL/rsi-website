Things in the Bush corner got a little crazy during the break. If there was any strategy discussed, it wasn't apparent. Bri was in full cheerleader mode. Screaming so loud, I could hear her all the way up in the box. Not the words. Just the screaming. It was wild... and a little bit unsettling.

Then I looked over to Cole's corner, and it all made sense.

Cheryl's style has all sorts of pitfalls associated with it. Most of them have to do with her lack of defense. When she lands you'll be in a world of hurt, but when she misses she's wide open. The literal embodiment of kill or be killed. But that also kept most of her fights very short. Meaning the other big shortcoming was rarely a factor. But here we were, sliding into the championship rounds. Otherwise know as overtime for a Cole fight... and her all-out style had clearly taken a lot out of her.

I hadn't noticed it during the round, but it was obvious with her sitting there slouched on the stool. I suddenly wasn't sure which she wanted more: to break Sophia's face or go take a nap.

My guess was that she was going to try to do the first one quickly so she could get to the second.

Which explained Briana's insane histrionics. Sophia was behind in almost every way, but it was now obvious she was way ahead in the fatigue department. So, Evigan was bringing everything she had. Loading up the adrenaline and figuratively shooting it into her fighter's arm. Because if any hope of a comeback still remained, it had to happen now.

It wasn't going to be easy, though. Cole might be tiring, but she was also more aware. It was forgivable that she hadn't picked up on Bush's little redirections in the thick of the action. Look how long it had taken me to spot it, and I wasn't getting pummeled while I was doing it. But if I could do it, so could her corner people. Which meant that now, Cheryl knew what was happening, too.

The fourth round started slower... especially for Cole. Was she pacing herself, or trying to sit on her lead? I was willing to bet on the former. She didn't seem like the type to be satisfied winning on points when the knockout was still an option.

Meanwhile, Sophia stayed patient. Using up time she really didn't have. But what was the alternative? To try and force the action? Going toe-to-toe with Cole was a no-win situation, no matter how tired she was.

A few light exchanges passed, with little damage done. I got the feeling Cole was doing a little measuring as she gathered herself. Plotting out the best way to go after Bush as she waited for her second wind. Apparently, a little fatigue had somehow made her smarter.

Then again, maybe not. When she finally made her move, it was the same old thing. Cole launched a flurry. Bush brushed it aside and countered to the body as she worked her way in close. Cheryl still seemed unable to solve that. Then Bush redirected her and looked for separation. That part, Cole had an answer for. Quickly finding her balance, she launched a big right hand while Bush was right in prime range. It did little good. One of the first things I'd noticed about Sophia's approach was how wary she seemed as she made her escape. She knew Cole was eventually going to find a way to try and do some damage during the retreat, and she was ready when it happened. A well-placed step and a little slip of her head let her get clear with no damage done.

That's the way the rest of the round went. It was clear Cole wanted to dig her fists into Bush some more, but she remained abnormally patient about it. She tried a few wrinkles to make it happen, and she found some success here and there. But Sophia was mostly able to keep things under wraps, and she quickly contained the situation whenever it started to get out of hand. Between the control she demonstrated and the points she scored with her counter game, she took the round easily. Unfortunately, it fell far short of what she needed. Cole's two round lead was reduced to one, but it still felt like Bush was miles behind. Cole had won her rounds with power and violence. Bush had won hers with technical proficiency. I've never heard of any judge who treated those two things as equals.

The final break was unremarkable. Cole still looked tired, but the plodding pace of the fourth had helped. Sophia looked fresher and Bri was once again busy trying to fire her up, but I couldn't see how it would make a difference at this point.

Bush was my kind of fighter. Though she possessed no particularly notable skills, she had guts and heart. She was a survivor. A grinder. The kind who just keeps coming and literally wrings the fight out of you. But that kind of thing takes time, and there just wasn't enough of it left.

One glance at Danni told me she was thinking the same way. Neither of us would say it because there was always a chance. But this one felt small and much too far away, and Danni was feeling the doubts that Sophia could not afford to. I had never really been behind their little plan. The two of them facing each other felt like a bad idea, and I didn't think they really knew what they were asking for. But they had wanted it, and Danni had been excited about it. Now, it was slipping away. But knowing Danni, that's not what she was thinking about. Instead, she'd be thinking about how her friend was on the cusp of losing what might be her only shot at the divisional belt.

The final round got under way. Cole's approach was still restrained. I wondered if she had someone new on her team. Someone who had finally gotten it in her head that there were times when being an insatiable savage was not in her best interest. She got her jab working in a way I never knew she could. I'd only seen her use it to set up whatever haymaker she had in mind, but now she was putting it to use in a defensive capacity. Sticking it in Bush's face whenever the two got close. Ineffective as far as damage went, but it was just what she needed to disrupt anything Sophia might be planning.

Speaking of planning... something about the looks Cole was giving told me she still had something in mind. She was gauging Bush's movements, again. Testing her occasionally with the cross. Because restraint was fine, but a knockout would be oh-so-glorious. And she could afford to take a few shots, couldn't she?

I didn't know how to feel about that. On the one hand, if Cole opened up, she might give Bush the chance she needed. On the other, she might connect and drop Sophia like a rock. I found one of those possibilities very unappealing.

The halfway point came and went. The round had been mostly flat. It was just as I'd feared. Bush lacked the offensive tools to mount the comeback. She couldn't find a way past Cole's jab. She tried harder, which meant taking more chances. It did little good. And the more she did it, the braver Cole became. Suddenly, she was the one scoring off the counter. Getting another little piece of Bush every time they tangled. She seemed to be enjoying herself, too. Maybe she didn't need the knockout. Maybe she could just beat Sophia up for the rest of the round. Mark her up some, so people would remember who the real queen was around here.

Cheryl Cole was not a complicated person. It wasn't hard for me to imagine what was going through her head.

The two closed again. Sophia tried to get inside, and Cole countered with the jab. Bush guarded it easily but lost her momentum. Then Cole launched the right. It landed with a sensational crack and spun Sophia's head to the right. Bush stumbled away and got herself righted. She was a tough cookie. I had to give her that.

Bush waded in again. Cole hit her with the jab, then put the right downstairs. It sunk in deep. Real damage was done. Sophia was hurt, and Cole was smelling blood.

Sophia came back for more. Mostly because she had no choice. Cole responded. Jab high, cross low. Sophia scampered out of range and the right hand fell short. Cheryl looked painfully disappointed.

Sophia came forward. One more time, for old time's sake. Cole threw the jab. Bush guarded and backed away. Cole threw the right. She put a lot into it and made it long. She stretched. She wanted it. Wanted that feeling of her knuckles digging into Sophia's abs. Maybe she could fold her over. Leave her crying and helpless in the middle of the cage.

She stretched too far.

Nothing about what happened next looked planned in the slightest. Her head was just there. As Cole extended, she leaned out. And there was her head. Right where Sophia could grab it. So she did. No technique. It was almost like an accident. Cole's head was there and Bush's left arm was there. So Bush hooked the arm around Cole's neck. The importance didn't seem to register. Cheryl just knew she didn't like it. It was annoying, so she stood up. She didn't recognize the danger. Sophia went with the motion. Now she was behind Cole, with the arm still looped. She reached up with her other hand. Again, there was nothing crisp or proficient about it. If anything, it was too slow. Like neither of them really understood what was happening. Sophia latched her right hand onto her left... and then they both seemed to figure it out at once.

Bush thinking, Holy shit! I've got her! and squeezing for all she was worth.

Cole thinking, Holy shit! She's got me! and scrambling to pry Sophia's arm off of her.

But it was too late. Bush had it locked in. A straight up, no frills, ugly as sin bulldog choke. And it was sunk in deep.

Cole's chances of retaining the title were suddenly sunk, too. Gone in an unbelievable instant. Escape was impossible. Still, she had to try. She got her legs churning. Carrying them backwards and driving Sophia into the cage. Fuck that. There was no way Bush was letting go. Cole's efforts left her off-balance, allowing Sophia to arch her over backward as she viciously strangled her. McAndrew hurried over for a closer look. Cole had only seconds, and no way out. She panicked and squirmed, but there was nowhere to go and nothing to do. She willed herself to hold on, but there was still more than a minute left. There would be no miracle reprieve. The crowd was going crazy. All eyes were on Cole. Her right hand came up. She reached for Sophia's arm. To fight or to tap?

Then the hand fell limply away.

Cole had waited too long.

McAndrew immediately waived the fight off. Sophia released the hold, and together they eased her now-unconscious opponent to the canvas.

Bush stepped away to make room as the medical staff hurried in to tend to Cole. Then she just sort of wandered aimlessly around the cage, looking even more stunned than I was.

Danni never moved an inch.

Bush's team rushed in and Evigan stormed over and wrapped her up in a huge hug. Bush still showed little reaction. Perhaps Briana was going to have to slap some sense into her.

I was starting to wonder if I was going to have to do the same for Danni.

I nudged her with my elbow.

"I don't know if you noticed, but your friend just made herself a champion."

I got no response... but she was smiling from ear to ear.

"You gonna say something, or just sit there looking stupid?"

"I really don't know what to say."

The answer was utterly unremarkable, yet completely appropriate. I really didn't know what to say, either.

In the cage, Cole had recovered to a seated position. The medics were peppering her with questions, but she looked fine. Actually, she looked furious... which was essentially the same thing. It was really all gone for her now. Danni had taken the tourney title and thoroughly embarrassed her in the process. Now Sophia had stolen away the coveted divisional crown.

Here sits Cheryl Cole. Champion of nothing.

Meanwhile, Sophia was finally showing some signs that she was starting to understand what she'd done. Smiling broadly as she traded back slaps and hugs with her team. I wondered how long it would take for reality to really sink in. My guess was it would happen hours from now. In a quiet moment, long after everyone else was gone.

McAndrew called the fighters to the center for the official announcement.

"Ladies and gentlemen, your winner by technical submission and NEW welterweight champion.... Sophia Bush!"

The announcer dragged the "a" and the "u" in dramatic fashion. Danni and I stood and clapped. Bush smiled like a fool as her arm was raised. We stood and clapped some more when the league president came out and presented Sophia with the belt. It was awesome to watch. This shit never gets old.

After all the standing and clapping, we sat and thought.

What was Danni thinking about? Was she letting herself peek ahead to the coming title contest between her and her fellow stable leader? Maybe, but my guess is that she was saving those thoughts for tomorrow. That right now was all about enjoying her friend's big win.

My thoughts, on the other hand, were wandering a little.

I was happy for Bush. After the risks she'd taken and the hard work she'd put in she deserved this.

I was happy for me, too. A defeated Cheryl Cole makes me giddy. Not a nice thought, but news flash: I'm not really a nice person.

Most of all, I was astonished. What the hell did I just witness? Despite some impressive moments, Bush had been losing this fight the entire time. From the opening bell, right up until the moment she won. How did she do that? Just by hanging around long enough for an opportunity to come along?

It takes something special to be a champion. Something you can't exactly put your finger on, but you know it when you see it. But if Bush had it, I hadn't noticed. As far as I could tell, she'd just survived her way into a title. That's not something you see very often, and I had no idea how she'd done it. A fact that made me uneasy. Especially with Danni lined up as her next opponent.

Speaking of that showdown... Bush's shocking victory had changed everything in an instant. With the way the fight had been going, my concerns about Harris vs Bush had nearly faded into oblivion. Now they were back, and I only had two months to work them out.
