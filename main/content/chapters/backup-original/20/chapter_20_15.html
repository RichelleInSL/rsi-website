Five titles, all gained or defended by Rampage fighters... and yet, my first stop was the Dragon's Den locker room.

I didn't know exactly how to go about this because it's not something that's done. Ever. You don't show up in enemy territory while the opposition is still licking its wounds. But everything else about this fight had been outside the norm, so why not this, too?

I wasn't insane enough to just walk in though, deciding instead to go through my Dragon's Den counterpart. Nice guy. Very down to Earth. Calls himself Dragon.

Not everything in life has a logical explanation.

He was surprised to see me and even more surprised when I asked to be let in to see Sophia.

"I'm not sure that's a good idea," he said. "Emotions are running high around here."

"Yours included, I'm sure."

His eyes came up to mine. I saw the pain he held in them, and I fully understood. Because Danni isn't the only stable leader who happens to be dating her boss, and I knew how I'd feel if this one had gone the other way. Hell, I was worried enough about the shape she was in as it is.

"I'm not here to make anything worse," I said. "I just want to check in on her."

He searched my face for something. People do this to me a lot. I never know exactly what they're looking for.

"I think she'd appreciate that," he said.

Which was a nice thing to say, but I strongly suspected it wasn't true.

He seemed to be thinking the same when he added, "If she lets you get that far."

Five minutes later, I was following behind him, thinking I might not even make it to where Sophia was. Whispers swirled around the locker room, and I could feel every pair of eyes on me. But we somehow made it to the treatment room without incident.

That's when things slid a little sideways as Briana confronted us before Sophia even noticed me.

"What the fuck are you doing here?" she snapped as she spun to block the doorway.

"Now, Bri..." Dragon began.

I touched a hand to his arm. There was no sense in him defending me. As expected, I was becoming the lighting rod for emotions that had very little to do with me. These women would either work that out for themselves, or I would leave. There were no other acceptable ways for this to go.

Bri maintained her sentry-like position in front of me, but said nothing more. In fact, nobody said anything for a long while. Because we were all waiting for Sophia to make the final call.

"It's OK," she said finally.

The tension ebbed a little, but Bri stayed right where she was.

"If you're up to it," I said to Sophia, "I'd like to talk to you alone for a moment or two."

Bri eyes flared, and her stance grew a little more aggressive. Which nearly drew an automatic response from me. Some deep-seated instinct that told me to get my hands out and set my base. Preparing to defend myself, if necessary.

The urge was an echo from a time long past, and I fought it off quickly. The only danger here was emotional, not physical.

"That's fine," Sophia said softly.

With the decision made, Briana seemed to deflate like a balloon. All of the steely posturing gone in an instant. She and Sophia were good friends, and the loss had taken a lot out of her. It seemed a little ridiculous, being exhausted from just watching. But my experiences as a spectator when people I cared about were fighting had taught me a lot about how torturous it can be.

I let those memories play into my expression, and Bri seemed to notice. We exchanged little nods of recognition. Foes somehow bonded by the common experience of being wounded while standing on the sidelines.

It was a thought that once again made apparent the rare and complicated nature of the situation. Because we were not foes. This was not my stable, but these people were friends. At least, of a sort. Though I was extremely happy Danni had come away with the victory tonight, I was not at all pleased at the pain it was causing in this room.

Bri moved aside and headed back toward the locker room. Dragon gave me a little look, then followed behind.

After they were gone, I realized I had gotten exactly what I wanted but now had no idea what I was going to say.

Sophia filled the void.

"How's Danni?" she asked.

"She seemed fine, but I haven't gotten the full report, yet. I came here, first."

"Why would you do that?"

"Because she's going to ask me the same question about you, and I wanted to have an answer for her."

"Yeah, well... tell her I'm fantastic."

And sarcasm aside, she sort of was. In fact, the sight of her was nothing short of amazing. Though signs of the damage done were still apparent all over her face, she looked nothing like she had when the fight ended. Her open wounds had been treated and bandaged. The swelling had been massively reduced. A woman who less than thirty minutes ago looked like she'd been pounded with a mallet now looked like she'd maybe had a bad fall.

"Now, why are you really here?" she asked.

There was some venom in the question. Enough that I briefly forgot my objective of not making waves.

"I came to find out exactly how big a piece of shit you really think I am."

"I don't think you want me to answer that."

"Are you serious?"

She was quiet for a long time. I kept my mouth shut because I had no idea what to say.

"No," she said finally. "It's just that things between you and me..."

"Things between you and me are not..."

I let the thought hang for a moment, then changed direction.

"Well, they're clearly not what I think they are," I concluded.

"It's obvious you don't like me," she said.

"Not true."

"The last real conversation we had was you hissing at me in a hospital hallway about how you blamed me for everything."

"Also not true," I said. "In fact, the last time we spoke, didn't I offer you a spot at RSI if you ever wanted it?"

"You only said that because you knew I'd never take it."

"That's a load of crap. I don't offer contracts as consolation prizes. You should at least know me better than that."

She glared at me. I glared back. I also wondered why I ever do things like this. They never go the way I want them to, and the reason is always the same. I waste time stumbling along the high road when what I really needed to do was go right at the problem.

Well, we were here now, so no time like the present.

"Look, we got off on the wrong foot back then," I said plainly. "You stepped in shit you couldn't even see. You got between me and Danni at a time when none of us realized there was even gonna be a me and Danni, and you got rolled up in feelings I didn't know I had. I let my emotions get the best of me, and I'm sorry about that."

Even through the bruising and swelling, I saw the way her face changed.

"The truth is, I respected what you did," I continued. "A lot. I know the chance you took, going to Danni like that. You put it all on the line for one last Hail Mary shot at turning it around. Your career, your friends, the Den... you could have lost it all. But you made it stick, and now look at you. Look at this stable. I would never have said it back then, but you guys were about a half-step from the trash heap. That's why I made the offer. I figured you might need a place to land, and I wanted you to know RSI was open to you. But you proved me wrong. You led this group back to credibility, and now you've got legitimate title threats all over the place."

"But not me," she cut in. "You don't really think I can compete."

Oh, good. This again.

"Is that what this is about? That I don't respect you enough?"

She shrugged.

"Danni said..."

"Danni can kiss my ass, and so can you while you're at it."

Do I know how to defuse a situation, or what?

"If Danni's been talking about me, then she should have told you I don't hand out the kind of respect you're talking about for free, either. And I don't follow you two around or study your training sessions. You think you can do it? She says you can do it? Well, good for both of you. But for everybody else to buy in, you have to prove it. And you haven't done that."

She was glaring at me silently again.

"Not until tonight, anyway," I added.

Her expression changed drastically. Her silence did not.

"That's why I'm here," I said. "I'm not going to stand here and count off the things you accomplished tonight. You've got people for that, and I have no doubt they're itching to tell you once you're ready to listen. I'm just here to say that I'm with them. That I was watching, and I saw it, too. And I learned I had you all wrong."

After that, there was silence for a while. I had nothing more to say, and I had pushed things too close to sappy status as it was. But I found myself wondering where this was gonna go. Would she take the compliments, or push back some more? Or maybe play a little poor me regarding her lost title? A feeling I would understand, but didn't really want to talk about. That was a conversation she should save for Briana.

To my surprise, she opted for the roll with the punches approach, instead.

"Your girl played me," she said with a little smile.

"She does that," I said. "Just when you think the whole hand has been dealt, she pulls a couple of aces out of her sleeve."

"Some would call that cheating."

"In cards, sure. But here, we call it veteran savvy."

She nodded and smiled some more.

"I'm gonna be coming for her," she challenged.

"I know. I'm sure she knows it, too. But don't be surprised to find more of the same waiting for you when you get there."

"Because she does that."

"Mmm, hmm."

Another short silence followed. Both of us feeling the requisite pride and awe at the things Danni could do, mixed with the thorough aggravation that comes from being one of the people she's done it to.

It was a nice moment that seemed to inch us closer together. Which meant it was time for me to get out of there before I screwed it all up.

But first, I had a suspicion I needed to confirm.

"What was Danni so pissed about at the end?" I asked.

Bush's head dropped to her chest. Because she'd done a bad thing, even if it was also the right thing.

"We had a deal," she said. "One I almost broke."

"Let me guess... if a submission got locked in, the other fighter was supposed to give it up."

Sophia nodded. I did, too. It was an agreement that covered the worst-case scenario. Because battling it out with your friend was fine, and beating the hell out of each other was par for the course. But tearing up joints risked permanent injury and threatened careers. It's not something any decent fighter would relish, but it's also not something you're trained to give much thought to. Your job is to win the fight. Your opponent's health and well-being is her own responsibility.

Tonight was a different story though, and Sophia knew she was out of line.

"I got caught up in it," she admitted. "I really wanted the win, but if I made her..."

She shook her head slowly as she let the thought fade away. Neither of us willing to voice how big a disaster an ending like that would have been.

She gave me a look, and I answered it with a little nod. One that said I got it, and also told her to let it go. It's a violent sport, full of all kinds of heartbreak. No sense spending time on catastrophes that could have happened, but didn't.

There was one thing my expression didn't say. That I doubted Danni would have been able to do it. Not to Sophia. She'd have taken the loss before going that far. An act that would have violated the unwritten rule about always following through and never stopping until the ref says it's over. Which would have been a whole different kind of disaster once the media got involved.

I pushed the thought away because again, there was no point in thinking about the ramifications of what didn't happen. And with my theory validated, I started to make my exit.

"I know it doesn't feel like it, but you had a good night tonight," I said. "Heal up and get ready. You've got a lot of work in front of you, now."

She nodded quietly as I turned to go, but then it turned out she had a little more.

"I guess I learned something tonight, too," she said with a wry smile.

"Oh?"

"You're clearly not as big a piece of shit as I thought."

"Thanks... I think."

I left it at that and continued on my way. We had cracked the ice a little. No need to thaw the whole mess in one day.
