Adelaide Entertainment Centre, Adelaide, Australia

Danni was totally prepared. I was certain of it, all the way to my core. Our sessions had been sharp, and she seemed totally at ease. Steph, Mandy, Emma... I had grilled anyone who would have any insight at all into potential problems. Everyone agreed. Danni was ready.

So why was I tied up in knots?

Because this was MMA, and nothing is ever for certain. Especially against the likes of Julie Benz.

So, yeah... I was worried. But if those same prefight nerves were eating at Danni, she did a great job of hiding it.

Things are always crazy at an event, and Team is among the craziest. So, we didn't get much chance to talk, but I would catch sight of her here and there. Each time, she looked perfectly normal. Despite the fact that I try to pitch in and lighten the load when she's on the card, she just can't seem to slip out of stable leader mode. And that's what I saw. Her, focused on her teammates, with no signs of distraction.

As fight time drew near, she disappeared into the bowels of the trainer's area to get ready. I went from room-to-room until I found her, looking cool as a cucumber.

"How you feeling?" I asked.

"Fine," she answered, "but you're clearly a little worked up."

"What makes you say that?"

"Well, you felt the need to come check on me, for the first time ever."

Looking relaxed and giving me shit. Clearly, her confidence was high.

"I decided to make it a new tradition."

"Uh, huh."

"Don't make me slap you, OK?"

She smiled, I returned it, and I felt the bands constricting my chest release. Chance was chance. Random by definition. There was no point letting it torment me because you can only control so much, and we had done all we could do. Danni was as ready as she could be.

So, how did the fight go? Well, if your name happens to be Julie Benz, not so great.

I mean, she tried. She really did. There just wasn't anything she could do. Right out of the gate, the blonde surged forward behind her jab. Looking to take the fight right to Danni and force her to immediately answer the questions that had been swirling around her. That answer came in the form of a neat slip outside when Benz finally uncorked her right hand, then a vicious knee that drove Julie's chin straight up.

And that might have been the best moment Benz had all fight.

Despite the wicked impact, Julie impressed me by keeping the offense up - immediately countering with a combo designed to drive Danni away. The problem? She hit nothing but air because Danni had already retreated out of range.

The blonde kept coming, stepping into a body kick that Danni easily blocked.

Then it was Ms. Harris's turn. Exploiting Julie's miss, she pressed forward behind a pair of combos that Benz managed to partially absorb on her guard. Danni finished with a middle kick. Benz saw it, and easily got her arm down to block it. Except...

Danni's foot landed with such force that an audible CRACK echoed through the arena. Julie's face contorted in pain, and I swear I thought her arm was broken. She pushed past the pain and threw her guard back up, but Danni had already shifted strategies. She stepped in close and hooked her arms under Julie's. Then she put that newfound strength Steph mentioned to work and just plain muscled Benz into the fence.

Watching Julie struggle in Danni's grasp, I almost felt sorry for her. She tried to push back but got nowhere. Striking wasn't an option with her arms tied up and Danni pressed in close. Her only choice was to wait and see what Danni had planned, then try to react. It was a crappy option, and it worked about as well as you'd expect.

After grappling her opponent into the position she wanted, Danni released and threw a short left hook. Benz couldn't even see it coming until it was too late, and the punch landed flush on her cheek. After that came a chain of strikes from the taller brunette, with Benz continuously a step behind getting her defense in place. A right hand shot straight into Julie's nose. Danni moved to throw the left again, Benz cowered behind her guard and Danni changed targets... pushing a perfect kick into Benz's chest and crushing her against the mesh. The blonde's guard came down and Danni pulverized her with a left hook that opened up a nasty cut above her right eye. Benz wobbled right. When she got herself steadied, Danni was there - latching in the underhooks and smashing her up against the fence, again.

There was no grappling for position this time. Danni immediately let loose with a series of knees that pounded Julie's ribs and belly. Desperate to make it stop, Benz brought her leg up and her elbow down to close the hole in her defense. No problem. Danni simply went to town on her now unprotected face.

Left hook-straight right-left hook.

All of them landed before Julie could even react. Blood streamed from her nose and a second cut had opened under her left eye. She hid behind her guard again, and Danni shifted her attention back down with a pair of hooks to the gut. It was so easy, she looked she was working the heavy bag back at the gym. Benz seemed to sense the desperate position she was in and launched a hell-or-high-water combo that was all power and zero technique.

Left-right-left-right-left-right-uppercut.

Nothing. Danni's defense was so perfect, it looked like she was reading her opponent's thoughts. And when she pulled her head out and let the uppercut sail by, everyone in the arena knew Julie's night was over.

Danni wove back in behind a vicious left hand, then squared up for the finale.

Right hook-left hook-right uppercut.

Goodnight, Julie.

The ref jumped in as Benz collapsed. Danni retreated to the opposite corner, making way as the medics poured in. Julie's team was next, with Steph and Marcy behind them. They went straight to Danni, and the three engaged in subdued conversation as a flurry of activity erupted around Benz. After an agonizing thirty seconds or so, Julie started to move. Shortly thereafter, the crowd around her thinned and she sat up. That's when the smiles broke out on the RSI side.

By the time the official announcement was made, I was waiting in the locker room. When Danni came in, her teammates mobbed her, as expected. Then they seemed to become aware that I was waiting and parted like the Red Sea.

I know I should have played it cool with everyone watching, but I couldn't help myself. I walked straight through the crowd and threw my arms around her, trapping her own arms at her sides.

She looked at me and smiled.

"Guess I did okay, huh?"

"Eh, not bad," I smiled back.

Exactly how good was she? Well, for those who lost count, allow me to summarize.

Danni hit Julie with everything but the kitchen sink. The official tally would show she scored with an impressive eighty percent of the strikes she threw. The price for exacting such brutality? None. Not even so much as a scratch. In a fight that lasted just over three and a half minutes, Benz was never able to beat Danni's defense and land a clean shot.

And that, ladies and gentlemen, is how you silence the doubters.

There was little time to savor the victory, though. With three teams comprised of a total of twenty-four fighters, we had a lot going on. And, when the hugs and high-fives were all over, it was time for one of our legendary debriefs.

How did they become legendary? Well, it all started with such a simple idea. A year or so ago, I decided to do away with the typical post-event article for these team events. With so many fighters in action, it just didn't make sense. Instead, I would hold a live round-table with Danni, Steph and the respective team leaders, then have the whole thing transcribed and let that serve as RSI's magazine contribution.

Genius, right?

Well, then the team leaders got to thinking they would each pick a Fighter of the Night and bring that individual to the session, too.

Fair enough.

Then they had trouble choosing, so they started bringing extra people.

Hey... hold on a second.

This time around... well, I had a plan before it started, anyway. Despite the fact that I couldn't stop thinking about it, I was determined not to focus on Danni's win. Her performance had been practically flawless, which, as far as I'm concerned, is exactly what she normally does. Maybe others had questions about her, but, here at RSI, we knew the real Danni and that's what we got tonight. Ho-hum. Par for the course. Next question, please. Anything more would only lend credence to the whispers.

Lucky for me, tonight had been a banner night for us. Britney and Elisha's groups had dropped only a few fights between them, while Aly's crew had cleaned house. So, I expected to have plenty of non-Danni topics to keep me busy.

Turns out, I got a lot more than I bargained for.

The problem? With so many great performances, my fearless leaders had trouble picking their top-performers.

The solution? They just brought everybody.

Three teams. Twenty-four total fighters. Plus me.

Oy vey.

It took us half an hour to find a room big enough to fit everybody. Once we got settled... well, with so many varied and outsized personalities, insanity was bound to ensue. But it was the little twist at the end that became the craziest moment of all.

Though Emma has long been the champion of her weight class, that particular group is viewed as somewhat weak, overall. While Victoria Justice and Xenia Deli are quickly proving they've got what it takes, experts and fans alike often view Emma as untested. Her teammates and I know better, but it's only the league-wide events like the upcoming tournament where she gets to prove it. To date, she has done very well... even advancing to the final of this tournament last year. But she has yet to win it all on the bigger stage.

I've never given that fact any thought before. It's not something to worry about because I know it's only a matter of time.

Apparently, Emma wanted that time to be now.

"I'll talk to her," I said.

I started toward the door, but Tiff stopped me.

"I think you kinda ticked her off," she said. "I might give her a little time."

I turned to her and let my confusion show through. The brief exchange at the end was nothing new for Emma and I. Had it really bothered her that much?

Before I had the chance to say anything, Danni stepped forward.

"I'll go," she said.

My initial instinct was to protest. I hadn't meant to insult Emma, and, if I had, I wanted to set it right. But it only took a moment to remind myself that Danni was as capable of handling the situation as I was. Maybe more so, if Emma was really angry with me.

I nodded, and Danni went after Emma. Meanwhile, I just stood there and stewed. Playing back the earlier conversation to see if I had said something truly stupid.

"She's not really mad," Tiffany said.

"Huh?"

"Emma," she said. "She's not really mad at you."

"Then, why..."

Tiff motioned with her head toward the windows that looked out onto the hallway. Danni had already caught up to Emma, and the two were involved in a serious-looking discussion.

I took a few seconds to process the situation.

"She wanted Danni?"

Tiffany nodded, and I spent another moment or two working things out.

Emma wanted Danni, specifically. Why?

Something I did? That didn't seem right, and Tiff just said she wasn't really angry.

Something about Danni, then. Some reason...

"You guys are up to something," I said as it started to come together.

"Who? Us?" she said too innocently.

I just kept my eyes on her and waited for more.

"We've all been talking," she began. "We enjoyed being able to return some of what Danni has given us these past few weeks. We really want to keep it going, but we know she's been sort of letting us get away with it. That she doesn't really like being the center of attention."

I nodded, though I wasn't sure it was entirely true. While it's true Danni hates attention, she definitely seemed to enjoy going at it with her more accomplished teammates.

"So, we're thinking maybe there's a better way," Tiff continued. "Maybe help her by letting her help us."

"And that's where Emma comes in."

"To some degree," Tiff said.

"And what does that mean?"

"It means Emma may be playing it up, but it's not really an act," she answered. "She wants to win this thing, and she thinks Danni can help."

There was no arguing that point. Getting a little extra education from Danni could be exactly what Emma needs to push her over the top. Especially considering how close she's already come.

That thought led me to muse about who it was that had ended her run last time. I didn't have to think hard or look very far.

"If she's going to win it, she'll likely have to go through you," I said.

The blonde shrugged.

"That's part of the deal," she said. "I stopped her last time. It wasn't fun, but it's what we agreed to. We knew these moments would happen."

Despite the fact that I knew what her answer would be, it was still hard for me to accept. It's hard enough to compete against a teammate, but to have to fight someone so close to you?

I shook my head. It makes me thankful that Danni and I never have to go beyond sparring.

"So, what's the plan?" I asked. "I mean, this gets Emma involved, but it sounds like all of you want to be a part of it."

"Hmm," she said suspiciously, "let's just see what develops."

The whole idea probably should have given me pause. After all, Emma wasn't the only one who needed a big performance at the upcoming tournament. Despite the dominant win over Benz, Danni still had things to prove. And the best of the best usually rise to the top in the tournament format, meaning she was likely to find herself in the cage with a number of dangerous opponents. Now, more than ever, she needed to buckle down and get to work. It was not the time to be distracted by fooling around with Emma and the others.

But the little voice inside me said I should let this play out. Danni's teammates knew the stakes as well as I did, and they would never do anything that might jeopardize her chances. And I could still use our evening sessions as a barometer. If it seemed she was losing her edge, I could always step in then.

Above all, there was something that felt right about putting Danni in the capable hands of the women she leads. They had done so much for her already, they wanted to do more, and they seemed to be getting something out of it themselves.

I couldn't imagine a better way for the stable to run.

"Let me know if I can help," I said.
