The team event had just ended, and I was hustling through the bowels of Allstate Arena on my way to the RSI locker room.

All in all, it had been a good event for us. All three teams had increased their point totals, and we had a number of big wins... including a tremendous performance by Britney Spears against the legendary Doutzen Kroes.

Unfortunately, none of that was on my mind, at that moment.

No one said a word to me as I made my way through the locker room to the medical evaluation area. Trish had just exited one of the rooms, and she stopped and waited when she saw me coming.

"How is she?" I asked.

"She's alright," Trish said. "Banged up and sore, but otherwise she's in decent shape."

That should have made me feel better. It didn't, and that fact must have been apparent on my face.

"Hey," Trish said as she laid a hand on my shoulder. "She's gonna be fine."

My eyes held on hers for a moment before I nodded gratefully.

She clapped my shoulder again before she turned to go.

With my hand on the doorknob, I took a deep breath and tried to relax. I could not get emotional once I opened this door. Contrary to popular belief, tough girls get to cry for all kinds of reasons. Someone taking a beating in a fight, however, is not among them.

No matter who that someone is.

Once I was sure I had myself together, I turned the knob and the door popped open.

Danni was sitting upright, with her legs dangling from the edge of the examination table. In her hand was an instant cold compress, which she held against her face in an attempt to reduce the swelling. Though the domination had been almost total, the fight had been mercifully light on blood, with Danni suffering only a few small cuts.

Still, the damage Candice Swanepoel had done was painfully obvious. Enough so that I had to fight off the urge to step outside and compose myself, again.

I let the door close behind me, and we looked at each other for a few beats before I spoke.

"Jesus," I said. "You look like you let somebody punch you in the face."

She smiled.

"I don't know about the letting part so much, but the result is the same."

"No, I was watching," I said. "You never even tried to stop her."

"Fuck off," she said plainly.

Neither snow, nor rain nor gloom of a one-sided beatdown stays Danni from being Danni.

In the room there was a high-backed circular stool that the medical personnel use during examinations. I grabbed it and straddled it backwards, my arms resting on the backrest.

"How you feeling?" I asked.

"Pissed off," she said.

I nodded. I knew exactly what she meant. I also knew it wasn't Candice she was pissed at.

"Shit happens," I said with a shrug.

"I know," she said.

That was the difference between Danni and Brittany. Danni had been doing this long enough that she'd seen it all. She knew there was no shame in losing to someone of Swanepoel's caliber. Sure, the fact that it had been a rout added a lot of sting to it, but she knew enough to just let it go. Tonight had not been her night. The only way to deal with it was to put it behind her and move on.

On the other hand, Danni and Brittany shared one personality trait I knew I would never break them of. In fact, it was a similarity shared by every fighter I'd ever known.

They would not quit, even when all reason dictated that they should.

It was that stubbornness that kept Danni standing for nearly two and a half rounds while Candice had her way with her.

It was also the reason I had silently chewed my lip until it bled as I watched.

Though I had given Brittany a little bit of a hard time about not knowing when to quit, I would not dare say such a thing to Danni. She knew me well enough to call my bluff. Even though she had never seen me fight, she would still know that I had been that way, too.

"Brittany didn't seem to be suffering any lingering effects from her last outing," Danni said, happy to steer the conversation away from herself.

Brittany had been in action again at this event... this time against a much more experienced opponent. The odds were definitely against her, but she didn't seem to care much. She had been cool and confident throughout the fight.

She had also been victorious.

"No, she looked just fine," I said.

"Guess whatever you said to her worked," Danni said.

"Maybe. Or maybe it was the training she received," I said, pushing the credit back in her direction.

"Maybe she's just good," Danni said.

"Maybe," I agreed.

"Did you talk to her, yet?"

"No," I answered. "You're the first person I've talked to since the event ended."

She looked at me silently as a smile spread across her swollen face.

"What?" I said.

"You were worried about me."

The way she said it and the wording she'd chosen made me wonder... just for a second... if she'd let herself lose deliberately to teach me a lesson. I set the ridiculous thought aside quickly, though. I've had my ass kicked many a time. Nobody lets that happen to them just to prove a point. On top of that, Danni's team had been counting on her tonight. So... no way.

"Nah," I answered. "I knew you'd be alright. You were just easier to find because I knew where you were."

It sounded like complete bullshit, even to me.

"You were worried about me," she said again.

"Fine," I sighed. "Maybe I was, you know... a little teeny bit worried about you."

Her smile broadened.

"Now, you know how it feels."

"You mean the fact that you getting your ass kicked means I need a sedative?" I asked. "Yeah, I know how that feels."

Truth is, I've long known what it feels like to worry about Danni. I had seen her fight before. I had even seen her lose. Though a rout like this was rare, the possibility is always there. And the worrying is always the same.

She nodded, satisfied that I had a proper understanding.

"So, what do I do about it?" I asked.

"Learn to live with it," she said.

I nodded.

"I guess I could do that."

I reached out and gave her hand a quick squeeze... only a minor violation of the "no emotions" rule... then stood to go.

"So, does this qualify me for the Richelle treatment?" she asked.

I arched an eyebrow at her.

"I'm sorry. The what now?"

"You know," she said. "That thing you do when you think someone's in trouble."

"Are you in trouble?" I asked.

"I don't think so," she said, "but you seem to be better at ferreting out those deep psychological issues. Maybe I have one of those."

I narrowed my eyes at her.

"Are you angling to get in the cage with me, Ms. Harris?"

"Not at all," she said in a serious tone.

I gave her a long, disbelieving look, then moved toward the door.

"I'm offering you the chance to get in the cage with me," she said to my back.

I felt a little tingle of excitement as I stopped and smiled without turning. Then, I shook my head and exited back into the locker room to make the rounds.

The first person I found was Brittany.

"Told you I was better than that," she said, referencing the disaster that was her previous fight.

"And I'm pretty sure I told you I already knew that," I said with a smile.

We chatted for a few minutes before I broke away and started to work my way out of the room. As I went, I offered a few words of praise to Jennifer Lawrence and Petra Cubonova for their solid victories. My last stop was a quick chat with Jennifer Stano, who had what I felt was a certain victory stolen away by the judges.

Then, it was off to the post event round table with team leaders Britney Spears, Elisha Cuthbert and Alyson Michalka. With them were the fighters they had chosen as the top performers for their respective teams. Britney had selected Emily VanCamp, Leesh had brought Julianne Hough and Aly had tapped the lovely, yet annoying, Ms. Emma Watson. That group, along with development coordinator Stephanie McMahon, were the perfect combination to get my mind off of everything else and keep me focused on the task at hand.

Meanwhile, all along, Danni's words were nibbling at the back of my brain.

It was two weeks later... late, after everyone else had gone... when I stepped into her office doorway, clad in black bike shorts and a red compression sports bra. On my hands were a pair of black, fingerless MMA gloves.

When she looked up at me, there was no mistaking the excitement in her eyes.

"If you need me, I'll be in the training room," I said. "Alone."

I didn't wait for her to respond. Just turned and walked away.

When she got there ten minutes later, I was waiting in one of the cages.

Her attire was identical, except that her top was black. As soon as she came through the cage door, I could see the look was still there.

"You sure you want to do this?" I asked.

"Never been more sure of anything in my life," she said.

"I don't think you really understand what you're getting yourself into."

"I'll take my chances."

I wiggled my fingers in the gloves, as I started to circle to my right.

"You know these ain't focus mitts I'm wearing, right?" I said.

"You know it's your face I'm going to be focusing on, right?" she called back as she began to circle, as well.

"Ohhh-ho-ho... bring it on, sweetie."

We closed on each other and... the rest shall remain a private matter between she and I.

I'll give you this much, though... the battle exceeded my expectations in almost every way. I mean, I knew Danni was good, but she was even better than that. I was thrilled just to keep up with her.

Though we went at it for the better part of two hours, neither of us had had our fill. So, we met again the following evening. And the evening after that.

Right now, I'm sitting at my desk with a million things to do. We have the Global Rivalry tournament coming up, and then all hell is going to break loose when the UMMA finalizes the franchise reorganization. And what am I doing? Sitting here, watching the clock, just waiting for the time to come when Danni and I can strap the gloves on and work the stress off of each other.

If this keeps up, I'm not going to have to worry about squeezing gym time into my schedule, anymore. Danni is all the workout I can handle.

Which of us is the better fighter, you ask? There is no consensus opinion on that, yet.

But the research continues.
