I jabbed at the heavy bag like I wanted to destroy it - snapping my left into it twice before putting my weight into the cross. The impact caused the bag to drift away, but it swayed back only a second later, ready for more.

I was only too happy to provide.

My goal is to prepare myself. While Danni's training may have been ineffective these past few months, mine was non-existent. That had to change. When she was ready to get back in the cage, I would be ready to play my part in rounding her game back into shape. That's why I'm here when everyone else has hit the showers.

I slipped an invisible right hand from my imaginary opponent, stuck the bag with my left, then hammered it with a right uppercut.

As the minutes ticked by, I slowly lost sight of my original objective. The more I thought of Danni, the more my mind drifted. Back to the argument. To the problems we needed to face and the new ones I had created. Mostly, I thought of the empty feeling I get as I walk past her darkened office each morning.

This is not the first time she hasn't been here. But it's never felt like this before.

Jab, Jab, Cross, Hook.

I was putting everything I had into every punch. Trying to crush my imaginary adversary.

The bag swung back and forth lazily. Indifferent to my anger and frustration. As if all my effort had amounted to nothing.

I should have been a little happier. At least, I wasn't still trapped in a flying metal tube. I knew from experience that the trip home from Australia would be a torturous experience. Nearly fifteen hours of flight time from Melbourne to Los Angeles, a two hour layover, then another five hours in the air before we got into Newark. A brutal journey, no matter the circumstances.

Without Danni, it felt like it had taken a week. And the worst part was I didn't care. Our fight in the training room had left me listless and unmoored. Floating aimlessly from one place to another. Trapped on a plane. Uselessly twiddling my thumbs in my office. It was all the same. Six days had passed since the event, and I couldn't tell you a damn thing I'd done. I paid little attention to where I was or what I was doing because none of it felt like it mattered.

Steph had tried to help, saying exactly what I'd said to Danni during the argument. That we had gotten into it at the wrong time. That we needed a chance to digest what had happened. Time would make a difference.

The trouble was we didn't wait. Despite my promises, I had let the conversation slide the wrong way, and now I couldn't shake the idea that it was too late. That, in the course of just a minute or two, I had done some sort of irreparable damage. Something time couldn't fix.

I felt that way mostly because I didn't understand what I had done. Yes, I'd said things I shouldn't have - especially at that moment. Yes, things had quickly gotten out of control. But that happens all the time with Danni and I. Both of us tend to be a little inflexible. We like to think of ourselves as principled and self-assured, but I suspect others might call us obstinate or mulish. Pigheaded, perhaps.

On top of that, we both tend to run a little hot. I annoy her. She annoys me. In no time at all, it's a verbal brawl.

Stubborn and ill-tempered. Always a good combination.

Jab, Cross, Left hook to the body, Right hook to the head.

Solid, satisfying thumps accompanied each impact. I was punching so hard, it made my knuckles hurt.

I liked the feeling.

Sweat poured down my face and neck, staining my clothing and making my shirt stick to my skin. I looked like I was melting.

I liked that feeling, too.

Regardless of what you call it, the personality conflict between Danni and I is more of a defining relationship characteristic than a serious problem. We knock heads all the time, but we do it with the knowledge that it would pass. Whatever the topic, we would work it out. We just did it in our own way.

This time was different. Normally, I know when I've said something that pissed her off. Hell, I usually say those things deliberately, just to get a rise out of her. But I didn't know what I'd done to make her shut down the way she had. To make her send me away.

Yes, I had said stupid things. Things I regretted almost immediately. But, let's face it... that's also something that happens all the time. It's an established process. First, I say it. Then, I regret it. Finally, I take it back while admitting how stupid I was. It's not really that complicated.

Nothing I had said this time was any worse than my norm, yet I was left with the feeling that something else was going on. That some emotional undercurrent had been flowing for a long time without my realizing it. The events in Melbourne had somehow broken through and brought that flow to the surface, but it had also washed me out of the room before I had a chance to understand where it had come from.

I've tried to find out. Danni and I have spoken every day since but she won't let me steer the conversation in that direction. Redirects and deflections abound. I've been kept well-informed of every weather front that passed through the region of Oceania, but I have no idea why my best friend is so upset with me.

So, no... it doesn't feel like we're going to work this one out.

Jab, Jab, Cross, Hook kick.

The bag swung hard and the chain rattled loudly against the anchor. Then it swung back. Mocking me.

While I was busy being sad, angry and useless, the cogs and wheels of RSI kept turning. Danni's absence left a big hole, but the group was managing. Steph has assumed full control of the day to day training activities, and she's been getting plenty of help. Success, I've discovered, has a snowball effect. Julianne Hough. Emily VanCamp. Emma, Britney, Daniela Hantuchova... every weight class has at least one leader. A decorated fighter with skills to share and the cachet to make her less experienced teammates pay attention. Our training structure has long made use of these women, but being without Danni has forced Steph to really lean on them. To no one's surprise, they've risen to the occasion.

On top of that, the plan that Danni and Steph had come up with for dealing with the rookies was beginning to take shape. It's too early to tell how well it will work, but, for now, my hopes are still high.

All of this made me proud, but it didn't make me happy. My organization was without it's most important piece, and yet it kept churning away. But all I could think about was that missing piece.

Jab, Cross to the body, Left hook to the head, Right hook to the head.

Murderous strikes, powered by anger and frustration. Blows that could fell a tree. But the bag was steadfast. Affected by neither my blows nor my emotional state.

A voice cut into my thoughts.

"You're carrying your right a little low."

I thumped the bag one more time, then turned and found Emma and her trademark smirk looking back at me. She was wearing a pair of black, flared yoga pants, and the strap of a red duffel bag cut across a loose fitting grey t-shirt. The top was clean and free of sweat stains, and her still wet hair was gathered in a loose, low ponytail behind the nape of her neck. She had obviously come fresh from her post-workout shower.

Em had been notably quiet since the event. Though she hadn't been with us for the trip, I was certain she'd heard every detail of what happened. Yet, she'd been smart. Smarter than I'd been with Danni. She'd given me my space and let me work my emotions out alone for a while. Giving me time.

No doubt, she was here now because that time was up. And, in truth, talking things out with someone would probably be good for me. I've been walking around here for a week, totally disconnected from everything. And the person who normally works me through moments like that was currently unavailable because she was on the other side of the globe, refusing to talk to me.

In the meantime, it was clear I needed help getting my shit together, and Emma could undoubtedly provide it. All I had to do was let her.

I went back to hitting the bag.

"You need something?" I asked between combinations.

"Nope," she said. "I'm just really interested in seeing how this battle turns out. In case you're wondering, my money's on the bag."

I kept punching and didn't say anything.

"How do you win, anyway? Do you have to actually knock it down?" she asked.

I still didn't answer.

"I don't know," she mused as she studied the bag's setup. "Anchor's pretty thick. Chain is, too."

She rubbed her chin. Pondering the problem.

"You keep it up long enough, maybe you can get the bolts to pull out of the wall. Something tells me they planned for that when they put it in, though."

I concentrated on the bag, proceeding through a series of increasingly difficult combinations. Emma stood quietly and watched me.

"Is this some sort of experiment?" she asked after a minute or so. "Are you trying to figure out how long you'll have to ignore me to make me go away? Because, trust me, you're not gonna like the answer."

I sighed deeply, put my hands on the bag to still it, then spoke without looking at her.

"What do you want, Em?"

We both knew the answer to that, so she didn't waste time trying to find a casual way to start the conversation.

"You can't keep going like this."

"I'm doing just fine, thanks."

"No, you're not. You're sulking. You're totally withdrawn. You haven't been on the training floor all week."

"I've been keeping tabs on things."

"Keeping tabs?" she scoffed. "A week ago, you were in it up to your neck, and you loved it. Now, you're keeping tabs?"

"I've had some things on my mind."

"Yeah, and we both know what those things are."

I raised my head and turned to face her.

"I don't want to talk about this."

"I don't really care what you want," she said evenly.

I sighed again. Apparently, I wasn't going to have much of a say in whether or not this conversation happened. I could hit her, but that seemed rash even for me. I guess I could storm out, but she'd probably just follow me. Hell, knowing Emma, there was a damn good chance she'd chase me all the way home.

My options exhausted, I nodded toward the bag.

"If you insist on staying, you can at least make yourself useful."

The little smirk reappeared as she recognized the statement for what it was. I wasn't going to make it easy on her, but I had opened the door just a crack.

She looped the strap over her head and set her duffel on the floor. Then she slipped in behind to steady the bag as I went back to trying to beat the stuffing out of it.

Another minute passed, then she asked, "Have you talked to Danni?"

"Every day," I said curtly.

A look of disappointment crossed her face. I was mentally gearing up for the impending inquisition, so I couldn't figure out where the look was coming from. Why would she be disappointed that I'd talked to Danni?

She wiped it away before I could ask.

"Any progress?" she asked.

"Nope."

"Nothing?"

"She always has plenty to say, but nothing on the topic you're currently sticking your nose into."

If the remark offended her, it didn't show.

"You need to deal with this," she said.

"That's why you're here?" I asked. "To help me deal?"

"Somebody has to," she said.

"No, they don't. I'm fine."

"No, you're not."

"God dammit!" I shouted as I came upright. "You see me here, hitting this bag? This is how I deal with things, OK? Now, either go away, or shut up and hold the bag."

She didn't respond and she didn't leave. I took that to mean she'd selected the second option, so I went back to my workout.

More time went by. The only sound in the room was my fists smacking the bag.

I finished a quick combination, then pulled back to guard and reset my positioning.

"One, two," Emma called out.

I came out of my stance and looked at her, puzzled.

"You deaf?" she said. "I said, 'one, two.'"

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Helping you deal."

We looked at each other, both of us holding steadily on the other.

Then, I brought my guard up and worked myself back into rhythm.

"One, two," she said again.

I popped the bag with my jab, then thumped it solidly with a right cross.

"One, one, two."

I doubled-up the jab, then stabbed the bag with the cross, again.

"One, two, one, two."

Jab, Cross, Jab, Cross.

"One, two, three."

Jab, Cross, Hook.

We went like that for a few minutes, with Emma putting me through my paces with basic combinations. Then, she turned it up a notch.

"Five, two, one, two."

Uppercut, Cross, Jab, Cross.

Tricky to throw, but harder to defend. In this one, the uppercut - frequently a power punch - is relatively light. What Steph calls a tapping uppercut.  It's designed more to confuse and score points than to do damage. It's the rights that do the heavy work here, while the left-hand punches are meant to keep an opponent busy. But it only works if it's fast. Confuse her with the uppercut, then catch her with the cross, get her guard moving with the jab, then drive the second cross right through her head. It takes a lot of hand speed and attention to body positioning to pull it off.

I thought I nailed it. Emma wasn't impressed.

"Faster," she said.

I threw it again, pushing my hands harder.

"C'mon... faster!"

I backed off and gave her a hard look without breaking rhythm.

She smiled at me, took her right hand off the bag and challenged me with a little bring it on  gesture.

Little bitch.

I stepped back in and ripped through the combination, again. The impacts echoed through the room.

Tap, smack, tap, BOOM!

"Again, like that."

Tap, smack, tap, BOOM!

"There you go! One, two, three, two!"

Tap, smack, BOOM, BOOM!

"C'mon! Let your hands go! One, four-b, three-b, four!

The RSI combo list uses the b to denote a body shot. A lesson I once learned the hard way.

Tap, thump, thump, BOOM!

Upstairs, downstairs, upstairs. I did what she asked... concentrating on the numbers and the footwork and letting my hands fly free.

Emma let the combos fly, too. She even started moving the BOOM  around a little. Hiding the power punches in the middle.

"One, two, five, two!"

Jab, Cross, Uppercut, Cross.

Tap, smack, BOOM, smack.

She called them out faster, pressuring me to keep up. The rhythmic sound of my fists on the bag became a form of music. Sweat flowed down my neck in torrents and droplets cast off the stringy ends of my hair like rain.

I was gonna have to burn this shirt, but I didn't care. I wasn't gonna let her beat me.

The combos got longer and harder.

"Two, one, one, six, one."

Speed to power, with a little bonus on the end. Mix things up by leading with the right, then pop the jab twice. Pesky punches that aggravate an opponent and make her tighten her guard. Like she was trying to shield herself from a cloud of gnats. Then things slow down as you shift right and whack her with the uppercut. She thinks it's over and - if she tries to counter - ends up eating the jab at the end.

Smack, tap-tap, BOOM, tap

"One, six, five, two!"

Uppercuts to bring the guard all the way down and open up a window for the right. Power needed all the way through, and perfect positioning to get behind the final punch. Hard. But, if you nail, the fight is probably over.

Tap, bang, bang...

I had it. I could feel it. The perfect harmony as my left came back and my weight flowed into the cross. Emma saw it, too. She hugged the bag tight and braced it with her shoulder.

BOOM!

Still, she didn't stop. Every time I finished a sequence, she called out another. I kept up for a while - my arms and legs as heavy as lead - but things quickly reached a comical level. I was windmilling like a cartoon character as it all became one long string of hurried and poorly thrown punches, with both of us struggling not to laugh.

"Oh, ENOUGH!" I finally yelled.

My hands dropped to my sides all by themselves, and I couldn't raise them back up. They pulled my shoulders down behind them, and I ended up stooped over with my arms dangling freely. Like I was imitating a gorilla.

"That was some of the worst form I've ever seen," a voice behind me said.

I turned and found Steph, with Emma's training - and romantic - partner Tiffany Mulheron standing beside her.

"Sorry," I panted. Then, I turned back to Emma and added, "I lost my focus because I was being tortured."

She gave me the smirk again, with a knowing eyebrow waggle to go with it.

I laughed and shook my head, then collapsed against the bag and let it hold me up for a while. I was utterly exhausted, but it felt good. Like I had somehow punched out my stress. And the way Emma kept pushing me had actually been fun.

It took a little bit of thinking for me to recognize the way she'd also pushed my buttons. She had worked me over as thoroughly as I'd worked the bag. Sure, it was temporary. But I felt better. Like maybe talking wouldn't be such a bad idea.

Her technique had been... well, Danni-like. She hadn't forced me. She had handled me. Working with what I gave her, yet somehow getting me right where she wanted me.

That made me wonder what she wanted to do with me now that I was here.

I turned and looked at the newest arrivals, then sniffed the air suspiciously.

"I smell an intervention," I said.

"More like a preventative measure," Steph said.

"Oh, yeah? And what are we preventing?"

"Me from having to slap you upside the head if you don't pull it together."

I nodded agreeably. That sounded like something that should be prevented.

"So, how does this work?" I asked. "Do we need to get a chair so I can sit in the middle while you take turns yelling at me, or what?"

"You can start by telling us what's going on," Steph said.

"You know what happened," I said to her. "You were there."

"But we don't know what's happened since then."

"Nothing's happened since then," I said darkly. "Absolutely nothing."

I was talking about things between Danni and I. Accounting for the lack of progress made on fixing what had gone wrong.

Turns out, I was missing the more obvious meaning.

"I think what Steph is saying," Tiffany began, "is that we have questions. We're worried."

"And we can start with how Danni is doing," Emma added. "Is she getting better?"

My mouth opened, then closed without my saying a word.

Now I knew where Emma's earlier disappointment had come from.

Though my daily phone calls with Danni - not to mention the multiple conversations a day I'd been having with Trish - had produced precious little regarding the argument, my information on Danni's condition was complete and just about up-to-the-minute.

Around me stood three people who cared a great deal about her, and I hadn't shared any of what I knew with them... or with anyone.

The realization made me angry with myself. Disgusted with my selfishness. But I was careful not to misdirect that emotion.

"How'd you let me get away with that?" I asked Steph.

She shrugged and made a face. Like maybe she hadn't let me so much as others had persuaded her to show restraint.

"I was giving you time," she said, "but, like I said, we're getting close to something bad happening."

I nodded again. She'd have been right to slap me.

"I need to clean up," I said. "You can either wait, or we can do this first thing tomorrow."

"We'll wait," Steph said, with a little edge that warned me she'd already waited long enough.

"Ten minutes," I said. "I'll tell you everything I know."

She nodded, clearly appreciating the apology in my tone. I had recognized my mistake, and I would do whatever I could to make up for it.

I looked at Emma.

"You gonna come with me and help me lift my arms?"

"Uh... nope."

"Better make it twenty," I said to Steph.

It took nearly thirty. I needed ten just to get the shampoo bottle open.

The three were already arranged in my office when I returned. Emma and Tiff occupying the guest chairs, with Steph leaning against the bookcase that covers the back wall. She does that even when there's seating available. I've given up on trying to figure out why.

"First of all, I'm sorry," I said as I settled onto the front edge of my desk. "This has been tough on me, and I appreciate the way you've all given me time to work through it. But there's no excuse for not keeping everyone informed on Danni's condition."

I went from face to face. Everyone seemed to accept my apology.

"The good news is that everything is going much better than expected," I continued. "Danni's scans all came back normal, and her ImPACT results were markedly better when Trish did the follow-ups."

"Thank god," Steph said as they all breathed a collective sigh of relief.

"Trish was wrong?" Tiff asked.

"Trish was overly cautious," I said. I gestured toward Steph and added, "She told us as much that night. She felt there was cause for concern, so she was taking no chances. Given the way the fight went, there's no arguing she made the right decision."

"Danni's just tougher than Trish thought," Emma said.

I smiled at her.

As practitioners of a sport where heavy contact is the goal, we're all well-versed in the evolving science surrounding concussions. We know toughness has nothing to do with how a concussion affects you. There's no way to power through. You can only be stupid, ignore the symptoms and risk severe and permanent brain injury. But I liked the sense of unconditional support Em was trying to send.

"So, she's OK?" Steph asked.

"She's not perfect, no. There's no doubt she suffered a concussion. She's had symptoms. Mostly headaches and the like. But those have been improving, and the cognitive results are excellent."

"They'll be heading home soon, then?" Tiff asked.

I checked my watch.

"They're already in the air," I said. "In fact, they'll be in LA any minute now. The connecting flight should take off in about two hours."

The three of them gave me a collective look that asked how the hell I hadn't told them this sooner.

"I know," I said. "I fucked up. Like I said, there's no excuse. I'd go back and handle it the right way if I could, but I can't."

They nodded. My failure - and accompanying remorse - were probably easier to accept because of the positive news on Danni's condition.

The group grew quiet. As the seconds ticked by, that silence became tinged with unease. We had arrived at the point where the conversation was going to tip from the things I should have been telling them all along to things that really weren't any of their business. Emma, in particular, seemed to be studiously measuring her approach. Trying to gauge how long she should wait before wading into the personal side.

After all the effort she'd put into softening me up, it seemed wrong to deny her.

"Em, you look like you have a question," I said.

She spread her hands and said, "You already know what I'm going to ask."

"I do," I said, "and I'm sorry to say that progress on that front has not been nearly as rosy. As I told you earlier, Danni hasn't been willing to discuss what happened. She hasn't been nasty. In fact, I'd call our conversations cool, but cordial. But she won't let the topic come up."

"Did you try pushing her?" Emma asked.

"Sure, but I was hesitant to push too hard. First, because it seemed particularly selfish to force her to deal with this while she was stuck in a dark room, literally on the other side of the planet. Second, if another argument is coming - and I think we can all agree that it is - I didn't want to do it over the phone. I don't have a real good feeling about how this is going to turn out, but I like the odds a little better if we're in the same room."

No one looked too happy with these revelations, but Emma tried to focus on the bright side.

"Well, you're talking," she said. "That's a good sign."

"I don't know," I said. "It... I just don't know."

"What were you going to say?" she asked.

I sighed. I thought it was apparent I'd stopped myself for a reason. Still, there didn't seem to be much point in refusing her. She would only find another way.

"It feels lost," I admitted. "The way it happened... the way it ended... I don't know what I'd say to her if she was standing right here."

Emma waved my doubts away.

"You're filling in blanks," she said. "Assuming things about what Danni's thinking."

"Nothing's ever clear in an argument like that."

"I hope you're right."

I lifted my eyes to Steph. Had she provided a written transcript?

She ignored me, but Emma fielded the question on my face.

"I have my ways," she said. "You know that."

I looked to Steph again and got a little what are you gonna do? shrug in return.

Fair enough.

That did not sit well and I watched a whole range of emotions cross Emma's face in a ten-second interval. Like there were a million things she wanted to say, but couldn't figure out where to start. The most interesting one made it look like she might reach out and wring my neck, but she smoothed it out quickly.

That had to mean something, didn't it?

"It's a long flight, so... maybe tomorrow, but probably the day after."

"Don't make me come in here and drag you out," she said.

"Oh, I'd love to see you try."

"No," she said steadily, "you wouldn't."
