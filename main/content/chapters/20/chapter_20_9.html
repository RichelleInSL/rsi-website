While the Battle of One Tree Hill was gathering momentum off-stage, we had the BTB event to get through.

What's BTB, you ask? It stands for Battle Tested Brawlers.

Don't look at me. I don't make up the names. I can only tell you how we got here.

It seems the concept of a celebrity fighting league draws a lot of interest, and the league has grown exponentially over the years. From a few dozen fighters in the beginning to a cast of nearly a thousand today. Organizers quickly realized you can't have that many fighters competing for little more than a handful of titles, so a solution was proposed. Instead of one massive league, the roster would be divided into four "franchises," each with it's own belt for every weight class.

The hard part? Figuring out exactly how to split things up.

The first approach they tried was to group the fighters by experience level. An idea that made perfect sense on the surface, but the numbers didn't work out because there weren't enough veterans at the time. Only those dozens who signed on early on would have qualified for the more advanced levels, while nearly eighty percent of the roster would have fallen into the lowest group.

The next idea was to group the fighters by age, and it quickly became clear that would be a better match for the complexion of the league. The early sign ons weren't too heavily weighted toward any single age category, and the league continued to draw interest from across the generational spectrum as it grew. Management knew periodic reorganizations would be needed to keep the rosters balanced, but this solution was as good as things were likely to get.

And so the plan was set. Four groups of similarly aged fighters, with each group battling it out for their own, separate championships. Mix in the occasional league-wide event - where the best of each franchise come out and compete with one another for even more hardware - and you have yourself a much more manageable situation that's even more exciting than the original.

Now... what to call these new franchises?

At first, there were only numbers. Franchise 1, franchise 2... you get the idea. Clear, simple and admittedly boring. So, when it came time for the first of those periodic reorgs, somebody decided it would be better to give the franchises names. And what they came up with... well, I just don't know.

Franchise 1 became Youth Gone Wild, commonly abbreviated as YGW. As the name suggests, this group consists of the league's youngest fighters, but don't let young be confused with inexperienced. This group actually features some of the best the league has to offer, including RSI's Emma Watson and Jen Lawrence. Of course, there's plenty of newer talent too, such as the promising young rookie crew of Olivia Holt, Dove Cameron, Zendaya Coleman and Kat McNamara.

It's the same story with the other groups. Our Franchise 2 participants - who were now known as Battle Tested Brawlers - run the gamut from the up-and-coming Anna Kendrick and scrapper extraordinaire Ashley Tisdale, up to long-time champions Julianne Hough, Daniela Hantuchova, Tiffany Mulheron and Emily VanCamp. Franchise 3 was recrowned as Prime Time Pugilists, and features the likes of Cobie Smulders, Jennifer Morrison, Elisha Cuthbert, Britney Spears, Petra Cubonova, and, of course, the lovely and talented Danneel Harris. And the oldest group is now known as The Golden Gladiators, and includes the likes of Torrie Wilson, Stephanie McMahon and Catherine Bell.

Are these names an improvement over the purely functional number approach? I suppose they are, but that's not the same as saying they're good.

Anyway... before we could focus our attention on Danni and the Pugilists, we had to make sure the Brawlers were ready for... Battle?

OK... that's enough of that.

The point is that, as always, we had a lot going on. And, as always, our hard work showed on fight night.

Breaking Down The Defense

That recent franchise reorganization I mentioned has made life hard on Julianne Hough. She's used to working fast and furious when she's in the cage, and her brutal battles with Vikki Blows fit that mold perfectly. In seven fights with her longtime foe, only two ever made it out of the first round. So, win or lose, Jules could always be assured of a short night.

Nowadays, it's nothing but work, work, work.

The reorg brought Jules a new kind of trouble. One that took the form of the vastly talented Keira Knightley. And Keira... well, she likes to take her time. With a defense that protects her like a stone wall, she prefers to chip away at her opponents - frustrating them into making mistakes. It's a technique that few can manage, but Keira makes it work with ease. That fact is especially surprising, given the skill level of the fighters she usually has to face.

But Jules... she doesn't make a lot of mistakes, and she has quite the defensive skillset of her own. And so far, so good. The two have met twice since the realignment, and Hough has managed to hang onto the title despite the fact that Keira has refused to give an inch. But those wins were situations where neither fighter could really get anything going, and the judges decided that Jules had done just enough to get the win. With the difference coming down to the thinnest of margins, arguments persisted about which was truly the better fighter.

So, the question tonight was, would one of these women finally exert her will upon the other?

Well, I don't know about exerting her will, but Jules sure put a dent in Keira's defense this time around.

The first round went the way they all have. A whole lot of patient plodding as the two searched for a way to do some damage. There were a few offensive outbursts, but mostly just a lot of clinching and shoving as the two jockeyed for position.

Ditto for the second. And then again in the third. We were well on our way to yet another close decision that would settle nothing.

The fourth round saw a new wrinkle. Jules started to gain control behind a precision striking attack. Keira stayed with her, but you could visibly see the scales tipping in the RSI fighter's direction. Suddenly, she was landing more often and doing more damage. Then, just when it looked like Hough might really open things up, Keira went low and dragged her to the mat.

It was a veteran move by one of the cagiest veteran's in the league. Things weren't going her way when they were standing, so she switched things up to try and find a different path to victory. When you posses the extensive skills that Keira does, all avenues are open to you. But Jules is no slouch on the canvas. She hasn't shown the ability to produce a lot of offense down there, but she can handle herself pretty well. And she did a fine job of keeping Keira at bay long enough to get to the end of the round.

Still, the move had paid dividends for Knightley. Not only had she ended the rally Jules had going, but groundwork takes a lot out of you. Had she managed to take some starch out of the champion?

Uhm... nope.

I don't know if the takedown pissed her off or if she was just sick of playing games, but Julianne was set on full attack mode when the final round began. A few seconds of shuffling ended abruptly when Hough scored with a vicious hook as she closed the distance. The uppercut that followed landed flush under Keira's chin. Suddenly, the older fighter was dazed and bleeding, and Hough wasted no time taking advantage. Grabbing her opponent with both hands, she jammed her up against the fence. A barrage of punches, kicks and knees followed. There was no letup in the assault. Keira managed to get off the cage, but another uppercut from Jules knocked her completely off her feet. Knightley bought some time by crab-walking around to keep Jules off of her, but she wasn't going to win the fight that way. When she finally climbed back to her feet, Julianne was there to put the pressure on again. Keira danced. She slid. She countered. In all honesty, Knightley did all she could. But there was no stopping Jules.

Even with a full round to work with, Julianne couldn't get the finish. Keira may have been wounded, but she fought it out to the bitter end like the warrior she is. Still, there was no doubt about this one, and both fighters knew it before the decision was made official.

Afterward, Jules was asked what inspired that final round performance. The answer was one I definitely should have expected.

"Steph", was her simple and oh-so-obvious response. "I was all pissed off after the way the fourth round went. I expected something new during the break, but Steph just said keep up the striking attack in the final round. In fact, she said I needed to turn it up a notch."

That, of course, led to questions for Ms. McMahon, who had no trouble providing insight into her decision.

"Keira got the takedown in the second, and that threw things off," she said. "But, before that, it was working. Sometimes, you just have it, and I could see Julianne's accuracy was way up tonight. She was hitting everything right on the button, and Keira was having a lot of trouble with it. It was a great move by her to force the takedown, but that didn't change anything for me. The strikes were bothering her, and I just wanted Jules to keep it up."

It was quite a gutsy decision, when you really think about it. It was that final round that sealed this one for Jules, but if Knightley had gotten another takedown things easily could have gone the other way. Did Hough have any doubts about Steph's approach?

"Nope," she said plainly. "Steph is there for a reason. I have enough to think about during a fight. She's there to set the strategy, and I'm going to listen to what she says. If she's ever wrong, then that's how it is. I'll go down with that ship every time because she's come through too many times in the past for me to second guess her."

Just listen to Steph. We should have that printed on a t-shirt. Her counsel has become invaluable to the fighters of RSI, and now it's given Jules her first no-doubt-about-it win of this rivalry. We shall see what Keira counters with next time out.

Rivalry Cut Down

Another event. Another stupid injury. This time, the fighters involved where Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik. But hey... nobody wanted to see those two fight anyway, right?

Yeah... right.

By now, the story is legend. Irina was the perennial champion... seemingly on a level of her own. Then Emily stepped up and not only took the title away, but also established that she could go toe-to-toe with the vaunted Russian superstar whenever and wherever. That was five years ago, and what's happened since then is the kind of stuff fans dream about. Back and forth the two went, trading the title nearly every time they met. As a result, their rivalry became one of the most popular in league history. Each woman developed her own legion of followers, and even fans who didn't have a favorite were compelled to tune in.

More recently, though, Emily has been on a bit of a tear. When she won back the title a few Marches ago, the loss dropped Irina out of the top contender slot. Making room for someone else to get a shot for the first time in forever. The lucky candidate? Ashley Greene. But her first attempt left her disappointed, as Emily finally mounted her first successful title defense when they met that July. Greene used the wide-open format of Open Fight Night to take another crack at the champion in November, but Em beat her back once more. Along came March again, and it was Sheik back in the top spot. But Emily was on a roll, and even Irina's elite skills weren't enough to slow her down.

So, everything was coming up roses for RSI's sweetheart as the Winter Tournament rolled around. Not only had she won three championship matches in a row, but her success had pushed her to the very top of the UMMA rankings. The way she'd been fighting, there was no doubt she'd make a deep push in the tournament. Most were expecting her to win the whole damn thing. But waiting for her at the end was yet another new challenge, this one coming in the form of the talented Ali Larter.

Though Larter was the underdog, she was very much considered to be a threat. Then the fight started off in the worst way possible. Ali connected with a massive shot on just the second exchange, and Em stumbled badly. Though she quickly managed to right the ship, damage was clearly done. Things evened out as the two settled in, but Larter continued to get just a little more in each exchange. She maintained that edge through the opening round. A buzz spread through the crowd. An upset was in the making.

Emily refused to play along with that script, and round two saw her take over the fight. There was nothing fancy about the way she did it. Larter couldn't keep up the blistering pace she'd set early, while VanCamp only seemed to be getting stronger. That trend only accelerated in the third, as Em really started to knock her opponent around. Soon the fight had gotten away from Larter completely. Desperation set in, and she started swinging for the fences.

And somehow, she connected.

For the second time in the fight, Ali landed a thunderous hook and this one left Emily badly hurt. I thought she was out. At cageside, the usually unreadable Steph was obviously worried. The crowd went crazy. Not only was the upset back on the table, but it looked like it might already be over.

Yet somehow, Em survived.

For nearly three whole minutes, she stayed alive despite some serious cobwebs and made it to the break. To say it was a testament to her toughness is an epic understatement. It would more appropriate to say it was a miracle, except that no divine intervention seemed to be involved. Instead, Emily made her own miracle. Lumbering around the cage with only half her cylinders firing, yet repeatedly finding ways to keep Larter from getting the finish.

Survival mode continued into round four, but with a better plan, this time. Relying heavily on her well-known Muay Thai skills, she found ways to not only buy time but also frustrate her opponent into mistakes. By the halfway, she was back in the driver's seat. Reasserting control like that whole scene we all saw where she was damn near knocked into the middle of next week never even happened. And a few minutes later, it was Larter who was unconscious. Dropped like a ragdoll by one of Emily's famous high kicks.

I have to be honest... I know I'm not doing this whole thing justice because words cannot adequately describe what happened that night. Even seeing was not believing, as none of the so-called experts seemed sure of what happened. Most went with the idea that Emily might be superhuman because that was easier to sell, but a fair number believed they had seen the beginning of her end. That she'd relied on an insane amount of luck to defeat an opponent she should have beaten handily. But all agreed on one thing. Emily's upcoming rematch with the greatest nemesis she'd ever known was going to be a must-watch event.

So, here we were... VanCamp and Sheik going at it again. With all kinds of questions to answer and things to prove. The entire building held its breath as the bell sounded to get this one started.

Fourty-four seconds later, it all ground to a disastrous halt. Because Irina had a bloody gash on her nose.

I really want to say that it didn't matter. That Emily had burst out of the gate and taken it to the challenger. That the stoppage was a merciful ending to a one-sided fight that Em was clearly winning. But none of that was the case. Fourty-four seconds? The women had barely broken a sweat. Em had simply caught Irina with a punch that landed funny. Instead of an impact, it was more of a swipe. Maybe a fingernail went awry. Maybe it was some part of the glove. We'll never know for sure. What we do know is that there had to be some way to figure it out. Some way that didn't include leaving the whole arena disappointed.

Look, I'm always happier when these things go for us instead of against us, but those who know me will tell you that, above all else, I'm a fan of the sport. I love to watch a good fight, and there are none better than the ones Emily and Irina put on. So this was just... crappy.

Rotating Rivals

For years, Kristanna Loken was a thorn in the side of Danneel Harris. Though Danni was mostly successful in holding the Terminator actress at bay, Loken never backed off and rarely lost her grip on the top contender's slot.

Now, through some mystical weight class recalculation, Kristanna has moved up to become the lead competitor to light heavyweight champion Daniela Hantuchova?

Well, you have to give her this much... Loken can't be accused of changing weight classes in order to take the easy road to a title.

In a clear-cut case of going from the frying pan into the fire, Kristanna went from taking on a champion who rarely loses to being forced to fight one who is practically invulnerable. Three years ago, Daniela came to RSI with the title belt already draped over her shoulder. And six thoroughly dominating title defenses later, she's still got it. So, Kristanna certainly has her work cut out for her.

Tonight's matchup demonstrated exactly what I mean. No doubt, Kristanna had this date circled on her calendar. The date she made her light heavyweight debut against the woman many consider the top pound for pound fighter in the league. It was certainly a chance to make one hell of an impression.

After a brutal beating that lasted just over two minutes, Hantuchova was left untouched while Loken was left unconscious.

That was probably not the impression she was hoping to make, but the bold blonde managed to have an impact on Daniela, just the same.

"I watch the videos. I follow her career," said the Slovakian tennis star. "She is never afraid. She has very strong heart."

Indeed she does, and no one knows that better than Danni.

"Kristanna... she's had a tough time of it," said Loken's former rival. "I always had a lot of respect for her because of the way she hangs on. She just can't seem to make that final push, but she never fades away. She's always right there, ready to try again. And I shouldn't say she can't make that last push. She did grab the title away from me for a while, and she was impressive in that win. She gets this label as never being quite good enough, but you can't take her for granted. No matter what happens, she consistently holds everyone else off and stays in contention. You don't see that very much."

It's true. Despite a horde of title hungry fighters constantly nipping at her heels, Loken never seems to get swallowed up by the pack. So I pay no attention to the naysayers. This rivalry is definitely going to be one to watch.

Where Did You Come From?

Emma Watson. Nadine Coyle. Kate Lawler. Grace Park.

As the reigning BTB featherweight queen, Tiffany Mulheron regularly steps into the cage to face the best featherweights the league has to offer. Sometimes she loses. Sometimes she wins. But she knows these women, and they know her. And everyone knows what they're up against.

But where the hell did Lacey Chabert come from?

Riding a wave of success that started early last year, Lacey quietly clawed her way to the top of the BTB rankings. Along the way she beat a number of quality opponents, including a pair of decisive victories over Park. The run earned her a date with Mulheron, who admitted beforehand that she wasn't quite sure what was coming.

"Lacey's always been kind of hit and miss," Tiff said in the days leading up to this event. "Until recently, she's been a mystery. She's repeatedly shown she can beat just about anyone, but every big victory seemed to be followed by a string of tough losses. Now, she's been on this kind of soft run that nobody really noticed. So, I have to admit I didn't know as much as I should have about her game."

Indeed, Lacey's history has been very up and down. An impressive, hard-fought win over the iconic Rachel McAdams gave the eager fighter a huge boost in the rankings, but she gave over two thirds of that gain back by losing her next two outings. Likewise, she scored a shocking win over Coyle at the Fall Tournament a few years ago that earned her nearly two hundred ranking points. Then, she shed nearly three quarters of that by dropping three of her next four fights.

Still, a gain is a gain, and Chabert has been much more consistent over the last fifteen months. So, Tiff knew not to take things lightly. She did her homework and it showed. As expected, Lacey was no pushover, putting up a stiff fight against the champion. But, in the end, Tiffany was simply too much. Mulheron really poured things on in the second round and the prolific voice actress had no answers, finally succumbing to the assault a little over three minutes into the frame.

The win exposed the sizable gap between Lacey's skill level and Tiffany's. She simply wasn't ready for everything Mulheron had to offer. But, can the cheerful brunette find a way to bridge that gap?

"Who knows?" Mulheron said frankly. "She's shown an almost unreal ability to raise her game when she needs to. So, I wouldn't put it past her. She's proven she can adapt."

Well, she's very likely going to get the chance. Kate Lawler's somewhat surprising loss to Christina Milian leaves Chabert in the top contender's slot, meaning she'll likely get another crack at Tiffany. I don't think anyone would be too surprised if she shows up more prepared than ever when that moment comes.

Show Stopper

It's happened many times to the women of RSI. You've spent months preparing. Setting up a gameplan, and visualizing how the fight will go. Now, you're out there doing your thing, and it's going great. Everything is playing out just the way you saw it back at the gym. A fighter's nirvana.

Then it all goes wrong in an instant. Next thing you know, you're staring up into the lights, surrounded by a concerned looking crowd of people you don't know. Behind them is your corner team. That's when you know. Your team will give you the details later - because you sure as hell don't know them - but the overall picture is clear. The fight is over, and you didn't win.

The bad news? It was RSI's Amanda Seyfried getting outclassed in the early going. But the good news is that it was opponent Candice Swanepoel who took the fight-ending blow.

It all went down in a single round, so there isn't much to break down. After some initial feeling out, Swanepoel started to take over. In the past, she's been a bit wild at times, but the accuracy was there tonight. Defensively, she was fast and slippery, frequently frustrating Amanda's counter attempts. As the clock wound down it was clear Seyfried had some adjustments to make if she wanted to turn things around.

Then again, maybe not.

It was just one, tiny error. Thinking she had Amanda reeling, Swanepoel got aggressive and tried to force the action. She pressured her toward the fence with a withering assault. The left hand landed, followed by a right to the body. Seyfried countered and missed by a mile. She ate the left again, then a right to the jaw. Candice came forward confidently... just as Amanda fired off a wild high kick. A big move meant to make her opponent back off, but it paid much bigger dividends as Swanepoel walked right into it. Foot met face in a brutal collision, and that was the end of that.

A lucky win? It's hard to say. The kick showed savvy. Seyfried needed some breathing room, and that was a good way to get it. But you can't exactly call it a solid victory, either. Truth is, it was much too soon to say where this one was headed. Things weren't going her way, but Amanda has recovered from quite a few bad starts in the past.

This time... well, I'm just glad it was the other fighter who got dropped, for a change.

A Familiar Face

Everything old is new again.

For years, Vikki Blows was the bane of Julianne Hough's existence. When Jules first ascended to the division title, it was Vikki she took it from. And when Hough lost the title? Blows was the one she lost it to. That's the way it went for a very long time. Each could look forward to facing the other almost every time she stepped into the cage, and the title changed hands nearly every time.

While Jules now has her hands full with new nemesis Keira Knightley, Vikki has been having a tough time of it. Some tough losses - and a particularly poor showing in her last time out - have knocked her out of contention and she found herself ranked fifth in the division coming into tonight.

And who do you suppose was her sixth-ranked opponent this evening? None other than RSI's Ashley Tisdale.

I've told the story many times about how much work we put into getting Jules ready for her first title matchup with Vikki way back when. Well, based on how tonight's fight turned out, I think Blows probably should have spent a little more time getting ready for Tiz.

The match lasted just past the two minute mark of the third and final round. That suggests it was a fairly even battle. It wasn't. Not even close. It was essentially an eight minute beating. I'd love to break down the action for you, but I don't know how. Where Jules is known as a striker with pinpoint accuracy, Tiz's style is much less refined. Lots of elbows and knees. Plenty of ugly striking in the clinch. Just... messy. And Blows seem to have no clue how to handle it.

The former champion's demise started slowly, with the first round being something of a snoozer. Lots of pushing and shoving, with little in the way of damage done. Still, the early signs were there. The few big strikes that landed came from Tiz, while Vikki just looked frustrated. I think most expected her to pull it together during the break, but things got worse instead. Ash came out fast in the second, and fast and messy make for a very weird combination. She grabbed, punched, shoved, kicked, grappled and even mixed in a judo-style throw. There was no sign of any greater plan or coordinated assault. She just kept banging away. Blows still didn't seem to know what to do with any of it, but hope persisted. Surely, a fighter with her level of accomplishment would figure out a way to handle this plucky scrapper. But she never did, and it soon became apparent that the scrapper was actually slowly mauling the ex-champ. Damage was starting to accumulate. A little swelling here. A little blood there. Worse yet, Blows suddenly looked tired. Not that anyone on the RSI side was surprised. All the ugly grappling had taken a toll on both fighters, but - in a scene that's played out many times before - Ash appeared to have more in the tank. We call it the Tisdale Effect, and it is a phenomenon without explanation. No one knows how she sucks the life out of her opponent and somehow makes that energy her own, but it was clear she'd just done it again. A fact underscored by the extended pummeling session she gave the near lifeless Blows along the fence as the round closed.

The third round was pure formality. Vikki was behind and out of gas. The only question was whether or not she could avoid the knockout. The answer was not. A few more ugly exchanges seemed to take away every last bit of strength Blows had left. She barely seemed interested in the fight anymore. The poor girl looked like all she wanted was a good long nap, so Tiz decided to help her out. With a minute and forty-three seconds on the clock, Ash caught her square with a looping right hand and Vikki collapsed.

Another victory for Tiz, accomplished in a manner I couldn't fully explain. The win moved her up to fifth in the rankings. Close enough to breathe in a little of the rarefied air. Could she keep climbing? The logic said no. Ash was a solid fighter, but she lacked the kind of next-level ability needed to compete with the likes of Jules and Knightley. Yet, here she was, having just thoroughly outclassed the former champion. The latest accomplishment in a career built on winning fights she wasn't supposed to. So, logic be damned. There was no way I was betting against her.

For the record, it wasn't lost on me that I had these same thoughts about Sophia Bush not that long ago. A fact that scared the hell out of me, given who her next opponent was.

Learning On The Job

In many ways, tonight had the potential to be the start of a new career stage for Anna Kendrick. The fighters ranked below her were either newer competitors or those who had already hit the peak of their potential. Above her was where things got real. A space occupied by the divisions best and the serious contenders chasing after them. But entry into the ranks of the 'serious' would not be easily gained, and the gatekeeper standing in her way was the very talented Emmy Rossum. Rossum was not only good, she was experienced. The kind of opponent you couldn't afford to make mistakes against. But Anna had an edge most fighters in her weight class would kill for: she just happened to have the top welterweight in the league at her disposal on a daily basis. And Danni is a rare breed of champion who is not only willing to share what she knows, but thoroughly enjoys it. She's proven many times that she's willing to go all out to help her teammates succeed, and Anna is always a very receptive pupil.

Have the two united to turn Kendrick into an unstoppable force? Well, no. Not yet, anyway.

From the moment the bell rang, Rossum made it clear she would not be simply brushed aside. The veteran came out in a style I like to call cautiously aggressive, and she was damn good at it. Applying pressure in all the right places, while maintaining a solid defense that frustrated Anna'a attempts to counter. As a result, Kendrick spent the entire opening round on her back foot. Though she suffered no serious damage she got absolutely nothing going, and the frame clearly belonged to Rossum. With only three rounds scheduled, Anna was in a deep hole early.

The commentators didn't have anything good to say about Kendrick's chances during the break, either. Instead, the talk was about how she needed something big to get back in this thing, and I'm certain they were not alone in that thinking. Most probably expected Anna to come out a little wild, and maybe take some crazy risks because she was behind. But those people forget who's in charge of the strategy here at RSI. This was not a do or die situation, and no one knew that better than Stephanie McMahon. The road to the top was long, and filled with ups and downs. They key to finishing the journey is improving yourself at every turn. And improving her fighters is now and always will be the goal Steph strives for most ardently. It would be much better for Kendrick to stay in this fight and keep thinking strategically. If that meant taking the loss, so be it. A loss you can learn from is far more useful than a lucky win, and it's infinitely better than getting knocked cold because you threw caution to the wind.

So what advice did Steph give her charge during the break? Same thing she told Jules. Stay the course. Reminding Anna of the strategy they'd worked on in the weeks leading up to this and the reasons why they'd chosen it. Because Anna Kendrick is a great many things, but the one thing she will never be is tall. Rossum enjoyed a considerable reach advantage over her, and she was putting it to good use so far. Pressuring and controlling your opponent is easier when you can hit her before she can get close enough to hit you.

The problem Anna faced was not unique. Nearly every fighter has to contend with it at some point, and shorter fighters are forced to deal with it constantly. The solution is well-known. You have to get in close and make things ugly. To grab and push and get nasty on the inside. It's a strategy that requires confidence and fearlessness. Qualities Kendrick normally had in spades. But it had been a much more tentative Anna in the cage so far tonight, and she had paid the price accordingly.

Steph's job was to find the daring Anna we all knew and drag her out here. And I don't know what she said to make that happen. I only know it worked.

Second round Anna was a completely different animal. Far from the desperate fighter the pundits had predicted, she came out looking... well, like she was in command. Like she had somehow taken control of the fight during the break, rather than in the heat of battle. Which, as far as I know, is not a thing you can do, but I'm just telling you what I saw. Rossum continued to throw strikes early in an attempt to keep the RSI fighter at a distance, but the new Anna was having none of it. Slick dodges and timely counters opened lanes for her to get inside. Time after time, she fluidly turned defense into offense. A clear perk of having Danni as her mentor. One of her most effective techniques was to bob under Rossum's punches while simultaneously weaving her way in. Every time she did it, she popped back up right in Emmy's face with her hooks already sunk in. And once she got in there, it was time to let the mauling begin. Short punches and nasty knees. Elbows were substituted where fists wouldn't work, and there was plenty of grabbing and shoving. It was not a pretty thing to watch, but what it lacked in elegance it more than made up for in effectiveness. Anna was doing damage and grinding her opponent down, while also screwing up the things Rossum wanted to do. Pretty much the textbook definition of what professional fighting is all about, and round two went overwhelmingly for Kendrick as a result.

Round three: one to go for all the marbles, and each fighter gave it everything she had. Anna continued to have decent success getting inside, but Rossum had gathered herself nicely during the break. Her attacks were sharper and her movement was crisper, which helped to keep her out of trouble. She also showed she had some infighting skills of her own. Quite a bit of damage was done, but the seconds kept ticking by with no clear winner emerging. Entering the final minute, all signs pointed to a draw or a highly disputable decision.

Then it all changed in an instant.

As the fighters closed again, Rossum attacked from distance. Again she went high, so Anna went low. Ducking under and weaving her way inside. But Emmy was ready for her, and a fierce-yet-tiny battle broke out. The two pummeled and hand-grappled and clawed for position. Anna got a grip. Emmy shook her loose. Rossum tried to turn her opponent, but Kendrick knocked her arm away. Back and forth they went until Rossum grabbed again and suddenly got the grip she wanted. She snaked a leg around and behind Anna's. Setting up the trip. A move that would likely give her the win. Even if she couldn't capitalize, it would let her control the action through the final bell. The perfect closing optic.

Too bad for her that's not how it went.

Rossum's move was polished and quick, but Kendrick righted the ship just as quickly. Steadying herself by latching onto Rossum with her left hand, she somehow untangled her foot and got it down on the canvas behind her. Balance restored, but the act opened up about a foot of space between them. That led to the big surprise. A huge right uppercut. Thrown without any sort of setup, and, if you ask me, without any sort of plan. Like Kendrick noticed the gap and simply decided to shove her fist into it. If so, it was a good instinct. At worst, Rossum would see it coming and be forced to back off.

But she didn't see it coming, so... boom.

Fist hit chin, and Rossum hit the canvas. Followed by an instant where no one seemed to move. Neither Anna nor the ref seemed to comprehend what had happened. Same went for the crowd, myself included. Literally a full second of suspended animation. Then reality started to dawn. Anna realized her opponent was down and cocked the right again in case she needed to finish the job. The ref woke up and - recognizing that the job was already as finished as it could get - threw herself in-between as she signaled for the bell. The crowd reaction was weird. Some started to cheer. Most were still perplexed.

What the... what?

There was never really any roar. By the time most people wrapped their heads around what had happened, the moment had passed. But the official announcement gave them a second chance, and that's when they really let the victor have it.

Meanwhile, Kendrick... what was there to say? After the shaky start, she could have easily folded up. Instead, she got back on plan and good things started to happen. Was it a dominant victory? Not particularly, but maybe this was better. It's easy to feel confident when everything is going your way. But having to reach down deep and pull your own ass out of the fire? That's where real confidence comes from.

Anna's still got a long way to go if she's going to reach the top. Wins like this remind her she has the tools to get there.
