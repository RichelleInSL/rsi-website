I pried around the edges a few times over the next few days, but Danni gave me nothing. I even tried plying her with sexual gratification... which she graciously accepted, but it didn't get me what I wanted.

Well, OK... it got me quite a few things I wanted. But as far as challenging Cole went, she stuck to the story that she wanted to wait. Plain and simple... yet I couldn't help thinking there was something else going on. Something that was happening right under my nose.

Meanwhile, the YGW event played out pretty well for RSI. The rookies came through again, with solid unanimous decision wins for Dove Cameron and Zendaya Coleman, and a stunning, first-minute knockout victory for Olivia Holt. That made four in a row for the blonde pair, while Daya had now won three out of four. A million things could still go wrong, but the buzz was starting to grow. It was getting hard to deny that these kids were going places.

As for Selena Gomez, her result actually made Steph's earlier estimate look a little silly. Opponent Alex Morgan was known for her power and endurance. She had also been around for quite a while and more than proven she's got some talent. Was she a world-beater? Not so far, but she'd taken down a few big names in her career, and she was always improving. But none of that was apparent against Selena. From start to finish, Gomez was calm, methodical and in-control. I saw a few opportunities she failed to capitalize on, but mostly I saw a fighter who had the right plan and the right skills and mental toughness to make it stick under fire. The fight was not incredibly exciting, but the way Selena owned it tells me we weren't wrong about her potential.

Next up was Jennifer Lawrence, who absolutely wrecked Gracie Carvalho. Which really didn't make much sense. Gracie was an experienced fighter, who many saw as having championship potential. She should have been a tough out, but she honestly didn't look like she belonged in the same cage as Jen. The early feeling out period quickly gave way to a series of brutal assaults from Lawrence, and Carvalho didn't seem to know how to respond. By the two minute mark, an emboldened Jen had moved to nearly pure offense, and it only took another thirty seconds or so for her to put the Brazilian fighter away. I really didn't understand how it happened, but I also didn't care. Jen had done exactly what she was supposed to so, and she'd done it perfectly. Barring some sort of surprise at Open Fight Night, she had earned herself a shot at Debby Ryan and the divisional belt.

Emma continued to make things look easy in yet another successful title defense. True to Steph's prediction, challenger Victoria Justice put together what was probably the best performance she'd ever had. There were even a few times where she seemed to control the action early. But Em is simply a different class of fighter, and she had the whole thing well in hand by the end of the opening round. An early knockdown set the tone for the second. Justice sprang right back up, but what little had been left of her confidence was obviously shattered. Emma dominated the entire frame, taking full control of the center of the cage and constantly forcing Justice to the fence. The challenger had no answers. When Emma knocked a bewildered Justice down a second time in the closing seconds, that was enough for the ref to step in and put an end to things. Though disappointed, neither Victoria nor her corner argued the decision.

All that said, I thought our best moment of the night came from heavyweight contender Dianna Dahlgren. Titles are great, and they certainly serve as the ultimate measure of success. Seeing someone like Emma continually thrive in the big moments is always a thrill, but what I really love to see is a younger fighter starting to establish herself. Dianna certainly did that here... but only after being thoroughly tested by opponent Bar Refaeli.

Unwilling to square up with the power house Dahlgren, Refaeli showed her experience advantage by consistently finding ways to get inside and grapple without taking too much damage. Dianna successfully fought off the first few attempts, but Bar finally got the takedown she wanted early in the second frame. The small victory didn't do much for the Israeli in the long run though, as more frustration followed. RSI has been making a concerted effort to get better on the ground this past year or so, and Dianna's performance showed just how far we've come. Though not exactly a threat on the canvas, she was far from a victim, either. Attempt after attempt got Refaeli no closer to a submission as Dahlgren carefully maintained her defenses. Eventually she managed to tie things up and forced the ref to intervene and stand them up. Bar went back to her wily ways, but it was soon clear something had changed. Was it frustration showing on Refaeli, or more precise striking from the American? It was hard to tell, but Dianna definitely started to land more punches as her opponent worked her way in. Refaeli stayed with it and scored a second takedown late in the frame, but again it added up to nothing once the action went to the ground. The round ended with the two still scrabbling on the canvas, with little damage done.

The third round started quickly. The fight had been been exciting but even up to that point, so there was no telling which way a decision might go. Within the first minute, it was clear Bar wanted to make a statement as she pushed harder than ever to make her strategy work. But after a few close calls, Dahlgren seemed to get the measure of the Israeli's more determined pace. Things got ugly for Refaeli after that. Dianna started to take away her angles of attack and land some heavy strikes every time her opponent tried to come inside. Knowing the fight was slipping away and the clock was ticking, Bar started to throw caution to the wind. Grappling wasn't working, so she tried more striking. Not a good decision. Dianna was still fairly new to the cage, and exactly what kind of fighter she's going to be is still unknown. But one thing that's been perfectly clear from the start is that she's very comfortable using her fists. Fast, accurate and powerful, she'd already finished some very good fighters who dared to stand and trade with her. And Refaeli was about to have her name added to that list.

The toe-to-toe battle looked pretty even at the start, but closer inspection showed Dianna was connecting more often and hitting harder. Technically speaking, Bar's defense was solid, but Dahlgren just smashed her way through. The further behind she fell, the more chances Refaeli had to take. Which only made Dianna's life easier. With just under a minute left, the Israeli made her final stand... and it really wasn't that impressive. She set things up with a double-jab that Dianna guarded well. Then Bar planted and looked to uncork a bomb of a right hand... but Dianna staggered her with a right of her own, first. Refaeli wobbled, then seemed to right herself... just in time for Dahlgren to step in and finish the job. A straight left hand followed by a vicious right hook, and that was all she wrote. Dianna Dahlgren had just put the league on notice. Her performance at the tournament was no fluke.

From the RSI-perspective, everything was coming up roses. All that was left was Aly, and her bout with Erin Heatherton was every bit as exciting as usual. Four rounds of red-hot, white-knuckle action resulted in two beaten, bloodied fighters, but no advantage to either. All would be determined by the fifth and final frame. Three more minutes of intense back and forth changed nothing. Every grapple was thwarted. Every strike was met with a return salvo. It was still anyone's fight. Then Aly saw her chance and launched an all-out barrage that seemed to be working. Quite a few shots were landing as she surged forward and pressed Heatherton toward the fence. Knowing time was running out, Erin made her stand and returned fire. A wild yet intricate battle broke out. The fists were flying as the clock ticked into the final minute, but each woman knew she could not abandon her defense. To do so would lead to instant disaster. So they punched and they blocked and they dodged as each tried to give more than she received while also hunting for a chance to emphatically take the decision out of the hands of the judges. It seemed Aly was getting the better of it. With forty seconds left, she started to back Heatherton toward the fence again. Victory was so close, she could taste it.

Thirty-seven seconds. A murderous left hook grazed Erin's cheek. A scant few centimeters from a sure knockout blow.

Thirty-five seconds. Aly missed with a straight right hand. It was not a bad miss. Her form stayed good, and her recovery started instantly. But it left a tiny crack. A small hole in her defense that lasted just a split-second. A hole Erin was quick to find with a vicious uppercut.

With thirty-one seconds remaining, Alyson Michalka was out cold on the canvas.
