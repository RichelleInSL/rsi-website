By the time the PTP event came, the buzz for the showdown between Danni and Sophia had reached near manic levels, with much of the interest coming from the One Tree Hill backdrop. The Brooke Davis/Rachel Gatina relationship started off nasty. Like dirty, rotten, boyfriend-stealing, nose-punching nasty. Who wouldn't want to see that play out in the cage to a definitive ending?

And the women themselves... well, they were only making it worse.

The pre-fight press conference started as they all do, with both fighters entering and taking seats at opposite ends of the table.

Then Danni looked straight at Sophia as she leaned into the microphone and said, "Slut."

To which Bush calmly responded, "Bitch."

Then Danni concluded the exchange with a plain-spoken, "Whore."

I didn't get it. I don't think most in the room got it, either.

Then the clip dropped on social media, and everything went bananas.

Apparently, this was the standard Brooke/Rachel greeting ritual, and it reverberated across the web as a call to fans far and near. Those who weren't already aware of the upcoming battle knew about it ten seconds after the video hit, and even people who had no idea what One Tree Hill was were sucked in by the excitement once the little routine started trending everywhere.

All of this was great from the marketing angle, but I suspected people who came looking for Brooke vs Rachel: The Return were going to end up sorely disappointed. There was no script here, and there was no way a real fight could compete with it's soap opera counterpart in the areas of drama and visuals.

Meanwhile, with all this going on, it was easy to forget that Danni was only one of eight RSI fighters on the card, and many of the others had a lot on the line and big things to prove.

Wait No More

The harder the struggle, the more glorious the triumph.

So said Thomas Paine. But, is it true? Does success really taste sweeter when you've had to work so hard to attain it?

Emily VanCamp, who had to wait quite a while to make her way past eternal champion Irina Sheik, says that it is. Alyson Michalka hopes to find out for herself some day, if she can ever put together the kind of fight she knows she's capable of when the title is on the line.

And what does Britney Spears think?

"Fuck yeah," said the finally triumphant new DEF middleweight champion.

Britney would certainly know. Despite the fact that she consistently and successfully competes with the best in the business, she has long struggled to get her hands on the hardware. First, it was Malin Akerman, who Spears could only seem to beat when the title wasn't up for grabs. Then, it was the return of an old foe in Laura Vandervoort, who she just couldn't seem to beat, period.

"There were times when I thought it just wasn't going to happen," Britney admitted. "The more I wanted it, the further away it seemed to get."

Rather than giving in, Britney came out tonight determined to bridge that gap all at once. You could see it in her eyes from the minute she stepped into the arena. She was going to get that title from Vandervoort this time, or die trying.

Thankfully, it didn't come to that.

It was clear from the outset that this one wasn't going to end in a submission. These were two hard women beating the hell out of each other. Laura came out strong, showing the kind of skill and confidence that's made her the champion she is. But Britney wasn't going to be denied on this night. Every punch Laura landed earned a response twice as strong. Every time Vandervoort turned the pressure on, Spears rallied and put the champion back on her heels. Then, as the opening round drew to a close, Britney let loose with a perfectly timed flying knee that exploded under Laura's chin. In yet another moment that proved Vandervoort's mettle, she not only kept her feet, but managed to offer a decent response. But it was clear the shot had rocked her, and Britney poured it on. Though Laura continued to show an unbelievable level of heart and resilience, she was never the same after that. Nearly a full round later, she finally succumbed to a devastating high kick, and Britney finally got hold of the belt that had eluded her for so long.

Once the fight was over, the assembled media had to wait quite a while before the new champion arrived for her press conference. The theory in the room was that she was celebrating with her teammates. That theory was shot to hell as other RSI fighters trickled out and completed their post-event obligations, while Brit remained notably absent. When she finally did show up, the fresh tears streaming down her face only added to the mystery.

Make no mistake though... she was ready for this press conference. In fact, she's been ready for years.

"Never say you can't," Spears said when asked if she had been starting to lose hope that she would ever lay claim to the title. "Never give up. Get the right people around you. Keep working. Keep fighting. There is no other way to win in this league."

Spoken like a woman who had earned every bit of the title she now held. And what kind of vitriol did she have stored up for the woman who had stood in her way so many times?

"She's a warrior," Britney said of her opponent. "Even though I want to sometimes, I can't say a bad word about her. Laura's always fought me clean. She's solid as a rock, and tough as nails."

Unhappy with the tameness of the answer, the group pressed her for more. But Britney wasn't going to give them anything because she had nothing to give.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I know you want me to hate her, but I don't. I mean, sure... sometimes I feel like I do. Facing her is as frustrating and aggravating as it is challenging. But that's what she's supposed to do. I can't hate her for that."

Given the way Laura has remained at the top of her game, I agree completely. Her skills are plain to see, and her toughness is unquestionable. True, she's had something of a big mouth in the past. But her record against Britney is simply stunning. So, if she wants to talk, she certainly can.

Something tells me she won't have much to say after this one, though.

As the question and answer period drew to a close, there was one answer the group was still waiting on: what took the new champ so long getting to her triumphant press conference?

Oh... she was just waiting on a friend.

The story of Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert is a saga in and of itself. It has action. It has adventure. It has heartbreak.

But mostly, it has nonsense.

They came to RSI as divisional competitors. It was a relationship I worried about from the beginning, and I will forever wonder what made me bring them both on board. Everything about the situation said it was doomed to fail. One was a burgeoning superstar who had tasted life at the top, only to have it quickly slip away. She had all the talent in the world, but couldn't seem to put the pieces together and get the consistency she needed to climb the ladder and stay there. The other was a feisty and determined scrapper, whose heart and shameless temerity had already carried her well beyond the point where her talent dictated she should have plateaued.

So, how did the hungry and focused Britney react to training and competing with Elisha the pit bull? Well, we barely got a chance to find out before the 2013 Summer Tournament brought the new stablemates together in the cage as opponents. The result was an unmitigated disaster. Elisha came away with a shattered ankle, Britney came away with a heavy heart and I came away with an ulcer.

The story of what came next is well known, at this point. There was no way I could put the three of us through that again, and I decided very quickly that a change had to be made. With Britney seemingly on the verge of the league's elite, I made the difficult decision to release Elisha. I wasn't happy about it, and I knew she wouldn't be, either. When I called her in to break the news, I expected some resistance and probably some tears. What I got instead was full-on verbal warfare. I explained my decision and the reasons behind it, which the fiery Cuthbert firing back at every turn. Nothing I said was good enough. Every point I made was countered and dismissed, regardless of relevance. Because apparently the best way to win an argument is to ignore the other side entirely. Still, it was my call to make, and I had already made my decision. Then, just as the battle seemed to be drawing to its sad but inevitable conclusion, in walked Britney herself. Jumping into the fray with both feet and taking up Elisha's cause. Which was ludicrous on the surface, but I soon realized there was something much bigger going on. It seems the Talent and the Tigress had done more than find a way to coexist. They had found a way to combine themselves into something more than the sum of what they were individually. They drove each other in a way no one else had ever been able to, and neither was willing to give that up. Something was happening beyond the cage, too. A bond was being forged, the strength of which I don't think any of us fully understood back then.

Now, who was I to break up a thing like that?

The years that followed have been a lesson for all of us. I think I've learned what it takes to make a stable run, or, at least, how to fake it with panache. Danni has stepped into her own as Stable Leader and learned to let her natural leadership skills do the work. Development Coordinator Steph McMahon has learned that she needs to share what she knows to really be happy. Or as happy as she's going to get, anyway. But all of us can thank Britney and Elisha for showing us what it means to be a friend and teammate in a world where violence and competitive fire normally rule the day.

True, they're a couple of goofballs. Talking to the two of them together is often like being dropped in the middle of an improv comedy routine. One in which the joke is most definitely on you. But try to get between them, and you will quickly discover how serious they can be. Go after one, and you'll find the other standing steadfastly at her shoulder. Ready to do whatever is needed. They are an almost absurdly ideal version of what it means to have each other's back, and I firmly believe they are a model for the other fighters in the stable. Because once you appreciate what they have together, you immediately want it, too.

Britney and Elisha... the happiest accident I ever had.

Getting back to Britney's delayed post-fight appearance... Elisha's own match didn't go very well tonight. We'll get to those details later, but, for now, I'll just say the result had Leesh in with the medical team for a full and lengthy checkout after the event. So, Britney came into the locker room after her big win and shared her new title with her teammates, just as everyone expected. Then, when the celebration died down and it was time to meet the media, she walked to the back of the room and sat down on a bench and waited.

Part of my responsibility at these events is to get these athletes where they're supposed to be somewhere close to on time. This task becomes much more critical when the fighter in question was involved in a title matchup because those are the ones that garner the most interest. So, I took it upon myself to go to where Britney was sitting... and sit right down and wait beside her. Because both of us knew there was nothing anyone could say or do to get her to move until she saw Elisha.

When the door to the treatment room finally opened, I watched as Leesh dumped her troubles on the floor and rushed across the gray concrete slab in a blonde streak. Poor Britney barely got a chance to get to her feet before she was wrapped up and crushed by her Canadian confidant.

Knowing Brit would be out when she was damn good and ready, I left them there... in the middle of a celebration much more special than any we'd had so far.

When I spoke to Britney later, I told her to make sure she took the time to really enjoy her well-earned and long-awaited title. To take a day... hell, even a whole week... and not think about fighting at all. But when she does come back, she'd better be ready. Because I guarantee that Leesh is going to waiting for her. Ready and willing to do whatever it takes to make sure she keeps that title for a long, long time.

Who Are You, Again?

Jennifer Morrison went into the recent Winter Tournament with something to prove. Getting out of the first round wasn't going to cut it. She wanted some quality wins. Something that showed she was more than just good. She wanted people to see her as a threat.

It wasn't going to be easy, and her journey took her through tough opponents like seventh-seeded Ayumi Hamasaki and sixth-seeded Ksenia Solo. Jen dismissed both summarily, controlling both fights from beginning to end. I'd call that proving her point.

But wait... I feel like I missed something.

Oh, yeah... how about teammate and well-established opponent destroyer, Julianne Hough? How did Jen fair against her?

Victory, baby. Achieved in a dominating fashion that left both winner and loser mystified as to how it happened. So much so that Jen was almost sorry for bringing title-hopeful Hough's tournament to such an abrupt and early end. Jules was quick to straighten her out, though. For her, there would be other tournaments and opportunities. This was Jen's time to shine.

And shine she did because if you add in first round opponent Carrie Underwood, that gave Jen four wins. And since these tournaments are only five rounds long...

Yep. The fighter who came in looking for some quality wins suddenly found herself in the final. Waiting for her there was top-seeded Keira Knightley. And that, unfortunately, is where the Cinderella run came to an end. Not that Jen didn't give a good account of herself. For nearly three rounds, she gave as good as she got. Even winning the early rounds. But the format for the title fights is different from the others. Five, five-minute rounds instead of the three, three-minute rounds Jen was used to. It doesn't sound like much until you do the math. Nine minutes of fighting suddenly becomes twenty-five. That's a big adjustment to make. The kind of thing you can't just gut out. You have to train for it. You have to learn to pace yourself better, both within each round and over the entirety of the fight. That was training Jen didn't have because... well, because we never expected her to get that far.

It was a mistake that really got under Steph McMahon's skin. For my part, I thought she was overreacting. With a whole stable to prepare, there's only so much that can be done, and Jen's run had come out of nowhere. Steph didn't care. Preparation was her job, and she had let Jen down. An oversight she made sure would not be repeated. She started Jen started training for the longer title fight format the minute they got back to the gym.

Overreaction or not, it was a damn good decision because another surprise was waiting for us around the corner. When you win fights against the likes of Jules, you earn a lot of ranking points. So, we knew Jen was going to be movin' on up. We just didn't realize how far she'd go. The answer? All the way. Numero uno. Her tournament success landed Morrison as the top ranked fighter in the PTP bantamweight division, just ahead of title holder Natalie Portman. And being number one means all the technicalities about rivalries and such go straight out the window. If you've got the points, you get the shot. Simple as that.

And so it was that Jen found herself headed for her second title fight in as many trips to the cage. And to be honest, it had a lot people asking, "Jen, who?" Because her tournament run may have put her on the radar, but she remained a relative unknown to most.

Well, let's just say they know her now.

There are a lot of ways to handle the longer rounds of the championship format. Most involve gauging your opponent, and learning to push her while conserving your own energy. It's a tricky process that can take a long time to master.

Jen opted for the simpler approach. Just hop in the cage and blow your opponent away.

Make no mistake. Natalie Portman is a vaunted superstar who doesn't have to prove a damn thing to anybody. But Jen's meteoric rise has left a lot of people wondering just what they're dealing with here, and Nat can clearly be counted among them. Her early approach tonight seemed to be about feeling things out and adapting to Jen as she went, but she was having problems from the outset. Jen came out firing on all cylinders, and Portman's normally solid defense didn't seem quite up to the task. Then the plan went completely to hell when Morrison cracked her in the mouth with a high kick with just over half the round to go. With no place to hide and too much time to kill, the suddenly woozy Portman was a sitting duck. When Jen's next flurry of punches sailed through with almost no resistance, even the normally passive Fearne Cotton knew it was time to call a halt to the action before Natalie was seriously injured.

The victory left me just as stunned as anyone else. I mean, I believed in Jen from the start, but this was a little ridiculous. Only a few months ago, she was a good fighter with tons of upside, who was just starting to find her footing as far as rankings were concerned. Now she's suddenly Jennifer Morrison, Divisional Champion?

Stuff like that doesn't happen very often.

That said, there's no reason to believe this was some kind of fluke. Since the start of the tournament, she hasn't just been winning. She's been carving people up. Yes, some of that is because her opponents aren't familiar with her, but that doesn't really explain what's been happening. These are accomplished and experienced fighters who have been at the top of the rankings for years. Dealing with - and dismissing - newcomers is something they do quite regularly. So, Jen's repeated success tells me the elite had better make some room. I think they might just have themselves a new member.

Dark (and Familiar) Territory

Well, it was fun while it lasted.

After making her wait an eternity to get there, fate - and Adriana Lima - conspired to make Petra Cubonova's run at the top decidedly short-lived. Tonight marked Lima's first opportunity to win back her title, and - despite a terrific effort from Petra - she wasn't about to let it get away from her.

"I am... how you say? Disappointed," the Czechian model said afterward. "Adriana is great fighter. I know this, and I prepare for it. But, tonight... was not enough."

Well, maybe not... but it sure as hell wasn't bad. For the better part of two rounds, this was a battle fought on equal footing, and neither fighter was timid about tearing into the other. It was ferocious, entertaining and not at all for the squeamish. Anyone who views these two as just a pair of pretty faces should definitely think again. This was war, and it was either woman's fight to lose until Adriana scored a perfect takedown toward the end of the second round. In my opinion, that was Cubonova's only real slip-up of the fight. Unfortunately, against someone of Adriana's ability, one is all it takes. Though Petra's mat skills are normally very good, they seemed to abandon her when she needed them most. Lima calmly worked her position until she got an arm around the Czechian's throat, and then the Brazilian easily choked her opponent out.

To many, Lima's win represented a simple return to the norm, and Petra counts herself among them.

"This is not surprise," said the deflated supermodel. "It has been this way many, many times."

As for me, I had no intentions of joining the pity party.

Yes, this loss is one of many she has suffered at Adriana's hands, but this was not a case of same old, same old. Though Lima may have gotten the crown she was after, Petra clearly showed she's not going away. This was a battle worthy of a title contest, and, rather than revealing Petra as clearly second-rate to Lima, it actually elevated their rivalry to a new level.

Not that I don't understand where Petra is coming from. It's hard to appreciate the beauty of a fight like this when you're the one who comes out on the losing end. So, I looked elsewhere to get some informed perspective.

"They both fought a great fight," said Petra's training partner, TGG champion Catherine Bell. "Adriana put together a brilliant string of attacks at the end. She got Petra out of position for just a second, but that was enough. It's no secret how good she is, but really... she executed that whole series to perfection. I don't think she could have done it better."

Then, without prompting, Mac switched from fight fan to friend and teammate.

"This isn't like before," she said. "Petra's much better than she used to be, and she showed it out there. But the old trend isn't going to reverse itself. Just because Petra improved, that doesn't mean Adriana is going to forget how to win. I think this is how their fights are going to be from now on. Hard-fought, brutal and even."

There's not much I can add to that. When you have to compete with a fighter like Lima on a constant basis, that's going to be a rough life and losing is going to be a part of it. Give her a little time, and Petra will realize she has no reason to hang her head over this.

A Unique Riddle

Elisha Cuthbert didn't need any extra reasons to keep her post-event focus squarely on Britney's big victory, but she got one anyway. Because her match against Lindsay Ellingson was certainly one to forget. I won't dive into the details, because there are no details to dive into. Leesh got her head handed to her in short order, and that was the end of that.

This is now her third straight loss to Ellingson, and each result seems to get worse. It's a puzzling situation, considering Leesh has beaten heavy hitters such as Karen Gillan, Josie Maran and even Britney. Sure, she's had her share of struggles too, but no one seems to present her with the difficulties Lindsay does.

Not Quite Ready

The conventional wisdom going into tonight's fight between Cobie Smulders and Holly Valance was that Cobie was out of her depth. That a rash of injuries among the fighters ranked above her had cleared a path to an opponent she was woefully unprepared for.

Woefully? I don't know about that. But there's a noticeable hole in Cobie's skillset, and Holly eventually found her way through it.

The early action, though, was about as even as it gets. Holly came out strong. Looking to put Smulders on her heels from the outset. But Cobie proved more than capable, matching the decorated former champion nearly blow for blow. Defense was in short supply for both fighters, but the offensive fireworks were a sight to behold. By the time the bell sounded to close the round, plenty of damage had been done, but there was no clear winner.

Round two brought a change in Valance's approach as she made her first concerted efforts to exploit Cobie's weakness. It started with quick flurries of punches that seemed to go nowhere. Two, three and four punch combos coming with plenty of speed and movement. But none of that movement was forward. Sheer volume forced Smulders to make adjustments, but Holly never got close enough to make any real contact. Staying at distance also meant there was no opportunity for Cobie to counter, but overall it just looked weird. But just as I started to think Cobie might have to switch gears and start driving the action, Valance cleared up the mystery for everybody. Another flurry came, this time followed by a well-executed takedown. Cobie didn't fall for it, though. She saw through the punches, recognized what was coming and defended it perfectly. Head level with her opponent's, base set, hands out... all of which allowed her to easily push Holly's attack off her line as she slid away in the other direction.

It was a bad miss for Valance because the element of surprise was now gone. Not that she didn't continue to try. The more she tried, the more it became obvious that this was her plan, all along. Get Smulders to the canvas, where she is notably weak. It's a strategy many others have used successfully, and it's a problem I know Steph and Cobie have been working on. But until she proves she can handle it, opponents are going to continue to test her this way.

One thing very quickly became obvious: her takedown defense is much improved. After slamming the door shut on the first attempt, she seemed to be able to stop each subsequent try more and more easily. To the point where maybe Valance should have considered giving it up, for a while. Defense soon turned to offense as Cobie found ways to land a few counter punches against the persistent Valance. Enough so that by the time the bell sounded, Smulders was the clear winner of the round.

Round three brought more of the same. Holly doggedly looking for the takedown, while Cobie expertly fended her off.

But then she finally slipped through.

It wasn't pretty. More like Valance ran Cobie over than a legitimate takedown. But it got the job done. Holly scrambled immediately for the mount position, and ended up caught in Cobie's guard. Then it became a game of seconds and inches. The nearly full minute remaining was an eternity when it comes to ground fighting, and each second that passed brought Valance closer to victory. But the win was there for Cobie, too. If she could just hold on, I was confident she would get the nod from the judges.

My theory was never put to the test. As Valance improved her position, Smulders seemed to have no way to stop her. Twice she made sudden and violent adjustments to try and improve her position, but both seemed to work out in Holly's favor instead. With less than twenty second to go, Valance managed to roll Cobie over. It didn't take long after that. She secured the leg, and flipped Smulders into a knee bar. The tap was practically instantaneous.

All in all, I'd say Cobie did well tonight in holding her own against a very formidable opponent. But that glaring problem of hers still persists, and she's never going to get very far if we can't get it fixed.

Grounded To A Pulp

Unfortunately, Cobie is not the only RSI fighter still struggling with her groundfighting. While our renewed focus in that area has brought significant improvement for most, some are lagging a little behind. Kristen Bell definitely falls into that latter group.

There really is no way to sugarcoat this. What Mila Kunis did here qualifies as a clinic. The course was titled Executing Your Plan To Perfection, and I have to admit that Kristen made for an excellent teaching prop.

The first round played out like a conditioning drill back at the gym. Kunis had Kristen on the canvas within about fifteen seconds, and suddenly Bell was struggling for all she was worth. Protecting limbs, fighting for position and quickly closing whatever holes developed in her defense. And in truth, she was good at it. Mila kept the pressure up throughout the round, but Kristen not only lasted but was never really in any deep trouble. If we were back at the gym, Steph probably would have grumbled something almost positive and Bell would have retrieved her water and towel and enjoyed a well-earned breather.

In the cage, of course, there is no reprieve. A quick minute to get your act together, and then it's back into the fray. Not nearly enough time after what Kristen had just been through, and Kunis made sure to take advantage of her opponent's state. After a couple of quick tests to see if Bell had anything left, Mila was satisfied that the time was right. A thunderous right hook landed on the next exchange, and that was the end of that. Kunis chased the stumbling Bell and landed another heavy right hand before the ref could put a stop to things. Though Kristen never actually fell, it was blatantly obvious she couldn't fight on. I'm not even sure she knew where she was.

And that's the way it's done. Though the true experts make it look easy, getting the finish on the ground requires tremendous timing and technique. But even if you don't have that kind of proficiency, there's still plenty you can accomplish down there. Kunis isn't a submission specialist, but she knew exactly what she was doing tonight. Grind your opponent down for a few minutes, then stand her up and knock her the hell out. A simple plan, executed to perfection.

There are some positive things Kristen can take from the way she survived the first round, but it'll be weeks before she's ready to talk about stuff like that. For the foreseeable future, I'm sure the only thing she'll be thinking about is the way she got worked over and blasted into the middle of next week.

Beckett On The Rise

Leesh might want to watch her backside, but she's got a familiar face sneaking up behind her.

For over a year now, Stana Katic has been very busy doing things the old-fashioned way. Stealthily climbing the rankings with solid wins over solid opponents. First came Eva Green. A very technical battle that Stana won in every measurable category. There was nothing really memorable about it beside the fact that the Castle star walked away with an indisputable unanimous decision victory.

Next came Yvonne Strahovski, a knockout artist with plenty of skill and experience to back it up. Stana showed no signs of being intimidated, as she calmly maintained control throughout. Though the two are the same height on paper, Katic seemed measurably taller. Staying away from Strahovski's power while scoring almost at will from the outside, she again made it easy for the judges to make their decision.

Katic's last big win came against Jennifer Metcalf. A bout that was remarkable only because of Metcalf's inclusion. A powerful fighter known for her high-risk, high-reward style, she had her whole bag of tricks on display against Stana. There was a spinning backfist, a superman punch and some sort of somersault kick that I'm sure has a cool name but I don't know what it is. None of it even made a dent as the oh-so-fundamentally sound Katic was never fooled by the antics, and she made damn sure to punish every big miss that came her way. This time the damage to her opponent added up, forcing the ref to call a halt to the action midway through the final round.

Tonight's opponent was Kirsten Dunst, a fighter who has somehow become public enemy number one to both Britney and Elisha. How this happened isn't completely clear. It started when Dunst fouled their then stablemate Jennifer Stano. Admittedly, it was an egregious offense. Dunst was losing, got frustrated and lashed out in a bad way, blatantly hitting Stano too low. To make matters worse, it turned out to be blatant to everyone but the ref and it allowed Dunst to turn the tables and get the win. When all is said and done, though, Kirsten is not a dirty fighter and she apologized for what happened. Neither Britney nor Elisha cared. They didn't care about her track record, they didn't care about her apology and they haven't cared about anything she's done since. It's a little ridiculous, but hey... if it motivates them, why should I worry about it? I even used their common hatred as a weapon once when I needed a way to get their attention. So, whatever.

Stana, of course, wasn't involved in any of this. She wasn't even with us when it happened. But she gave Dunst a thorough whipping just the same.

Once again, fundamentals were the difference. Contrary to the opinion of the Bobbsey Twins, Kirsten is a pretty talented fighter with a decent resume. Her skills were of no use against Stana, though. Defense, movement and irritating little counters were all the weapons she needed to thwart everything Dunst tried. The frustration went on for two full rounds. There were no real moments of importance. No heavy damage done. Just Stana stacking up points, and slowly driving Dunst insane. The payoff came in round three. By then, Kirsten had about all she could stand. The fight was still there for her to take. She just had to find a way to get her offense going. But the quest to do some damage led her to take bigger risks, which was exactly what Katic had been waiting for. Dunst tried to bully her way through Stana's defenses a few times, which only led to her paying a heavier price on the counter. No matter. She wasn't going to lose like this. She was going to make her stand. Her next surge was relentless. Forcing her way through a flurry from Katic, she finally got the position she wanted. She snapped the jab to set Stana up, then unleashed a mighty right hand. No luck. Stana weaved outside and the punch sailed through empty space. At that point, I'm sure Kirsten knew what was coming. That Katic's own right was on it's way. But there wasn't anything to be done. No way to protect herself. No time to move to safety. The punch landed right in the center of Kirsten's cheek, with all of Stana's power behind it. It was literally perfect. To her credit, Dunst tried to keep her feet, stumbling a step or two before dropping to her knees. Her hands instinctively went to the canvas to stop herself from falling. Which was entirely the wrong instinct for a fighter to have. He eyes were wide open, but totally vacant. Stana recovered her stance and moved forward. Prepared to finish, but in no particular rush. Because she knew it was already over. The ref knew as well, and she stepped in front of Katic and steered her away as she called for the bell. The crowd knew, too. In fact, the only one who probably didn't know was Dunst. Then again, I'm pretty sure she also didn't know where she was anymore. Or what her name was, to be honest.

And so it is that the PTP middleweight division is suddenly top-heavy with RSI fighters, with Britney at number one, Leesh in fourth and Stana now occupying the fifth slot. As long as everyone stays healthy, Leesh will face Ellingson again while Stana's next fight will be a rematch with the resurgent Yvonne Strahovski. But I'm pretty sure it won't be long before the Tigress finds herself standing across the cage from the stalwart Kate Beckett, with the bright lights on and the roar of the crowd ringing in their ears.

Two more RSI fighters beating the hell out of each other for real. You can just imagine my excitement.
