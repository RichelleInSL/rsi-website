Hough Enough

Rampage Sports is now in it's sixth month of operation in the UMMA. In that time, I've never been at a loss for something to say. But, I truly am having difficulty finding words to describe Julianne Hough's performance in this tournament. Prior to this event, Julianne has participated in five events under the RSI banner. Early on, my recaps included phrases such as: "struggling", "slow-paced" and "needs to improve." She showed marked improvement in her last two matches, and the recap terminology picked up accordingly. But, after this, Julianne's going to find herself in a whole new class of adjectives. She came out in this tournament with a ferocity and determination that bordered on insanity. Seeding be damned, she was going to win this thing. And, she didn't just win... she made it look easy. She was the heavy underdog in her opening match against Amy Lee. Two minutes and eighteen seconds later, Amy was prone on the canvas and Julianne was on her way. Her next bout featured what was, on paper, a more evenly matched opponent in Tila Tequila. That one ended with Tila wondering what her name was, while Julianne emerged virtually untouched. And then came the fight people will be talking about for quite some time to come.

Only three short months ago, Julianne faced off against top-seeded Keira Knightley in the Hit the Beach tournament. In that bout, it took Keira less than one round to completely destroy her overmatched opponent. Julianne was left bloodied and unconcious, while Knightley was still so fresh, she could have fought a second match right away. I have no doubt the bitterness of that loss was on Julianne's mind as she entered the cage to face the top seed in this tournament... the very same Keira Knightley. And, in a performance that shows just what focus and determination can get you, it was Julianne who made Keira look overmatched this time. She attacked from the opening bell, and she would not let up. She would not let Keira think... she would not let her rest... she would not let her breathe. Standing, clinching or on the ground... no place was safe from the suddenly lethal Julianne Hough. She was, in a word, superb. And, if she's going to make a habit out of performances like this, I am going to be in desperate need of a thesaurus.

After such a physically and emotionally draining fight, there was a lot of concern among her team that Julianne would come out flat in her late round matches. And, with a couple of dangerous rookies looking to prove themselves, one wrong move could have spelled disaster. Both Annasophia Robb and Lyndsy Fonseca had scored upsets of their own to get to this point. Would Julianne suffer a dreaded "come down" loss? "Not a chance," said a clearly proud Hough afterward. "Don't get me wrong. The win over Keira was huge. But, it wasn't enough for me. Once I got past her, the only thing I could think about was winning the whole damn thing." And that's exactly what she did, dispatching both rookies in less than a minute and a half each.

This is RSI's first big win as an organization. The excitement is palpable, and we're definitely going to build on it. But, more importantly, this was a huge win for Julianne. "I really can't put into words what this means to me," said Hough as a tear rolled down her cheek. "It was just a few months ago that I considered giving up. I really didn't know how things were going to turn out. And now, this." Julianne went on to give credit for her turn-around to her RSI team. Nonsense. While I'm proud of what the team has accompished as a whole, the credit ultimately goes to the fighter. She put the work in. She allowed herself to be taught. And, she's the one who put her ass on the line when the cage door closed. Congratulations, Julianne.

Next up, Danneel Harris certainly made an impressive showing here. But her road was not easy, and, in the end, it was just one veteran too much for her. After looking fantastic in the early rounds against experienced competitors Scarlett Johansson, Stacy Ann Ferguson and Emmy Rossum, Danni faced off against Corrupt Souls Welterweight champ Julie Benz. In a highlight packed and nearly flawless performance, Danni made short work of Julie. After that display, she seemed primed to win this thing. But, maybe she was a little too primed, as she came out very tentative against knockout artist and I Hate You Welterweight champion Cheryl Cole, and she paid the price. "I thought too much," a plainly disappointed Harris said afterward. "Too much planning... waiting for my chance to strike. I usually take the initiative. That's my style. I let her make the first move this time, and that's always a mistake with Cheryl." Indeed, it took Cole only seconds to destroy our girl with a brutal series of uppercuts. A bitter defeat, but Danni took it in stride. "Cheryl's the top fighter in this weight class, and she just showed you why," she said with a shrug. "But, she knows I'm coming. I'm improving every time out, and that's going to continue. There's no quit in me."

Like any sport, MMA is complex and difficult to predict. The best fighter does not always win. You train, you study... you prepare every day to be the best. Then, fight time comes and pretty much anything can happen. One wrong move... one slight misstep... and all of that preparation becomes moot. For three fights, Stephanie McMahon executed her game plan perfectly, emerging victorious in the first round of each. Then, the dreaded misstep... after scoring with a brutal counter, Stephanie attacked. But, Kelly Brook was ready, evading the assault and laying Steph out with a counter of her own. Though clearly disgusted with herself, Stephanie made no excuses. "She beat me," she said curtly. "It wasn't chance or luck... she made a great move, and she beat me. Plain and simple. I wish her luck in the rest of the tournament." Though it would be Jennifer Ellison who would eventually steal the show in the Super Heavyweight division, we agree with Steph. That was a nice fight and a nice win for Kelly.

Nothing for Alyson Michalka to hang her head about after her showing in this tournament. She faced a difficult path from the start, with her opening match against the highly favored Erika Christensen. The odds didn't seem to bother Aly, though. After weathering an early onslaught from Erika, she stood toe-to-toe with her opponent... exchanging punishing blows that would have been the end of most fighters. Both women were amazing, and the fans were treated to a fantastic display of power and ferocity. It couldn't go on like that forever though, and a vicious counter by Aly late in the first round put an end to the show. Taking a liking to the role of the experienced underdog, Aly stepped in against the vastly more experienced Heather Graham. At first, it looked like another slugfest was about to break out, but a crisp right hook from Aly landed flush on Heather's chin, settling the matter in just over a minute. This lead to another showdown with familiar nemesis Erin Heatherton. These two know each other well, but the much anticpated showdown did not live up to it's billing. Once again, Aly lead with her chin, but it cost her this time. A brutal looking kick from Erin left Aly's eye badly swollen. As always, Aly was eager to press on. But referee Holly Madison said no, and we have no qualms with this one. There's no doubt her vision was effected by the swelling, and continuing would simply have been too risky. And, Aly would fight blind with one arm broken, so asking her if she can continue is not a valid test. Still, all of us at RSI were very impressed with her work in this tournament. Certainly, she put on an offensive show for the ages. Now, if we can just convince her to try a little defense...

Well, one look at the seeding for this tournament told me I was going to need some strong antacid and bag to put over my head, as it seemed clear that Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert were going to end up pounding each other in the cage, once more. And, sure enough, both dispatched their first round opponents easily, leading to the showdown that everyone but me wanted to see. And, unlike last time, my nerves were not saved by an overzealous ref. Nope. This time, the girls fought it through to the end, exchanging bone-shattering blows throughout. And clearly, these two women are capable of putting their friendship on the shelf when they need to, as they exchanged a few pleasantries during the match, as well. As would be expected, the free-for-all couldn't go on for long, and a series of knees from Elisha ended things a little over a minute into the first round. Though disappointed, Britney wasn't exactly shocked, afterward. "Hey, look... I KNOW what she's capable of. She's one of the most dangerous fighters I've come up against. So, just think how much fun it is for me to spar with her day after day. I'll go back and train... she's knows what I can do, too. So, maybe next time, she's the one laying on the canvas. But, right now, I'm happy for her." Indeed, after gaining clearance from the medical staff, Britney was in Elisha's corner for her third round matchup against the much more experienced Doutzen Kroes. A lot of people thought Elisha would be vastly overmatched in this one, but Elisha didn't let it get to her. She looked calm and confident in the early going, handling Doutzen's attacks and responding with some of her own. Unfortunately, though, a fighter of Doutzen's caliber can end things with a single strike, and that's what happened here. Still, a great showing and a sure sign of things to come from Elisha. It's also a sign that she should be on high alert when she and Britney get together back at the training facility.

What would a UMMA event be without some injury controversy, right? After a couple of easy victories, Emma Watson looked poised to advance deep into this tournament. However, to get there, she would have to go through the always challenging Grace Park. Things were not looking good for Emma early, and she needed to turn things around. Then, it happened. A little cut, a little blood and it's game over. Obviously, this is not the first time this had happened, and it won't be the last. We at RSI have just come to accept it as just a part of the game. Emma, however, was not so dismissive. "It's rubbish," said a clearly miffed Watson. "You work you arse off to train for a shot at the championship, and some gormless git takes it away from you because the sight of a little blood makes her woozy. She bolloxed a perfectly good match, if you ask me." Like I said, this is an old argument, and I think we've all grown tired of it. I have to admit, though, that it does sound different coming from Emma.

A difficult, but not exactly surprising loss for Ashley Tisdale in her opening round matchup against Courtney Cox. Though Ashley has shown improvement recently, her earlier difficulties left her in the bottom third of the seedings. And, while she gave a performance to be proud of, Cox was just too much for her. Tisdale, however, was not satisfied. "I feel I gave a good effort tonight, and that used to be enough for me. But, I'd be lying if I said I hadn't hoped to get a win or two in this tournament. My game is improving, and I'm expecting a lot more of myself. So, I'm disappointed, for sure." This is definitely what we want to hear from Ashley. She should expect more because she's capable of more. With a little more seasoning, we'll see how she can improve that seed in the next tournament.

Not much to say about what happened in the match between Jennifer Lawrence and Malin Akerman. A divisional champion with 63 matches under her belt against a woman with only 10 fights and a .500 record. It didn't take a brain surgeon to figure out how this one would go. It's almost unfair, and the brutal beating she took at the hands of the champion could certainly shake her confidence badly. But, this is how it is when you're starting out, especially at tourney time. Hopefully, she'll learn from this and come back stronger as a result.

Continuing the one-sided theme, Marzia Prince never really stood much of a chance against Traci Bingham. This time it was a true rookie with only a single fight behind her facing up against a highly experienced opponent with 47 fights, including 28 victories. Credit to Marzia for the heart she showed in this one, making Traci work into the third round before she finally put it away. But she took quite a beating in the process, and suffered a scary looking knockout as a result. Thankfully, the RSI medical staff said the injury is not as bad as it first appeared, and Marzia should be ready for her next fight. The faster she can put this one behind her, the better.

And she'll get the chance to do that in short order, as the women of Corrup... er, I mean the women of Steel Cage Queens will be staying here in Russia to prepare for the upcoming Collision Course event. Though the card hasn't been finalized, both Marzia and Stephanie McMahon will be ready for action. See you then.
