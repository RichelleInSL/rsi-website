Tournament Torrie

It can't be easy living in the shadows of Stephanie McMahon. The responsibilities that come with her role as Fighter Development Coordinator keep Steph at the forefront of the RSI story line. Add to that Steph's triumphant return to the super heavyweight divisional title, and it's easy to see how Torrie Wilson gets lost in the shuffle. But when it's tournament time, Torrie takes a back seat to no one.

For the third straight time, Torrie turned it on max and blasted her way deep into a tournament. She took the title at last year's Red Carpet tournament — even going through Steph to make it happen. She also made a deep run at the Lingerie Invitational event less than a month after a disappointing loss at the team event. And now she's fought her way to the top once again here in the Bahamas.

This run started with a dominating win over another RSI tournament darling, Sabine Jemeljanova. Though Sabine has made deep runs a habit of hers, she never really got anything going against her stablemate. Then it was on to Rio Natsume, who advanced with a stunning and inexplicable victory over McMahon. Despite the win over her formidable friend and training partner, Torrie showed no fear... flattening Natsume in less than a minute. That brought her nose-to-nose with RWB sensation Abi Titmuss.

Abi's record and reputation speak for themselves, but Torrie was on fire as she tore straight through the perennial contender. Name a super heavyweight contender, and Abi has beaten her. That list includes a win over Steph in their only meeting, as well as three victories in four meetings with Torrie. So, any way you slice it, Wilson should have had her hands full.

Instead, she made it look easy.

Torrie spent nearly four minutes hammering Titmuss from pillar to post. Punches were landing in flurries, but it was the way Wilson used her feet that made the difference. Busying Abi with her hands, the former pro wrestler forced her opponent off-balance, leaving her wide open for one vicious kick after another. As Titmuss tired, Torrie seemed to soak up that energy like a sponge. A huge haymaker staggered the RWB fighter, and Torrie went for the kill... nearly ripping Abi's head off with a blistering high kick. Titmuss wobbled, but — in her typical fashion — she refused to fall. But she was defenseless with nearly a minute to go... far too much time to hang on. Torrie reset and repeated the brutal kick, and this one proved to be too much as Titmuss dropped to the canvas.

Afterward, Wilson knew that getting past Abi was an accomplishment in and of itself.

"Any time you beat Abi, you know you're having a good day," Torrie said. "Abi's not unbeatable, but she is always on her game. You know you're going to have to be perfect to beat her. One slip and she'll crush you.

With Titmuss out of the way, the going was anything but easy for Wilson, as she found the formidable Monica Belluci waiting for her in the semi-final round. But once again, Torrie didn't seem too concerned with how difficult the battle was supposed to be.

In just over a minute, this one was in the books. Torrie was so quick and so ferocious, it almost seemed like Monica never got her hands up.

Afterward, Torrie was quick to point out that the ease of her victory belied the danger she faced against the Dragon's Den veteran.

"Monica knows me," Wilson said. "We train together several times a week, so she knows my game, inside and out. And I know how cerebral her style is. So, my plan was to try to overwhelm her... to get to her quickly and not let her take advantage of my habits. I felt like I had to finish it in the first. If it went longer, I was worried she'd get in my head. So, it's hard to say I didn't get a little bit lucky, in this one.

The win set up the final against Kate Upton, who had overcome the surging Aida Yespica along with usual stalwarts Sophie Howard and Veronika Zemanova to get there. A championship battle between two surprising mid-ranked contenders would have been treat for the fans, but an early injury to Upton denied them the pleasure. But that didn't detract from what the pair accomplished in this tropical paradise.

When it was all over, Wilson reflected on just how crazy this tournament was.

"These tournaments are always nuts," she said, "but this one was insane. There were upsets everywhere."

When asked how she managed to stay alive through it all, she revealed her simplistic strategy.

"I just focused on each opponent and what I needed to do to get through each round. I didn't think about the title until I reached the final with Kate. Prior to that, it was all about surviving."

She was then asked how this win ranked against last year's Red Carpet victory.

"That one will always be special, because it was my first," she said. "But this one is a big deal, too. I feel like we've seen some amazing tournament runs over the years, and I feel like this win shows last time was not a fluke. I feel like this helps establish me among the better fighters."

That's an interesting point that illustrates just how 'lost in the shuffle' Wilson has been. She came to RSI with a record well below .500, leaving her buried at the bottom of the SCQ rankings. Coming into this event, however, she was actually above the water line for the first time in forever. She had also worked her way up to a respectable position in the middle of the rankings.

And I never even noticed.

Well, I'm definitely looking now, as she leaves Nassau with a nearly three-hundred and fifty point jump that slots her into second place in the division.

Now that's a comeback story.

I knew you were a workhorse, Torrie. I just hadn't realized how far you'd come these past few years. So, congratulations on another great title and your new place among the best of your division. Oh, and good luck dealing with Steph, now that you outrank her.

Lookup the word 'consistency' in your dictionary. If you find it, and there isn't a picture of Emily VanCamp there next to it, go out and get a new dictionary. Because the one you have is outdated.

Forty-four. That's how many fights Em has had since the start of 2014. Her record? Thirty-seven wins, seven losses. Considering how inconsistent her early career was, that statistic is simply astounding.

When she joined us in mid-2013, Em's record told the story. At 22-12, she was capable but underachieving. She had flirted with title contention a few times, only to fall off when it was time to step up. She was good, but far from great. And I would love... LOVE... to take full credit for the powerhouse she has become since then. But honestly, it just isn't true. Her results may not have shown it, but she was already on her way to greatness before she came to us.

In her first fight under the Rampage Sports banner — barely a month after she signed — she fought the nearly untouchable Irina Sheik to a draw. So, first fight for RSI, she fearlessly goes blow for blow with the long-time champion in a battle many who saw it felt Em had won.

Now, I take pride in the success RSI has experienced since it's inception. I have top-notch people on my team, and they do top-notch work. But there was no way they could pull a performance like that out of Emily in just a month.

I mean, they're good... but they're not miracle workers.

No. What happened that night came from Emily and Emily alone. She put Irina and the world on notice... Emily VanCamp was done screwing around.

From that day forward, she was no longer the gutsy underdog with an outside shot at the title. She knew she would always have to battle to get past the best of the best... including Irina. But she was going to make sure they realized they would have to battle to get past her, too.

Since then, she has been nearly unstoppable. She has faced the best around, and she has beaten them all. She has silenced every critic and dismissed every doubter. She has repeatedly shown that she is a fierce, fearless warrior who yields to no one.

And she showed it again here in Nassau.

Tonight, however, brought Emily a new slate of challenges. As she advanced through the tournament, there was no sign of the usual suspects. In a series of stunning upsets, Ashley Greene, Ali Larter and Irina were all picked off before the seedings brought them into the cage with VanCamp. When the dust settled, it was the upstarts who were left standing.

First came Joanna Krupa, who battled tooth and nail to get past Greene in a shocking second round win. And while the arena was still buzzing about that one, she went back out and tore Irina to shreds.

That brought her face-to-face with Em in the semi-final. Would VanCamp put an end to her amazing run, or would Krupa stand tall and show Emily she wasn't impressed by her resume?

The answer is both.

Joanna had Em chasing her tail through the entire first round. Strikes, takedowns, submissions... every time VanCamp got clear of one danger, Krupa threw something else at her. Twice, it looked like Emily was done for, only to have her scramble and save herself at the last second. And there aren't many fighters who can make Em scramble.

The second round started off better for our girl, as Emily scored with a flurry of punches that opened up the Bad Ass Bunnies fighter. That meant Krupa's moment of glory was over, right? Nope. Joanna came right back with some strikes of her own, then slammed VanCamp to the mat with another perfectly executed takedown. Another scary submission attempt followed before Em slipped free and got back to her feet. Then... FINALLLY... Emily started to take control. She finished out the round dealing heavy damage from the clinch, and Krupa was never able to mount a serious threat after that. But that's not to say she didn't try. She battled Emily for every inch until she finally succumbed a little over a minute into the third round.

Even though she came up short, Joanna turned a lot of heads at this event. Among those most impressed was Emily.

"She's not easily intimidated, that's for sure," said the ESL champion. "She wasn't getting lucky in there, either. I didn't care for some of her tactics, but it was clear she had a strategy and she was determined to make it work. That was one of the hardest wins I've ever gotten. So take that for what it's worth."

The battle with Krupa should have earned Emily a title all by itself, but she still had another opponent to get past if she wanted to take the hardware from this event. And once again, it was not one of the names you'd expect.

Miranda Kerr was the thirtieth seed in this tournament. That made her the underdog in every single fight she was in. Along the way, she destroyed RSI's Roselyn Sanchez and beat the living hell out of SCQ champion Larter.

Seriously... just what was going on here in the Bahamas?

Once again, Emily found herself standing across from an opponent who had no earthly business being there. And once again, that opponent showed zero respect for Emily's long list of accomplishments.

Things started badly for Emily again, and it seemed certain she was going to be just another rung on Miranda's amazing climb to the title. The first round was an outright disaster for VanCamp, as Kerr was beating her in every facet of the game. To be honest, I don't know how Em lasted to the bell.

Miranda kept coming in the second, but Em seemed to be finding her groove. While Miranda dictated some of the early action by getting things to the ground, it all came to naught as Emily did a much better job of keeping up with the young model. By the halfway point, it was Emily who was forcing the action on the mat — even trying a submission or two of her own. She was never able to gain enough control to get back to her feet, though, and by the time the ref decided to intervene and stand things up, the round was all but over.

Two rounds in the books, and Miranda had gone step for step with Emily the whole way. But it all got away from her in the third, as Em took total command. Keeping her feet the entire time, VanCamp picked Kerr apart with her striking game — both in the clinch and from distance. Miranda was a bloody mess halfway through the round, and Em just kept pouring it on. Still, the Australian refused to give in, and she stayed in it until the very end.

Emily's work in that last round made the judges decision easy, but the fight was as even as they come before that. Again, Emily was left to explain how a fighter not considered to be among the lightweight elite gave her such a battle. As usual, Em didn't let ego get in the way of her answer.

"I put it all out there," she shrugged. "I gave her my best, and she took it and gave it right back to me. So, credit to her. I felt like I was doing what I always do. She's the one who was different, tonight."

Emily was also asked if she felt the performances from Krupa and Kerr were just a pair of fighters getting hot at the right moment.

"It's possible," she answered, "but I don't think so. Sometimes you see some nice wins and a big upset or something like that. But Joanna repeatedly fought against the best in the league at this event, and she beat a couple of us along the way. Miranda was a beast against Ali, and both of them gave me all I could handle. Neither of them were intimidated, at all. So, I don't think this was just a one time thing. Something changed tonight. I think Joanna and Miranda showed everyone that they're coming, and the rest of us need to get ready."

This win coupled with the crazy success she's had over the last couple of years have sent Emily zooming to the top of the lightweight standings. In fact, when it comes to ranking points, she's practically ready to lap the field.

When asked about the incredible size of her lead, Em's answer was clear and concise.

"It's insane," she said, without a trace of humor. "I'm nearly a thousand points better than Irina and Ashley? That's ridiculous. I think I still have a losing record against both of them. I think that's true for Ali, as well. So obviously, the points mean nothing."

That's not totally true, but Emily has a point. With the competition she faces, that lead could vanish in no time. And with new faces like Joanna and Miranda getting into the fray, it's only going to get harder to stay on top. So, congrats on another great win. Enjoy it while you can.

Meanwhile, two other members of the RSI crew came up just short here in Nassau. Julianne Hough was methodically taking care of business until she fell to Keira Knightley in the final, while Daniela Hantuchova had to fend off Mallika Sherawat and training partner Alyson Michalka before succumbing to Heidi Klum.

Both losses are disappointing, but neither was shocking. Julianne and Keira fought one of the closest title fights in recent memory at the recent ESL event, so we know how closely matched they are. It's no surprise that Keira took advantage of the opportunity to even the score. Meanwhile, Klum has been making quite a name for herself these past couple of years, as she's been enjoying an even more amazing run than the one Emily's been on. Still, it's a little surprising that Daniela has failed to beat Heidi in two tries, now. Perhaps some adjustment is in order.

Catherine Bell had an excellent performance at this event, coming up just short in the semi-final matchup. However, her performance was little more than a footnote in the heroic tale of the woman who beat her.

Samantha Hoopes has shown flashes of greatness throughout her burgeoning career, but she's had her struggles as well. Her most glaring problem has been her repeated failures at the UMMA team events. In four such events, Hoopes has yet to pull out a win for her team. Only once did she even make it out of the first round. Those performances had many wondering if she was capable of stepping up and taking on more experienced competition.

She answered that question here with an emphatic YES.

Hoopes was seeded dead last in the heavyweight bracket at this tournament. That meant a date with ILF legend Adriana Lima right out of the gate. And right away, the Brazilian beauty found out why they call Sam 'The Hammer.' Hoopes attacked as soon as the bell sounded. By the thirty second mark, Lima was in survival mode. By the end of the round, she was hanging on for dear life. She got no break in the second as Samantha came out swinging again. Forty seconds later, Hoopes had the biggest win of her young career, while Lima was left to wonder what the hell had just happened to her.

A dominating victory over the woman many regard as the greatest heavyweight there is was certainly enough to silence Samantha's critics and gain her the respect she deserves. But it wasn't nearly enough to satisfy the young Dragon's Den phenom. A win over the dangerous Kelly Hall led to a matchup with ESL champion Lindsey Anne Strutt. Sam pushed past her in less than a full round. That led to the face off with Cat, who is also a divisional champion. Showing no respect for Mac's title and experience, Hoopes again finished things before the opening frame ended. That put Samantha in the stunning position of tournament finalist.

Her final opponent was the very experienced Veronica Varekova. To her credit, Veronica was definitely game for this one. In fact, it looked like Sam's championship dream was going to fall just short early on, as Varekova dropped her with a traffic-stopping right hook less than a minute into the fight. But a bloodied and bruised Samantha climbed back to her feet and gutted out the rest of the round. Still, things weren't looking good for the Dragon fighter. But when the bell rang to open the second, Hoopes wound up and unleashed the Hammer. Shrugging off the beating she'd taken in the opening round, Samantha battered Varekova from one end of the cage to the other. I don't know how Veronica found the heart to withstand the withering blows Hoopes was laying into her, but she managed to survive that round and most of the third. She was rewarded for her courageous performance with a brutal rib injury that will have her sidelined for quite a while. Such is the price of valor, I suppose.

Meanwhile, the injury takes nothing way from Sam's amazing run to the title. She brushed aside the criticism she was facing and stormed through the toughest competition the UMMA has to offer. That's not luck or chance. That's guts, power and talent. The heart she showed in getting off that canvas in the final is something you just can't teach. The RSI heavyweight division contains two celebrated champions and a pair of rookies who routinely win fights their experience says they should lose. So, there's no need to even consider adding someone new to the group. But mark my words... I would snap Sam up in a heartbeat if I had the chance.

Just a hunch, though... I don't think Dragon's going to be willing to listen to anything I have to offer.

A few other RSI fighters made also semi-final runs at this event. Britney Spears, Alyson Michalka and Amanda Seyfried all made good showings, but the most notable of them all was Danneel Harris. And unfortunately, Danni's performance was notable for the wrong reasons.

That's not to say Danni wasn't good. With strong early round performances and a hard-fought victory over Brittany Daniel, Danni has a lot to be proud of. Then she got her head handed to her by Gabriela Cilmi, which, while disappointing, was not exactly surprising. What was dumbfounding though was what I found when I took a look at Harris's record against Cilmi.

The two have met four times. And Danni has yet to win.

Given the depth of Danni's success and the number of top-notch fighters she's beaten, the Cilmi stat damn near knocked me out of my chair.

And when Danni was asked about it, the answer she gave finished the job and put me flat out on the floor.

"She's better than I am," Danni said evenly.

The answer was so unexpected that no one knew what to ask next, so Danni took the opportunity to expand on her response.

"There's really no other way to look at it," she continued. "This makes four wins for her, and I've barely made a dent. If anything, I've fared worse each time. So, do I believe I can beat her? Yes. But I haven't come close to proving it. Until I do, you have to say she's better."

Jesus, Danni. You know I love humility from my fighters, but... Jesus.

Well, when she was done with her fit of brutal honesty, RSI's team leader was asked about the performance of her friend and Dragon's Den counterpart, Sophia Bush.

Danni has been steadfast in her belief that Sophia is much better than what she's shown in the past. In fact, she took some pretty risks in the past to help her friend make the most of her potential. That saga eventually worked out for the best, and Sophia has been on a slow, steady rise since then. But her quest found another gear here in Nassau, as she took RWB warrior Cheryl Cole off the board in the first round of this tournament.

It was a shocking result to many, but, for Danni, it was confirmation of what she knew all along.

"Sophs is very capable, and she's shown it these past few months," Harris said. "She can dominate a fight, but she can also grind out a win when she has to. More than anything, she's learned how to finish when she gets the chance. That's what she did tonight. She found an opening, got Cheryl on her heels, and she just kept coming. That's the killer instinct you need to have to beat someone like Cheryl."

The 'someone like Cheryl' group includes Danni, and she was once again asked if she thought Sophia was a threat to her title. And once again, she danced around answering the question.

"Sophia needs to stay focused on what's in front of her," she said, "and I need to stay focused on what's in front of me. She has a long climb ahead of her if she wants a shot at the title, and I have Cheryl to worry about. So, we'll see."

Well, don't look now, Miss Harris, but that climb is not as long as you think it is. The win over Cole has vaulted Sophia to fourth in the division, putting her in touch with an old friend of Danneel's... Kristanna Loken. Danni and Kristanna used to compete regularly for the DEF welterweight strap before Loken was supplanted by Cole as Danni's chief rival. I'm sure Kristanna has her sights set on moving back into that top contender position, and Sophia will have her work cut out for her if she wants to overcome the winsome blond. But I sincerely doubt Sophia put in the hard work to get here just so she could give up now.

Anyway... that's all she wrote for this event. Next up is Open Fight Night, and, honestly, I have no idea what to say about that. Challenges are flying. Tournaments are popping up like daisies. I don't really know what the hell is happening, or who's even going to be fighting. So, you know... tune in, and maybe we can figure it out together.
