You Just Gotta Believe

Less than two months ago, Emma Watson approached us about joining the RSI family. At the time, she said she was looking for a solid team that could help her develop her game as she moved into the heart of her MMA career. And after only a few days of training, we were well aware of her impressive skills. Tough and deceptively strong, we had no doubt this young woman was going to be a marquee name in the UMMA. The only question was how long it would take her to get there. Well, tonight, that question has been answered. Say hello to the new featherweight champion of Fallen Angels Combat.

Only a few short weeks ago, Emma faced off against featherweight champion Christobelle Grierson-Ryrie in a non-title fight. That bout was mostly a sloppy affair by both fighters, and the first round just about put the crowd to sleep. But Emma opened the second with a clean right that ended things in dramatic fashion, and certainly gained her some fans. Beyond that, it opened the door to a title shot, in more ways than one. Certainly, beating the champion made UMMA management take notice. But it also gave Emma the confidence she needed. Suddenly, she could see what we had seen all along. "From the start, my team has been telling me I needed to believe in myself. And I felt like I did believe," said Watson, beaming with pride after her title victory. "But the team still felt like I was holding back, and I guess they were right. Because, after that win, something changed. I suddenly knew I could do it."

Back at the RSI facility, Emma was focused and determined. She had the title in her sights, and nothing was going to stop her. "The title was so close, I could taste it. It was the only thing on my mind." Indeed, she was at it every day, all day. The only way to get her to go home was to literally turn the lights off. But there was one thing her team just couldn't seem to train out of her. "They say I think too much," Emma said with a laugh, flashing her trademark smile. It's easy to laugh when you win, but that certainly is an interesting point. Great fighters work on "autopilot," relying on instinct and training. Even tonight, Emma was tentative. She was clearly in command throughout the fight, and likely could have ended things quickly. But she seemed more concerned with not making a big mistake, instead of closing out the fight when she had the chance. Is this something to worry about? Hardly. In fact, it's exactly the opposite.

Her win tonight makes it easy to forget how new Emma still is. The featherweight division of the FAC is a very young division, and that makes it wide open. Indeed, both Emma and Christobelle before her are among the least experienced champions in the UMMA. Will the pressure be too much for someone so young? The question left Emma momentarily stunned before she answered simply, "No, I don't think so." Quite the modest response from someone who first rose to fame as the 11-year-old star of the Harry Potter film franchise. With the eyes of the world upon her, that little girl grew into a graceful and confident young woman. Pressure, I would think, will not be an issue. So, rather than worry about that, we'll simply admire a young champion who is only going to keep getting better. Congratulations, Emma.

Speaking of titles, I believe Julianne Hough was looking to send a message to UMMA management: she would like a shot at the FAC bantamweight title, thank you. And with her first round dismantling of current champion Vikki Blows, the message was emphatically delivered. Indeed, Julianne never gave Vikki the chance to do much of anything in this one, overwhelming the champion with powerful strikes. But what was with the early fascination with Vikki's breasts? "If she wants to leave them hanging out like that, I'll be happy to tenderize them for her," said a stone faced Hough. Yikes. I'm starting to suspect her big win in the recent tournament has done something to the lovely Julianne. And the change is not limited to the UMMA arena, as she recently made waves online by posting pics of herself wearing nothing but an American flag. Patriotism is nice, but what's the story, Julianne? "I don't know... I was just feeling good. Having some fun. It probably wasn't a really good idea, but I didn't think too much about it." Having only recently considering giving up her UMMA career, Julianne is now a tournament title holder and she's in position to take the divisional crown, as well. I guess I'd be feeling pretty good, too. And while she may be feeling footloose and fancy free outside the cage, she's been all business inside it. If the UMMA grants her that title bout, she'll be ready.

The fear coming into tonight was that Alyson Michalka might not bring her best game for her matchup with rookie India Reynolds. Having only recently faced off with superstars such as Erin Heatherton and Brooklyn Decker, one might think Aly would take a relatively inexperienced fighter for granted. But there was no let down. In fact, Aly looked to be in top form as she finished off India a little over a minute into the second round. Any secret to staying motivated? "Just look at her record," said Michalka, looking relieved afterward. Indeed. She may be a rookie, but India has already built herself an impressive resume. And she's doing it by beating more experienced fighters, too. But Aly has her priorities set, and she wasn't going to let India hold her back. "I took her seriously. She's powerful, and she's dangerous. I have no doubt we'll meet up, again," said Aly. "But right now, my focus is on getting another shot at that title."

In my press release for the last event, I said precious little about Jennifer Lawrence's matchup for tonight. That wasn't because there wasn't anything to say. It was because the back story for this fight could fill a press release of it's own.

A few months before signing on with RSI, Lawrence faced off against Michelle Vawer at the Stabbed in the Back event. What happened that night is not something Jennifer likes to talk about. From the opening bell, Michelle was all over her. She pummeled Jen brutally, and Lawrence simply had no answers. Vawer, for her part, showed no restraint, ending the fight my literally breaking Jennifer's face. It was difficult to watch, and I distinctly remember feeling sorry for Jen that night. For whatever reason, she wasn't ready for that fight and Michelle clearly was. It was a painful defeat, both physically and mentally, and it was the kind of humiliation that would make most women give it up. But not J-Law. After her recovery, Jen returned to training, determined to make sure that never happened again. Not finding the support she wanted with her existing organization, she negotiated her release and came to RSI. Though I heaped nothing but praise on her at the time, I now admit that I was skeptical of bringing Jennifer on. What if what we saw that night was the best she could do? But Jen did all she could to convince me she had more in her, and I had little choice but to admire this tenacious young fighter. I was there that night. I saw what happened. And I knew what it took for her to be standing here, imploring me to give her another shot at it. We eventually agreed on a contract for five divisional matches. She would also fight under the RSI banner at any tournaments she qualified for. And almost immediately, I was glad I'd taken a chance on her.

Jennifer's training discipline has been outstanding. She clearly possesses a wealth of natural talent, and she seems intent on getting every ounce she can out of it. Unfortunately, Jen suffered another horrific defeat at the recent tournament, after the seeding left her in the unfortunate position of being matched up with seasoned veteran, Malin Akerman. We feared that loss might undo her progress, but it simply didn't. Outside of the cage, Jen is one of the friendliest people you'll ever meet. Always smiling. Always happy to stop for a chat. But inside the octagon she's like a machine, operating as if driven by some unseen force. But driven to do what?

Well, I got the answer to that question about a week ago, when I got the schedule of matches for tonight's event. When I saw Jen had drawn Michelle Vawer as an opponent, my heart literally dropped. I stopped her on the way in the next morning and took her up to my office, deciding to see for myself how she would react to the idea of reliving that nightmare defeat. And I must say, it was a scary experience. Her beautiful smile disappeared almost instantly, and her soft blue eyes hardened into cold steel. After a few moments, she spoke, saying only two words. "Thank you." Then she rose and headed for the locker room to change for the day's training. A shiver ran down my spine as I watched her leave. Clearly, someone was in deep shit. And I was just glad it wasn't me. When I stopped by later in the day to see how thing's were going, four members of Jennifer's team were dragging her off of a bewildered and bloodied Jennifer Stano, her usual sparring partner. For the rest of the week, Lawrence trained with Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert, both of whom reported that they thought Jen had lost her mind.

Deep shit, indeed.

None of this made me feel any better about tonight's fight, though. In fact, I was afraid Jennifer might be so hell bent on exacting her revenge that she would throw all of her training out the window and simply go nuts. But I should have known better. Jennifer is an intelligent and focused young woman. She would not let her emotions keep her from her goal. Instead, she looked calm and confident. No doubt, Vawer was going to try to get in her head, but Jen wouldn't allow it. Michelle had something to say before the fight even began. Jennifer bided her time, then kicked Vawer right in her smart mouth. A few minutes later, Michelle taunted Jen once more. Jen responded with a brutal kick that nearly broke her opponent's leg. That was the last we heard from Michelle, as she seemed very busy just trying to stay alive. It was not to be however, as Jen turned the lights out for her in just under three minutes. Afterward, Lawrence acknowledged that payback was on her mind. "I don't have anything against Michelle. She's a capable fighter, and I have a lot of respect for her. But yeah... I wanted this win pretty bad." Speaking publicly for the first time about that loss, Jen knew where the blame lied. "What happened last time was my fault. She totally destroyed me. Tonight wasn't about getting revenge on Michelle. It was about erasing a horrible memory. It was about showing everyone, including myself, what I'm capable of." Speaking of destruction, a quick review of the scorecard shows Jennifer emerged from this bout completely untouched. Think about that for a second. From completely destroyed to completely untouched. The moral of our story, boys and girls, is that you do not piss off Jennifer Lawrence.

Rounding out the RSI news for this event, Brittany Tacy and Selena Gomez put on quite a show for the fans tonight. Selena has quite a bit of experience on Brittany, and there was some concern as to how the rookie would handle the Disney starlet's ground game. And though Selena certainly challenged Brittany on the mat, our concern was unwarranted. Each time, Brittany was able to weather Selena's ground assaults and get the fight back to standing. Once there, it was Brittany who had the advantage, picking Gomez apart with her striking skills. Much like Brittany, Selena worked hard to overcome her opponent's strength, and she did a respectable job. But in the end, Brittany's power was just too much for her. Afterward, Tacy was happy, but realistic. "I'll take the win, but I know I still have a lot of work to do. I need to get some sort of ground game going. Every time she got me down, I felt like I was just hanging on. I'm not going to be able to keep getting away with that." Fair enough, but I also think it's fair to enjoy the win. Selena was a formidable challenge for Brittany, who was fighting for only the second time. For someone with so little experience, she handled herself pretty well out there.

Well, with the team event coming up, RSI will have a light week. Rest assured, though, that we'll be hard at work preparing for the tourney that follows. Until then, take care.
