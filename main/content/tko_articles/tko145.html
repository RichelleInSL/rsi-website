A Magical Moment

It seems Once Upon a Time actress Jennifer Morrison is not just magical on-screen. Working a little real life sorcery, the confident, friendly blonde managed to transform a startling performance at one event into the crown jewel at the next.

But what am I doing, starting at the end like this? In order to tell this story properly, we need to start where all things start. At the beginning.

When Jen came to us nearly two years ago, her career was a mess. With a record of nine wins and twenty losses, she wasn't headed for great things. But she didn't come across as panicky or desperate. She had a problem. She'd considered her options. And she'd decided what she wanted to do about it. Considering how badly things had been going for her, her calm confidence and reasoned approach told me all I needed to know. If she could stay patient, I was convinced we could help.

So, we brought her in and got her setup. We adjusted her training program and her new bantamweight stablemates quickly absorbed her into their midst. And in her next three fights, all of that... uhm... well, it didn't really help, at all.

Jen had come in with a two fight winning streak, and RSI then helped her achieve a three fight losing streak. Not exactly the results any of us were hoping for. But, at a time when a lot of fighters would have flipped out, Jen stayed steady. She wanted to give herself time, and see if her new surroundings started to work for her.

"To be honest, it felt a lot like being a rookie," she admitted. "Being here was such a different experience... it was a little unnerving. But going back out on my own wasn't the solution. I really wanted this to work. I NEEDED it to work."

She kept at it, and we started to learn to work with her natural abilities instead of against them. Training with Julianne Hough and then stablemate Danica Patrick had its advantages, but we slowly realized Jen needed someone more like her. Someone who couldn't rely on pure physical talent to get by. Someone who knew how to get down in the trenches and grind out a win when nothing else was working.

And who does that sound like?

"Jen's all heart and no skills," said the stylish Ashley Tisdale.

"Easy with the 'no skills' stuff, blondie," Jen shot back.

"Oh, Jen," Tiz said with a sigh, "how many times do I have to tell you? One blonde can't call another 'blondie.' It makes no sense."

"Yeah, but you're not a real blonde, dimwit. So, I can call you whatever I like."

This is what passes for friendship and respect within the hallowed halls of Rampage Sports.

Speaking of friends, I should mention here that the RSI bantamweights have very much become a little family. So, when they realized Jen would be fighting in the biggest fight of her life here in South Korea, every single one of them insisted on being here. Even the whippersnappers. So, when you see the comments flying later in this article and you're wondering what the hell all these women were doing at Jen's press conference... well, now you know.

Anyway... the grind it out approach is typically referred to as brawling, and it's not so much a fighting style as it is a way of life. Typically, a fighter's strategy is to take the fight to their opponent. To force the action and take advantage of her greatest skills. Improvisation is always a necessity, but if things go too far afield, chances are you're not going to win. But there is comfort in knowing what you want the fight to look like and reassurance in seeing it mostly play out that way.

Brawling is not about planning. It's about reacting. It's about adjusting. It's about believing you can overcome anything and anyone. Because a brawler doesn't take the fight to her opponent. She waits for the fight to come to her. In a world where one wrong move could result in you lying flat and broken on the canvas, the wide-open, 'let's just do this' approach of the brawler requires a tremendous amount of intestinal fortitude. And when you need fortitude... well, at RSI, that means you need Tiz.

Despite her high-fashion, girly-girl exterior, there is no one who can dig deep like Ashley can. The more we saw Jen's 'take it as it comes' attitude in action, the clearer it became that gutting it out might just be the right approach for her. So, we turned to Ash to show her how it's done. Together with the comedically gifted and sharp-tongued Kristen Bell, the women quickly gelled into their own little group. And boy do they love to push each other.

"It's like a game," Morrison said. "We try to outsmart each other. To be the one to do the most unexpected thing and try to catch her opponent napping."

"A lot of it is guessing," Tisdale said. "You need to be able to read your opponent and know when to make your move."

"Guessing, and whole lot of fearless commitment," Jen said. "Once you go, you have to go hard. There is no going back. You commit and you live with the consequences. But when you get it right, the results can be spectacular."

"And when you get it wrong," Kristen added, "the failure is ALWAYS spectacular."

You see what I mean about it being a way of life? If this group had a motto, it would be "No guts, no glory."

And speaking of the group... when Danica made the decision to move over to Dragon's Den, that left Jules without a dance partner. The only option we had was to throw her in with the Brutal Bunch and see what happened. The result?

"We hate her," Kristen said.

"Yeah," Ashley added. "She messes up everything."

I have no doubt they actually do hate Jules. But they hate her for all the right reasons.

"She's practically unstoppable," Jen said. "Most of the time, she makes us look like idiots."

Well, despite the fact that these three do a fine job looking idiotic all on their own, it's certainly true that Jules brought a new dynamic to the group. Mostly, she taught the others that toughness and heart are great, but you'll need every ounce you've got if you expect to beat a true talent.

Julianne's proficiency regularly gives the group fits. But that doesn't mean she hasn't learned a thing or two, as well.

"When you execute the way you want, you expect a certain result," the ESL champion said. "So, when everything goes right, you expect to dominate. But these three just hang around and really get under your skin. They are not easy to deal with."

Still, when one of them faces off with Julianne, it's usually the dancer's talent that carries the day. Except, there was that one time...

...and that brings us back to that startling performance I mentioned in the beginning.

Back in July, the UMMA universe was focused on the beautiful Australian costal city of Perth, as the annual Red Carpet tournament was in full swing. As with every tournament, there was plenty of craziness to be had. And among the craziest of them all was a finals run by the fifteenth seeded fighter in the Bantamweight division... none other than Miss Jennifer Morrison.

Although Jen didn't quite have enough to get past top-seeded Keira Knightley, she had to pull off a number of dramatic upsets just to get that far. The most shocking came when she brought the 'unstoppable' to a stone cold halt.

Yep... in a performance that even Jen herself couldn't fathom, she put a whooping on second seeded Julianne Hough.

"What a total bitch," said a disappointed but smiling Jules after the fight.

Despite her shock, Jen didn't miss a beat in her response.

"That's for all those times you kicked my ass in training, Miss High and Mighty," she quipped. "Now we know which one of us can step up when the pressure's on."

"Alright, settle down Savior-girl," Jules retorted, referencing Jen's character on Once. "Come talk to me when we get back to the facility, and we'll see what's what."

Seems to me Jules is fitting right in the others. Isn't that wonderful?

Along with weeks - if not months - of bragging rights, Jen's win came with a nice little bonus: a whole pile of ranking points. The victory shot her up the divisional chart, vaulting her right past the sitting champion.

And who was that champion?

Why, Danica Patrick, of course.

It seems life at the Den has been pretty good for Miss Patrick. While her time with RSI had it's moments, it was also filled with a lot of frustration. Every time she seemed to get some momentum going, she'd suffer a tough loss that would put the brakes on everything. It was time for a change, and it seems Sophia, Bri and the Den mob were just the thing she needed. In less than a year, she not only got herself straightened out, but even got her hands on every fighter's true dream... the divisional title, which she took from Alizee Jacotey after a brazen Open Fight Night challenge.

And now, here came the upstart Jen, looking to swipe the crown for herself.

Her superior ranking meant Jen didn't have to wait to achieve rival status with the new champion. She would get her first shot at the title right here in Seoul.

She did not let the opportunity go to waste.

In typical Jen fashion, the fight was anything goes. Strikes, takedowns... even a surprise submission attempt. She simply took whatever Danica gave her and went with it. But it was Morrison's boxing skills that truly ruled the day. She consistently beat her opponent to the punch, and, once she found an opening, she attacked mercilessly... wearing Patrick down with viscious, precise flurries. Early in the second round, an exhausted Danica tried to press forward and back Jen off of her. She ended up stepping right into an uppercut that landed flush on the point of her chin. The rest was just a formality.

Just like that, Jen's life was changed forever. With a suddenness that is very rare in the fight game, she had emerged from the pack and snatched the Holy Grail. No matter what happens from here forward, no one can ever take this accomplishment away from her.

What do I think will happen next? How the hell should I know? If you'd asked me a few months ago if I expected Jen to become a title threat, I'd have said she needed to get some momentum going before we started talking titles. Now, she's far more than just a threat. She's the champ.

Jen, I really don't know what to say. After your tournament run, I playfully told you I was going to start expecting bigger things from you. I have no way to add to that because nothing is bigger than what you did at this event. So, my only expectation now is that you'll continue to be the same steady, eve-keeled fighter you've been since I met you. Where will that take you? I have no clue. But I'm properly positioned on the edge of my seat and I've got my popcorn in hand. Show me what you got, Savior-girl.

Unhappy Return

Soon-to-be recrowned champion Danneel Harris stood a few steps from her corner, methodically arguing her case to referee Fearne Cotton. Cotton wore an understanding expression, but shook her head nonetheless. The call was simply not hers to make.

Suddenly deposed champion Sophia Bush stood on the opposite side of the octagon and glared angrily at the back of the referee's head as a stream of blood trickled from the bridge of her nose. She had already had her run at the blonde official, and she had been far less calm about her approach.

It was all for naught, though. Cotton stepped away to confer with the judges, but the decision was made. Harris approached her opponent, offering sympathy and even a meager apology, despite the fact that she hadn't done anything wrong. But Bush wasn't interested in apologies or commiseration. She was whole and she was healthy and she was ready to fight on... and she didn't understand why she was being denied the chance.

This is not the way anyone expected the scene to look when these two finished what was supposed to be an epic battle.

It was not the way Danni wanted to reclaim the divisional title, either.

"I really just wanted them to let her fight, you know?" a disappointed Harris said afterward. Then she shrugged and added, "The cut didn't look like much. I really don't know what the doctors saw that made them stop it."

Indeed... in what may be the silliest decision I've ever seen, the ringside physician decided that a cut on Sophia's nose was severe enough that it wasn't safe for her to continue. I mean, it was a cut. On her NOSE! Yeah, it was bleeding. But it's a fight. Blood happens.

Sometimes, I seriously wonder where the UMMA finds these people. I also wonder just how many times this has to happen before somebody does something.

Let me try to paint the complete picture for you, so you can get the full sense of all that was lost here in Seoul.

Sophia and Danni have known each other since their days on One Tree Hill together. On the show, their characters had a long-running feud that provided many of the moments fans remember best. In fact, the two even got physical with each other on more than one occasion. So, the idea of Brooke Davis and Rachel Gatina fighting to a finish in a closed cage is... well, let's just say people found it intriguing.

Need more? How about the struggles Sophia has faced and the lengths she's gone to to make herself better? For a long time, Brooke vs Rachel was just a fun thing to think about. There was no real hope it would ever happen. Danni was the celebrated champion. One of the most feared welterweights in the entire league. Sophia was persistently struggling - forever mired in mediocrity. Surely, she would never be able to catch up and give the fans what they wanted to see.

Then, one day, she decided she'd had enough. She was done being stuck in the middle. She wanted to win. To move up and compete and prove that she could be a threat. She sought out help (from a very unlikely source), and she put in the hard work to make herself better. And so the slow march began, with the Dragon leader making steady progress as she clawed her way up the standings. Suddenly, the upstart Brooke was proving to be much more than anyone thought she could be. And she was coming for Rachel's crown! Could a showdown between the two really happen?

And then, it did.

In a crazy, open-weight, novelty-seeded mini-tournament at the 2015 Open Fight Night, the two welterweights somehow found each other. Suddenly, Brooke and Rachel stood mere feet apart. Alone. In the cage. With the door locked. The excitement generated by the unexpected development was intense and the crowd was nearly uncontrollable. Could the women possibly deliver a fight that would live up to the expectations?

Damn right, they did.

Any questions about whether Sophia could stand up to her decorated counterpart were answered within the first few minutes. This wasn't going to be some litmus test or learning experience for the underdog. She had come to play. For more than two and a half rounds, the women absolutely hammered each other. Sophia took everything Danni threw at her, and paid her right back for it. Before it was over, both fighters were covered in their own blood. And it was anyone's fight until the very end. It wasn't until the closing minutes that Danni's experience finally shined through as she put her opponent away.

So, round one may have gone to Rachel, but Brooke came away with much more than a moral victory. She didn't just hang in there. She had taken it to the champion, and she had proven she was up to the challenge. The fans were crazy for an encore. One that was done right. One with months of hype and the title on the line. A 'brawl for it all,' so to speak. But first, Sophia would need to finish her long climb to the top.

Or would she?

A poor showing from the champion back in March resulted in a true rarity. She let the title slip away, falling to a highly-motivated Cheryl Cole. But Danni's disappointment quickly became Sophia's opportunity. Though she still trailed in the rankings, a new route to the title had suddenly opened up for her. Her history with Cole meant the two had an established, formal rivalry under the league rules. That meant Sophia had the right to call Cheryl out when Open Fight Night rolled around, and that's exactly what she did. And with her big chance right in front of her, Sophs would not be denied. She made good on the challenge and got her hands on the prize - outhustling her opponent for a quick and efficient submission victory.

Just like that, the tables were turned. Now, it was Brooke who wore the title, with Rachel eager for the chance to win it back.

So, the follow-up to Danni and Sophia's epic brawl was set, and it was literally gilded in gold. Not only would this be Brooke vs Rachel II, but both fighters were coming in with something to prove. Danni needed to show that her slip-up against Cole was just that... a slip-up. That she was still the champion her fans believed she was. Sophia needed to prove her time had truly come. That her sudden leap to the title was more than just a gimmicky stroke of luck.

All things considered, you could not possibly write a better script.

And then the ring doctor went ahead and totally blew the ending.

I think it's safe to say that everyone involved in this left Mokdong Stadium disappointed. The UMMA brass had just watched one of the most anticipated fights in the league's history turn into one of its most controversial disasters. Sophia got obviously and royally screwed, and so did the fans. And Danni was left with what was likely the most hollow victory of her career.

Oh, and then there was the other thing...

"I was really looking forward to tonight," Danni lamented at her post-fight press session. "Sophia's worked so hard and she's proven so much. I wanted her to get out there and show everyone what I already knew. I mean, I wanted to win, but not like this. To see her chance snatched from her that way... it's just... I really don't know what to say."

That's right. It's the open secret no one talked about it much because nobody wanted to rain too heavily on the hype parade. Rachel and Brooke may have had an intense hatred for each other, but Danni and Sophia are actually good friends. Danni was the one Sophia turned to when she needed help... a move some may call desperate, but I prefer to see as gutsy. To me, the Dragon leader proved she could swallow her pride and keep her focus squarely on the prize. Meanwhile, despite the fact that she had a million reasons to run the other way, Danni took some big risks to help her friend. I generally refer to that move as being monumentally stupid, but she established long ago that she doesn't really care what I think. Also, the whole episode eventually led to the joint training arrangement that exists between the Den and RSI. When you look at all the good that's come out of that, it gets pretty hard for me to support my argument.

The point is, the fighters were just as excited as the fans about this one. These two care about each other, and they have the ultimate respect for one another. But they also wanted the chance to compete with each other. To put on the show they both knew they were capable of. So the premature stoppage ruined it for them, too.

I really don't know how to end this segment. I'm happy for Danni, but this really isn't one to cheer about. Sophia has her work cut out for her now, as she has a very unhappy Cheryl Cole to deal with. Meanwhile, Danni has a new challenge rising in the form of Jessica Alba. If the two friends and fellow stable leaders both perform the way I know they can, then Brooke vs Rachel III shouldn't be too far off. In the meantime, we can all sit and stew over this missed opportunity together.

I am up, down, touch the ground...

The rivalry is intense and the fights are must see. But the story was getting a little old.

For years, that story was about Petra Cubonova, the little engine that couldn't. Despite a highly successful career that kept her constantly in position to win a divisional title, she just couldn't make it happen. Great performances. Terrible performances. Brutal injuries. The details were always different, but the net result was always the same. She went home dejected and empty handed.

Oh, and there was one other that other big similarity. Every single time, it was Adriana Lima that she lost to.

Six straight times she lost to Lima between 2012 and 2015. The whole thing was quickly becoming a joke. She came to RSI searching for answers, but the cycle continued just the same. Only now, she had friends and teammates behind her. Most importantly, she had Catherine Bell to stand with her.

The two training partners have pushed each other to heights neither could have reached on her own. Soon enough, Mac had a title to show for their efforts, and she promised Petra then and there that she would not quit until she had the belt in her hands, as well.

And, sure enough, in March of last year, Petra came out and put on a performance for the ages. She didn't just beat Lima. She wiped the mat with her. Year's of pent up frustration came roaring out in two and a half minutes of pure fury. Finally, Petra Cubonova had the title she had yearned for... the return on the blood, sweat and tears she had invested in her quest for greatness.

Almost exactly one year later, Lima took it back.

Three years of torment for one year of reign? Kinda makes a girl feel shortchanged.

Petra was good in the loss - definitely not a performance to hang her head over. But Lima was just so much better.

Now the questions were back, and they were being asked more loudly than ever. Had Petra simply gotten lucky when she won the title? After years of futility, had she simply bored Lima into submission to get that single victory? Was this the end for her? Or would she stick? Would she rebound and prove that she was no longer the perennial also-ran?

Asked and answered, my friends. Petra Cubonova is here to stay.

In a reversal of the March fight, it was Petra who was clearly the better fighter here in South Korea. She didn't overwhelm Lima. She outfought her. Standing, clinching, on the ground... Petra was comfortable and in-control. She was no longer the upstart praying to score the upset. She looked and acted like Lima's equal. And tonight, she simply outworked her.

"I'm so proud of her," said Cat Bell afterward. "There was nothing lucky or flukey about tonight. Adriana had her chances. Petra was just better."

Without a doubt, I'm with Mac on this one. But a moment like this calls for an expert opinion.

"Petra was great," said RSI Development Coordinator Stephanie McMahon. "Lima is a fighter worthy of the kind of respect she gets. She's dangerous in every situation, and she can end a fight at any moment. The first time she won, I think Petra just surprised her and got the finish before Adriana could react. There's nothing wrong with that. That's what she's supposed to do. But it left a lot of room for people to talk. Then Adriana won and the talk got louder. But I don't think there's anything left to talk about after tonight. Lima is a great fighter, and so is Petra. This is what a rivalry should look like."

And there you have it. Sometimes she's up, and sometimes she's down. But Petra is done being forever second best.

Middleweight Misery

I'm about to do something I never would have thought was possible. I'm going to sum up the plight of two of RSI's most well-known athletes in the span of just a few short paragraphs. The brevity is not because I don't like them, or because I don't care. It's because there simply isn't much to say.

First up, Britney Spears. After years of frustration, Britney final grabbed hold of the divisional title at the last DEF event. Well, it's gone again. And her reign lasted not one second beyond the absolute minimum, as tonight's loss to nemesis Laura Vandervoort happened after only one punch, and took all of 17 seconds. I have reviewed the video numerous times, and I still have no idea what happened.

Meanwhile, Britney's friend and training partner Elisha Cuthbert had herself a war with Kirsten Dunst tonight. I'll admit, it was a little surprising, given how easily Leesh took the win over Dunst only a few short weeks ago. Of course, when you think about it, that's probably the reason this one was so different. Dunst was angry about what happened last time, and she refused to let it happen again. I think she took Cuthbert by surprise, and the responsibility for that has to fall on Leesh. But, despite the fact that things weren't going like she hoped, Elisha was definitely in this fight and the battle was entertaining as hell. Then, the unthinkable happened. As the third round opened, Kirsten caught Leesh with a clean right hand, and the plucky blonde crumpled to the canvas in agonizing pain. The strike had broken a bone in her face. Not only did she lose, but she'll now be out of action for months.

I don't know why I said the injury was unthinkable, when, in fact, it's all too common for Leesh. Add to that the frustrations Britney's been through and... well, I just don't know what it is with these two. They just can't ever seem to get two things to go right in a row. It's a good thing they have each other to lean on. That's really all I can say.

Rotating Rival

For years, Kristanna Loken was a thorn in the side of Danneel Harris. Though Danni was mostly successful in holding the Terminator actress at bay, Loken never backed off and rarely lost her grip on the top contender's slot.

Now, through some mystical weight class recalculation, Kristanna has moved up to become the lead competitor to light heavyweight champion Daniela Hantuchova.

Well, you have to give her this much... Kristanna can't be accused of changing weight classes in order to make her own life easier.

In a clear-cut case of going from the frying pan into the fire, Kristanna went from taking on a champion who rarely loses to being forced to fight one who is practically unvulnerable. Three years ago, Daniela came to RSI with the title belt already draped over her shoulder. And six title defenses later, she's still got it. So, Kristanna certainly has her work cut out for her.

Tonight's matchup demonstrated exactly what I mean. No doubt, Kristanna had this date circled on her calendar. The date she made her light heavyweight debut against the top pound for pound fighter in the entire UMMA. Certainly she was looking to make an impression.

After a brutal beating that lasted just over two minutes, Hantuchova was left untouched while Loken was left unconscious.

That was probably not the impression she was hoping to make. But the bold blonde managed to have an impact on Daniela, just the same.

"I watch the videos. I follow her career," said the Slovakian tennis star. "She is never afraid. She has very strong heart."

Indeed she does. And no one knows that better than Danni.

"Kristanna... she's had a tough time of it," said Loken's former rival. "I always had a lot of respect for her because of the way she hangs on. She just can't seem to make that final push, but she never fades away. She's always right there, ready to try again. And I shouldn't say she can't make that last push. She did grab the title away from me for a while, and she was impressive in that win. She gets this label as never being quite good enough, but you can't take her for granted. No matter what happens, she consistently holds everyone else off and stays in contention. You don't see that very much."

It's true. Despite a horde of title hungry fighters constantly nipping at her heels, Loken never seems to get swallowed up by the pack. So pay no attention to the naysayers. This rivalry is definitely going to be one to watch.

Where did you come from?

Emma Watson. Nadine Coyle. Kate Lawler. Grace Park.

As the reigning DEF queen, Tiffany Mulheron regularly steps into the cage to face the best featherweights the UMMA has to offer. Sometimes she loses. Sometimes she wins. But she knows these women, and they know her. Everyone knows what they're up against.

But where the hell did Lacey Chabert come from?

Riding a wave of success that started early last year, Lacey quietly clawed her way up to the top of the DEF rankings. Along the way she beat a number of quality opponents, including a pair of decisive victories over Park. The run earned her a date with Mulheron, who admitted beforehand that she wasn't quite sure what was coming.

"Lacey's always been kind of hit and miss," Tiff said in the days leading up to this event. "Until recently, she's been a mystery. She's repeatedly shown she can beat just about anyone, but every big victory seemed to be followed by a string of tough losses. Now, she's been on this kind of soft run that nobody really noticed. So, I have to admit I didn't know as much as I should have about her game."

Indeed, Lacey's history has been very up and down. An impressive, hard-fought win over the iconic Rachel McAdams gave the Raccoon fighter a huge boost in the rankings, but she gave over two thirds of that gain back by losing her next two outings. Likewise, she scored a shocking win over Coyle at the 2014 Lingerie tournament that earned her nearly two hundred points in the rankings. Then, she shed nearly three quarters of that by dropping three of her next four fights.

Still, a gain is a gain, and Chabert has been much more consistent over the last fifteen months. So, Tiff knew not to take things lightly. She did her homework and made sure she was ready. And the prep work showed. As expected, Lacey was no pushover, putting up a stiff fight against the champion. But, in the end, Tiffany was simply too much. Mulheron really poured things on in the second round and the prolific voice actress had no answers, finally succumbing to the assault a little over three minutes into the frame.

The win exposed the sizable gap between Lacey's skill level and Tiffany's. She simply wasn't ready for everything Mulheron had to offer. But, can the cheerful brunette find a way to bridge that gap?

"Who knows?" Mulheron said frankly. "She's shown an almost unreal ability to raise her game when she needs to. So, I wouldn't put it past her. She's proven she can adapt."

Well, she's very likely going to get the chance. Kate Lawler's somewhat surprising loss to Christina Milian leaves Chabert in the top contender's slot, meaning she'll likely get another crack at Tiffany. Don't be too surprised if she shows up more prepared than ever when that moment comes.
