The Agony of Victory

Celebrating a new divisional champion is supposed to be more fun than this.

It's not that Torrie Wilson's accomplishment isn't worth celebrating. These past eighteen months or so have easily been the best of her lengthy career, and that alone is worthy of a party. Going back to the 2014 Red Carpet tournament, she's won an astounding twenty-two of her twenty-seven fights. Along the way, she picked up titles at that event and last year's Hit The Beach tournament. On top of all that, she came into tonight riding an eleven fight winning streak.

Tonight was her night to shine. Her chance to put an exclamation point on one helluva run. And she went out there and made it happen.

"This what you dream about," RSI's new champion said afterward. "It's why we all do this, to begin with."

And, make no mistake... the women of RSI are happy about this. One after another, Torrie's teammates lined up to sing her praises.

"When Torrie joined us, she was buried at the bottom of the division," stable leader Danneel Harris said. "Her climb has been as amazing as it's been difficult, and it's been stunning to watch. This title... I don't think there was really any other way for the story to end."

"There's no question she deserves it," said Emily VanCamp. "She's worked her ass off, and she's been proving herself for over a year now."

"She should carry the belt with her everywhere she goes, if you ask me," said Emma Watson. "With the run she's had, she's earned that right."

There's really nothing I can add to those statements. Under normal circumstances, the women of RSI would be going absolutely crazy. But, there's something that's holding them back... and that's where the agony comes in.

In order to win the title tonight, Torrie had to take it from her friend, stablemate and training partner... Stephanie McMahon.

Winning tonight wasn't just the culmination of a dream for Torrie. It was her job. When you step in that cage, you fight to win. It doesn't matter who the opponent is. But, when you cap off a run like the one Torrie's had by winning the title and cementing your place as the best fighter in your division... well, you're not supposed to have to say things like this afterward:

"Winning tonight was one of the best moments of my life, but I hate what it cost Steph."

Sad as it is, that's the reality. Not only are Torrie and Steph close friends, but Steph is the one Torrie credits with getting her game on track.

"I would not be here tonight if it weren't for her," the teary-eyed blond said of her teammate. "Training with Steph was the reason I came to RSI. She's the one who got me where I am. She's the one who taught me how to win."

For her part, McMahon has been mostly silent. She attended her post-fight press conference, but only to offer her public congragulations to the new champion. She refused to answer questions about her performance or her future, and she eventually cut the session short. She was more polite than I expected she'd be, but the fact was that she's simply not ready to answer those questions, right now.

In other words, she's pissed. Not about losing to Torrie. No one knows better than Steph what Torrie is capable of. And Steph is not afraid of a little competition. So, I'm sure she's not happy she lost, but I'm also positive she would love the opportunity to compete with Wilson to get that title back. But that isn't what's going to happen, and that's what has her so ticked off.

As RSI's Fighter Development Coordinator, Steph goes to great lengths to make sure every RSI fighter is prepared every time they step in that cage. Take a poll, and I guarantee every one of them would say they'd be lost without access to the extensive library of knowledge Steph has to share with them. And McMahon's reward? One god-damn setback after another. Befuddling losing streaks. Momentum killing injuries. She's had to repeatedly fight her way to the top, only to have some new obstacle drag her back down. Now, another long comeback road lies before her.

Normally, when a new champion is crowned, her predecessor gets first shot at taking the title back. That seems logical, since the previous champion almost always becomes the top ranked contender.

Almost always, but not in this case.

Yet another injury cost Steph significant time last year, which caused her to slip to fourth in the divisional rankings. Of course, the injury also protected her status as champion, and the hope was that she could rebuild her ranking with the belt in her hands. Tonight's loss laid waste to that idea. Now, it seems she'll have to do it the hard way, one more time.

Though I know she's kicking herself now, believe me when I say that Steph is the only one that's disappointed in her. Even as they celebrate Torrie's accomplishment, the fighters of RSI have a deep appreciation for everything Steph's prowess in the cage and for everything she's done for each of them. After all, when you go out there and spend a round getting your head beat in, nothing sets you straight like turning to find Steph waiting for you in the corner. Yeah, she's gonna spend the next minute handing you your ass over all the things you did wrong, but, when it's over, you'll be convinced that the next round is gonna be your round. That's the power of Steph.

Sure, she's gruff, ill-tempered, acerbic, curt and dismissive... but we love her anyway.

But this is not a night for lamenting Steph's situation. She'll be back and better than ever. Her teammates and I will see to it, and we don't care if she likes that or not. But tonight is about Torrie.

This title is the crown jewel of her career. She's put the work in. She's produced the results. She deserved the spotlight all to herself and every one of her teammates are going to help her celebrate what she's done here in New Zealand. That includes Steph, who, even in her darkest moments, knows how to be there for her teammates... through good and bad.

As for me, I expect to be celebrating for a long time to come. Because I no longer have a superstar to lead my superheavyweight group.

Instead, I have two.

Unrealized expectations

From the height of anticipation to the depths of despair. The matchup between Catherine Bell and Veronica Varekova had a lot of heat behind it, coming into tonight. After Cat quietly ascended to the SCQ title, no one was quite sure how long she'd stick. But neither Tricia Helfer or Elisabetta Canalis could make a dent in the champ's defenses, and Mac went from short-term surprise to established favorite in short order. Meanwhile, Veronica turned a lot of heads with a tournament run at Hit The Beach that included victories over Traci Bingham and the vaunted Caroline Wozniacki. The run ended with a nasty injury against eventual champion Samantha Hoopes, but Veronica got things right back on track with a couple of nice victories at Open Fight Night. So, expectations were high that she would be the one to give Mac a run for her money.

And, for nearly three minutes, she did.

This one started out looking like it was going to deliver the goods. Catherine jumped out to a fast start, but Varekova fought back, making it clear she wasn't going to wilt. Then Cat caught her with a wicked counter that opened up a gash on the blond's cheek. Thought Veronica was still game to continue, referee Drew Barrymore determined the wound was too severe for her to go on. Though Varekova was bleeding badly, she was clearly alert and aware, making the decision questionable, at best. Regardless, a fight that had been building toward the war the fans were hoping for was suddenly over, leaving the Kiwi crowd largely disappointed.

The loss dropped Varekova to third in the division. Worse yet, it appears the injury is going to cause her to miss at least one event. So, it may be a while before we get the chance to see these two go at it, again.

Mac made short work of the post-fight question and answer session, saying only that she was as disappointed as everyone else in the way the fight ended, and wishing Varekova a speedy recovery. In the meantime, all signs are pointing to Traci Bingham getting the chance to retake the divisional crown from Bell at the next SCQ event.

Not quite enough

I'd be lying if I said I was surprised about the way Roselyn Sanchez fell to Sarah Chalke tonight. Ros was working her ass off to improve her takedown defense in preparation for this fight, and we were all impressed with the strides she made. But there were still times when she slipped, and that left her vulnerable. Unfortunately, she slipped right out of the gate here in Auckland, resulting in a blink-and-you-missed it loss that's going to be hard to swallow.

"There's not much to say," the Devious Maids actress admitted afterward. "My training was spot on. I knew what she was going to do, and I let her do it. If you can't keep your feet against a fighter like Sarah, you'll lose every time. I knew what I needed to do, and I just didn't do it."

In all honesty, I don't have much to say, either. Ros obviously knows what went wrong, and she knows what she needs to do now. We'll keep working on it, and hope Ros can solidify things before these two meet again.

A striking victory

Kelly Hu has been tearing it up recently, including a stunning victory over teammate and ESL champion Tiffany Mulheron. But her record against Grace Park remained far less than stellar. With only one win in four tries, Kelly hasn't had much success solving the Hawaii Five-0 star in the cage. But, tonight was a big step in the right direction, as it was all Kelly all the time in this one. After an initial scrum that found the women tangled up on the mat, Kelly took control and never let up, hammering her younger opponent at every turn. Every punch and kick she threw seemed to find it's mark, while Grace was made to pay every time she tried to mount an attack of her own.

Despite the striking clinic she put on, Kelly was far from arrogant at her post-fight press conference. When asked if she felt her commanding win meant the tide was turning in this rivalry, she was quick to squash such talk.

"Grace has always been a tough opponent for me," said Ashley Tisdale's voice acting cohort. "My toughest, probably. So, I'm proud of this win, but I don't think it means anything bigger. I think every fight with her will continue to be a battle."

Well, then shine up your armor, baby. Because this win was only enough to have the two women swap ranking positions. That means a sixth meeting can't be far away.

The new 'kid'

Before I talk about how our next athlete performed tonight, perhaps I should publicly welcome her to the roster. After the recent collapse of the Kickass stable, there was a mad rush to negotiate new deals with the suddenly homeless athletes. Though I understand there was quite a brouhaha over Keira Knightley, my sights were squarely set on Stana Katic from the outset. And, despite Dragon's best efforts to thwart me, I was not going to be denied. Not with the one and only Kate Beckett available.

With little time to prepare for tonight's event, Kate... er, I mean, Stana... got to know her fellow middleweight teammates very well, very quickly. And, though it pains me to say it, I have to hand it to Britney and Elisha. They were putting in the overtime to get their new teammate prepped. A sudden — and highly questionable — injury to opponent Paula Patton put an end to things before Stana got much of a chance to show what the training had done for her, but she expressed her appreciation, nonetheless.

"I'd heard a lot about those two, but it was interesting to see things for myself," she said of the blond duo. "While it seems many of the rumors are true, I also found out a lot about what they're made of." When pressed to explain what she meant by that, Katic's response was much more direct. "It means they're exactly the goofballs people think they are, but they're the goofballs you want in your corner when things get tough."

Sounds to me like the new girl has a firm grasp of the situation.

Familiarity breeds... victory?

After getting slapped around in their first matchup several years ago, Marzia Prince has made quick work of Jenny McCarthy for the second time in roughly eight months. Both recent fights ended in just over a minute, with Marzia in complete control throughout... a fact that left the fitness model wondering why she couldn't perform that way more often.

"It's crazy," the age-defying blond said. "Sometimes, I feel like I can't do anything wrong. Other times, I feel like I have no idea what I'm doing."

In Marzia's defense, some of her recent losses have come against the likes of Adriana Lima and Caroline Wozniacki... both of whom make a living by making their opponents feel utterly useless. Though her record is the very definition of pedestrian, we've seen the things she can do in the cage. She just needs to keep grinding, and things will start falling into place for her.

That's all for this event, but a quick hop across the Tasman Sea will find us in Melbourne next, where the women of the DEF will take center stage. Stable leader Danneel Harris will be leading the charge for RSI, as she tries to hang on against the talented, powerful and aggressive Cheryl Cole. Meanwhile, Britney Spears will get yet another crack at Laura Vandervoort's middleweight title. Will this finally be the night the frustration ends? Join us then, and we'll find out.
