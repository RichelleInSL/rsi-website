Vindicated

The life of an MMA fighter is not an easy one. It requires endless training and preparation, all in pursuit of a championship dream that may never come true. Although many try, few achieve their ultimate goal. And the road is paved with disappointments, setbacks and heartache. Three RSI athletes came into this tournament having seen more than their share of tough times, recently. Alyson Michalka and Britney Spears both came up short in big spots at the recent team event. Both have also come tantalizingly close to winning their divisional belts, only to see opportunities slip away. Meanwhile, Tiffany Mulheron struggled back from a horrific beating suffered at the hands of her hated rival, Nadine Coyle, only to have it happen all over again in their latest title matchup. But MMA is a sport that requires an ability to constantly put the past behind you, and that's exactly what all three of them did in this tournament. And in the end, all three went home very happy.

Alyson is used to showing up on fight night prepared for pain. She regularly faces off against the likes of Brooklyn Decker, Erin Heatherton and India Reynolds. At tournament time, the list of potential opponents expands to include Caprice Bourret, Heidi Klum, Holly Willoughby and Olivia Wilde. Not to mention, Aly's friend and training partner, Daniela Hantuchova. It's an impressive and lethal line up, and it often makes me wonder my Aly bothers to get out of bed in the morning. But she's come to enjoy the challenge, and she has proven time and again that she can compete with any of these women.

In her last outing, Aly had a disappointing performance against Klum at Team Warfare. It was one of the few times I'd seen her look truly over-matched, and she was clearly angry with herself afterward. In the weeks that followed, she's been totally focused on preparing for this tournament. She was the defending champion coming in, and she was determined to have a good showing for herself and for her fans. Well, she certainly succeeded, as she was far more than good here in Boise... she was exceptional. In fact, she was so good, she'll be the defending champion again next time.

As usual, Aly's path was anything but easy. Reaching the final meant going through both Reynolds and Bourret. India is a tough and aggressive fighter, and she's tripped Aly up more than once on her quest for the FAC championship. Caprice, on the other hand, is among the most experienced fighters in the UMMA. But Aly was a woman on a mission, and she powered her way past both with relative ease. That brought her to the final and the title defense she longed for. The only one standing in her way was... you guessed it... Heidi Klum.

Aly may have looked over-matched against Heidi at the team event, but she looked right at home this time around. As in the previous fight, Klum came out swinging. But this time, Aly swung back. Those who've seen her in the past know that Aly never gives an inch to anyone, preferring to go toe-to-toe with her adversaries. It's a strategy that drives me nuts, and I have begged her in the past to find a less painful approach. But time has taught me that this is "the Aly way," and she's learned to make it work for her. The results are often brutal, though, and that was the case here as a two and a half minute exchange left both women bloodied. Early on, it looked like this one could go either way. About a minute into the fight, however, Aly scored with a solid kick to the head and some great footwork allowed her to get inside and tie Heidi up in the clinch. Klum seemed dazed by the kick and offered little defense as Aly hammered away. Heidi gathered herself and tried to fight out of it, but Aly was in control and on a roll. There was no way she was letting her opponent recover, and there was far too much time left for Heidi to hang on. As Klum started to wilt, Aly put her away with one... two... three knees to her unprotected face. Heidi dropped like a ragdoll and Aly emerged as champion. Again.

Coming in to this event, Heidi had put together an impressive twelve fight winning streak that included wins over both Aly and Daniela. It also saw her take the SCQ title away from long-time champion Bourret. Having been the one to put the brakes on Klum's runaway train was not something Aly overlooked. "Heidi seems to have found some kind of magic formula recently," said Michalka. "I felt prepared for her at the team event, and she rolled right over me, anyway," she continued. "When I saw it would be the two of us in the final, I knew I was going to need everything I had to stop her."

I don't know what I can say here that I haven't said before. Knowing her as we do, there isn't a single member of the RSI family who would question Aly's skill and heart. And there isn't a single one of us who doesn't audibly groan when fate sneaks in and bites her on the ass one more time. To see her hard work and dedication pay off once in a while is a treat for all of us, and it serves as a reminder that her FAC title hopes could still become reality. Keep swinging, Aly. Just keep swinging.

Not that long ago, Britney Spears put together quite a winning streak of her own, only to see it stopped cold when it counted most. During the streak, Brit defeated DEF champion Malin Akerman twice, winning convincingly both times. Riding high after taking home the title from the Hit the Beach tournament, Britney was set to continue her roll when she took on Akerman for the third time... this one with the title on the line. But Malin wasn't going down without a fight, and she proved to be too much for Britney that night. Spears has struggled some since then, including losing a second championship shot against Malin a few months ago. Britney needed something big to get her back on course, and she found just the thing she was looking for here in the mountains of Idaho.

Britney's quest started simply enough with a couple of fairly easy victories, but the third round held a surprise as Britney met up with a familiar foe a little earlier than usual. After a couple of easy wins of her own, Malin Akerman stood ready to put Spears to the test, once again. But Britney was more than ready this time, and the two women went to war. The first round proved why I'm not a judge, as I clearly saw it for Britney. Two of the three judges saw it differently though, and the round went to Malin. The women went blow for blow in the second, with blood spilled in both corners. Again, I scored it in Britney's favor, but this one was closer. The judges scored it even, and I'll admit I could see it that way. The third round was all Britney, as Malin looked completely exhausted. She was so wiped, she wasn't able to mount any offense whatsoever. Though the round went to Britney, Malin was still standing when it was over. So, one round to Malin. One round to Britney. And one round that was too close to call. Guess what? Bonus time.

With no clear winner after the three scheduled rounds, the women fought a fourth round to try and break the tie. As in the third, Malin just didn't seem to have anything left. But she refused to fold as Britney scored repeatedly with heavy shots. After about two minutes, the ref stopped the action to check a cut near Malin's mouth. I'm not sure what the doctor saw, but he decided Malin could not fight on with the injury. It was a pretty anti-climactic ending to a highly entertaining battle, but make no mistake... Britney was the better fighter in this one.

After four rounds with Malin, Britney probably could have used a month or two to recover. Instead, she drew top ESL contender Lindsay Ellingson. Still, Britney was close enough to taste the championship. With Malin behind her and Doutzen Kroes having been upset in stunning fashion by Hillary Swank, Brit knew the title was up for grabs. She dug deep and found a reserve somewhere, because she proceeded to tear Ellingson apart. As with Akerman, the official record of the bout will show Britney won due to an injury suffered by her opponent. But as with Akerman, the truth is that Britney was better. Hands down.

So, for the second time this year, Britney found herself in the championship round of a UMMA tournament. And for the second time this year, she found herself face-to-face with friend and stablemate Jennifer Stano. I keep wondering when Stano's bubble is going to burst. She came into this tournament with only 18 fights under her belt. However, when you consider that she's now made a finals run at the Hit the Beach tournament, a finals run at the Blood on the Red Carpet tournament and now a finals run here... it becomes easy to see why she's got fans so excited. Throw in not one but TWO victories over UMMA legend Doutzen Kroes... maybe it's time for me to stop expecting the other shoe to drop and simply start believing.

Unfortunately for Stano, this tournament ended the same way the earlier ones did... with her on the losing end. But holy shit... did she put up a fight in this one. By the end of the first round, Britney was a bloody mess. By the end of the second, they were BOTH a bloody mess. By the end of the final round, I just wanted it over with before somebody got killed. Though it felt like Britney held the advantage, it was impossible to tell for sure how this one was going to come out. And the judges seemed to feel the same way... with two of them giving the edge to Britney, and the third scoring it even. Though Britney won the fight, it's impossible to feel too badly for Jennifer. Three tournament finals, two wins over Doutzen and now an epic nail-biter with Britney. And she's still just a baby. The possibilities for her as just about endless.

Getting back to Britney... though the divisional title she craves continues to elude her, her results this year have gone a long way toward proving she can compete on the highest levels here in the UMMA. Not that I needed any convincing. I believed in Britney when we signed her, and I didn't make her a team leader on a whim. Though she intentionally grates on my nerves, I know quite well what she can do... both in and out of the cage. She's become part of the foundation here at RSI, and trust me when I tell you... the whole building is celebrating this win.

Meanwhile, just about everything went crazy in the featherweight division at this tournament. With FAC champion Emma Watson and DEF champion Rachel McAdams sitting this one out, the two remaining divisional title holders were Nadine Coyle and Portia de Rossi. Both were felled early in stunning upsets, leaving the road to the championship wide open. With the best of the best out early, the later rounds felt sort of lackluster for this division. Lackluster, that is, until the final round.

When the dust settled, the two left standing were Kate Lawler and Tiffany Mulheron. Both are the top contenders in their respective divisions, and both were eager to get their hands on the belt. The women came out tentative, neither wanting to make the big mistake. The first round had a a lot of back and forth, with Tiffany controlling most of the action to win the round. It was more of the same in the second, and again, Tiffany did enough to put the round in her column. Sensing her opportunity slipping away, Lawler turned up the heat in the third round, and each of the judges gave her the round by a 2 point margin. That evened the scoring, so, once again, it was bonus time.

What happened in that extra fourth round was the subject of some controversy afterward. The fighters spent much of the round grappling on the mat, which is primarily Kate's territory. And Kate did seem to be controlling most of the action. But Tiffany was far from passive, scoring whenever she could... even dealing some considerable damage from the bottom. When it was over, it was hard to say who the round should go to. All three judges saw it for Tiffany, though, and RSI had it's third champion of the night.

The evenness of the fight and the somewhat controversial decision were not lost on Tiffany, afterward. "I know how close this was," admitted the Scottish blonde. "If the decision had gone for Kate, I would have been pissed. So, I have a pretty good idea how she feels about this, and I don't blame her." I have to agree. I'm not saying that Kate was the clear winner, or that she was robbed. But I can't say Tiffany was the clear winner, either. This was one of the closest fights I've ever seen, and I'm glad I wasn't one of the ones who had to make the decision. While I'm happy with the way things turned out, I'm certain Kate and RWB management are not. All I can do is echo Tiffany's sentiments... I don't blame them.

Meanwhile, back on the home front, this was a big win for Tiffany. She has shown in the past that she can compete with anyone, and bringing home this title only helps to cement her position. I know she has struggled in the past, and I know she has her demons. But despite the setbacks, she's been willing to push through the adversity and work hard to better herself.

Overall, this was a fine event for RSI. Not only did three of our fighters bring home titles, but seven of the sixteen finalists were Rampage athletes. Aside from Stano, the others who came up just a little short included Petra Cubonova and RSI team leader, Danneel Harris. But the most stunning of all would have to be rookie Sabine Jemeljanova, who came all the way from the bottom of the bracket to make the Super Heavyweight final.

Coming in with only seven fights worth of experience, Sabine opened her tournament with an absolutely electrifying win over SCQ champion Veronika Zemanova. Veronika got her licks in, but Sabine just kept coming... overwhelming the veteran fighter with a precise and ruthless striking game. The rookie followed that up with a blowout win over the more experienced Kate Upton. Then came a sensational, blink-and-you-missed-it knockout of top FAC contender Caitlyn Winters. Next, it was top ranked ESL fighter Alice Goodwin, and, again, Sabine scored a sudden knockout to take the win. Unfortunately, though, that's where Sabine's luck ran out, as it all came crashing down in a big way against RWB's Sophie Howard. Not only did Sabine come out on the losing end, but she suffered a brutal injury to her face that's going to have her sidelined for a while. It was a disappointing and disheartening way to end an amazing run, and it really ruined what should have been a fantastic story. But worry not... RSI will get her the care she needs to get her back on track as soon as possible. Then we'll see if she was just having a good run, or if we have another potential Jennifer Stano on our hands.

Well, that's all from this event. Join us next time as the women of SCQ take to the cage. Both Torrie Wilson and Stephanie McMahon will be in action, but the big one for RSI is going to be the title matchup between Catherine Bell and Traci Bingham. I have to admit that, when I saw that Mac was in line for a title shot, I was a little surprised. Catherine isn't one to do a lot of talking, and she sort of snuck up the rankings without my realizing it. I guess it's like they say... it's always the quiet ones you have to keep an eye on. Well, rest assured, I'll be watching closely when the bell rings in Anaheim. We'll see you there.
