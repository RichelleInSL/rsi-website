DISAPPOINTMENT, PART TWO

Earlier this month, Aly Michalka suffered a tough loss to Brooklyn Decker when the ref put an early stop to things. The official scoring decision listed the reason for the stoppage as Small Cut - Right Cheek. Up to that point, the match had been very exciting, with Aly putting up more of a fight than anyone had expected. Still, she was clearly behind on points, and it would have been a tough comeback. So, we were disappointed, but we got over it. Now, he we are, only a few short weeks later, it's happened again. This time, it cost Stephanie McMahon her shot at a championship.

RSI is making an effort to let cooler heads prevail on this, but we're really struggling with it. While the Michalka-Decker situation was a bad ending to a great fight, this one was a terrible ending to a fight that had barely even gotten started. Stephanie and Veronika Zemanova are two of the finest strikers the UMMA has to offer. Veronika came out of the gate fast, and scored early. Stephanie was fighting back, crushing Zemanova with a straight right that would have dropped most fighters. The fight was ON, and the crowd was getting the war they had paid to see. And then, it was suddenly all over. A little blood on Stephanie's chin, and the marquee matchup of the night was basically cancelled.

The scoring decision reads Small Cut - Chin. A familiar and unsatisfactory end to another highly anticipated fight. The emotion of Aly's loss prevented Steph from giving comment that night, and she has continued to maintain that silence.

Steph was certainly emotional about tonight's situation, as well. Silent, however, she was not.

"This is bullshit," said a clearly disgusted McMahon. "We were just getting started. She tagged me good a couple of times, but I saw a little blood on her face, too. It's always like that with us. This is what? Our twelth... thirteenth fight? We pound each other. That's how we fight, and it's what the fans want. It's brutal, but so what? This is the Super Heavyweight division. It's where the big girls play. I wasn't hurt. I wanted more."

From a management standpoint, Steph is now 0-2 since moving to the Corrupt Souls organization. After a 29-2 start to her career, you have to wonder if her confidence might be a little shaken.

What does Steph think?

"If she wants to call herself the champion, that's fine. But, she knows this didn't decide anything. I'll be back, and I'm taking that belt. Even if I have to step over her broken body to get it."

Upon further review, we've decided her confidence level is not a concern.

Meanwhile, while the fight at the top of the card turned out to be a disaster, the one near the bottom featured one hell of a debut performance. Rookie Marzia Prince faced a tough opponent in Sarah Silverman. While Sarah lacks offensive power, she's the sort of control and defend fighter that can give an opponent fits. And, throughout much of the first round, Silverman was certainly running the show. But, Marzia seemed to find her range toward the end of the round, and she was much more aggressive in the second. A prolific boxer, Prince scored early with a big left that rattled her opponent, and Silverman was on the canvas not long after that. She tried to survive the round, but there was just too much time left. After beating her bloody, Marzia finally put her away with a bone crushing shot about halfway through the round.

Though she still has a lot to learn, Prince seemed pleased with the results of her first fight. "I felt good out there. I think I was a little nervous early on, but I started to loosen up as I got more into it. My corner was screaming at me in the first round, but they seemed a lot happier in the second," Marzia said. A big smile spread across her face as she added, "I know I was."

Well, this brings us to the point where we hype the RSI fighters featured in the next event. And, no doubt, there is much to be excited about. After a surprise early debut at Open Fight Night, rookie Holly Bricken will see her first divisional action against Laetitia Casta. Also, welterweight champion Danneel Harris will face top contender Kristanna Loken in a non-title bout. No doubt Kistanna will be looking to prove she deserves a chance to take that belt sometime soon. And then, we have the middleweight matchup of the night...

Earlier this month, RSI made some big organizational changes. I had expressed a desire to avoid having multiple RSI fighters fighting in the same division. These girls are training partners and, often, friends. Therefore, we wanted to avoid situations where they would also have to become opponents. Most of the changes were accomplished without issue, as the girls involved didn't really feel that their relationship with RSI was working out, anyway. But, there was one cut that I knew was going to be difficult. Still, it had to be done. So, I contacted Elisha Cuthbert and asked her to come to my office the next day. It was no secret why I had asked her to come, and Elisha knew better than most what was going on. After all, the driving force behind the new policy was the devastating injury she had suffered in her match against RSI stablemate Britney Spears. Injury is part of the game, but I just couldn't deal with the thought of one RSI fighter injuring another.

The following morning, Elisha came as I'd requested. But, she refused to go down without a fight, and I don't know why I thought she would. When we brought Elisha on, it was her scrappiness and heart that lured us in. She didn't have the physical tools most of the girls have, but she didn't let that stop her. And, now, here she was. Staring me down with those icy blue eyes, refusing to accept what had already been decided. What I'd expected to be a difficult conversation turned into a verbal brawl in my office. She had her points, and she forced me to defend myself at every turn. But, I stubbornly refused to budge. However, I knew I was done for when Britney, herself, chose to get involved and rally to Elisha's defense. I'd seen these two women beat the living hell out of each other on a daily basis during training. Now, they were standing shoulder to shoulder in my office, united in an effort to make me change my mind. Really... what the hell was I supposed to do? In an effort to save face, I made it clear that my new policy would remain in place. But, on that morning, what shall henceforth be known as the "Cuthbert Exception" was born.

And so it is that these two close friends and fierce competitors will face off again inside the octagon. It should be a great show. And, if anyone needs me, I'll be the one up in the RSI suite with my head under the seat cushion, refusing to watch.
