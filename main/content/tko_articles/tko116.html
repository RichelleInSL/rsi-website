The Unsung Hero

When I made the decision to start Rampage Sports, I hadn't the slightest clue what I was doing. Though I had some experience in the fight game, I was well aware that I could barely manage to dress myself, let alone manage a stable full of fighters. Clearly I was going to need a strong, capable team leader who could help me find my footing. So, I went out and found a fighter who had no leadership experience and no desire to gain any, and I gave the job to her. That makes sense, right? Well, call it genius. Call it dumb luck. Call it whatever the hell you want. Danneel Harris has been the yin to my yang since the very beginning, and it's worked out better than I'd ever imagined. Who was it that took Julianne Hough and personally transformed her into RSI's first home-grown champion? Danni. Who recognized the leadership potential inside Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert? Danni. Who was it that went out and dragged a depressed and disgusted Stephanie McMahon back into the RSI fold and literally slapped some sense into me, showing me how we'd been wasting Steph's abilities? Danni, again. So, when it comes time for me to celebrate RSI's accomplishments and laud it's champions, who gets top billing? Almost never Danni.

Sort of makes you wonder why she puts up with me.

Well, that nonsense stops today. Danneel successfully defended her DEF welterweight crown for the seventh time tonight, her fourth since she helped me found RSI. In between, she has lost the title twice. In both cases, she took it back the next time she got the chance. She is strong. She is smart. She is resilient. And, above all, she loves a good fight. It is HER personality that is woven into the fabric of RSI, not mine.

Challenger Kristanna Loken has given Danni some tough fights in the past. In fact, it was Kristanna who last took the crown away from RSI's team leader. It was a shocking loss that ended Danni's reign of nearly two full years. In that fight, Danni was victimized by the dreaded one-punch knockout. Many fighters would have proclaimed it a "lucky punch" and vowed to take revenge. What did Danni say? "She's (Kristanna) proven time and time again that she's no pushover. I knew she was going to win one eventually. It was just bound to happen." Rather than tearing her opponent down, Danni's statement seemed to legitimize Loken's victory. The press corp tried to give her the chance to take it back, specifically asking about the "lucky punch" theory. Danni refused to take the easy way out. "The bell rang, and she knocked me out," she said at the time. "I won't try to take that away from her. She won and she deserves credit for that." That started people wondering if Danni had the mettle it would take to get the title back. But, she wasn't stupid enough to let that kind of thinking get too far. "Take good care of my belt," she said, as she addressed Loken directly at the end of the press conference. "I'll be looking to take it back soon." True to her word, Danni did just that the next time the two faced each other.

You wanna know why the women of RSI keep their mouths shut and their eyes on the prize? It's because they learned it from Danni. I've said in the past that I don't like RSI fighters doing a lot of talking in the press. That's all well and good, but I'm fully aware that I'm not the reason they don't do it. It's because they look at the woman they call their leader... a woman who many consider to be RSI's single greatest champion... and they tell themselves, "Yeah. THAT's who I want to be."

Why don't I praise Danni more often? It's because I have neither the words nor the time to describe all that she's done for me. I've referred to her in the past as the rock that holds me up. Truth is, she's the rock that holds us all up. I've come to expect so much from her that I hardly notice how much she actually does. And she shoulders it all without the slightest complaint. She may not have wanted the team leader position originally, but she's certainly learned to own it since then.

So, in the end, this is really all your fault, Danni. I keep shoveling shit on your pile, and you just handle it like it's no big deal. The reason you don't get the praise you deserve is simple: you make the whole thing look too god-damn easy. But don't think the fact that I don't always say it means I don't see it. I know where RSI's real strength comes from. We all do. So, thanks... from all of us.

It takes a lot to make it to the top. The ability to fight on a championship level is rare. Even those that succeed usually find themselves in a constant battle to stay there. Just ask Julianne Hough, who swaps positions with rival Vikki Blows every time they meet in the cage. Or, ask Tiffany Mulheron, who managed to run down champion Nadine Coyle and even took the championship away from her for a short time. But Nadine has since reaffirmed her dominance while Tiffany has fallen back to the pack.

There are some champions, however, who separate themselves from the field. Champions who seem so far above their competition, they become almost untouchable. Doutzen Kroes sat atop the middleweight division since the inception of the UMMA. Irina Sheik held the lightweight championship for so long, no one could remember who the previous champion was. As great as both of those championship runs were, both did eventually come to an end.

Dominant champions like that are fewer these days, though Louise Glover is certainly headed that way. However, there are others who, despite having less dominant careers, always manage to step up when their title is on the line. Malin Akerman. Ashley Green. And of course, Adriana Lima.

Adriana's situation is unique in that all of her recent title challenges have come from the same fighter. Tonight represented Petra Cubonova's sixth opportunity to unseat Lima. All of them have been unsuccessful. So, while Adriana continues to celebrate her championship reign, there's no doubt that the frustration is mounting for Petra.

Four of those title fights came before Petra joined us at RSI. After that fourth championship loss, she started to slide backwards. It was a time when she could have given up. Many fighters in her position would have. But, with RSI's help, she has battled back to the position of top contender. Her performance has improved dramatically, including against Lima. And yet, that championship continues to elude her.

I suppose it's fitting that it should be this way. Adriana is a magnificent champion. Dethroning her should be nearly impossible. But, despite the setbacks, I'm confident Petra is going to get there one day. She has already shown she will not give up. And, as long as she doesn't quit, neither will RSI. We have a little experience in legend killing. After all, it was Emily VanCamp that finally ended Sheik's title run. And though it was Vassago's Laura Vandervoort who brought an end to Doutzen's historic reign, it was RSI's Jennifer Stano that got her started on the first losing streak of her storied career.

What's our magic formula? Hard work, a team approach and... something I'm forgetting.

Oh, yeah... NO FEAR.

Hang in there, Petra. We'll keep going until we find a way.

Fans that were looking forward to another all-out war between Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert received a shocking disappointment, as this one was all over in just a few seconds. Britney caught Elisha napping with a brutal high kick as soon as the bell sounded, and that was basically the end of it. I've said before that, though these two are close friends outside the cage, their all business once the latch slides into place. Britney proved it tonight as she immediately went in for the kill against her badly dazed training partner. True to form, Elisha refused to go down, and Britney continued to push forward and attack. Blow after blow landed unanswered, and it was clear that Leesh was out on her feet. With less than a minute gone by, referee Toccara Jones was forced to step in and put a stop to things. As the RSI medical team tended to Elisha, Britney stood on the opposite side of the cage, looking solemn and concerned.

"This wasn't the fight fans wanted to see," said the songstress afterward, "and it wasn't the fight I know Elisha is capable of." She shook her head lightly as she continued, "I know Leesh is angry with herself. I also know there's nothing I can say that will make it better. This kind of thing happens to all of us, and we all know it. But knowing doesn't help when it happens to you." When asked about the way she continued to attack a clearly defenseless Elisha, Britney was very matter-of-fact about it. "That's what we're trained to do, and it's the way she and I have agreed to do it. When we're in there, we're both fighting to win. I treated her the way I would have treated any opponent." She paused a moment before adding, "But, if you don't think it hurts me to see that happen to her, then you're wrong."

For her part, the usually chipper Elisha was in a predictably bad mood after the fight. "It goes without saying that I'm disappointed in the way I performed tonight," said the middleweight contender. "Britney and I know each other well, and we know we can each give the other a run for her money." She paused and ran her fingers through her blonde hair in frustration. "Look, it's no secret that she and I are friends," she said. "It's also no secret that we like competing with each other. So, I was looking forward to this fight." The actress looked off to the side of the room, seemingly staring at something no one else could see. "She and I play up our rivalry, mostly because it's fun. But, there's no shame in losing to Britney." She brought her focus back to the media members in the room and added, "But I am ashamed of my performance tonight. I expected the two of us to put on a good show for the fans, and it obviously did not go the way I'd planned."

I don't know what I can add here. I, along with the other members of the RSI family, can look at this from a somewhat unique perspective. We've seen these two in training. We've seen the way they compete... the way each of them constantly improves her game in an effort to one-up the other. They hold nothing back in practice, choosing instead to press each other. Both of them have improved dramatically as a result. So, I'm not disappointed for me. I get to see the Britney and Elisha show almost every day. I am disappointed for Elisha. I know how much losing this way hurts. I'm disappointed for Britney. I know that inflicting that type of loss on her friend could not have been fun. And I'm disappointed for the fans who were denied the chance to see something truly incredible. All is not lost, however. I fully expect Elisha to use this as fuel for her competitive fire. Something tells me a rematch is not all that far off.

A pair of nice victories for Danica Patrick and Kristen Bell here tonight. Danica scored her second victory in a row over rival Sienna Miller, and she did it in dominating fashion. Now, if she could just find a way to solve Tila Tequila, maybe we'd be getting somewhere. Meanwhile, Kristen was equally dominant as she put away Nelly Furtado. That makes three divisional wins in a row for RSI's princess of pain. Hmmm... I kinda like that one. If she keeps it up, who knows? Maybe I'll put it in capital letters and make it a thing.

A note about the upcoming Open Fight Night event: Rampage Sports has received the challenge from the Bad Ass Bunnies stable, and we are aware of the reasons behind it. The fact that this new stable tried to recruit Shelby Chesnes does not bother me at all. As it's owned and operated by Playboy Enterprises, this new stable has a clear theme to it. Shelby certainly fits that theme. On top of that, Shelby has shown a lot of promise in her fledgling career. I think she would warrant attention from any manager looking for a fresh, talented young fighter.

I also have no issue with Playboy seeking advice from Shelby. Though I consider her a member of the RSI family, I know that Shelby is also part of the larger, internationally recognized family that comes with being a Playboy model. Succeeding in the UMMA is difficult, and I don't fault Playboy for utilizing every resource they have available to them.

The problem here is that Bad Ass Bunnies management is angry with Shelby for letting me know what was happening. They claim that Shelby betrayed their trust by coming to me, and that this jeopardized their plans. Nothing could be further from the truth.

Let me be clear... Shelby is proud of her role as a Playboy Playmate. She's proud of what she's accomplished with Playboy's help, and she's grateful for all they've done for her. But here in the UMMA, she is, first and foremost, a member of the RSI family. She came to me because she didn't want me to hear about her being approached through the grapevine. In other words, she didn't want me to think she'd violated MY trust. She knows me well enough to know I would do nothing to ruin this new stable's plans. The growth of the UMMA is important to all of us, and the notoriety of the Playboy brand will bring a lot of interest our way. I would love to see their stable succeed.

I think the real issue here is that Playboy feels jilted by Shelby's decision to stay with RSI. I'm sure they're not used to being told no very often, and I'm certain it angers them that Shelby elected to remain with a tiny, privately owned enterprise such as this one. My suggestion: get over it. You're not likely to succeed in the UMMA if you focus on such petty nonsense. Being pretty and cool gets you nothing, here.

As for the challenge, Shelby has gladly accepted. Regardless of the underlying inspiration, her only concern is pushing herself and advancing her career. Though Kara Monaco has yet to debut in the UMMA, she has a reputation as a tough, intelligent fighter. She is exactly the kind of test Shelby is looking for.

On a separate note, I noticed that the Hellfire stable once again had some things to say about RSI in it's last press release. I'd like to take this opportunity to clarify a few things, and I'm hopeful that will be the end of it. Hopeful, but far from confident.

Anyway, Mr. X was good enough to state for the record that they do not believe RSI was actually cheating. He used the old "redhead and their temper" excuse to explain away Karen Gillan's earlier accusations. That was a good call, as he knows I'm very understanding on that front. Travis then went on to explain that Karen did not intend to accuse the RSI fighters of intentionally trying to injure their opponents. Apparently, she meant to say our fighters often try to "exploit a weakness in their opponents." Something tells me he didn't use the word "weakness" the way most people would. But I'll take the statement at face value and admit that it's true. After all, I believe exploiting a weakness in your opponent is more commonly referred to as "winning." And, I have to admit, the women of RSI do that all the time.

So, no tears on our end, Travis, but thanks for the sympathy. And I repeat my hope that this can be the end of it. You have a trio of fine champions on your roster, and a number of young fighters I have a lot of respect for. At the very least, let's try to keep things civil, OK? For example, I believe Karen had a suggestion involving myself and a leprechaun. As delightful as it sounds, I'm probably going to pass. But that aside, I have to admit that's much more the sort of thing I expect from a fine redhead such as Karen. She has problems controlling her temper? I have those problems, too. You want to hurl insults? Go ahead, if that makes you feel better. You want to make baseless accusations against my fighters? Then we have a problem.

Anyway, I'm not sure what the Hellfire stable is going to do with themselves this time around, as they had no fights against Rampage opponents. And, to make matters worse, we're going to be Hellfire-free next time out, as well. Wow... I hope Travis doesn't suddenly find himself with nothing to talk about.

Speaking of the next event, it's time for another round of the always entertaining war between Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik. Since Emily finally wrestled the title away from the ILF veteran, the two have gone back and forth in these championship matchups. Will Emily finally mount her first successful defense? Also, Olivia Wilde gets another shot at Daniela Hantuchova's light heavyweight crown. And Tiffany Mulheron looks to come back from the horrific beating she suffered at the hands of Nadine Coyle last time out, and prove she has what it takes to compete with the Vassago team leader. It's sure to be another night to remember, so don't miss it.
