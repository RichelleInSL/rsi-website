Feeling It

There is no question that Emily VanCamp is a warrior. At times, she has outlasted and outperformed the greatest fighters in the game. At others, she has been beaten and bloodied to the point that she had to be helped from the cage. But very time, she comes back ready for more.

But, while her heart and dedication cannot be denied, even she has to admit that winning makes everything easier.

"Getting back to training is a lot more fun when you see it's working," the Captain America actress said. "Honestly, I don't know what the secret is. I've just been feeling it lately."

And you can take the word lately with a big grain of salt. Over the last eighteen months, Emily has put together an astounding record of nineteen wins and only three losses. Take away the crazy, mixed-weight events such as Open Fight Night and Global Rivalry, and the record improves to a perfect nineteen for nineteen. That would be an achievement to be proud of, regardless of the circumstances. In the ultra-competitive world of today's UMMA, it's like accomplishing the impossible.

What kind of competition has Emily had to deal with, specifically? Top DEF contenders Roxanne McKee and Miranda Kerr. New DEF champion Joanna Krupa. Former FAC champion Ashley Greene. And, of course, the legendary Irina Sheik.

Tonight was the latest chapter in the never-ending story of Emily and Irina. Trading the title back and forth is a common occurrence between these two, and the show they put on is always can't miss entertainment. Even tonight, when Em consistently seemed just a step quicker than her ILF nemesis, it took the full fifteen minutes to come up with a winner. But, in the end, the champion had done more than enough to keep her title.

Strangely enough, in a rivalry so constant it's practically set in stone, tonight represented a small departure from the norm. For the first time in what feels like forever, one of the women successfully defended the belt. With this win, Em broke a pattern that goes back almost two and a half years, and includes nine title tilts.

With everything going her way recently, Em has plenty to crow about. So...

"Why do I have to answer these questions, every time?" VanCamp said when asked about her standing in relation to Sheik. "Has something changed since last time? Is she no longer one of the greatest fighters in the league? Of course she is. End of discussion."

No surprise there. Since the minute she first looped the belt around her waist, Em has shown nothing but respect for Irina and all of her opponents, really. And where does she learn such humility?

"I think it's pretty obvious that all of us follow Danni's example," she said. "She works hard and she's always ready when fight night comes, but she never takes herself too seriously. She's just having fun out there."

Harris. I should have known.

Truth is, I don't believe a word of it. No one else is responsible for Emily's unpretentious and unassuming take on her accomplishments. The fact is, modesty is as much a part of her DNA as that heart and dedication I mentioned earlier.

But hey, if there's any way I can blame Danni for something, I'm all for it.

Defensive Stance

What do you do when your opponent's defense is so tremendous that you just can't seem to lay a glove on her?

Keep your cool and make damn sure she doesn't hit you, either.

That was the strategy Julianne Hough broke out against Keira Knightley tonight. With Keira eating up everything she threw at her, Jules knew her own defense had to be just about perfect. Though she was never really able to shift into attack mode the way she wanted to, she chipped away when she could while keeping Knightley at bay. Maintaining that kind of control made an impression on the judges, who called the fight heavily in her favor. After all, with 82 victories on her record, it's a fact that most opponents end the fight unconscious at Keira's feet. Controlling the action against a fighter like that is a tremendous accomplishment. It can also be exahusting. Though the fight may not have given the fans the kind of excitement they crave, Jules was anything but bored while trying to contain Knightley.

"It's so intense," said the platinum-coiffed dancer. "You have to be perfect against her. One mistake, and she'll make you pay."

Though she was happy to retain her belt, even Hough recognized that she has a ton of work to do if she wants to continue to hold off Keira.

"Winning is nice, but I don't feel comfortable against her," she said. "I need to find a way back to my style. Dancing is for the stage, not the cage. I need to find a way to get after her. I can't keep leaving it up to the judges."

My position on past judging disasters should tell you that I'm with Jules all the way on that one. Hopefully, she'll find a way to crack through Knightley's defenses without making herself vulnerable in the process. But that doesn't mean I can't appreciate what she did here in Sydney. Bottling up a fighter like Keira is an achievement to be proud of, for sure.

On the Cusp

The climb has been slow and rocky for Amanda Seyfried. Every time she starts gathering momentum, she suffers a letdown. Frequently, she gets them in pairs. But the numbers say she's been on a fairly steady incline for a couple of years, now. Tonight's resounding victory over Emmy Rossum puts her back within reach of the division's best. Now, she just has to find a way to stay there.

It doesn't seem like that long ago that Mandy was competing admirably with once and future (and now once, again) champion Megan Fox. Truth be told, they last fought only a year ago. At the time, it appeared the pair - who were friends away from the combat arena - were going to be the ones to show Danni Harris and Sophia Bush how such friendly competition was done. But Amanda's rise has been almost snail-like since then, while Megan took off like a rocket... grabbing the championship before having an Open Fight Night for the record books.

But slow and steady has paid off for Seyfried, who now finds herself trailing only Fox and the powerful triumvirate of Catrinel Menghia, Candice Swanepoel and Rosie Huntington-Whiteley. Can she make it stick this time, and get in there and help her friend battle the ILF trio? Those who know her best say, 'you bet.'

"Mandy is a workhorse," said Danni, Amanda's most frequent training partner. "Sure, she's had some setbacks, but she's battled through them. Just look at her record. It speaks for itself."

The younger members of RSI's welterweight quartet agree.

"She's the quietest of all of us, but she's so tough," said Cobie Smulders. "Every time I think I've got her, I find out I don't have anything."

Cobie may not feel ready to step in the cage with Amanda, but we do have one fighter on board who has not only fought her, but won.

"Biggest thrill of my life," Anna Kendrick said of her victory over Seyfried a few years ago.

Bigger than beating Holly Valance and Kristanna Loken?

"Oh, hell yes! Imagine trying to beat the person who taught you what you know. That's what that was. I know a lot of it was luck. I mean, Mandy's the only one of the three of us who can put it to Danni, so let's be serious. But I surprised her that night, and I won't lie... I've never let her live it down."

True story?

"Every day," Amanda sighed. "I have to hear about it every god-damn day."

Maybe it's just me, but I don't think antagonizing someone with Amanda's skillset is a great idea. Of course, it's Anna. So...

Catching On

Speaking of the irreverent yet endearing Miss Kendrick... Amanda better watch her butt, because her 'lucky' teammate is on the rise. Though her record is spotty, Anna has made a habit of getting the big win when she needs it. How big are we talking? Well, aside from Amanda, Valance and Loken, Kendrick has even beaten Fox. How does she do it?

"Hell if I know," said the always self-deprecating actress. "I just go in there and start swinging. Sometimes it works. Sometimes it's a disaster."

Development coordinator Stephanie McMahon says Anna knows a lot more than she lets on.

"Anna makes me nuts," McMahon grumbled. "She has every skill she needs to beat anyone, and she knows it. But if you ask her, she pretends she doesn't even know how to put the gloves on without help."

Sounds like just what you'd expect from the self-proclaimed 'Kanye West of humility.' I... think.

Anyway... if Anna's so skilled, then what's with those disasters she mentioned?

"She still needs time," McMahon said. "She needs more fights. More experience against the better fighters. She'll get there. You'll see."

Pretty confident talk from the woman who usually has nothing to say. But, Steph's not the only one praising Kendrick's potential.

"It's all there. You can see it," said Danni Harris. "Most of the time, Anna looks like the young fighter she is. But, every once in a while, she really taps into her talent and just catches fire. Once she figures it out, you're going to see big things from her."

High praise from both your stable leader and your lead trainer. But, hey... no pressure Anna.

Tiz Good

RSI is not known to pay much heed to the rumor mill, but it's become hard to ignore what's been happening with the UMMA's, uhm... most well-endowed stables. While the theories have grown wilder and ever more disturbing, the facts are undeniable. Video evidence clearly shows that fighters from the three British stables have shown an amazing aptitude for reinventing themselves from one fight to the next - physically and mentally mastering new techniques and skills at record speeds. Proficiency levels that take most fighters years to achieve are being reached by these women in a period of mere weeks. How is this type of excellence being achieved? The rest of the league is mystified, and it's left RSI's highly self-critical development expert feeling hopelessly inadequate.

"I can't explain it," said a frustrated Steph McMahon. "Their preparation and training capabilities are incredible. I can barely get our rookies to speak English."

Most amazing to McMahon has been the way British women are able to identify and exploit every weakness an opponent has.

"They're like machines," she said. "They see where an opponent is weak, and they immediately develop the skillset they need to take advantage. They literally re-mold themselves into the perfect weapon to defeat whoever they're facing."

While the league as a whole struggles to comprehend how these results are being achieved, fighters are left with the question of how to prepare when the draw puts them in the cage with one of these women. For decades, the training process for combat sports has largely been the same. Gather as much information as you can about your opponent, then develop a plan to use your own skills to overcome her. But the process dissolves into vapor if every scrap of information you can find is rendered useless. You can watch all the video you want. Become an expert in every nuance and tendency of your opponent's game. But what good will all that preparation do you if the woman that shows up on fight night is completely reinvented and reborn?

This was the situation Ashley Tisdale faced when she drew RWB's Amy Green as an opponent. Now, it's true that Green's career is still in it's infancy. So, it's common for newer fighters to develop and add to their game as they gain experience. But, still... Amy had tendencies that could be identified and prepared for. The scouting report certainly bore that out.

Strengths: Powerful. Great hands. Lethal feet. Excellent conditioning. Capable on the ground. Highly intelligent and confident.

Weaknesses: Ineffective and vulnerable in the clinch. Poor submission defense.

Certainly a fighter to be wary of. But could Tiz depend on the report? Would Green try to strengthen her ground game and surprise Ash that way? And what about those weaknesses? Tiz has all the submission skills of a limp noodle, but she does have a fairly decent clinch game. Was that the way for her to attack?

With no way to know, the plan was to go in with no plan, at all.

"Back to basics," Tisdale said of her training regimen. "Steph had me focus exclusively on the fundamentals. 'No fancy shit.' That's exactly how she put it. She said we'd have to see how the first round went, and she'd help me adjust during the break. In the meantime, she warned me to be ready for anything."

She was ready, alright, and it was a damn good thing. Because Green's first offensive move was to go for the clinch. You remember... the one area where she was 'ineffective and vulnerable.'

Well, we'll never know what the statuesque blond had in mind, because Tiz didn't wait to find out. Immediately realizing she was seeing something new from Green, Tisdale went on the attack - brutalizing her opponent with rapid-fire combinations before fighting her way free. Again, Green tried to clinch, but Ash rocked her with a bone-shattering uppercut. STILL, the Brit pushed forward, looking to get her hands locked onto the RSI bantamweight.

Now, it was obvious. Green had seen something in Ash's clinch technique, and she was programmed to attack it. So what do you do when your opponent knows everything about you, while you clearly have no idea what she's up to?

Shake shit up.

In a fit of inspiration, Tiz found a hole in Amy's defense and shot in for the takedown. Did it work? Well, kinda. You see Tiz and takedowns don't really go together. Not to put too fine a point on it, but she's terrible at them. I mean, god-awful terrible. True to form, the attempt was laughably ugly. Like watching a fish try to walk on land. So, if Tisdale was looking for style points, she came up empty. But if she was going for shock factor, then it was mission accomplished.

The move caught Green so off-guard, she couldn't respond quickly enough. Both fighters sort of flopped to the canvas, and it was Tiz who came up on top. And that was bad news for Green. Because takedowns might not be Ash's forte, but she's right at home in a desperate, clawing struggle.

Ashley got the mount and opened up with bombs from every direction... unloading with both fists, two-handed hammer blows and even a sneaky head butt during the melee. Effective? Hell yes. Legal? Only when the ref's not looking.

All along, Tiz was careful to maintain and improve her position, expertly stepping her way through guard while Green was desperately trying to survive. Eventually, Green was caught, rendered helpless as Ashley tied her up. Then came the elbows. One after another, they slammed into the defenseless Brit. Referee Anne Vyalitsyna moved in quickly, but it was already too late. Green was out cold.

Who needs between round adjustments when you can just pound your opponent into mincemeat?

"What can I say? It's Tiz," McMahon said afterward, throwing her hands up when asked about Ashley's ad-hoc takedown attempt. "There are a lot of ways to pick apart her skillset. She'll tell you herself that her game isn't perfect. But never underestimate her. Her fight knowledge is her greatest asset. If there's a way, she'll find it."

Want another tip? Never try to out-scrap Ashley Tisdale. Cause grinding it out in the trenches is what she does best.

The Roundup

It was a mixed bag of results for the rest of tonight's RSI fighters, with some of the rookies learning some hard lessons.

Krystal Forscutt looked solid, getting her third victory in as many tries over Ciara. Now, if she can just build on it instead of slipping back, maybe the UMMA will see fit to give her someone else to fight, next time. Not that it's all Fors's fault. More than anything else, it's her team event fights that are killing her. So, if you're looking for a villain in this... it's me.

Meanwhile, both Brittany Tacy and Allira Cohrs got their 2016 campaign off to good starts after ending last year on the down beat. Both are desperately in need of some forward traction, and their impressive performances tonight might be just the thing to get them going.

Brittany was like a machine in this one, scoring with every single thing she threw at Anna Popplewell. Everything. The assault was so sudden and overwhelming, it almost looked like Tacy was cheating. Popplewell barely got the chance to get her gloves up before Brittany knocked her flat.

"I needed this," the Floridian powerhouse said afterward. "Last year was an ugly year for me, and I really needed a good start to this season."

Meanwhile, Allira really took it to RWB rookie Leah Francis, needing only a minute or so to get the knockout. Much like Tiz, Cohrs said she stepped into the cage ready for anything the RWB machine could throw at her.

"She definitely seemed different in person than what I'd seen in the scouting videos," Cohrs said, "but it didn't effect me much. I stayed with my game plan. Caught her with a few good counters early. That seemed to rattle her, and I just poured it on looking for the finish."

Things didn't go quite as well for Michelle Lewin or newcomer Paige Hathaway here at the Allphones Arena. Both laid it all out there, but neither got the result they were looking for.

Michelle put up a good fight, but she was simply outclassed by the vastly more experienced Naya Rivera. Naya's frequent struggles aside, it's pretty tough for a fighter to make a dent in an opponent who was thirty more fights under her belt. So, I give Lewin credit for doing as well as she did, and I hope she can get back to learning against more similarly matched opponents next time out.

Meanwhile, this was probably not the debut Paige was looking for. I won't sugarcoat it. She looked pretty terrible against fellow first-timer Tatyana Chernova. But she's only been with us for a month. So, I'm not ready to start assessing anything based on this one. We'll just chalk it up to experience and move on.
