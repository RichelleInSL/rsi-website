Breaking Down The Defense

Life since the UMMA franchise reorganization has been rough on Julianne Hough. She's used to working fast and furious when she's in the cage, and her brutal battles with Vikki Blows fit that mold perfectly. In seven fights with her longtime foe, only two ever made it out of the first round. So, win or lose, Jules could always be assured of a short night.

Nowadays, it's nothing but work, work, work.

The reorg brought Jules a new kind of trouble. One that took the form of the vastly talented Keira Knightly. And Keira... well, she likes to take her time. With a defense that protects her like a stone wall, she prefers to chip away at her opponents - frustrating them into making mistakes. It's a technique that few can manage, but Keira makes it work with ease. That fact is especially surprising, given the skill level of the fighters she usually has to face.

But Jules... she doesn't make a lot of mistakes, and she has quite the defensive skillset of her own. And so far, it's working for her, as she's managed to hang onto the title despite the fact that Keira has refused to give an inch. But those wins were marginal, at best. They were situations where neither fighter could really get anything going, and the judges decided that Jules had done just enough to get the win. I think those decisions were right, but, without a truly clear winner, the fans were left unfulfilled.

So, the question remained. Would one of these women finally exert her will upon the other?

Well, I don't know about exerting her will, but Jules sure put a dent in Keira's defense, tonight.

The first round went the way they all have. A whole lot of patient plodding as the two searched for a way in. A few offensive outbursts, but mostly just a lot of clinching and shoving as the two jockeyed for position.

The second round saw a new wrinkle. Jules started to gain control behind a precision striking attack. Keira stayed with her, but you could visibly see the scales tipping in Julianne's direction. Suddenly, she was landing more often and doing more damage. Then, just when it looked like Hough might really open things up, Keira went low and dragged her to the mat.

It was a veteran move by one of the cagiest veteran's in the league. Things weren't going her way when they were standing, so she switched things up to try and find a different path to victory. When you posess the extensive skills that Keira does, all avenues are open to you. But Jules is no slouch on the canvas. She hasn't shown the ability to produce a lot of offense down there, but she can handle herself pretty well. And she did a fine job of keeping Keira at bay long enough to get to the end of the round.

Still, the move had paid dividends for Knightley. Not only had she ended the rally Jules had going, but groundwork takes a lot out of you. Had she managed to take some starch out of the champion?

Uhm... nope.

I don't know if the takedown pissed her off or if she was just sick of playing games, but Julianne was set on full attack mode when the third round began. A few seconds of shuffling ended abruptly when Hough scored with a vicious hook as she closed the distance. The uppercut that followed landed flush under Keira's chin. Suddenly, the Raccoon fighter was dazed and bleeding, and Hough wasted no time taking advantage. This was the first time Jules had seen the slightest sliver of offensive daylight in three straight championship fights with Keira. There was no way she was going to let the moment get away from her. She grabbed her opponent with both hands and jammed her up against the fence. Punches, kicks, knees. There was no letup. Keira managed to get off the cage, but another uppercut from Jules knocked her completely off her feet. Knightley bought some time by crabwalking around to keep Jules off of her, but she wasn't going to win the fight that way. When she finally climbed back to her feet, Julianne was there to put the pressure on again. Keira danced. She slid. She countered. In all honesty, Knightley did all she could. But there was no stopping Jules.

Still, even with a full round to work with, Julianne couldn't get the finish. Keira may have been wounded, but she fought it out to the bitter end. Because that's what a warrior does. But there was no doubt about this one, and both fighters knew it before the decision was made official.

Afterward, Jules was asked what inspired that third round performance. The answer was one I definitely should have expected.

"Steph", was her simple and oh-so-obvious response. "I was all pissed off after the way the second round went. I expected something new during the break, but Steph just said keep going with the strikes in the final round. In fact, she said I needed to turn it up a notch."

That, of course, led to questions for Ms. McMahon. But she had no trouble providing insight into her decision.

"Keira got the takedown in the second, and that threw things off," she said. "But, before that, it was working. Sometimes, you just have it, and I could see Julianne's accuracy was way up tonight. She was hitting everything right on the button, and Keira was having a lot of trouble with it. It was a great move by her to force the takedown, but that didn't change anything for me. The strikes were bothering her, and I just wanted Jules to keep it up."

It was quite a gutsy decision, when you really think about it. It was that third round that sealed this one for Jules. But if Knightley had gotten the takedown again, things easily could have gone the other way. Did Hough have any doubts about Steph's approach?

"Nope," she said directly. "Steph is there for a reason. I have enough to think about during a fight. She's there to set the strategy, and I'm going to listen to what she says. If she's ever wrong, then that's how it is. I'll go down with that ship every time because she's come through too many times in the past for me to second guess her."

Indeed... Steph's counsel has become invaluable to the fighters of RSI, and it paid off again here. Now, Jules has her first no-doubt-about-it win of this rivalry. We shall see what Keira counters with next time out.

Rivalry Cut Down

Another event. Another stupid injury. This time, the fighters involved where Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik. But hey... nobody wanted to see those two fight anyway, right?

Yeah... right.

By now, you know the story. Irina was the perennial champion... seemingly on a level of her own. Then Emily stepped up and not only took the title away, but also established that she could go toe-to-toe with the Russian legend whenever and wherever. That was three years ago, and what's happened since then is the kind of stuff fans dream about. Back and forth the two went, trading the title nearly every time they met. As a result, their rivalry became one of the most popular in league history. Each woman developed her own legion of followers, and even fans who didn't have a favorite were compelled to tune in.

More recently, though, Emily has been on a bit of a tear. She won the title again in March of 2015. The loss dropped Irina out of the top contender slot, making room for Ashley Greene to take a shot. But she left disappointed, as Emily finally mounted her first successful title defense when they met that July. Greene used the wide-open format of Open Fight Night to take another crack at the champion in November, but Em beat her back once more. Along came March again, and it was Sheik back in the top spot. But Emily was on a roll, and even Irina's elite skills weren't enough to slow her down.

So, everything was coming up roses for RSI's sweetheart as the 2016 Red Carpet event rolled around. Not only had she won three championship matches in a row, but her success had pushed her to the very top of the UMMA rankings. The way she'd been fighting, there was no doubt she'd make a deep push in the tournament. Most were expecting her to win the whole damn thing.

Too bad she never even made it out of the first round.

In a simply stunning upset, the far less experienced Pamela Horton stared the champion in the face and then flat-out beat her. In a very rare circumstance, the upset was not the result of a poor performance by the favorite. Instead, it was a case of the underdog stepping up.

In other words, Em didn't give the fight away. Pamela took it from her.

There was nothing lucky or flukey about it, and Emily was among the first to give her opponent the credit she was due. But, despite the fact that she fought well, the loss looked terrible for VanCamp. Naysayers started to see cracks in the armor. Maybe the run was finally coming to an end. To make matters worse, all of this was happening just a few months before Em was scheduled to face Irina yet again. And there was no doubt the former champion was hungry to have the crown back in her hands.

So, here we were... VanCamp and Sheik going at it again. With all kinds of questions to answer and things to prove. The entire building held its breath as the bell sounded to get this one started.

Fourty-four seconds later, it all ground to a disastrous halt. Because Irina had a bloody nose.

I really want to say that it didn't matter. That Emily had burst out of the gate and taken it to the challenger. That the stoppage was a merciful ending to a one-sided fight that Em was clearly winning. But none of that was the case. Fourty-four seconds? The women had barely broken a sweat. Em had simply caught Irina with a punch that landed funny. Instead of an impact, it was more of a swipe. Maybe a fingernail went awry. Maybe it was some part of the glove. We'll never know for sure. What we do know is that there had to be some way to figure it out. Some way that didn't include leaving the whole arena disappointed.

Look, I'm always happier when these things go for us instead of against us. But those who know me will tell you that, above all else, I'm a fan of the sport. I love to watch a good fight, and there are none better than the ones Emily and Irina put on. So this was just... crappy.

Bits and Bobs

That's all for the championship contenders, but a few other RSIers put in notable performances here in Osaka.

Amanda Seyfried provided a demonstration of just how quickly the tide can turn, as she got the win over Candice Swanepoel via a single, devastating kick. Just as it looked like Swanepoel was asserting control over the fight, she made just one, tiny error. Thinking she had Seyfried reeling, she got aggressive and tried to force the action. She ended up stepping right into a brutal high kick from Mandy, and that was the end of that.

Despite the significant point difference between she and Swanepoel, the win wasn't enough to move Amanda up the rankings. However, it did cement her hold on the fourth place slot and set up a likely date with her friend/rival, Megan Fox. If the rankings hold, that one should be interesting.

While Jules had her hands full dealing with Keira Knightley, her former nemesis Vikki Blows found herself face-to-face with Julianne's stablemate, Ashley Tisdale. Now it's no secret that Vikki is tough to handle, but Tiz has shown many times that she can step up when the situation calls for it. And she did it again tonight, dismantling the former champion in just over three minutes of one-sided action.

It was a somewhat surprising result, considering Ash was coming off a marginal performance at this year's Red Carpet tournament. But that's the way it is with Tiz. One night, she's stuggling. Next time out, she looks like championship material.

That said, it's been more good than bad for Tiz recently, and her fourth place ranking puts her within sight of the divisional crown. I don't think Jules is having a panic attack over that prospect, but the one thing I know about Ashley is that you just never know.

A solid win for Anna Kendrick tonight, as she stubbornly refused to give Lilly Allen a chance to mount any offense. All in all, it was kind of an ugly scene, as Kendrick just overwhelmed her opponent from the outset.

This marks Anna's second victory over Allen, and it gave her the ranking points she needed to move up a couple of spots. There's still quite a bit of talent between her and the title, but her seventh place position is pretty damn good for a fighter of her experience.

Sometimes fights are just not as close as the final tally would lead you to believe. Despite the fact that tonight's fight between Krystal Forscutt and Rihanna lasted over twelve minutes, those who were watching will tell you that Fors spent every single one of those minutes simply having her way with the Work songstress. And there really isn't much that I can say about that. Aside from a tremendous amount of intestinal fortitude, the singer really didn't show much of anything in this one, as Krystal just hammered her from pillar to post.

The win put a little space between these two in the rankings and moved Forscutt up to fifth. If everything holds, that puts her in line for a faceoff with Bunny fighter Jayde Nicole at the next ESL event. Won't that be delightful.
