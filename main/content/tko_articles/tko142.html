Calling Her Shot

It took some stones, I'll give her that.

Near the end of RSI's Team Warfare round table - in a moment I will now never be allowed to forget - Emma Watson interrupted my customary sign-off to make a little announcement of her own. At the time, I was listing off the RSI athletes who were a threat to take home a title from this year's Red Carpet tournament. Emily VanCamp, Tiffany Mulheron, Danni Harris...

"And me," Emma said as she cut-in.

Sure, she was being funny. But there was a seriousness to her, as well. Despite her numerous accomplishments in the cage, tournament success has consistently eluded Emma. She always competes well. In fact, she advanced all the way to the final of this event, last year. But then her own girlfriend handed her her head. And that's the way it's gone for her in these things.

So, yes... she was trying to be amusing, but she also meant what she said. Emma is always a threat to win, regardless of who she's facing. So, there was no reason her name shouldn't have been on my list. Did I admit that at the time? Not a chance. Instead, I took the opportunity to take a shot at her... saying that, based on her past tournament record, it would take some sort of unpredictable craziness for her to win it all here in Perth. It was a response that could only be viewed as expected, given the way Emma and I make a habit of antagonizing each other... both publicly and privately. And besides, what was the risk? In a field of 32 fighters that included Tiffany, Rachel McAdams and Nadine Coyle, what were the odds that THIS would be the tournament where Emma finally broke through?

Better than I thought, apparently. And by winning it all, she not only made me look silly, but she also backed up her bold prediction. In essence, she called her shot.

So now the question is: how long will I be made to suffer for my big mouth? And the answer, it seems, is eternity.

"Oh, just you wait," the diminutive champion said when asked about my earlier remarks. "She's going to be paying for that one for a looooong time."

You know what? Bring it on, Watson. Because if giving you the chance to make me look like an idiot is what it takes to light a fire under your tiny little butt, so be it.

When you're the champion of a division that isn't all that competitive, it's easy for people to question just how good you really are. Though Victoria Justice and Xenia Deli are on the rise... and don't think Emma doesn't know it... it's been Emma's division for a while now. So, it's easy to see why quite a few experts view the long-standing champion as untested. But Emma put all the doubters on mute here on the beautiful Australian coastline. In a performance that finally... FINALLY... showcased everything she can do and then some, she proved beyond a shadow of a doubt that she can go toe-to-toe with anyone. And her win was not the product of a lucky draw, either. After making her way through the early rounds, she found both the first and second seeds waiting for her at the end. And the challenge they presented... both physically and emotionally... was immense.

In the semi-final round, she came up against friend, training partner and romantic partner Tiffany Mulheron. As I said earlier, Tiff and Emma met at this same event, last year. The result was not all that Emma was hoping it could be. Did the chance at revenge help her get over any concerns she had about going to war with her lover?

"Not really," Emma admitted. "I mean, we knew when we started this relationship this was going to happen, sometimes. We've talked about it. We talk about it all the time, actually. But it's not easy."

Tiffany - who, with the help of the RSI medical staff, had recovered nicely from the battered condition Emma had left her in - gamely joined her partner to do their post-event press conference together. Her response to the question was right in step with Emma's.

"What choice do we have?" she said. "We both fight to be the best, and one of the things that makes us who we are is the way we push each other along. I would never give that up, but it means we're constantly pushing ourselves toward moments like this. So, we go out there and give each other our best. That's the deal."

Any sore feelings over the lopsided loss Emma handed her?

"Well, let's see..." the Scottish actress began. "My legs are killing me. My head hurts. Got some sore ribs. Maybe a bruised ego. But otherwise, no. Mostly, I'm just proud of her. Proud of the way she fought. Proud of the way she went for it, even though I know it hurt her a little."

She paused and gave a mirthful smile, then added, "It definitely hurt me worse, though."

"It's called defense, sweetie," Emma teased. "You gotta put your gloves up. When we get back to the gym, I'll show you how it's done."

Tiffany blew out a long sigh and mumbled, "It's gonna be a long flight home."

For all of us, Tiff. For all of us.

Getting past Tiffany was only step one for Emma, though. Because blocking the finish line was her sometime training partner, Rachel McAdams. I've said many times that one of the most amazing aspects of RSI's training agreement with Dragon's Den is the awe-inspiring scene of Emma, Tiffany and Rachel at work. The combined magnitude of their accomplishments is enough to make your head spin, and it is a joy to watch them train. But the best part is what happens when they decide to let loose on each other.

Here in Perth, however, McAdams vs Watson didn't turn out to be all it could have been. After coming so close to winning this title last year, there was no way Emma was going home empty-handed again. Pressing the action from the second the bell rang, she showed an unfettered willingness to take chances against the SCQ champion. The move paid off in spades, as she seemed to catch Rachel off guard. And with Emma's motor running in high gear, it was all over before the Den star could get caught up.

Despite the ease of the victory, there was only one word to describe Emma's feelings toward McAdams.

"Respect," the Beauty and the Beast actress said. "Rach knows I have the utmost respect for her and everything she's accomplished. For the most part, she's held the divisional title since the league came into existence. I say that as if it's a single title, when, in fact, she's taken the title in every division she's been through as her career progessed. All along the way, she's been the perfect champion. She's earned the right to a little superiority, but, instead, she's totally accessible and extremely down-to-earth. She's the same way in training, too. There's no reason why she should have agreed to work with Tiffany and I, but she did it, anyway. She's exactly the model of the kind of champion I want to be."

Speaking of the co-training, does Emma think it's wise for McAdams to continue to show her cards to her RSI counterparts?

"Probably not," Watson laughed, "but I'm betting she will. That's just who she is."

Alright, Emma... time to give you what you've been waiting for. Despite the fact that you knew I was messing with you when I made my comments in the last article, I know you're waiting for the moment when I publicly eat my words. Well, pass the salt, 'cause here it comes.

Like anyone who's watched you closely, I knew all along you had this in you. Your skill level is incredible - a fact I once learned first-hand - but it's your dedication that makes the difference. You go beyond what's needed, often sticking your neck out for others when you don't stand to gain a thing for it. You just...

You know what? This isn't coming out the way I wanted. Let me try again, in terms I know will work for you.

Nowadays, celebrities doing only the bare minimum and worrying only about themselves is pretty much the Way of the World. Either that, or they take up some cause and begin to tell everyone what's right and wrong. They tell people how to act and how to live and even how to vote. But you don't conform to the standard. In your own quiet way, you simply Break Every Rule. Yes, you've become the face of a cause. But you don't slam people over the head with it. In fact, you don't even seem too concerned with becoming the perfect champion for that cause. You continue to live your life as you see fit. Because with you, What You Get Is What You See. You share and you inform. You encourage the people of your generation to vote and get involved and be a part of things, but you don't tell them how to vote or what part they should play. Because you know you don't know it all.

For me, the titles you win are simply a consequence of who you are. You not only work hard, but you work well. You draw a crowd to you that wants to help you, if for no other reason than because you've helped them. And yes, you can include me in that group. Together, we are always behind you for Whatever You Need. And with a support system like that... well, there's no telling how far you can go. To us, you are simply The Best. And I know you would never... EVER... agree with that statement. Which only proves my point.

Sorry. I tried like hell to get a Steamy Windows reference in there, but I couldn't make it work. You're just gonna have to get Tiff to help you with that one.

Yeah, that's right. I said it.

Congratulations, Emma. And thanks... for finally showing everyone else what we here at RSI knew all along.

Feeling Better

When last we left poor Danneel Harris, she had just lost her divisional title belt to rival Cheryl Cole. Danni - who apparently took one too many punches to the face during the bout - seemed almost happy about the turn of events. Excited about being part of a rivalry with some heat to it. But the rest of RSI was left stunned. Yes, we know Danni isn't perfect. But she's always seemed perfect to us. So the reality of her being without her belt had us all feeling a little bit adrift.

We feel better, now.

Being the top seed in a tournament is like being given a shiny trophy made of polished lead. Sure, it's great to be recognized, but now you gotta lug this two-ton piece of shit around with you everywhere you go. That little 1 next to your name means you're the best. Sounds good, until you realize it also means anything short of winning it all is considered a failure. That can be a heavy weight to carry.

Danni's weight was much heavier for this tournament, though. This time, she had questions to answer and things to prove. How would she respond to the loss to Cole? Was this the beginnning of the end for her? Was she still worthy of the top seed?

Well, in a performance that proves the only person not concerned about Danni's state of being is Danni herself, RSI's stable leader answered all of those questions in emphatic fashion. And it looks like she's going to be just fine.

Though a rematch with Cole wasn't in the cards for this event, Danni had plenty to keep her busy. Being the top seed does have some advantages, and things started off easily enough with a couple of straight-forward wins in the early rounds. In the third round, however, she found friend and training partner Amanda Seyfried waiting for her. Would Amanda's insider knowledge of Danni's training strategies be a problem?

Not on this day.

Despite the fact that the fight nearly went the distance, the outcome was never in doubt. Danni steamrolled poor Mandy. Early on, Seyfried weathered the onslaught and tried to fight back. By the end of the second, it was clear she was out of gas and out of options. The third round was ugly, and no one was more upset about that than Danni was.

"I love Mandy, and what I love most about her is her heart," Harris said, "but god-damn that was hard. Fighting your friends is something we all have to deal with. You try to tell yourself it's just another opponent and go out there and do your job. But that third round... that was one of the hardest things I've ever done. I'm just glad she's okay."

For her part, Amanda admitted she probably shouldn't have dragged her friend through that. But it's not in her makeup to quit. And when it comes to how she developed that unbending nature, she knew exactly where the blame needed to go.

"I follow the example set by stable leader," said Seyfried - smiling despite her heavily-bandaged face, "and giving up is not the way she taught me to fight."

Maybe it came a little too late, but you have to admire the way Amanda waded through Danni's defenses with that one.

Next on Danni's plate was former ESL champion Megan Fox. With both women having been deposed within weeks of each other, there was a lot of interest in this battle of the former champions. Then Danni had to go and ruin it.

Much like Emma had done against Tiffany and Rachel, Danni entered the cage for this fight with the pedal already mashed to the floor. Megan is not only a world class striker, but she's found tremendous success on the ground over the years. None of that mattered in this one. Danni assumed control from the outset and had everything in hand by the end of the first round. What happened from there was not Fox's fault. She fought and she clawed and she stubbornly pushed on despite the fact that she couldn't seem to get anything going. Meanwhile, Danni could do no wrong.

That streak continued into the final, too. Her opponent there was Catrinel Menghia. Because if the former champion didn't get the job done, why not let the current champion have a crack at it? It made no difference, though. Danni was like a woman possessed, and she was not going to be denied.

Having taken on all comers while making the whole thing look easy, surely Miss Harris was willing to crow a little after this one, right?

Yeah. Cause that's just the sort of thing Danni would do.

"I've been there. We've all been there," she said of the way she ran through Fox and Menghia. "Just look at my record. It's happened to me plenty of times. Megan and Cat each have a long track record of success. I don't think this takes anything away from them."

In typical fashion, it seems I'm forced to do what Danni will not. Because, you see, even I have never seen a performance like this from her. Though she hates when I do it, I have no trouble referring to Danni as being great. I feel very comfortable saying it, because it's not a judgment call. Her record clearly shows that it's a fact. But tearing through the competition this way? Shredding three top-10 opponents like they were tissue paper? Despite my soaring (and highly subjective) opinion of Danni's skills, even I was amazed by this one.

Care to reveal the secret, my dear?

"Sometimes things just go your way," she said.

Seriously, why do I even bother?

However, for those paying close attention, there was a very telling statement buried in the RSI leader's avalanche of nauseating humility.

"I think this shows what I can do when I'm right," she said mysteriously. "When I have my head straight and I'm focused on my training."

That sent the media fishing for the reason she didn't feel focused before. In typical fashion, she gave them nothing, saying only that she was "distracted." She followed that with an emphatic "no" when asked if she felt that distraction cost her in the title fight with Cole.

It's not my place to explain exactly what Danni was talking about. For that, you'll have to wait a few years for RSI: The Untold Story to hit bookshelves. But I'll give you a hint: she was talking to me when she said it.

Oh, and here's another little tidbit: she's not distracted, anymore. Her focus is right where it belongs.

Congratulations, Danni. I know this title means as much to you as any other, and it's an accomplishment to be proud of. But damn... you have no idea what it means to the rest of us.

The Second Fiddle

What does it take for Catherine Bell to get a little respect around here?

After rising from relative obscurity, she quietly - and somewhat surprisingly - grabbed hold of the SCQ Heavyweight title. At the time, it was viewed as a nice little footnote to her extensive career. A job well done in taking advantage of the chaos that followed the UMMA franchise reorganization. What's overlooked is the fact that she's maintained her hold on that championsip for nearly two years now. Also, no one seems to notice the 15-2 record she's compiled over that span.

But surely her performance here in Australia has earned her some accolades. Winning a tournament in which she took on the likes of Caroline Wozniacki and Lucy Becker has to make people stand up and take notice, right?

Well, kinda... but not really. It seems that - while the crowd here certainly appreciated and respected what Mac accomplished - no one could stop talking about the woman she faced off against in the final match. Because while Catherine did a marvelous job of performing up to the high expectations of her second seed status, young Dianna Dahlgren was simply fighting out of her mind.

Perhaps it was no surprise that Dianna managed to overcome the vastly more experienced Tricia Helfer in her first round matchup. Despite her numerous accomplishments, Tricia has been struggling mightily recently. Meanwhile, Dianna has had a very nice start to her career, and she's had a few big wins along the way. So, maybe that one wasn't too hard to digest.

In the second round, she found herself up against the Hellfire's Katy Perry - who had performed what can only be described as a miracle in her first round matchup, handing top seed Adriana Lima an absolute thumping. It was a result so unexpected that both Catherine and stablemate Petra Cubonova - both of whom are intimately familiar with the talent Lima possesses - had to be reminded to breathe when they heard the news. So maybe Perry was still riding high on her big accomplishment. Or maybe it was just a case where she wasn't able to maintain the level of excellence she'd shown against Lima. Or maybe... just maybe... Dianna got a little bit lucky. Because the fight was still very much in doubt when the cageside medical experts determined a small cut in Katy's mouth warranted that the fight be stopped. A cheap victory, for sure, but you take what you can get in this league.

Besides, everybody knew the party was over for Dianna at that point. Because the third round found Dragon's Den phenom Samantha Hoopes waiting for our girl. Samantha Hoopes, protégé of FAC champion Caroline Wozniacki. Samantha Hoopes, winner of last year's Hit the Beach tournament - a tournament in which she took some time out of her busy schedule to slap Mac around for about four minutes before advancing to secure her crown. Samantha Hoopes, the woman they call The Hammer.

So, it had been a nice run, but this was clearly the end of the road for Dianna. She wasn't going to be able to work her magic against Hoopes. No way.

Oh, yes way.

Dianna didn't just win. She owned Hoopes. With nothing to lose, she came out firing at the bell. And for whatever reason, she never missed. Hoopes tried to respond, but she just couldn't find an opening. She couldn't lay a glove on Dahlgren, and she couldn't get out of the way when the return salvos came. By the end of the first round, Dianna's shocked expression told the story perfectly. By the end of the second... well, you didn't have to be a master of lip-reading to know what Dianna was saying.

"How the fuck am I doing this?" she asked head trainer Stephanie McMahon during the break.

Steph just shook her head and snapped at her to stay focused. In other words, she didn't know how the hell Dianna was doing it either.

The win over Hoopes was stunning, but maybe it shouldn't have been. After all, Dianna and Sam have similar levels of experience. And Dianna certainly has shown she's got the talent to go places. But it's the quality of Sam's wins that elevates her. Especially the victories she picked up during her run through the swimsuit tournament. She beat Mac. She beat Lindsey Anne Strutt. I mean, holy hell... she beat Lima! Surely a woman who can win like that is more than just a flash in the pan. And I don't think losing to Dianna changes that. On the contrary, I think it shows maybe Dianna has a little flash in her, as well. And she was given the chance to prove it, immediately. Because up next was former SCQ champion Traci Bingham.

Bingham, in case you were wondering, is the woman Cat beat to win that division title. She also happens to be one of the best heavyweights in the UMMA. Clearly, this one was going to present a monumental challenge for Dahlgren.

Then again, maybe not.

Bingham tried to slow the Dahlgren Express early by taking things to the mat. That's not Dianna's favorite place to be, but she proved up to the task as she held the SCQ fighter off long enough for referee Fearne Cotton to intervene. Not much damage done, but had Bingham managed to take the starch out of the RSI fighter? Hardly. Once the women were back on their feet, Dianna picked up right where she'd left off... punishing her opponent with hard, precise strikes for the rest of the round. Several times, it looked like Cotton might have to stop it, but the ref fought the urge and let Bingham survive until the break. A narrow escape, but she would have a lot of work to do if she was going to come back. And only a few seconds had ticked off in the second before it became obvious that wasn't going to happen. Bingham was hurt, and she wasn't defending herself at all. Still Cotton chose not to get involved. Dianna continued to do what she was trained to do, carefully stalking her opponent while working to finish the job. But Bingham refused to go down and Cotton still refused to stop it. The result? A horrific injury to Bingham that simply didn't have to happen.

While the injury was upsetting, it did nothing to diminish what Dahlgren had done. Two momentous and resounding victories had her headed for her first ever tournament final.

By the way, Cat... did you notice that - even though this article was supposed to be about you - you somehow ended up playing second fiddle again? Well, let's see if we can fix that.

Like Dianna, Bell's first big test came in the third round when she met up with the LMF's Becker. And boy oh boy, did she ace it... whipping the younger fighter in short order while barely breaking a sweat. And it was a good thing to. Because the next test she faced was going to be the Mensa level exam.

While young Ms. Dahlgren was off doing battle with the protégé, it was Cat who had to deal with the teacher... Dragon's Den superstar Caroline Wozniacki. I don't think I have to spend much time explaining how much excitement this matchup generated, either. Both Mac and Woz have proven in the past how good they can be. They've trained together. They know each other. So the expectations headed into this fight were enormous.

The ladies did not disappoint.

The first round was, in a word, epic. Fists flew. Blood spilled. The action was intense as control seesawed back and forth. It was everything the fans could ask for, and the two warriors seemed to be enjoying themselves, too. If there was anything to complain about, it was that the battle was too short. Near the end of the round, Cat let loose with a wild roundhouse that caught Wozniacki napping. With the Dragon fighter stunned, Bell pressed forward for the finish. Crushing shots landed from every direction as the tennis star tried to cover up. She somehow managed to last until the bell, but the damage was done. The break wasn't enough for the young champion to recover, and Cat finished the job quickly in the second.

So, the final was set. Teammate versus teammate. The established veteran against the upstart youngster. And the older, wisened fighter had a message for her young pupil.

Sorry, young grasshopper, but it is not yet your time.

If there was any doubt about how this one was going to go, Cat quickly made things perfectly clear as she smushed her teammate into the canvas in less than three minutes.

Take that, ya' good for nothin' Millenial.

Despite the ribbing she's bound to take over this, Dianna didn't seem too upset over the loss. Still, there was one little disappointment that was eating at her.

"I knew what I was up against," the former fitness model said, sitting beside Bell at the post-event press conference, "but I really wanted to give her something. To push her, at least a little, ya' know? Let the teacher know the student was coming. That kind of thing."

At that point, Cat reached over and playfully tousled the blonde's thick mane.

"Get outta here, little girl," she teased. "You ain't got nothing for me."

When the laughter died down, Bell gave a more sincere appraisal of her teammate's performance.

"She has a lot to be proud of," the JAG actress said. "It's hard to win in this league, and it's a lot harder to do it consistently. I think I know that better than most. But if you're gonna do it, you have to start somewhere. And this feels like just the beginning, for her."

Indeed, the show Dianna put on here was extremely impressive, and it portends great things for her future. But I've said this before, and I will, no doubt, have to say it again: Don't think I don't see you, Mac. I know your quiet demeanor and straight-ahead style make it easy for you to get lost in the noise. But I never asked you to put on a theatrical performance. I asked you for hard work and an honest effort every time you take to the cage. You have yet to let me down.

Congratulations on a well-earned title, Catherine.

The Silent Slovakian

Speaking of champions who fly under the radar, you never hear a whole lot from Daniela Hantuchova. Maybe it's the language barrier that holds her back. More likely, it's that she prefers to let her actions do the talking. And wow... did she ever have a lot to say at this tournament.

Like Danni, her road to the title took her through three top-10 opponents. And the first was former SCQ champion Caprice Bourret. Unfortunately, there isn't much to talk about in this one. As the first minute came to a close, Daniela snuck inside and got off a vicious flurry of punches. As the last one landed, Bourret turned her back and dropped to the canvas in pain. It was obvious something was very wrong, and the cageside medical staff quickly confirmed the terrible news. The punch broke a bone in Caprice's cheek, ending the fight before it even got started.

Afterward, Daniela's comments were short, curt and to the point.

"She is great fighter and proud former champion," she lamented. "This is shame, what happened."

Next in line was current ESL champion Brooklyn Decker. Though Brooklyn has struggled a little recently, she's known for being one of the most inventive and elusive fighters in the league. She's also known for coming up huge when it matters. But Daniela is known for those same abilities, and she was quick to assert her will in this one. Fact is, she never even let Decker get started.

Daniela's final opponent was a bit of a surprise, as Amber Heard emerged after stopping the seemingly unbeatable Heidi Klum. And she did it in impressive style, leading pundits to wonder if she could keep things rolling against Daniela. And the truth is, Amber managed more offense against the tennis star than Bourret and Decker combined. She even had Hantuchova in trouble a few times. But Daniela has been there and seen it all, and she calmly reclaimed control each time. Just when it seemed the women were going to coast to the end of the round, Heard missed with an aggressive attack and gave Daniela the opening she needed. A brutal high kick had Amber seeing stars, and, just like that, the Slovakian swarmed in and put her away.

Honestly Daniela, I don't know what to say to you that hasn't already been said. You had already amassed a a sterling career by the time you joined us, and your performance since then has been nothing short of amazing. It's an honor to have a fighter with your talent in the RSI fold. Congratulations on another fine championship.

Taking Her Medicine

All this talk of top seeds, and the expectations that come along with that distinction. But what happens when you fail to meet those expectations?

Well, for one thing, your stable owner doesn't really want to talk about it.

RSI lightweight superstar Emily VanCamp came into this tournament bearing the heaviest burden of expectation possible. Not only was she the top-ranked lightweight, but she was the top-ranked fighter in the entire UMMA.

How did she handle those expectations?

She lost. In the first round. To the fighter ranked dead last in her bracket.

Yuck.

There is no way around it, and I won't try to sugarcoat it. Emily is not just an elite fighter. She is one of my favorite people. I love almost everything about her. From the way she carries herself in and outside of the cage to that sweet smile she wears both before and after she gets done knocking her opponent around. So, it kills me to have to say this about her, but... her performance here was a huge disappointment.

Not so much that I'm going to hold it against her, mind you. I mean, after all she's done, how could I? But it's the kind of disappointment that just... hurts. It hurts the stable. It hurt to watch. And most of all, it made me hurt for her. It hurt so much that I decided almost immediately that I wasn't going to talk about it.

So, why am I?

Because Emily made me.

And why did she make me?

Because she's a bigger person than I am.

"Pam fought a great fight," VanCamp said. "This is a big moment for her, and I'm not going to take away from that by hiding. She beat me, and she deserves to hear me say so."

The Pam she's talking about is Bad Ass Bunny fighter Pamela Horton. A young fighter who has shown a ton of potential in her burgeoning career, but was facing her first real test against Emily.

Well, looks like she passed that one.

The truth is, what Emily said is probably what makes this one hurt the most. If Horton had landed a perfect punch or somehow gotten lucky, I could shrug it off. Crazy stuff like that happens all the time. But this was different. For two plus rounds, Pam went toe-to-toe with Emily and never gave an inch. Yes, it's true that both fighters were still going strong when Horton suddenly ended things with a brilliant combination. So it's not like Horton was just finishing the already beaten champion. But when that combination lands over a minute and a half into the third round, you can't call it lucky. You have to call it well-earned.

"She worked hard for this," Emily said. "I thought I fought a pretty good fight, up until that point. I just couldn't get to her the way I wanted. She deserves credit for that."

The inevitable question came about halfway through the press conference, when Em was asked if - in a tournament loaded with talent like Joanna Krupa, Ali Larter and Irina Sheik - did she, maybe, look past her first round opponent?

"No way," she said plainly. "The Bunny motto seems to be no fear. They never think they're overmatched, and they're always calm and confident. The willingness they show to take on any opponent has earned them some brutal losses, but it's also scored them a lot of big upsets. Everyone knows they're the ones to watch out for. So my focus was on Pam, and nowhere else."

I happen to think that some of that is a lie. I've already mentioned the expectations that were piled on Emily. It's only natural that she entered this fight with an eye steered toward what would come next. But by the end of the first round, you can bet Horton had her full and undivided attention. And still, the younger fighter held her ground.

Even in my disappointment, I have to admit it was impressive.

Later in the session, it was pointed out to Emily that Open Fight Night was coming up. Would she use it as an opportunity to even the score with Horton? Again, the ESL champion's response was emphatic.

"No," she said firmly. "I'm not going to cheapen what she did by trying to steal it back from her. She should have her moment. I have my own things to worry about in getting ready for Irina in a few months. That's where my focus is going to be."

The final question asked was whether Emily could see a real rivalry devloping between she and Horton.

"We'll see," the Captain America actress answered. "I'm pretty sure her win tonight is going to land Pam in with some very tough company as far as the rankings are concerned. She may have had the right gameplan to face me, but there are all sorts of skillsets at this end of the spectrum. So, first she needs to find out if she's got what it takes to stick around before we start talking rivalries."

Was she simply reciting the facts or tossing out a subtle challenge? I'll let you be the judge.

Close Calls

With four champions coming out of this event, RSI has no complaints. But we do have some other athletes I'd like to give a nod to. I've already mentioned Dianna's incredible run, but there were three other RSI-ers who fought their way into the final round, only to come up just short. It was fantastic to see Steph McMahon looking back in form, even if she did suffer a huge upset to Stacey Massey in the final.
