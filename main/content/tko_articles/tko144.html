Tornado Torrie Strikes Again

2015 was an up and down year for for RSI Development Coordinator Stephanie McMahon. It started off so promising, as she completed a nearly two year journey back to the SCQ championship. Then a devastating injury at the Global Rivalry event in May sidelined her for months. On top of that, her triumphant return fell flat when she took a shocking loss in the opening round of the Hit the Beach tournament the following September.

She needed to turn things around in 2016, but the campaign got off to a terrible start when she lost her divisional title to friend and stablemate Torrie Wilson. At that point, there were serious questions about just how much more she'd be willing to take. But Steph is no stranger to the comeback trail, and, with the help of her fellow RSIers, she dug in her heels and got herself right. A big win over Abi Titmuss at the team event served notice that McMahon wasn't ready to give up quite yet. Then came a finals run at this year's Red Carpet event. Suddenly, 2016 was looking a whole lot brighter.

But it all came to a screeching halt here in Hong Kong, as she once again ran headlong into the buzz saw that is Torrie Wilson.

Of course, it's hard to call this loss a disappointment. More like just par for the course. Because while Steph has had her struggles, Torrie has barely struggled at all. In a span of 42 fights - a period that stretches all the way back to 2013 - Torrie has only lost 8 times. In that same span, she has faced off against 8 of the current top-10 super heavyweights in the league. She has beaten 7 of them at least once.

Oh, and let's not forget that she also vaulted from the very bottom of the division to grab hold of the title belt - stopping off to pick up a pair of tournament crowns along the way.

Not bad for a fighter who had basically been left for dead only a few years ago.

The most ironic part? Torrie owes much of her success to Steph.

"She's been amazing," the blonde said with a shrug. "Steph believed in me when no one else did. She is, without a doubt, the reason I am where I am."

As it happens, Torrie came to us during one of the lowest points in Steph's career. In fact, the night Torrie's turn-around began was the same night Steph dropped her second straight title fight to Veronika Zemanova. More accurately, it was the second time Zemanova had beaten Steph to a pulp, as both fights had to be stopped by the ringside physician. Not because of sudden, devastating injuries but because Steph had been hammered to the point where she simply could not fight on.

The losses were humiliating... so much so that Steph made the announced right then and there that she was done. It was a decision made in frustration after a brutal loss, but it didn't feel like just an empty threat. When she left the arena that night, I'm certain she thought she had fought her last fight. In all honesty, I thought the same thing. But Danni Harris had other ideas. She tracked Steph down and dragged her back. The story of what happened next will remain forever shrouded in the secrecy of RSI's inner circle. But the one thing that is often overlooked is the bond that formed between Steph and Torrie over the months that followed.

When Steph came back, there was no hesitation. Whatever Danni said to her landed with quite an impact, because Steph returned motivated and committed. Come hell or high water, she was getting that title back.

As Steph's regular training partner, Torrie got swept up in the force of that renewed determination. Steph was going full bore. If Torrie wanted to live through it, she was going to have to step up. And that's exactly what she did. Steph pushed, and Torrie pushed right back. She dedicated herself to giving McMahon the daily competition she needed. It didn't take long for Steph to realize the effort her partner was putting in, and she tacitly decided to return the favor. Together, the women worked endlessly to sharpen their skills and make each other better.

For Steph, the payoff for all that hard work came when she won the title back from Zemanova.

The payoff for Torrie came when she rose up and took that title away.

And how does Steph feel about all that?

"I'm going to kick her ass," said a smiling McMahon in a rare, unguarded moment. "I obviously haven't figured out how yet, but it's coming. This may have been her year, but I call dibs on 2017. I'm tired of taking a back seat to her."

"Uh, just a point of fact," Torrie began as she lowered her head to the bank of microphones on the press table. "Title or no, Steph McMahon never takes a back seat to anyone."

Amen to that, Tor.

Meanwhile, as the owner of the stable both of these women work for, all I can say is that this is a happy problem to have. RSI fighters are forced to compete with each other all the time, but rarely do they find themselves vying for the same title. These two women have been through some tremendous adversity over the years, and it's been fantastic to watch them battle through all of it together and have both emerge as the leaders of the pack. Even more fantastic is hearing Steph talking about 2017 when it doesn't seem like all the long ago her 2014 was in doubt.

I can't wait to see where this story goes next.

Cruising Toward Rivalry Status

There are two ways for a fighter to earn a shot at her divisional title. The first is to achieve an official rivalry with the sitting champion. The second is to reach a point where you actually outrank that fighter.

The good news for Traci Bingham is that she's only one fight away from becoming a formal rival to champion Catherine Bell. Which is good, because she's not having much success with the second option.

While Bingham's recent record has been excellent, Cat's has consistently been just a notch better. Every time Traci gets a win, Cat comes through with a higher quality victory that steals her thunder. The result is that Bingham is always right there, but can't seem to close the gap on Bell. But tonight was her big chance. If she could beat Cat head-to-head, she would put a serious dent in that points lead, while also reminding the champion that she was not untouchable.

Instead, it was Cat who proved that's exactly what she was.

Still riding a wave of confidence from her recent Red Carpet tournament title run, Mac was absolutely perfect tonight. Executing her standard block and counter technique as if she were putting on a demonstration, Bell made short work of her challenger while emerging completely unscathed.

The victory marks Mac's second successful outing against the former champion, with both wins coming with relative ease. Does Cat believe she simply has Bingham's number?

"I hope so," she said with a sunny smile. "That would certainly make my life easier."

After a quick moment of laughter, the champion gave a more honest response.

"First of all, it's no secret that Traci has a lot of talent. So, no. I don't think I have any secret advantage over her. Beyond that, I know she's just signed on with the Bad Ass Bunnies. Obviously, that's a well-organized and dedicated group. The signing came late, so I don't think Traci really had the chance to gain anything from her new stable yet. But I have no doubt things are going to change for her, and I'm certain the changes will be for the better. So, I won't say I'm concerned, but I'm... aware. I expect she's going to be a very different fighter the next time we meet."

There's not much that I can add to that. The Bunny way was repeatedly proven effective, whether they're breaking in a new rookie or rejuvenating the career of a cagey veteran like Bingham. I'm certain Traci will be at the top of her game in short order. We'll do everything we can to make sure Cat is ready to deal with it.

A Troubling Victory

Like any fighter, Kelly Hu entered the cage tonight expecting to come away with a victory. You have to think that way, even when your opponent is the highly respected and exceptionally talented Rachel McAdams. You have planned and you have prepared and you need to believe in yourself and your game plan. Otherwise, you'll fail.

So Kelly came in confident that she would succeed. But this was ridiculous.

This one was over in the blink of an eye. The women came together and exchanged punches at the center of the cage, and suddenly McAdams collapsed in a heap. Replay after replay was shown, each detailing the action from a different angle. None provided any conclusive answers as to what the hell happened.

It was a shocking and surreal situation for Kelly. At first, she just stood there, stunned to see McAdams on the canvas. Then the referee stepped in and there was a brief moment of victorious celebration. But that quickly gave way to somber concern for a woman Kelly thinks of as a friend.

"That was scary," Hu said afterward. "I caught her pretty good with my first shot, but she seemed to shrug it off. Then we traded punches for a bit, but I didn't feel like I had landed anything solid. Next thing I know, she's on the ground. I just hope she's OK."

Indeed... with no clear indication of what had happened, the whole thing was mostly frightening. The good news is that - after some attention from the medical staff, Rachel was alert and responsive and she left the cage under her own power. Even better - subsequent reports coming out of Dragon's Den are that the popular SCQ featherweight champion is doing well, and that preliminary testing revealed nothing out of the ordinary. Unfortunately, that just deepens the mystery as to what happened, but Rachel's health is the most important thing. We're all glad to hear the issue wasn't serious.

Meanwhile, the mysterious victory has cemented Kelly's status as the top contender to Rachel's crown. There's still quite a bit of work to do before she gets a shot at it, and I'm not sure that tonight really proved anything. What we do know is that the women will get a chance to do this again... and I can guarantee you that Rachel will be looking to prove something when that time comes. So, you know... good luck with that, Kelly.

The Roundup

There were a few other notable results for the women of RSI here in Lantau. Stana Katic scored an impressive knockout victory over Hilary Swank, serving notice that she may have just what it takes to get herself involved in the title discussion. Roseyln Sanchez made short work of fellow lightweight Rachel Stevens, a win that keeps her in touch with the elite in that division. And Marzia Prince continues to impress, swatting away the much more experienced Jeri Ryan for the third time in her relatively brief career. Don't let Marzia's record fool you. She has all the tools you look for in a young fighter, and she knows how to put them to work for her, too. Her early success has her playing with the big girls now, and she's still holding her own. Don't be surprised if she starts to surge after she gets a little more experience under her belt.
