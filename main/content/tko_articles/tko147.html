Unprepared For Victory

The crowds were still surging toward the gates of Nippon Gaishi Hall, but I wasn't about to let that deter me. Employing a pair of sharp elbows, I left a flurry of empty apologies in my wake as I cut a path to the RSI locker room.

I had been waiting years for this moment, and I wasn't willing to wait a second longer.

I banged my way through the door with such force that I nearly made rookie Zendaya Coleman jump out of her skin.

"Sorry, I didn't mean to scare you," I said quickly, my eyes darting from face to face around the room as I searched for the one I was looking for.

"Pfft... scare me?" she said, trying to come off as cool while waiting for the adrenaline to drain from her system. "Please! I ain't scared. I was just jumping for joy after my big victory."

"Uh-huh," I said absently.

I paced left and right, craning my neck to see over people as I looked down the rows of lockers.

"Have you seen Aly?"

"Sure. I finally get a little winning going, and all anybody wants to talk about is Aly," Zendaya said.

I turned and actually focused on her for the first time.

"I'm sorry, Z," I said. "I saw you out there, and you were awesome. But this thing with Aly..."

"I hear you," she said with an understanding smile. "It's been a long time coming. Too long."

Yeah, like three years too long, I thought.

"That's why I need to find her."

"I hear you," she said again, making we wonder if there was some reason we had to repeatedly confirm that my words were reaching her ears.

"I saw her come in," she continued, "but I don't know where she went."

"Thanks."

I saw a cluster of fighters gathered in the back of the second row, so I headed in that direction. Though I didn't find who I was looking for, I did discover featherweight champion Emma Watson at the center of the group, fresh off yet another successful defense of her title.

"Had to work a little overtime tonight, eh?" I said as I slung an arm over her shoulder and pulled her in for a little congragulatory hug.

Tonight's match between Emma and top contender Victoria Justice had gone the distance. Despite the fact that Em was in control throughout - including the majority of the third round where she really poured it on - Victoria simply would not yield. This was not exactly a surprise, as the Hellfire fighter has pushed Emma hard in the past. In fact, she actually stole the show the first time they met - putting an abrupt end to a fight Emma was clearly winning by knocking the champion cold with an uppercut from hell.

The title had not been on the line that night. At that point, Victoria hadn't earned her shot, yet. But the win did much to change that. It put the world - and, specifically, the champion - on notice. She was going to be a threat to Emma's dominance. And that threat has grown a great deal since then.

As Justice continues to improve in every facet of the game, the challenge she presents increases exponentially. Her upward trajectory has not been lost on Emma, who definitely saw tonight as a major advancement of that trend. But, more than anything, it was Victoria's fearless mettle that made the greatest impression in this one.

"She's so tough," Emma said of her opponent.

"I noticed," I said with a smile.

"I mean, she's too dangerous to take any chances with," she continued. "I just wanted to stay on her. Keep the pressure on. But I couldn't put her away."

"She's resilient, I'll give her that. But you've got the skills to deal with anyone. You showed that tonight. And the judges saw it."

"Hmm... I don't know," she said, shaking her head as she was obviously thinking forward to future contests against Justice.

"Just keep thinking that way," I said. "Bring those doubts with you to training, and see what happens."

She smiled and gave a little nod.

For the record, that was me being nice to Emma. I admit it was odd, but it seemed necessary. After all, she'd earned it. However, I would like to have this moment properly documented, so I can throw it at her the next time she claims I don't show her enough respect.

"Hey, have you seen Aly?" I asked.

Emma frowned as she scanned the faces around her.

"I could have sworn she was here," she said. "I know I saw her."

"OK, thanks," I said as I moved to continue my search.

I rounded the corner and nearly bumped directly into heavyweight contender Dianna Dahlgren. I took a moment to congratulate her on her resounding victory over the more experienced Johanna Lundback. The win continued an amazing string of positive results for the young phenom, and it put her in touch with division leaders Samantha Hoopes and Caroline Wozniacki.

It was a nice moment and a rare opportunity to spend some time with our budding star. But she didn't have any idea where Aly was, either. So, it brought me no closer to accomplishing my original goal.

I continued my trek, stopping here and there to chat with one of the night's participants. The most notable was a chance to applaud young Demi Lovato for finally putting together a quality, end-to-end win over a divisional opponent.

Still, no one knew where Alyson Michalka was. Quite a few said they'd seen her, but that only told me that she was probably still in the building somewhere. This information was not entirely helpful.

As I rounded into the final row of lockers, I was met by another small group - this one assembled around middleweight champion Jennifer Lawrence.

"You looked fantastic out there, Jen," I said. "It looks like you've got Debby Ryan pretty much figured out, at this point."

She shook her head. Slow, but emphatic.

"I don't think so," she said. "I got my ass slapped around in that first fight, and I still don't know how she did it. Can't stop it if I can't figure out what happened."

Gee... do I once again hear the hushed tones of self-doubt?

Just as Emma won't ever take victory over Victoria - or any opponent - for granted, Jen's view on the gap between she and her nemesis was very pragmatic. Confidence is a wonderful thing, but having a healthy respect for your opponent is important, too.

Don't fear her. Prepare for her.

And if you think that sounds like something you might hear Stephanie McMahon say, well then you know exactly where I got it from.

Still... taking a little pride once in a while is not a sin.

"Why don't you just take a little time to enjoy the win?" I suggested. "Save the negative for another day, huh?"

She actually laughed at that one.

"Negative? Moi?" said one of the most self-aware and self-deprecating women I've ever met.

"Maybe a little," I said, holding my hand up in front of me - my thumb and forefinger about an inch apart.

She looked up and away in an exaggerated manner. Like she was pretending to think hard about that idea.

"Hmm... maybe," she agreed playfully.

I congratulated her again, then asked if she'd seen Aly. And for the first time, I actually got some real information.

"She went thatta way," Jen answered, pointing me down the short hallway that led to the trainer's area.

As soon as I turned down the corridor, I knew something was up. Because that's where I found team leader Danni Harris, meandering in a small circle outside of one of the rooms.

"Aly?" I asked.

"Yeah," Danni said with a tiny little nod and a thin-lipped expression.

For a moment, I thought something serious had happened.

"Is she hurt?"

"No. She's just..."

She shrugged.

"I think she's having some trouble accepting what's happened."

"What does that mean?"

We held on each other for a moment, but she didn't say anything more.

"Should I try to talk to her?" I asked.

Yet another shrug.

"It can't hurt," she said.

I kept my eyes on her as I reached for the doorknob, but she gave me no further clues. I quickly flashed through a few different expectations of what I might find inside.

I could have stood there all day. I never would have come up with the scenario I found when I opened that door.

The UMMA travels to so many locations, I doubt I could even name half of them. Each city is unique, and each venue has its own character and flair. But there are certain aspects that are common throughout. A locker room is a locker room. The colors change. The layout varies. But the basics are always the same. There's a row of lockers, and a bench to sit on. You get changed, then stuff your things in the locker and lock it up. Same in Japan as it is in the States... or Australia... or wherever.

The trainer's rooms also have a certain, universal familiarity. There's a long table in center of the room where the fighter sits when she's being wrapped and readied for her upcoming fight. It's also where the fighter returns to be examined when the fight is over. Perhaps for an extended stay if things didn't go as planned in the cage. Along one wall, there's a countertop with a plethora of storage compartments - its drawers and cabinets filled with the various items the trainers and medical staff require for both preparation and repair. And finally, there is equipment and lighting arrayed around the room, most of which is used for diagnosing and treating injury and trauma.

So, when I entered, I expected to find Aly sitting on the table. Maybe a member of the medical staff tending to her.

Instead I found darkness. Light from the hall slanted in behind me - my resulting shadow cast long and menacing across the cream colored floor tiles. But the lights in the room were off.

"Aly?" I said into the darkness.

"Yeah," answered a small voice.

Then I thought I heard a sniffle.

"What... what's going on?"

Another sniffle, then, "I don't know."

I glanced back into the hallway with a perplexed look on my face. Danni just looked at me and shrugged again.

"Can you close the door?" Aly asked.

"Can I turn the lights on?"

"Not yet."

Ooooookay.

I closed the door behind me, plunging the room into complete darkness. That's how it seemed at first, anyway. After giving my eyes a moment to adjust, though, I realized that wasn't entirely true. LEDs and status lights blinked from the various pieces of equipment. Along the back wall, a large magnifying glass attached to a jointed mechanical arm had its lamp turned on. And there, silhouted in the halo of its faint glow, was the unmistakeably head of flowing blonde hair that belonged to Alyson Michalka.

I focused my attention on the shadowy outline and said, "Aly, what the hell is going on?"

"I don't know," she repeated. Only this time the words came out wet and strangled by emotion.

As my vision continued to adapt, I started to make out other shadows and shapes in the room. Before long, I got a clear impression of the edge of the table. I stepped up to it, then felt my way along until I got to where Aly was.

"Are you okay?" I asked her.

I could hear the concern starting to grow in my voice. Only a short time ago, this woman fought her heart out in a grueling battle with nemesis Erin Heatherton. Though Aly has certainly had some success against Erin, more often than not things have not gone her way. And in those instances where the title was on the line, Aly's results have been solidly abysmal. But tonight was different. Despite her past failures, she kept her head in the fight and she stayed on top of the runway beauty. As always, though, Erin didn't make things easy. Rather than scoring the knockout or attaining a submission, Aly was forced to take the long road... outfighting and outlasting her opponent from wire-to-wire. It was a daunting task, but she pulled it off. She maintained full control throughout the fight. Every time Erin tried to gain some ground, Aly dug in and beat her back. In the end, there was no doubt and the decision had been easy for the judges. As tough and resourceful as Erin had been, both the win and the belt deservedly went to Aly.

So now, here she sat. Alyson Michalka, FAC light heavyweight champion. Finally, she had the belt she'd always dreamed of. And she was celebrating by hiding in the the trainer's room. Crying to herself in the dark.

I could not shake the feeling that something had to be terribly wrong.

"I'm fine, I guess," she said. "I just don't know what's wrong with me."

I shook my head lightly in confusion.

"I don't understand. Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Did something happen?"

"No."

"Then why are you back here crying?"

"I already told you. I don't know."

I spread my hands out in front of me in a what the fuck? type gesture. It was a pointless move, since Aly couldn't really see me. But apparently she still had enough sense to know I was bewildered without the need for visuals.

"I just... I should be happy," she said. "I know I should be happy. I mean, I am happy. But I can't stop crying. I feel like I'm losing my mind."

I sat down beside her, relieved that it seemed my earlier fears would not be realized. However, I found it difficult to be supportive regarding her mental state.

"If it helps," I said, "I feel like you're losing your mind, too."

She sniffed, then dabbed at her cheeks with a tissue she held in her hand.

"It doesn't really help," she said.

"I was afraid of that."

She was quiet again, but I could see her head was up. Like maybe she was thinking.

"I don't know what's bothering me," she said.

Her voice was firmer now. Less emotional and more matter-of-fact.

"I really am happy," she continued. "But I guess... I didn't expect to be."

"Didn't expect to be?" I repeated. "I don't know what that means. You certainly trained like you expected to win."

"Sure," she said. "I train hard. I train to win. But then, I don't."

Suddenly, I was beginning to see what was going on.

"That's not really true," I said. "You and I both know you've had a ton of success. You have two tournament titles to your name. Both of them required tremendous runs through the very best fighters in the league. Caprice Bourret. Heidi Klum. Even Erin."

"I know, but..."

"And you've also been nothing short of perfect as the leader of The Aly Cats. It's a younger team, and you're a younger fighter..."

"I have Danni and Emma and Tiz to help out," she cut in.

"Yeah, I know," I said. "But this may surprise you... Danni and I actually talk to each other. So, I know exactly how much of what goes on with the Cats comes from you."

My attempt at irony forced a subtle smile onto her face.

It's a well known fact that Danni and I have always done much more than mere talking. We scream. We yell. We physically come to blows.

And that was before our little romance started. Just imagine what we're like now.

"That teams respects you," I said. "Not because you command it, but because you earn it."

I felt her shift uncomfortably.

I've never understood this phenomenon, but I've seen it enough times to know that it's real: the greatest leaders just lead. They don't spend a lot of time considering - or even ackowledging - their greatness.

"And don't tell Danni I gave away her secrets," I continued, "but she respects the hell out of you, too."

She said nothing.

"So I don't understand why you wouldn't expect to win tonight."

"Because I never have before."

I waited, sensing there was more coming.

"I mean, I believed I could," she said. "But these fucking title bouts. I just couldn't get my shit together. And it just kept getting worse."

Talk about the mentality of a fighter. She won the damn belt less than an hour ago, and she was still angry about the losses that preceded it.

"You were protecting yourself," I said.

She gave a slow, steady nod.

"I guess," she said. "I was so damned disappointed after that last one. I just... couldn't. I couldn't do it again. So, I told myself to forget it. Just go fight, and don't worry about what it all means."

"And now?"

"Now I have the fucking thing, and I'm a total mess over it."

In a weird way, it made sense. The losses had hurt Aly. More than any of us had realized, apparently. To cope, she shut it out. Rather than give up, she found another way to get on with her training. That way meant not getting her hopes up. Instead of training for the glory, she trained for herself. For her teammates. She made herself ready for battle, but emotionally steeled herself to accept failure if necessary.

Instead, came away victorious. And that was a result she was totally unprepared for.

So, I could understand where she was coming from, but I had no clue how to help.

Then, as if by divine intervention, my phone chirped with a message from Danni.

TP is out here.

No, RSI's stable leader was not standing outside the door with an armload of toilet paper.

TP in this case stood for training partner.

I thought for a moment, then sent back to her.

Send her in.

It was time for a fresh perspective. A view that came from the one member of RSI who knew Aly best.

A moment later, the door swung open and the lights snapped on and there in the doorway stood Daniela Hantuchova - her wide and distinctive blue eyes focused squarely on Aly.

"What is this?" she said.

I squinted hard against the sudden onslaught of brightness as I heard Aly mumble, "Oh, no."

"Yeah, you better 'oh no,'" Daniela said sternly, her Slavic accent even more pronounced than usual.

Aly squirmed, her left arm brushing against the title belt beside her. Until that moment, I had no idea it was even in the room.

Daniela strode over and stood directly in front of her training partner and newly-minted fellow champion.

"What is this?" she demanded again. "Is your big moment. Everyone is waiting. Why are you hiding back here?"

Aly squirmed some more, but said nothing.

"Aly is having a little trouble adjusting to her new role as champion," I answered for her.

"What do you mean, 'adjust?' Is nothing different."

Even I looked at her a little funny at that point. This felt like Aly's six-millionth shot at the title, and she had finally put it all together and brought it home. How was that not different?

"I just," Aly began softly. "I didn't..."

"You didn't what?" Daniela asked, with an expression that was notably light on sympathy.

"It's been so long," Aly said, gesturing toward the belt. "I didn't think I'd ever win this stupid thing."

"Why would you say this? I see you. You work hard. You never give up."

"Sure," Aly said, "but how many times have I worked my ass off and still gone home empty-handed?"

"Oh, please," Daniela replied. Then she shook her head and said, "I will never understand Americans and their emotions."

Nothing for me to argue with there. I've never really understood the American psyche, myself. And recent events have only served to prove how totally clueless I am.

Daniela zeroed back in on Aly and said, "Is no different now than when you wake up. You are same."

Alyson and I sat side-by-side and stared at the Slovakian beauty, both a little shocked but entirely interested in where this was headed.

By this point, Danni had quietly wandered in. She stood at the end of the table, also looking properly confused and intrigued.

"You are not better fighter than you were," Daniela said. "Nothing is changed. You were already great fighter. You know this because I tell you."

She emphatically thumped her forefinger into her chest a few times to drive her point home.

The remark sounded a little conceited, but there can be no argument that Daniela is one of the most talented, respected, decorated and feared fighters in the league. If she calls another fighter great... well, that opinion carries some serious heft.

"But I..." Aly stuttered.

"Shush," Daniela said, "I am not done."

In the first sign that Aly was starting to come around, she gave her partner a crooked little smile and arched an eyebrow in a playful, who the fuck do you think you're talking to? expression.

Daniela ignored the look and pressed on.

"This," she said, gesturing toward the belt sitting beside Aly, "it is trophy. Nothing more. You should be proud of it, yes. But it does not make you different. And you do not need it to prove anything. The people that matter already know."

Aly raised her head and their eyes met, and I saw the connection that was made between them. It's a connection I've seen many times before from many different RSI athletes. An unspoken respect and friendship that forms between two women who not only train with each other, but also for each other.

It is always a beautiful thing to watch.

"Stand up," Daniela said sharply.

Aly eyed her a little suspiciously, but stood, just the same.

"Raise you arms."

The skeptical look grew, but Aly did as she was asked.

Daniela reached around her friend and fellow warrior and picked up the belt from the table. Then she looped it around Aly's waist and cinched it closed.

It fit her perfectly.

When Daniela spoke again, her voice was much softer and warmer than at any previous point in the conversation.

"Now go outside," she said, "and show them all what you have done."

Aly's eyes moistened again, but her voice was stronger than ever.

"You'll come with me?" she asked.

"Of course," Daniela replied. "I train you, so I should be there to get credit."

There was a moment's hesitation while her words sank in before the rest of us burst out laughing.

"What is funny?" Daniela said, her attempt at seriousness betrayed by a small, knowing smile.

When the uproar died down, she gestured toward the door with her head.

"Come on," she said to her friend.

Aly nodded, and the two turned toward the exit. I gave Aly's hand a squeeze as she went by, and Danni set a hand on her shoulder and gave her a warm smile and a firm nod. Then my stable leader slid over and sat next to me as we watched the two light heavyweight champions make their way out into the locker room.

It didn't take long for the roar to start as Aly's stablemates got their first chance to congratulate the new champion.

Danni and I decided to steal a moment alone while we let Aly soak it in.

"Oh Richelle, my darling," she said to me.

"Yes, Danni dearest."

"Do you think we'll ever get used to this feeling?"

I let the sound of the cheers wash over me for a few seconds.

"I sure hope not," I said.

Danni took a moment of her own, then leaned over toward me and kissed my earlobe, gently pulling the tender skin with her teeth.

"Mmmm," I said.

"You know, this feeling kind of makes me hot," she whispered.

I smiled as I turned and kissed her.

"Everything makes you hot," I said.

Then I kissed her again.

"It's true," she said. "It's a curse."

"Not really," I said. "But it has to wait. We've got responsibilities."

"I can wait," she said, her eyes both calm and hungry. Like she was just biding her time before she decided to devour me.

The first time I saw that look from her, I felt a soft, steady heat spread throughout my entire body. My reaction has not changed a bit since then.

Unfortunately, though, what I said was true. We still had a ways to go before we had time to play.

"Come on, el capitán," I said as I forced myself off the table. "Let's go join our crew."

"Why do you do that?" she asked as she stood.

"Do what?"

"Suddenly slip into Spanish for no apparent reason."

"I don't know," I said. "Who the hell cares?"

"It's just strange," she said. "And by the way, it should be la capitana, in this case."

"Danni?"

"What?"

"Shut up."

Congratulations, Aly. What Daniela said was absolutely true. This title doesn't change a thing about how we feel about you, but we all know how badly you've wanted it. So we're happy as hell to see it finally hanging from your waist.
