Wait No More

The harder the struggle, the more glorious the triumph.

That's the way the saying goes, anyway. But is it true? Does success really taste sweeter when you've had to work so hard to attain it?

Emily VanCamp, who had to wait quite a while to make her way past eternal champion Irina Sheik, says that it is. Alyson Michalka hopes to find out for herself some day, if she can ever put together the kind of fight she knows she's capable of when the title is on the line.

And what does Britney Spears think?

"Fuck yeah," said the finally triumphant new DEF middleweight champion.

Britney would certainly know. Despite the fact that she consistently and successfully competes with the best in the business, she has long struggled to get her hands on the hardware. First, it was Malin Akerman, who Spears could only seem to beat when the title wasn't up for grabs. Then, it was the return of an old foe in Laura Vandervoort, who she just couldn't seem to beat, period.

"There were times when I thought it just wasn't going to happen," Britney admitted. "The more I wanted it, the further away it seemed to get."

Rather than giving in, Britney came out tonight determined to bridge that gap all at once. You could see it in her eyes from the minute she stepped into the arena. She was going to get that title from Vandervoort this time, or die trying.

Thankfully, it didn't come to that.

It was clear from the outset that this one wasn't going to end in a submission. These were two hard women beating the hell out of each other. Laura came out strong, showing the kind of skill and confidence that's made her the champion she is. She ripped into the RSI fighter from the second the bell rang. But Britney wasn't going to be denied on this night. Every punch Laura landed earned a response twice as strong. Every time Vandervoort turned the pressure on, Spears rallied and put the champion back on her heels. Then, as the opening round drew to a close, Britney let fly with a perfectly timed flying knee that exploded under Laura's chin. In yet another moment that proved Vandervoort's mettle, she not only kept her feet, but managed to offer a decent response. But it was clear the shot had rocked her, and Britney poured it on. Though Laura continued to show an unbelievable level of heart and resilience, she was never the same after that knee. Nearly a full round later, she finally succumbed to a devastating high kick, and Britney finally got hold of the belt that had eluded her for so long.

Once the fight was over, the assembled media had to wait quite a while before the new champion arrived for her press conference. The theory in the room was that she was celebrating with her teammates and composing herself before meeting with the masses. That theory was shot to hell when she finally showed up with fresh tears still streaming down her face.

Make no mistake though... she was ready for this press conference. In fact, she's been ready for years.

"Never say you can't," Spears said when asked if she had been starting to lose hope in ever reclaiming the title. "Never give up. Get the right people around you. Keep working. Keep fighting. There is no other way to win in this league."

Spoken like a woman who had earned every bit of the title she now held. And what kind of vitriol did she have stored up for the woman who had stood in her way so many times?

"She's a warrior," Britney said of her opponent. "Even though I want to sometimes, I can't say a bad word about her. Laura's always fought me clean. She's solid as a rock. And she's tough as nails."

Unhappy with the tameness of the answer, the group pressed her for more. But Britney wasn't going to give them anything because she had nothing to give.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I know you want me to hate her, but I don't. I mean, sometimes I feel like I do. Facing her is as frustrating and aggravating as it is challenging. But that's what she's supposed to do. I can't hate her for that."

Given the way Laura has remained at the top of her game and the way she's gotten it done, I agree completely. Her skills are plain to see, and her toughness is unquestionable. True, she's had something of a big mouth in the past. But her record against Britney is simply stunning. So, if she wants to talk, she certainly can.

Even after the question and answer period was over, there was still one question that hadn't been answered: what took the new champ so long getting to her triumphant press conference?

Oh, she was just waiting on a friend.

The story of Britney Spears and Elisha Cuthbert is a saga in and of itself. It has action. It has adventure. It has heartbreak. But mostly, it has nonsense.

They came to RSI as divisional competitors. It was a relationship I worried about from the beginning, and I will forever wonder what made me bring them both on board. Everything about the situation said it was doomed to fail. One was a burgeoning superstar who had tasted life at the top, only to have it quickly slip away. She had all the talent in the world, but couldn't seem to put the pieces together and get the consistency she needed to reclimb the ladder and stay there. The other was a feisty and determined scrapper, whose heart and shameless temerity had already carried her well beyond the point her talent dictated she should have plateaued.

So, how did the hungry and focused Britney react to training and competing with Elisha the pit bull? Well, we barely got a chance to find out before the 2013 Hit the Beach tournament brought the two together in the UMMA cage for the first time as stablemates. The result was an unmitigated disaster. Elisha came away with a shattered ankle, Britney came away with a heavy heart and I came away with an ulcer.

No way I could put the three of us through that again. The story of what came next is well known, at this point. After giving it a lot of thought, I made the difficult decision to release Elisha. Then, with a fiery Cuthbert standing in my office absolutely refusing to accept my resolution, who should arrive to support her but Britney herself. It seems the Talent and the Tigress had done more than find a way to coexist. They had found a way to combine themselves into something more than the sum of what they were individually. And neither had any desire to go back to the way things used to be.

The years that followed have been a lesson for all of us. I think I've learned what it takes to make a stable run, or, at least, how to fake it with panache. Stable leader Danneel Harris has stepped into her own and learned to let her natural leadership skills do the work. Development Coordinator Stephanie McMahon has learned that she needs to share what she knows to really be happy. Or as happy as she's going to get, anyway. But all of us can thank Britney and Elisha for showing us what it means to be a friend and teammate in a world where violence and competitive fire normally rule the day.

Britney and Elisha... the happiest accident I ever had.

Getting back to the press conference... Elisha's own match didn't go very well tonight. We'll get to those details later, but, for now, I'll just say the result had Leesh in with the medical team for a full and lengthy checkout after the event. So, Britney came into the locker room after her big win and shared her new title with her other teammates. Then, when the celebration died down and it was time to meet the media, she walked to the back of the room and sat down on a bench and waited.

Part of my responsibility at these events is to get these athletes where they're supposed to be somewhere close to on time. This task becomes much more critical when the fighter in question was involved in a title matchup. So, I took it upon myself to go to where Britney was sitting... and I sat down beside her and waited, as well. Because both of us knew she wasn't leaving that room without Elisha.

When the door to the treatment room finally opened, I watched as Leesh dumped her troubles on the floor at the sight of her friend. She rushed across the gray concrete floor, and poor Britney barely got a chance to get to her feet before she was wrapped up and crushed by her Canadian confidant.

Knowing Brit would be out when she was damn good and ready, I left them there... in the middle of a celebration much more special than any we'd had so far.

Enjoy the well-earned and long-awaited title Brit, cause Leesh isn't going to stand for it if you don't. Take a day... hell, take a whole week... and don't think about Laura at all. Then, come back and think about her all the time. Because I guarantee she's going to be thinking about you.

Leader Down

As elated as I am about Britney's big win, this event has left me feeling mostly unwell. Because, while there was joy at the top of our middleweight group, there was shocking sadness surrounding the best of our welterweights.

Actually, I guess that isn't really true. There is nothing truly stunning about Danneel Harris falling to RWB powerhouse Cheryl Cole. But Danni without her belt is a tough thing for the women of RSI to get used to.

Well, for most of us, anyway.

"What a fight!" said a much too excited Harris after her razor-thin loss. "This is exactly the kind of thing I expect from Cheryl. The kind of fight I want from her. She really made me reach down and pull out everything I had."

Seriously... doesn't she make you nauseous? At least, a little bit?

In typical Danni fashion, all she could talk about was the quality of the fight, and what it means for her reignited rivalry with Cole.

"It's exciting," she said. "I think it's exciting for the fans. I think it's exciting for the two of us, as well. Cheryl and I have both been around a long time, and this is why we still do it. Winning is nice, but it's the competition we want."

It's hard to say she's wrong. No question, the fight was loaded with crowd-pleasing action, and the back and forth was unbelievable. So much so that even the judges couldn't agree on who won. Her point about it being exciting for the fighters also appeared to be accurate, given the way the two smiled wickedly at each other as each round drew to a close... each seeming to silently acknowledge how they had gone toe-to-toe for another five minutes with neither yielding an inch to the other.

Wondering just how much Danni was enjoying the grueling, seesaw battle? Well, according to her, fifteen minutes of war with Cole was not enough.

"Didn't these championship fights used to be five rounds?" RSI's in-cage leader asked the assembled media. "Maybe we should go back to that. Because I wasn't ready to quit, and I don't think Cheryl was, either."

Well, I hate to rain on her little defeat celebration, but I have some issues with tonight's result. Danni's teammates had some questions, too. Unfortunately, most of them were worded such that they were not fit to print. But there was one who managed to keep her phrasing respectable. One who has some first-hand experience in such matters.

"It's curious," ESL lightweight champion Emily VanCamp said of the judge's decision. "It's rare to see a title change hands when a fight is that close. Even rarer to have it happen when the judges can't even agree."

Curious, indeed. So much so that even Danni admitted she didn't understand how the result came to be.

"Sure, it's disappointing," she said of the split-decision loss. "I felt like I gave as good as I got, so I'm not sure how I lost. But Cheryl was great, too. The judges had a tough call to make."

No. It wasn't that tough at all. There is a precedent for how this sort of thing gets handled. One that was set decades ago, and holds in any judged combat sport. The challenger has to clearly win for the title to change hands. A fight this close usually goes to the champion. If the judges really can't decide, then they should call it a draw.

I've unloaded in the past about the UMMA's failure to follow this tradition. While the war between Emily and Irina Sheik is now the stuff of legend, it wasn't always that way. Not that long ago, Irina was the established champion, while Em was the upstart challenger. So, when one of their early battles was declared a draw - even though I felt Emily held the edge - I accepted it. Irina had been granted the benefit of the unwritten champion's advantage, which is exactly what should have happened. Then, after Em went on to win the title in unquestionable fashion, she had it stripped away again in a very close fight that went to Irina. The fact that the unwritten rule that had earlier protected Sheik did not seem to extend to Emily caused me to go absolutely ballistic.

And now, for your reading entertainment, I'm gonna do it again.

Just what the hell was this, anyway? You take the belt away from a longtime champion and give it to her opponent because you think she was maybe, kinda, sorta a little bit better? How silly does that sound? The title changing hands on a split-decision? Ridiculous. That means the judges themselves couldn't even get on the same page.

And don't anybody get it in their head that I'm just mad that RSI lost a title. I've said before, I don't judge RSI's performance by counting the number of trophies in the glass case. And remember, I sided with the decision when Emily was denied the belt that night back in 2013. So my opinion has been consistent throughout.

I also don't want to hear that I'm overreacting because it was Danni. Yes, she and I have become close as we've worked together to grow our little organization. Yes, it's different for me because it's her. But I took a long time to think about things before I wrote this, and I can say without question that my opinion would be the same, regardless of the fighters involved. This was wrong, plain and simple.

That said, there is one other thing I need to make clear: This is not an attack on Cheryl. This is no more about her than it is about Danni. Cole fought a great fight, and she deserves credit for that. On top of that, Danni and I have talked about this rivalry several times, and we know Cheryl isn't going to be denied. She's going to get hers, and I don't think Danni will always be able to stop her. We also know the fights won't always be this close. Tonight's fight was a rare gem... a night when two top flight competitors both brought their A-game and put on a spectacle for the fans. But there will be times when Danni slips up and Cheryl knocks her stupid. That's just the way the game works. Hopefully, there will also be times when it goes the other way, and Danni will get to Cheryl first.

Look, I can't make the case that Danni should have won this fight outright. I think, if you add up the numbers, they lean ever so slightly her way. But god-damn, it was close. And if you were just judging by the eye test, then it was even closer. I've never seen a fight that screamed to be called a draw any more than this one. Cheryl did what she had to, and she doesn't deserve an L on her record for it. My only point is that I don't see how she did enough to get the W, either.

Alright, that's enough of that. I'm gonna hop down off my soapbox now, and try to get in line with Danni's view of things. It's better for my blood pressure.

Besides, she's gonna have my ass when she sees what I wrote. So, I can put my energy into stressing over that, instead.

Dark (and Familiar) Territory

Well, it was fun while it lasted.

After making her wait an eternity to get there, fate - and Adriana Lima - conspired to make Petra Cubonova's run at the top decidedly short-lived. Tonight marked Lima's first opportunity to win back her title, and, despite a terrific effort from Petra, she wasn't about to let it get away from her.

"I am... how you say? Disappointed," the Czechian model said afterward. "Adriana is great fighter. I know this, and I prepare for it. But tonight... was not enough."

Well, maybe not... but it sure as hell wasn't bad. For the better part of two rounds, this was a battle fought on equal footing, and neither fighter was timid about tearing into the other. It was ferocious, entertaining and not at all for the squeamish. Anyone who views these two as just a pair of pretty faces should definitely think again. This was war, and it was either woman's fight to lose until Adriana scored a perfect takedown toward the end of the second round. In my opinion, that was Cubonova's only real slip-up of the fight. Unfortunately, against someone of Adriana's ability, one slip-up is all it takes. Though Petra's mat skills are normally very good, they seemed to abandon her when she needed them most. Lima calmly worked her position until she got an arm around the Czechian's throat, and then the Brazilian easily choked her opponent out.

To many, Lima's win represented a simple return to the norm. And the many actually included Petra, herself.

"This is not surprise," said the deflated supermodel. "It has been this way many, many times."

Sorry Petra, but I'm not joining you in this pity party.

Yes, this loss is one of many she has suffered at Adriana's hands, but this was not a case of same old, same old. Though Lima may have gotten the crown she was after, Petra clearly showed she's not going away. This was a battle worthy of a title contest, and, rather than revealing Petra as clearly second-rate to Lima, it actually elevated their rivalry to a new level.

Not that I don't understand where Petra is coming from. It's hard to appreciate the beauty of a fight like this when you're the one who comes out on the losing end. So, I looked elsewhere to get some informed perspective.

"They both fought a great fight," said Petra's training partner, SCQ champion Catherine Bell. "Adriana put together a brilliant string of attacks at the end. She got Petra out of position for just a second, but that was enough. It's no secret how good she is, but really... she executed that whole series to perfection. I don't think she could have done it better."

Then, without prompting, Mac switched from fight fan to friend and teammate. When asked if this win marked the return to Adriana's domination of Cubonova, Bell was quick to snap the lid shut on that line of thinking.

"This isn't like before," she said. "Petra's much better than she used to be, and she showed it out there. But the old trend isn't going to reverse itself. Just because Petra improved, that doesn't mean Adriana is going to forget how to win. I think this is how their fights are going to be from now on. Hard-fought, brutal and even."

There's not much I can add to that. When you have to compete with a fighter like Lima on a constant basis, that's going to be a rough life. Give her a little time, and Petra will realize she has no reason to hang her head over this.

Turning It On

Falling victim to a takedown attempt within seconds was probably not the way Tiffany Mulheron wanted to start out tonight's featherweight title defense. Credit to her for powering her way through it and forcing Kate Lawler to pull guard, but it still brought the action to the mat - a place Tiffany doesn't really like to be.

The next couple of minutes were a tense contest of wills. Lawler is perfectly capable of finishing a fight on the ground, and she fought like hell to improve her position so she could attack. But Tiff held firm, stalling the action long enough for referee Fearn Cotton to intercede.

Once the fighters got back to their feet, it was like somebody flipped a switch. The same Tiffany who had been holding on for dear life only seconds before was suddenly in complete control. Lawler couldn't hit her and Mulheron couldn't miss as the champion had her way with the challenger. Though Kate managed to survive the round, it was clear Tiffany's attacks had taken a heavy toll on her. She was obviously out of it as the next round began, and Tiff wasted no time - putting an end to things within seconds.

Afterward, Mulheron indicated she was pretty happy with her performance.

"I wasn't perfect, but perfect isn't what you're looking for against a fighter like Kate," said the Scottish actress. "With someone like her, you just try to live on and take your chances where you find them."

Emma Watson - fresh from her Esquire magazine interview with Tom Hanks - managed to make it to the Land Down Under in time to serve as Tiffany's second for the fight. So, she had a nice, close-up view of the action. She also happens to be the person who knows Tiffany best, and she was not a bit surprised by the way this one went.

"Tiff has really come into her own since finally getting past Nadine," she said, referencing Tiffany's long and arduous struggles with the elite Nadine Coyle. "Her skills were always there, but she couldn't stop doubting herself. That's not a problem, anymore. She knows what she can do. She knows she can compete with anyone. And that makes her extremely dangerous."

Not to mention the fact that she trains with a fellow champion on a daily basis, right Em? Oh, and then there's that co-training agreement with Dragon's Den, which grants both Tiffany and Emma the opportunity to work with the legendary Rachel McAdams. All that talent in one place is still something I can't get used to seeing.

Constantly pushing herself against some of the best in the game has brought Mulheron a level of confidence that she simply didn't have before.

"I don't feel the need to prove myself, anymore," she said. "With Emma and Rachel, I get all the proof I need that I'm good at this. So, now I can just focus on going out there and doing what I need to do to win, instead of worrying about what it will mean if I lose."

The quest for validation is often seen as a bad thing, and, in many walks of life, it is. There is no point in spending time and energy chasing after someone else's definition of success. But in a sport like MMA, the definition is the same for everyone. You have to win. Even Lawler - who has now dropped two title chances in a row to Tiffany - can lay claim to being one of the league's finest. Not because of any past or current titles she's held, but simply because her record says she is. She has stepped in the cage with the best of the best - including McAdams - and she has won. She has passed the big tests.

For a long time, Tiffany kept failing those same tests, but she's finally gotten over that hump. Now, win or lose, she knows she's one of the best. Not because of her title, but simply because she is.

A Unique Riddle

Elisha Cuthbert didn't need any extra reasons to keep her post-event focus squarely on Britney's big victory, but she got one anyway. Because her match against Lindsay Ellingson was certainly one to forget. I won't dive into the details, because there are no details to dive into. Leesh got her head handed to her in short order, and that was the end of that.

This is now her third straight loss to Ellingson, and each result seems to get worse. It's a puzzling situation, considering Leesh has beaten heavy hitters such as Karen Gillan, Josie Maran and even Britney. Sure, she's had her share of struggles too, but no one seems to present her with the difficulties Lindsay does.

"It's a problem," the actress admitted, as she took a break from her friend's championship celebration. "A lot of the time, these fights come down to style matchups, and there's just something about her style I can't seem to adjust to."

That was all she had to say on the subject, and she was too busy helping Britney enjoy her moment to let it get her down, right now. Of course, Britney couldn't help but take the time to provide a comment of her own.

"She covers it, but this has been hard on Leesh," said the Womanizer singer. "I know she's frustrated with it, and it's got me frustrated, too. She's better than this."

Whatever the problem is, we need to get it worked out soon. Because, despite her struggles against Lindsay, the two have not gotten much separation in the rankings. So it's very likely they'll be seeing each other again, in the near future.

First Impressions

Cobie Smulders' first ever meeting with Holly Valance didn't turn out the way she'd hoped. And to add insult to injury, Holly ended things with an embarrassing submission move that Cobie isn't likely to forget anytime soon.

"It's tough," Smulders said afterward. "I felt like I was holding my own, and I'm really not sure how it got away from me." Though she refused to comment on the way the fight ended, she openly admitted the loss was a bitter pill to swallow. "I didn't know how this one was going to go," the Avengers actress said. "Holly has a lot of experience on me, so I just stayed focused on the fundamentals. But as the fight progressed, I truly started to believe I could win. So, having it disappear so suddenly... that was rough."

The point she made about her relative inexperience is one I've been harping on for a while now. Cobie's career started off in sensational fashion, and those results now have her sandwiched in with some seasoned competition. So the sledding is going to be tough for her, but I think the struggles will make her a better fighter in the end.

Solid Wins

Rounding out the night's action, both Jennifer Morrison and Kristen Bell came away with decisive victories tonight, each bouncing back from a tough loss in her last outing.

Jen was able to get the better of sometime training partner Rachael Leigh Cook of Dragon's Den, in a fight that looked very one-sided. Afterward, Morrison admitted that a little luck was involved.

"Rach surprised me by swinging for the fences a couple of times early on. I was lucky to get myself out of the way, honestly. If she'd connected with one of those, things might have gone a very different way."

Meanwhile, Kristen had to survive some scary moments on the mat with opponent Noureen DeWulf in the early going. But she did a nice job of keeping her head - managing the action until the ref got the fighters back to their feet. Once they were upright, K-Bell was able to take advantage of DeWulf's relative inexperience, blitzing the younger fighter and overwhelming her with a near perfect striking game.

"I felt good out there," the former Veronica Mars star said. "Noureen has a very aggressive style, and she did a great job with that early takedown. That's an area I still need to work on, and she really knew my weakness. Those first few minutes were not fun."

Rookie Season

A little personnel news before we go, as RSI is excited to introduce a pair of new rookies to roster.

Paige Hathaway is a small town girl born and raised in Minnesota. After living several different places she is now living in the great southern state of Oklahoma.

"I always get asked how I got to where I am today. There was no strategically planned path I took to a career in fitness. It all started rather unexpectedly. In 2011 I was not the toned and fit woman I am today, as some of you know from my social media posts. I was very thin with hardly any muscle tone. It was then that I was approached by a trainer at the gym about proper health and nutrition. After speaking with the trainer I decided to step out of my comfort zone and enter my first bikini competition."

From that point forward, Paige decided the traditional modeling route was not for her. She became addicted to working out. Not being thin, but being healthy, fit and strong. Two years later, she entered the Flex Bikini Model Search and won. After that, there was no stopping her. Very early on, though, her focus moved from competitions to motivation. Now, she splits her time between modeling and working to inspire women to make themselves the best they can possibly be.

Too bad being so fit isn't very pretty. I mean, seriously girls... who would want to look like Paige? All ugly and muscle-y and stuff. I mean... ewww. No way boys would find that kind of stuff attractive.

Yeah, screw that. If there's any way I can look half as good as she does, I'm in. And if the boys don't like it... well, screw them, too.

Oh, and my favorite thing about Paige? Pizza. That's right. She loves pizza. So much so that she lists her official occupation as Fitness Model/Pizza Enthusiast.

So, that's our goal. She's gonna teach me how to eat pizza and look like that. I have the pizza eating part down pretty good, so we're halfway there. And if we manage to pull this off, then I really don't care if she ever wins a single fight.

Our second addition may not be as big a pizza fan, but she also has one of those ugly, strong bodies I would kill to have.

A down-home Texas girl, Hope Beel's possesses a body that obviously did not build itself. It took hard work, discipline and dedication. I'm sure most of you will agree that those are some scary words, but they don't seem to bother Hope at all.

"I love the challenge of transforming my body and pushing myself to the limits both physically and mentally. That love made me want to share my passion for fitness and nutrition with the world! Because of this, I started my own nutrition business in order to help others reach their health and fitness goals."

Hmmm... nutrition, huh? That doesn't sound like as much fun as pizza.

Either way, we have high hopes for both of these women, and we're looking forward to helping them put those bodies they've built to work in the cage.
