Czechian Resolve

If at first you don't succeed, try, try again. That's the adage we learn as children. It's purpose is to teach us the meaning of hard work and perseverance. But as we get older, we come to realize the saying is woefully inadequate. What happens if we don't succeed the second time? Or the third? There has to come a point where you just say, The hell with it. This isn't going to work, right?

Not if you're Petra Cubonova, there isn't.

Six times. Six times, Petra has faced off with Adriana Lima with the title on the line. Over the course of those contests, there have been one-sided beatings, brutal knockouts and devastating injuries. There have also been a few close, exciting fights. But there is one thing that has never happened.

Petra has never won.

Never, that is, until today.

Lucky number seven was the key for Petra, and she actually made it look easy. She landed a sensational high kick mere seconds after the opening bell, and a dazed and bleeding Lima never recovered as Cubonova hammered her from pillar to post. Though it looked like this one was over almost from the outset, Adriana refused to fall and Petra refused to yield. She knew that unconscious on the canvas was the only time Lima wasn't dangerous, and she wasn't going to stop swinging until that happened.

When it was over, there was no need to ask Petra how she felt about the win. The child-like smile on her face said it all.

Along with being unable to hide her emotions, Petra also seemed unable to hold her tongue after her big victory. When asked how she felt about her performance tonight, she said simply, "I finally kicked her ass."

Okay... so maybe that isn't quite the level of humility I prefer from the women of RSI. But after all that Petra's been through, I feel comfortable letting that one slide. Especially since the Czechian model clarified her stance when asked if she felt she was being disrespectful of one of the best fighters in the UMMA.

"Why I cannot say this?" she asked angrily. "She has kicked my ass many times. I not say she is bad fighter. She is great fighter. I know this better than all others. But tonight... is finally my turn."

When asked what helped her turn the corner and find a way past Lima, Petra's answer consisted of one word. "Mac," she said.

When RSI signed Catherine Bell, who is widely known for her role as Sarah 'Mac' MacKenzie on JAG, it was a move that was really born out of necessity. At the time, our entire heavyweight roster consisted of a pair of rookies. We were desperate for leadership in that group, so it seemed like a miracle when a quality veteran like Cat became available. We wasted no time snatching her up, and we were hopeful we had our answer. But then it was decided that RSI would enter the team competition, and we found ourselves short at that weight class, again. We brought in Petra to fill the void, knowing we were taking a risk. Cat was only just settling in when Petra's arrival shook up the group. On top of that, the two women weren't exactly cut from the same mold. One was a successful actress who prided herself on her professionalism, while the other was a model who made a living looking pretty and wearing practically nothing. Not really a match made in heaven. Though the friction was muted, the fact was obvious... they didn't like each other much. The question was, could they find a way to work together?

Well, they did have one thing in common: they love to compete. Though they are very different people, they're both after the same thing in the cage. And the more they competed, the more they learned that maybe they weren't so different.

"Petra wasn't who I thought she was, at all," Mac said. "She takes the training very seriously. She pushes me, and I push back. Being able to train with someone who enjoys that kind of competitive environment has been a blessing."

Petra also seemed pleased with the feel of their training sessions. "Mac is not just all the time training," said the Czechian beauty. "She has funny side. We enjoy training hard together. Training with her is no work at all."

Interestingly enough, this odd couple have paid bigger dividends than anyone could have imagined. Remember how I said they both want the same thing in the cage? Well now, they both have it. Only a few short months ago, Mac picked up her first title, soundly defeating champion Traci Bingham.

With Petra having struggled so mightily over such a long period, did Mac's big win threaten the relationship? Nope. Because Mac wouldn't let it.

"After she win belt, I go to her to say congratulations," Petra said. "She not say thank you. Instead, she look me straight in eye and say, 'Now, is you're turn.'"

Indeed, having trained so hard together to achieve their goals, Catherine was determined not to celebrate alone. She made it her mission to get Petra the title she longed for, and, tonight, that mission became a success.

"I knew she had it in her," a very happy Bell said after Petra's win. "She's a hard worker. She's tough, and very resilient." She thought a moment, then added, "I guess you have to be, if you're her. To keep bouncing off Adriana like that, only to come back time after time? I don't know if I could have done it, but she did."

I'm not sure what I should say to you, Petra. Both you and I... and everybody else for that matter... know that there is a very unhappy Brazilian over in the ILF camp, tonight. Adriana is going to come for you like a woman possessed. But if there is anyone on the RSI roster who was the strength and resolve to weather that storm, it's you. Enjoy your new belt. Hell, bring it in and hang it in the RSI lobby, if you want. You've earned that right.

What is there left to say about Danneel Harris? I've lauded her skills in the cage. I've trumpeted her abilities as stable leader. And I've freely admitted that I would be lost without her. So, when she steps into that cage and defends her title in the dominant fashion she did tonight, how am I supposed to write about that without repeating myself? Again.

I guess the answer is that I'm not. I guess I'm supposed to sing her praises again and again, and just hope people don't get bored with it.

I know I never do.

Danni was just about perfect tonight, as she turned aside challenger Kristanna Loken once more. Every time this happens, I talk about how skilled a fighter Kristanna is. I'm not just making that up to be nice. Loken is very talented, and she's beaten a number of good fighters during the course of her career. But Danni is just a bad matchup for her. On top of that, it would be hard to imagine that Danni isn't in Kristanna's head, at this point. Danni certainly seems to think so.

"I think she's starting to guess," Danni said of her opponent. "She's trying to get ahead of me by predicting what I'll do. That isn't going to work on me." As only Danni can, she seemed to commiserate with Kristanna, while stopping short of actually feeling bad for her. "Kristanna doesn't need, or want, my pity. But I think we've all been where she is. She's the type of fighter... if I wasn't her opponent, I'd probably root for her." When asked if she's tired of facing Kristanna over and over again, Danni refused to take the bait. "Kristanna occupies that top contender's spot for a reason," she said. "She deserves as many chances as she can get until someone else comes along and takes that spot away." After a brief pause, though, she did admit she'd like to see a change. "I wish there was some way we could take a break from it," she said. "Give her a chance to get her head on straight." That sounded remarkably like the pity Danni swore she wasn't giving out, but she was quick to clear things up. "I'm confident in my abilities," she said. "When I defend the title, I want to face the best my opponent has to offer. I know Kristanna has more to offer, and I think a little break will help her tap into that."

Alas, it doesn't appear Loken will be getting that break anytime soon. Though a win tonight by Jennifer Lamiraqui helped closed the gap, it looks like the next title shot will be Kristanna's, once again. As I've said before, Loken has proven she has what it takes to compete with Danni. Whether or not she shows it is another matter.

Meanwhile, there was a sobering moment amongst all the happiness, tonight. Elisha Cuthbert suffered a devastating injury in a one-sided dismantling by middleweight champion Malin Akerman. I'm not going to try and make excuses for Elisha. Malin is a skilled and dangerous opponent, but Leesh has shown in the past that she's up to the task. She's certainly shown she's better than this. For whatever reason, she didn't show up for this one, and that's the cold hard truth.

Given the past tension regarding injuries between RSI and Hellfire, I've already been asked if I feel the injury to Elisha was a deliberate act on Malin's part. Just to put a lid on that conversation, I will say emphatically that I do not. Malin's job in that situation is to press her advantage until the fight is over. That's what every fighter is told to do. Protecting Elisha was not Malin's responsibility. End of story.

Meanwhile, the injury just adds to the woes suffered by RSI's middleweight group. Britney Spears is just coming back from a bad injury suffered at Open Fight Night. The hope was that her return would make the group whole again, allowing them to get back to their normal training program. Elisha's injury means that hope is now dashed.

However, the situation brings to light a "co-training" agreement that's recently been reached between Rampage Sports and Dragon's Den. I admit this wasn't really my idea, and I haven't been all that thrilled about the concept. It seemed to me we were doing pretty well, all on our own. But as we know, I don't run RSI by myself. Certain influential members of the RSI family convinced me that expanding our training will give the fighters more variation and experience, so I grudgingly went along. Now, Elisha's injury has helped me see what they were talking about. Having the opportunity to train with a talent like Josie Maran while Elisha is on the mend cannot be a bad thing for Britney.

Getting back to the positive, all of the remaining RSI fighters emerged victorious tonight. Cobie Smulders scored a dominating win over Elodie Frege, cementing her place in the welterweight rankings. Kristen Bell continued her rebirth here at RSI, as she looked crisp and confident while knocking off Nelly Furtado. Danica Patrick got back in the win column, as she answered an early takedown attempt by AJ Lee with a right hand that dropped the WWE star where she stood. And Jennifer Morrison showed the kind of talent we knew she had in her triumphant RSI debut. Working her way past a takedown by opponent Roxanne Pallett, Jen stayed cool and stuck with the plan. An injury ended this one too soon, but I'm happy with what we saw from her out there.

Plenty going on at the next event for RSI, as the ESL takes center stage. Tops on everyone's list will be the next installment of the ongoing saga between Emily VanCamp and Irina Sheik. These two have always seemed to have a healthy respect for one another, but that never seems to stop them from trying to destroy each other in the cage. Also, Olivia Wilde will get another shot at Daniela Hantuchova's title. Olivia has a new stable behind her now, and many are thinking it's only a matter of time before she gives Daniela a run for her money. Will it be now? Plenty of other action in store, including rookie Michelle Lewin trying to finally get into the win column and fan favorite Anna Kendrick taking on the vastly more experienced Emmy Rossum.
