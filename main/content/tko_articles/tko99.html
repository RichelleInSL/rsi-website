Back on top

Five fights in less than five rounds. And, every single second jam packed with action. Julianne Hough and Vikki Blows went at it again tonight, with the bantamweight title on the line for the third straight time. Vikki carried the belt into the first fight, and Julianne carried it out. When Julianne brought it to the rematch, it left with Vikki. And tonight? Vikki carried it in, and surprise, surprise... it's going back home with Jules. Rivalries just don't get any better than this one.

These fights go so fast, it's impossible to break down the tactics. That's not to say that the women are just windmilling at each other. On the contrary, these two are so fast and accurate, you need to watch everything in slow motion to figure out what happened. One fighter misses, and the other is all over her. It was Vikki who missed first tonight, and that gave Julianne the upper hand early. But, Vikki fought back, driving Jules back against the fence and hammering away from the clinch. The blows were clearly hurting Hough, but she stayed focused and narrowly avoided a crushing knee strike that surely would have ended things. The miss gave the advantage back to Julianne, who was immediately on the attack. Neither fighter gave her opponent any room to breath. If you missed, you would pay for it. And, Vikki paid dearly as Jules blasted her with an uppercut that sent her to the canvas. Match over, right? Not a chance. Vikki rolled right back to her feet and attacked again. Julianne blocked and countered. Vikki side-stepped and delivered an open hand slap that rocked Hough badly. But, her guard came up automatically and blocked the follow-up attack, and then she countered again. Again, Blows managed to evade, but Julianne was on the move this time and Vikki whiffed on a counter of her own. Finally, Julianne had the opening she needed, and the brutal punch she used to end things made MY head hurt. And, all of that happened in a minute and fifty seconds.

These two women... I mean, Jesus Christ... these two fucking women....

Afterward, Julianne made it clear that the fighters were well aware of the show they put on. She entered the conference room with her hands raised to the ceiling and, in the deepest voice she could manage, asked, "Are you not entertained? Is this not why you are here?" All present had a good laugh, but Jules was quick to clarify she was not mocking her opponent. "Months ago, I made the decision to push myself as hard as I could. I wanted Vikki's belt, I went after it and I got it. In fact, I started to beat her pretty easily. After that, it was up to Vikki. She needed to push herself, too, if she wanted to get back at me." With a small smile and a knowing shrug, Julianne gave Vikki the credit she deserves. "Unfortunately for me, she did." That's not to say that Julianne isn't enjoying herself. "The truth is, it's awesome. I think both of us are fighting on a whole new level." With a wicked smile and a glint of excitement in those blue eyes, the young actress practically purred. "When we go out there now, we rock that cage. And, we both know it."

So, this is how it's gonna be, now. No doubt, the next rematch isn't far off, and Vikki will be looking for a piece of Julianne. And, Julianne will be there, ready to give her the chance. Do they hate each other? I don't think so. But, they sure do seem eager to beat the shit out of each other. I don't really know if there's a word for that mix of emotions.

I signed off last time after asking the obvious question about Aly Michalka: After two stunning losses to young phenom India Reynolds, could Aly pull herself together mentally and challenge champion Brooklyn Decker? Aly's answer? You bet your ass, she could. And she did it by reaching down deep inside herself and pulling out an Aly I haven't seen in a long time.

In her last two fights with India, Aly has looked like a tightly wound spring. The bell would ring, and she would fly into action, attacking with reckless abandon. And, reckless abandon is exactly the game India likes to play. Both times, it looked like the women were racing to see who would land the crushing blow first. And, both times, Aly lost the race. When asked tonight what she thought the problem was in those earlier fights, Michalka had the answer ready. "I was afraid." Excusez-moi, mademoiselle? Alyson Michalka has never been afraid of an opponent a day in her life. So, what gives? "I was afraid of what she could do to my championship hopes," admitted the former Hellcats star. "I had worked so hard for so long to catch up to Brooklyn and Erin (Heatherton). I didn't want this rookie coming along and screwing things up. I just wanted to get past her and keep moving up." Well, despite the setbacks, Aly still got a shot at the champion. And, she did not let the opportunity pass her by.

Unlike her fights against India, Aly looked calm and collected as she entered the cage for this one. And, when the bell rang, she showed the kind of grit and determination that won her the belt at the most recent Lingerie Invitational tournament. She immediately had Brooklyn back on her heels, attacking with skill and accuracy instead of the wild flailing she'd employed more recently. But, Brooklyn has been there and seen it all. Weathering Michalka's early onslaught, Decker slowly started to impose herself and gain control. Then, just when it looked like the champion was ready to turn the tables, Aly unleashed the one weapon she's been leaving in the holster too often recently.

She started talking shit.

Did Aly's words strike fear into Brooklyn's heart? Yeah... probably not. If anything, it gave Decker a little extra drive to win, as if she needed it. But what it did for Brooklyn is beside the point. It's what it did for Aly that matters. It was proof that worried and scared Aly had left the building. In her place was nasty, angry Aly. Take no prisoners, Aly. And all I can say is: where have you been, baby?

Did Aly's smart mouth win her the fight? Nope. In fact, Brooklyn smacked her right in that mouth a few times before the round was over. But I knew. I knew as soon as I heard it that, win or lose, Aly was back. As the second round got underway, she attacked the champion, again. And, this time, she was too much, leaving Decker unconscious in the center of the cage.

Afterward, a much more relaxed Michalka answered questions for the media. One reporter pointed out that Aly had looked pretty dominant tonight. Should Brooklyn be afraid of losing her title soon? A huge smile spread across her face as she answered drolly, "Yeah, sure. I'm sure she's terrified." A follow-up question pressed Aly for a reason she had beaten Brooklyn so easily. "Simple. Brooklyn didn't know who she was up against. She was expecting a non-title bout against an opponent who looked like shit in her last two fights," the actress said plainly. "She had little to lose, and she didn't understand that I was literally fighting for my career. Trust me, she'll be more ready, next time."

When we signed Alyson Michalka, I saw a lot of myself in her. Maybe she wasn't the greatest fighter who ever lived, but she could get in there with the best of them. Whatever skill she may have lacked, she made up for with pure heart. She took chances and fought scrappy. She loved to fight, and she was never afraid to lose. And, she wanted to fight with the best. Which is exactly where she made her mistake. "I didn't give India enough credit for who she was. I knew she was good. I knew she had beaten a lot of good fighters. But, all I could think about was hanging with Brooklyn and Erin. They were the best, and that's who I wanted to fight. I saw India as a roadblock, not a challenge." And now? "Now, I know. She IS one of the best. But, her road to the championship still goes through me. She might take me down, but we're gonna have some fun finding out."

A few months ago, I stated that Aly would always have RSI behind her, as long as she refused to give up. But, I have to admit... after the losses to Reynolds, I was afraid she might be coming close to her breaking point. Something, or, more appropriately, SOMEONE, has pulled her back from the edge. And, trust me, that someone is far from a silent partner. "I saw last release," said a visibly angry Daniela Hantuchova. "I saw what Miss Richelle said."

Uh, oh. This can't end well for me.

Then, slapping her palm down on the table, the Slovakian let me have it. "She was wrong to question my friend Aly's heart. Aly would never give up. I will never let that happen."

First of all, I never questioned... oh, never mind. You're right, Daniela. And, I'm sorry. I know Aly's heart, and I'm starting to know yours. I should have known better than to think either of you would let me down.

So, I repeat the promise I made then. I had faith in you when we signed you, Aly. I had faith in you when times got tough. And, I have faith in you, now. Win or lose, your RSI family has your back. You know... in case Daniela needs a day off, or something.

When you look at Emma Watson, what do you see? Most people see a poised, sophisticated young actress who has weathered an almost unprecedented wave of fame and scrutiny with a nearly impossible grace. No one looks at her and sees a lethal fighter capable of reducing an opponent to a bloody heap in mere seconds. No one, that is, except for the women forced to face her inside the cage. With nearly 70 UMMA fights to her credit, Vanessa Hudgens has the kind of experience that would give most opponents nightmares. But, Emma entered the arena tonight with the same cool confidence with which she approaches everything. It seems impossible to rattle her. And, tonight, it looked impossible to beat her, too. She didn't just defeat Hudgens. She dismantled her. Scoring repeatedly with brutal combinations, Emma never gave her opponent the chance to do anything other than try to survive. With fists and feet whirling like a tornado, Emma never looked sloppy or uncontrolled. She was precise and powerful... exploiting every opening Hudgens gave her. And, after a non-stop, nearly two minute beating, Vanessa's body could take no more.

Is Emma Watson as invincible as she seemed tonight? No. We've seen her lose. We've even seen her lose badly. But, we have never seen her doubt herself. If she loses, she comes back stronger the next time out. If an opponent exploits a weakness, Emma makes that weakness a strength. No matter what happens, that same confidence is always there. Beloved actress, close friend, consummate teammate... all of that goes out the window when it's fight time. We've all seen what happens when it's time for Emma to get serious. Those soft, inviting brown eyes narrow and harden. Those delicate little hands ball up into fists of solid stone. And, that small smile... that wicked, devilish smirk that let's you know she's already imagining you lying beaten at her feet... slips onto her face.

In other words, the sound of cold steel that echoes through the arena when the cage door closes turns the sweet, lovable Emma Watson into a bad-ass bitch. Which only makes her that much more lovable, if you ask me.

Melissa was all over Lawrence from the second the bell rang in this one, and Jen just could not recover. As always, Jen made no excuses afterward. J-Law has been climbing the rankings with ease these past few months, and tonight's loss may be just the dose of reality she needs to make sure she doesn't get too confident. The question now is how quickly she can get focused and bounce back.

Another job well done by Jamie Lynn Spears, as she tore right through opponent Lauren Gottlieb. With two "blink and you miss it" victories in a row, Jamie was asked the secret to her sudden success. "I think being surrounded by successful people makes it easier," she said. "I spend at least an hour each day training with Danni Harris, and another hour with Amanda. Then, of course, there's Brit. So, I know what success looks like. And I know I want it."

Now, let's review the gameplan for Brittany Tacy's matchup against Hilary Duff. First and foremost, don't let her take you down. And, it took about 30 seconds for Brittany to screw that up. In her defense, Hilary was relentless, attempting multiple takedowns early until she got what she wanted. Brittany, however, did a masterful job of fending her off and tying her up, forcing referee Lauren Budd to stand the fighters up. Careful to avoid another takedown, Brittany picked Duff apart with precision strikes for the remainder of the round. The second round started off with more striking from Tacy, until Hilary was able to get herself set up for another takedown. Once again, though, the Floridian beauty hung tough, forcing Budd to step in. This time, Brittany could see her opponent was flagging, and she went in for the kill. Duff proved her toughness, though, taking a ton of abuse before finally folding with about a minute and a half left in the round.

I know I gave Brittany a hard time about the takedowns, but the truth is she was great tonight. Your opponent is rarely going to play along with your plan. If you want to win, you have to deal with what happens and improvise. Brittany stayed patient and handled herself brilliantly under adversity. And, when her opportunity came, she was ready. Facing Hilary was a big step up in class for Brittany. I'm very impressed with this win.

It was feast or famine for RSI's newest recruits tonight. At the head of the table was Sabine Jemeljanova, who pulverized Ana Ivanovic with a vicious onslaught that left the tennis star out cold in less than a minute. It was a fine victory over an experienced opponent, and it clearly showed Sabine's marked improvement since joining RSI. I'm not sure what goes on in the super heavyweight training sessions, but it's clear Steph and Torrie are having a lot of success with Sabine.

Dianna Dahlgren, meanwhile, had to work her ass off to get past fellow rookie Hansika Motwani. Motwani scored a fantastic takedown early in the first round, and Dahlgren was immediately on the defensive. Using a combination of brilliant technique and blatant cheating, Hansika quickly maneuvered herself to rear mount. It looked like Dianna was done for, but a superb reversal bought her some time. It didn't take long for Motwani to regain the upper hand, but Dianna hung on and survived the round. Determined to get control of things in the second round, Dianna beat back an early takedown attempt and attacked. Motwani countered early, but Dianna was just too much. After pummeling the Indian fighter's body, the motocross beauty went head-hunting, scoring the knockout less than a minute into the round. The win keeps Dianna's fledgling record perfect, and it highlights a fact I've been overlooking. The heavyweight rookies have been absolutely marvelous in the cage, often against more experienced competition. Holly, Marzia and Dianna are poised and ready every time out. So, I think it's time for a nod of respect in the direction of Catherine and Petra. This group has not been working together all that long, but they are enjoying a great deal of success.
