RSI Story #15Without Equal

-1-A Little Twist

October 30, 2015

Poor Olivia Holt was getting her head beat in.

No matter what we tried, there was no fix for it. She could study and learn from Jules or Kristen Bell or Jennifer Morrison. She seemed to idolize Ashley Tisdale, and Tiz was becoming something of a coach for the younger fighter. So, relationship-wise, everything was working great.

Effort-wise, the story was no different. She was a willing pupil and a quick study. Despite her irritation with me, it was clear she came to RSI every day willing to learn. When Steph was trying to show her something, she always seemed eager and attentive.

Of course, that last part might just be an act of self-preservation.

The problem revealed itself when it was time to put the things she learned to use. Liv is the first true rookie RSI has brought on in quite a while, and we didn't really consider the problems that might cause. Though she can train and learn from just about anyone, there is no one at her skill level for her to spar with.

Steph has tried every combination she can think of. The other bantamweights are simply too much for Holt to handle, and trying to have the more experienced fighters hold back wasn't working for anyone. Sparring with a fighter from a different weight class was an option, but nothing seemed to work out there, either. Training with the likes of Emma Watson and Tiffany Mulheron has pushed Shelby Chesnes to a level Olivia's not ready for, and the weight difference between she and Michelle Lewin is simply too significant for Liv to overcome. The best option we've found so far is Jenna-Louise Coleman, but that only works for the days RSI co-trains with Dragon's Den. The problem persists when it's just us, which is most of the time.

Today's experiment had Olivia in the cage with welterweight fighter Anna Kendrick, and it was not going well. Not only does Anna have quite a bit of experience on Liv, but she's already shown she's got the skills to handle herself against some of the UMMA's best. Her record is full of ups and downs, but she's beaten teammate Amanda Seyfried, as well as top DEF contender Kristanna Loken. And at the recent Hit the Beach tournament, Anna shocked the hell out of ESL champion Megan Fox. So, while she still has a lot of learning to do, it's clear the girl's got game.

Olivia, meanwhile, has nothing more than a couple of horrendous — and technically illegal — fights under her belt.

So, it really shouldn't have surprised anyone to see Anna smacking the young blonde around the cage with very little effort. Liv was trying... you could see the determination written on her face... but she just couldn't keep up.

I stood with Steph and Danni Harris at cage-side.

"You come up with that song, yet?" Danni asked.

"Nothing to come up with," I said. "I already know my answer."

"I know I'm going to be sorry I asked," Steph said, "but what are you two talking about?"

"Danni asked me this morning what my favorite Halloween song is," I said.

"And she refused to answer," Danni said. "Which means she doesn't have one."

"Bullshit. I have a perfectly good answer. I simply asked you to guess what it was. At which point you tersely announced that you don't like guessing games. Which led to me saying you could then kiss my ass."

"I don't recall being terse."

"Oh, you were definitely terse. Made it sound like such silliness was far beneath your royal stature."

"Well, now you're just making shit up," she said.

Steph's expression never changed. In fact, she never took her eyes from the action in the cage.

"I want to thank the both of you," she said, "for meeting my expectations perfectly."

"You're very welcome," Danni said.

"Reliability is our thing," I chimed in.

We watched together as Anna dodged an attack from the young bantamweight, then countered with a brilliant combination that knocked Olivia to the canvas. It was the third time she'd gone down in the last ten minutes, and, as with the previous occasions, she pushed herself up and climbed to her feet to come back for more.

If nothing else, Liv has proven the strength of her resolve and the depth of her resiliency.

"She's gonna get killed," Danni said.

Olivia retreated to the other side of the cage to confer with Ashley. You could see the pain, exhaustion and frustration all over her. The look on her face. The way her hands pressed into her sides, almost as though her arms were somehow holding her up. Hair flying wildly and soaked with sweat.

"There's no solution," Steph said. "She's too raw for anyone we've got."

Despite the situation, the conversation between Ashley and Olivia was very serious and animated. Olivia had little chance to make any headway against Anna to begin with. In her current state, she had none at all. But there she was, discussing strategy for the next volley, oblivious to the odds.

She had the skills of a rookie and the heart of a lion, and I could think of no better mentor for her than Tiz.

"What are you up to?" Danni asked, catching my look of admiration.

I turned and looked at her with a sly smile.

"Trying to read my mind again, Miss Harris?"

I knew what she was thinking. I have, from time to time, partaken in some extracurricular activities in the cage when I deemed it appropriate. The first time was against an angry and out of control Emma Watson. Though the long-term result turned out reasonably well, the immediate impact of that battle had been disastrous and nearly fatal. And some of the ill effects still lingered. Though the physical trauma to all involved has healed, it took a heavy emotional toll on Danni, who was left to fix what Emma and I had broken.

Since then, I've stepped into the cage to work with a number of RSI fighters, but never in a situation like that. These subsequent occasions have been about teaching and training and being able to share what I know. They are as safe as I can possibly make them, and they typically turn out pretty fun. In fact, I've even had a much more constructive session with Emma that worked out very well.

None of that mattered much to Danni. Whenever she found me in the training room after hours, she would be overcome by memories of that horrible day. I could wear a Victorian suit of armor in the cage, and she would still worry.

We have, more recently, devised a way to handle this issue. Danni doesn't find me in the cage anymore, because I make sure she's there from the start. Not only does this keep her stress level down, it also brings another knowledgeable resource to bear on whatever the problem of the moment happens to be.

Still, she likes it best when I just don't do it.

"I'll withhold my reply until I get the answer to my question," she said, a clear note of pre-disapproval in her voice.

Though I've given her plenty of past evidence to support her current theory, she was wrong on this one.

That doesn't mean I was going to pass up an opportunity to twist her tail feathers a little. Especially when she was setting it up so nicely.

"What if Olivia trains with me?" I asked, wandering away from the cage and toward the exit.

"I knew it," she said irritably, as she and Steph walked along with me.

Though she had kept a lid on the volume level, it was clear from her tone she was spoiling for a fight. Danni is one of the warmest people I know, but she has a limit. If you push her across the line, the reaction can be intense.

I have discovered that I am able to get to her like no one else. On top of that, I seem to permanently reside right on that line.

Steph casually slid to her left in order to gain a little distance from the upcoming fray. She didn't care much what we did, as long as she didn't end up getting sucked into it.

"What?" I asked innocently.

"You do know she hates you, right?"

"Hate is kind of a strong word."

"Very well. Then she dislikes you intensely."

"I am aware that young Ms. Holt is not fond of me, yes."

"Do you recall what happened last time you got in there with someone who was not fond of you?"

"I assume you mean Emma. In which case, I don't think you can realistically compare the two situations."

I was right and she knew it. Olivia was an aspiring young fighter, looking to find her footing. Emma was a destructive force, whose foot would smash your face before you even knew what happened.

Danni took a deep breath, then let it out slowly. Then, she took a different tack.

"Why is this always your first solution?"

"I don't think that's a fair statement."

"Really?" she asked incredulously. "Are you serious?"

I tried for a bewildered expression. I either got it right, or Danni was too worked up to notice I was faking.

"Don't look at me that way," she said. "You think climbing in that cage is the answer to everything."

"First of all," I said, "that's not true."

"The hell it's not..."

I pushed the crash bar on the big steel door and stepped out into the RSI lobby.

"I let you and Steph handle almost everything out here on the floor," I said.

"Right," she said, "and the minute there's a problem, you decide the solution is you."

We stopped outside my office, and I eyed her coolly for a moment.

"I'm sensing some hostility from you," I said.

"That's very perceptive," she said.

"You don't know what's going to happen," I said. "I might be the key to getting Olivia on the right track."

"You're too advanced for her," Danni responded. "What makes you think you can ease off any better than the rest of us?"

"I don't see how it can hurt to try," I said.

"Yeah, sure," she said. "Admit it. You see something in her, and you want the chance to test that something for yourself."

"That's ridiculous," I said. "She has a lot to learn before we'll have any clue how good she is."

"I agree," she snapped, "and she needs someone she can train with. Someone she can keep up with."

"You're right," I said.

"I... wait, what?"

I smiled as I put an end to my act.

"Danni, I have no intention of getting in the cage with Olivia," I said.

She took a moment to digest that, then said, "I don't believe you."

I considered what she said a moment ago about testing Olivia for myself.

"Well, I currently have no intention of getting in the cage with Olivia."

"Better," she said, "but I'm still not buying it."

"I know I'm not what she needs," I said. "She needs someone she can partner up with, full time. Someone she can compete with and learn with... both of them, together."

She regarded me a moment more, then nodded unconvincingly.

I turned to Steph, who managed to hang on the edge of the fray while simultaneously looking totally disinterested in everything that had transpired.

"Have Olivia come by my office at three o'clock," I said to her.

"Why?" Danni asked, still suspicious.

"I have some things to discuss with her. Meanwhile, you ladies have a meeting at two."

Danni looked at Steph, who shrugged. Then she came back to me.

"We have a meeting?"

"An interview, actually."

"Who are we interviewing?"

"With any luck," I began, as I took a step toward my office, "Liv's new training partner."
