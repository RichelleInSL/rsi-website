RSI Story #17 Trying Times

-43-Gutting It Out

With a sudden blast of a song I didn't know, Larter entered the arena first. I'd call her approach friendly, but business-like. Emily's workmanlike journey and ultimate triumph had built her quite a fan base, but Ali was no slouch. She could lay claim to a long and very successful career, and she'd earned a sizable following of her own. So she showed them some appreciation, but mostly kept her focus forward. Her fans would have plenty to cheer about if she took care of business in the cage.

Then it was Emily's turn, and it all started out as expected. One of the fringe benefits of 'legend-killing' is that you get your own dedicated walkout music, and Emily's has been the same for years. You Can't Take Me by fellow Canadian, Bryan Adams. I've always thought it an excellent choice. The title might be a little too on-the-nose, but a little effort reveals something just a bit deeper. Just like with Em.

The music started and Emily immediately stepped out of the tunnel. The spotlight followed her in, with Steph, Roselyn and Marcy right behind her. Like Larter, she stopped for nothing. A wave or two along the way to acknowledge the crowd, but otherwise it was straight to the cage. Work now. Play later.

The first surprise of the night was so subtle, I almost missed it. There is always a little noise along the edges as a fighter walks in. Fans trying to get a little closer as security politely discourages them. Emily's walk was no different. There were no incidents. Just a bit of frenzied excitement.

Enough that I didn't immediately notice Daya tagging along just a few steps behind the rest of the group.

She stopped at cageside as the other four climbed the steps. Just as she was no doubt instructed to do. This was crunch time, and everyone needed to be focused on the goal. No distractions or superfluous personnel were permitted in the cage. By order of Stephanie McMahon.

Something told me this was not an issue. That Z was fulfilling her role just by being exactly where she was.

You could feel the excitement building as the announcer ran through his duties. People were expecting a lot out of this one, and I had the feeling they were going to get it because the similarities between Emily and Ali went well beyond their entrance habits. This wasn't going to be a clash of fighting styles. It was going to be a force-on-force brawl between two women known for their offensive firepower. The contrast between them was subtle. Larter leaned toward being a boxer, while Emily liked to mix in her Muay Thai skills. Past experience had shown these differences meant little. Having split their four previous meetings, each fighter had proven she could handle what the other dished out.

The delivery of final instructions from referee Lena Gercke was unremarkable, and it was showtime before I knew it.

Larter came out fast and furious. Trying to seize control before Em had the chance to settle in. And when she connected with a looping right on the second exchange... well, I thought for a moment this might be a very short night for Emily.

VanCamp wobbled right and Larter surged after her to keep the pressure on. Suddenly Emily stopped, planted and fired off her own right hand. Ali moved right to get out of the way, but it cost her all of her momentum.

A mistake by an overeager Larter, or a savvy move by Em? A little of both, actually. Ali's been doing this a long time. She knows it's hard to judge how bad an opponent is hurt. All she knew was she'd landed a solid hit, and Emily was reeling. That's not an opportunity you get often, so she took her chances and pushed forward. No doubt, Emily would have done the same. Which is why she knew Ali would be coming and moved quickly and decisively to deal with it.

The fighters reset, with Larter charging right back in behind a flurry of punches. That quickly became the theme. Despite continuously running into Emily's jab, Ali was determined to press the action. She was willing to take a little punishment in order to dish out more in return... and it was working. Though Emily defended well, Ali's persistence seemed to pay dividends on each exchange. Emily made sure to get her licks in - punishing her opponent's legs with shin kicks every time she attacked - but there was no denying that Larter was getting through and landing the heavier shots.

I was quite certain those shin kicks where the reason Ali had chosen this path. The kicks didn't look like much. I mean, Larter would land a solid left hand to the body, and Emily's only answer was to kick her in the leg? I think you can score that one for Ali. But the kicks added up as the fight progressed. Ali's legs would get heavy. They might even start to fail her. All the while, Emily would just keep pounding away.

It was a brutal way to lose a fight. A fact I learned from Emily, and I still have the bruises to show for it.

So it was no surprise that Larter was trying to take it to the RSI fighter. Because a damn good way to keep the damage from adding up on you is to be the one dishing it out instead. Or, even better... just knock your opponent clean out.

Still, this was something of a dangerous game Ali was playing. Her approach was using up a lot of energy, while Emily was mostly conserving. No, this wasn't a variation on what happened to Jen Morrison. Ali has tons of experience, and she knows how to pace herself through a round. This was a deliberate choice. A belief that she could do enough damage early in the round to slow Emily down later.

The gamble didn't pay off, as the tide started to turn around the two-minute mark. Larter was tiring. She hadn't run herself to empty, but she couldn't keep the pressure up and she was having less success getting past Emily's defenses. Soon, Emily started to open up a little, landing solid counters as she began to win a few exchanges of her own. Banging punches and kicks into Larter's body, while also continuing to catch her with those shin kicks as she tried to retreat. The more things tilted toward Em, the less willing Ali was to come forward. By the time the third minute ticked away, Emily was the one driving the battle. Her pace was more methodical, but she was also landing at a much higher percentage than Larter had. By the time the round drew to a close, I was really liking what I was seeing.

Officially speaking, I scored the round even. Each fighter had owned about half the round. I felt Emily had been more effective, but it wasn't safe to assume the judges agreed.

On the unofficial side of things, I had the fight going heavily in Emily's favor. Not because of what had already happened, but because of what I suspected was going to happen.

I was pretty sure Larter's failed gamble was going to snowball on her. Coming out strong hadn't slowed Emily much, and it hadn't won Larter the round. But it had cost her a lot of stamina. She hadn't exhausted herself, but these round breaks weren't like charging up your phone. Neither fighter would be restored to full battery. Ali had gone into the break with less in the tank than Emily. She was going to come out of it the same way. More importantly, she was now sort of locked into the strategy. Though her defense was more than competent, it's her offense she's known for. She didn't have the skills to hang back and play it conservative against a 'legend killer.' She was going to have to come out fast and go on the attack again... only this time, Emily knew it. And if Larter blitzed and failed again, the stamina difference between she and Emily would become nearly insurmountable.

In other words, if she didn't succeed early in this round, then she probably wasn't going to succeed at all. And it didn't take long for it to become apparent that she wasn't going to succeed early.

The cycle from the first round repeated itself, almost to the letter. Larter came out hot, and she had a little early success. But she started to flag sooner this time, and it seemed like the round had only barely begun when Em started winning the battles. Not long after, the tables were turned and she was taking the fight to Ali. By the time the two-minute mark rolled around, Emily was in command and cruising. Driving forward and keeping Larter on the defensive. Using those long, powerful legs of hers to beat the last ounces of fight out of her opponent.

A particularly brutal combination sent Ali reeling into the fence, and everyone knew the end was near. Em chased her down and went to work. Gercke was right there, ready to jump in.

Realizing the trouble she was in, Larter took her last shot. A wild, looping right hand thrown without technique or design.

Why do fighters throw these desperate punches when they almost never land?

Because once in a blue moon, they do.

Larter deserved zero credit for what happened, and she'd be the first to admit it. With the way Emily was picking her apart, I don't even think she could see well enough to know what she was aiming for. But she fired it off just as Emily was changing levels after burying a combination in Ali's gut. So, in essence, Em unwittingly put her head into the path of Larter's punch. Worse yet, some sense that something was coming caused her to glance that way... and turn full-faced into the blow.

The impact was incredible. The force drove Emily straight back, and her knees actually buckled. I don't how she didn't fall, but I do know she was lucky Gercke had been so focused on Ali. Because if she'd seen the crazy, drunken stumbling that carried the RSI fighter across the cage, she might have waved the fight off right then and there.

Em hit the fence back-first. She got her hands into the mesh, and it was quickly clear that was the only thing holding her up. Her eyes looked like empty windows on some gaudy mansion. Huge and polished to a gleam, but otherwise vacant. By chance, she'd ended up right in front of her own team, and my gaze traveled to Steph. I saw something there I almost never see. Worry. Enough that I knew she was flirting with the idea of stopping it herself. Because she could see what Gercke had missed. There was nobody home inside Emily's head... and a reenergized Ali was full-on sprinting across the cage to capitalize.

Then something changed. Em's guard came up - high and tight. Not slowly and methodically, but instantly. Like something had scared the living shit out of her and she'd instinctively curled up to protect herself.

Was that it? Instinct? I couldn't tell. I only knew a fighter I thought was done had suddenly and totally taken her head out of the equation. Ali was going to need a crowbar to get in there.

So, Ali did what she was trained to do. She went to the body. Hammering punches into wide-open holes. She beat Em like a heavy bag for nearly thirty seconds, while VanCamp offered precious little in response. Though there was life to her, she was still trying to pull it together. Trying to find her legs... and maybe a few of the marbles Larter's punch had scattered around. Meanwhile, Larter just kept punching. Pain registered all over Emily's face. Ali wasn't just scoring points. She was hurting her.

Two minutes to go, and I wanted Steph to stop it.

Then Emily surprised me again. With a sudden lurch, she straightened up and fired off her jab, with the right hand following immediately. Neither punch was particularly crisp, but there was no way Larter could have seen the flurry coming. Emily had been dead meat only a second before. The strikes did no damage, but they forced a very surprised Ali to back off.

No matter. She waded right back in, looking to put her badly wounded opponent out of her misery. Emily had expected it. She leaned back into the fence and lashed out with her right leg... pushing it straight out at her oncoming opponent.

Larter was forced to change direction - sliding right as the kick caught her in the left thigh.

A strange look registered on her face as she circled away and reset. Some surprise, mixed with a heavy dose of what looked like respect. Emily was badly hurt, but she was still digging. Still surviving. Showing the kind of heart that real fighters always recognize and admire.

But Emily didn't want to be revered right then and there. She just wanted to get the hell off the fence.

She got herself up again, then circled away. Putting as much space as she could between her and her opponent. Blood from her face had run down onto her chest and stained her top. Though she was moving and obviously had her head back in the cage, you could still see she wasn't right. Her movements were slow and a bit unsteady. Like she had to will her legs to go where she wanted them instead of just walking. Between that and the blood, she reminded me of a zombie.

Still a minute and a half to go.

Larter owned the rest of the round, winning every exchange by a mile. Emily kept her guard high and tried to stay away. Ali was forced to chase, but willingly went to the body when she got her chances.

This wasn't good enough to change my mind. If I didn't see something better from Emily soon, I wanted this stopped. I only hoped Steph was down there thinking the same.

Then Emily took the decision out of our hands.

With about thirty seconds to go, Larter closed for another round of body pounding. Emily snapped her jab - quicker and sharper than any she'd thrown since Ali had walloped her. It caught Larter clean and brought her guard up. Too late. Em snuck a right hand through her opponent's forearms and hit pay dirt again. Larter was forced to retreat, and Em caught her with a shin kick on the out.

It wasn't much, but it was the 'better' I'd been looking for. A sign that she was finally shaking off the last effects of that punch.

Larter reset, and there was another little nod of respect that Emily didn't see because she was too busy trying to stay alive.

Then Ali waded back in and got the last laugh.

Having proven her point, Em has happy to fight the last exchange off her back foot. Defend, counter and retreat. No need to try and do damage. Just eat some clock without taking any more of her own. But Ali kept coming, bullying Em to the fence with a fast and furious flurry of punches. Then she kept right on coming, unleashing a barrage into Emily's body. The clacker sounded the ten second warning. There was no place for Emily to go, and countering meant exposing her head. Not a worthwhile risk with so little time left. So, the best option was the one that sucked least. Stand there and take her beating like a big girl.

It was a very long ten seconds.

The bell sounded. Though she'd survived, there was no real silver lining for Emily or her supporters. Larter had taken the round for sure, and beaten her up good in the process. But the worst part for me was Larter's reaction. No, she didn't gloat or get in Emily's face. Though she might not be my favorite person right this second, the truth is that Ali's a pro. She was damn good at what she did and more than one of my fighters had suffered at her hands. But she was no villain, and she certainly wasn't one for theatrics. That said, she did give the tiniest little fist pump as she turned to head back to her corner.

I knew what it meant because I'd been there myself a few times.

Just a few minutes ago, she was well on her way to losing this thing. Running on fumes with her hopes dwindling. Now, she was not only back in it, but she had her opponent on the ropes.

She knew she'd gotten away with one, and now it was my fighter who was left searching for answers.

Up in the box, I was struggling to conceal my emotions. Emotions that could be summed up in one word.

Fuck.

She HAD it. It was in the bag. But I've said it a million times - the only 'safe' opponent is a defeated opponent. Until then, nothing is certain. Ali Larter is a seasoned and dangerous fighter, and Em had done an excellent job of dealing with her for a round and a half. It looked like she had it won. Then a totally lucky break had turned everything on its head, and now you could feel the way this one was slipping away.

But those were my feelings. Down in the cage, nobody in the RSI corner was ready to push the panic button.

Em didn't look crushed or dejected. Most importantly, she looked like she had her head fully back in the fight. Beaten up for sure, but not ready to give in. Around her, it was business as usual. Roz took her spot as Marcy got to work on the injuries, cleaning up a little blood from a small cut beneath Em's left eye. Steph squared up in front of her fighter and got to work on the gameplan. There was no sign that anything was amiss. No emphatic hand gestures or stern looks. It wasn't necessary, and they all knew it. This wasn't one of the rookies. This was Emily VanCamp. She didn't need motivation or inspiration. She needed info, and that's what makes Steph such a key part in the RSI machinery. She knew what she was dealing with in Emily, and - more importantly - Emily knew what she'd get in return. Steph would give it to her straight, and tell her everything she needed to know. Em wasn't looking for miracle, but she knew listening to Steph would give her the best shot she was going to get.

I'm freely admit I had no clue what RSI's chief strategist would say. Larter had really dropped the hammer in the second half of the round, and things looked really bleak from where I sat.

But, Steph... well, it's like I said. She knows Emily. Inside and out. Most importantly, she knew it wasn't necessary to make some grand comeback. Everyday Emily had the confidence and the patience to grind her way back into this thing. Slow and steady could still win this race.

How to start? Well, when victory is so close your opponent can taste it, the first thing you need to do is snatch it away from her.

That meant getting back to basics... with a technique so simple, even a rookie could do it.

Emily rose as the teams made their way out. She still looked plenty the worse for wear, but Marcy had once again worked her magic to get the bleeding under control.

Meanwhile, winning is just as good for the soul as it is for the body, and Larter came out looking pretty refreshed for the third round. She came straight at Em, looking to get a few more rounds of pounding in before putting her away. She shot the jab, then surged in behind it.

THUMP!

Emily stopped her cold with the teep.

Larter backed up, unhurt but visibly annoyed. Then she put her gloves up, and tried again. A little more methodical this time. First the jab, then the cross. Just to get Emily backing up. Then she saw her chance.

THUMP!

Emily slammed the door shut again with the teep.

Larter got even more annoyed. She had already made the improbable comeback, and now she wanted to ice this cake. She had no patience for this nonsense.

She tried again. Using a little lateral movement to create an opening.

THUMP!

Emily pushed her away with the kick again.

Two more attempts ended the same way. It was enough to make fans at home shout at their TVs.

Hey Larter! I don't think that shit's going to work!

On paper, Ali and Emily were the same height. But RSI's kickboxing queen was so good with her feet, she might as well have been a full foot taller. And Larter being more of a puncher only increased that advantage.

But, wait... you say. That's a common misconception. Despite how it looks, arm length and leg length are just about the same for most people.

That's absolutely true, but this wasn't a question of limb length. This was purely about what each was trying to do.

The act of throwing a punch has a natural forward motion to it. The fighter leans in as she throws, bringing her upper body closer to her opponent.

The mechanics of the teep have the opposite effect. Emily actually leans away as she throws the kick, taking her torso further out of range.

The two movements should sort of cancel each other out. Larter leans forward as Emily leans back and the distance between them remains roughly the same. That's where Emily's skills make the difference. She reacts so quickly that the kick is up and out before Larter can finish. By the time Ali gets the punch off, Emily has already kicked her away.

Larter continued to look for ways to get inside. She succeeded a few times, only to be chased back out by a flurry of punches from Emily... usually concluded with yet another shin kick for good measure.

This repeated process had two benefits. First, it was wearing Ali down, little by little. Second, it was giving Emily more time to recover.

Eventually, Larter got the message that she needed a new approach. Unlike Jillian Grace, she had quite a few in her arsenal.

On her next approach, she came straight in. Emily fired the teep. Ali circled her right arm down and out, scooping Emily's foot aside.

The block actually turned Emily and left a pretty big opening in her defense. Not her front side, which was now turned away from her opponent. Not really in her flank, where her arm and elbow still had things pretty well covered. But, to put it bluntly, her ass was exposed.

Larter was fast. She knew where the hole would be, and she quickly pivoted into a strong low kick to take advantage.

Kicks like this were a fairly new wrinkle to Ali's game. She'd added them in mostly because Emily had forced her to. After all, if your opponent is going to come after you with all four limbs, you'd better be prepared to do the same.

As with everything Larter does, she'd gotten really good at it. But not as good as Emily. Em can not only break down your defense, but she can actually read it. Using how you defend to predict how you're going to attack. So, when Ali's block turned her, Emily also knew where the hole would be. By the time Ali got the kick out, Em had her left arm down and absorbed the blow on her forearm.

VanCamp circled away and reset. Larter pressed forward. The sequence repeated. Larter tried to move faster, and it looked like she did. But not fast enough. Emily blocked the kick easily.

Meanwhile, something much more important had happened. Larter's advantage had disappeared. Her momentum was gone, and it was clear that Em was back on point. No doubt her insides still felt like jelly from the beating she'd taken, but that wasn't slowing her down much.

Larter tried the scoop and kick again. No luck... and maybe something even worse. I caught a look on Emily's face. Like she was thinking. Measuring for something.

Something tells me Ali saw it too because she changed the routine on the next exchange. After some feinting and lateral dancing, she came forward to draw the teep. This time she scooped it aside but held the foot for just a second. Long enough to give a little tug and pull Emily forward.

Not a great development. Larter was pulling her into range. For what, I didn't know.

But Emily did.

There is a natural dropping of the guard when the teep is thrown because you need your arms to counter-balance the shift in your weight. The movement is slight and easy to recover, but there is a definite hole there in your defense. A nice window for a short uppercut thrown with the scooping hand if your opponent can reach you. And reaching you becomes a lot easier if she pulls you into her.

Ali recovered her hands and moved to throw the upper. Too late. Em had read the move and shot her left hand out. It was a sloppy punch, thrown off-balance with no hope of doing real damage. But that wasn't the point. This was all about simply putting a fist in Larter's face. Blocking her vision and spoiling what she had planned.

It worked perfectly. The uppercut came out half-hearted and wild. Ali tried to hold her ground as she gathered herself. Em was still out of position, and Larter was no doubt hoping to get into a little fist fight. It was a bad move. Em got her feet set and came across with a long, straight right hand. Larter was forced to retreat... but not before Emily got her again with the shin kick.

After that, Ali settled back a little. The two circled and feinted with no real commitment. Ali hunting for a way through. Em watching her closely... her face the picture of intensity.

When no new answers came to her, Larter resorted back to lateral movement to get inside. She scored some points, but Emily always made her pay. Ali slowed some more. She put less work into the feints, and started walking into the teep like she didn't care. Like she just couldn't take it any more. She just wanted to put a glove on Emily, and she didn't care what she had to take to do it.

A combination of frustration showing through and those shin kicks adding up. By now her legs had to be feeling like tree trunks.

Em was emboldened. Nothing crazy. After the way round two had gone down the tubes, she was happy to stay cautious and let this one coast out. But her counters got stronger. She started throwing that extra punch or kick on the end. Adding to the punishment when the opportunity was there.

Suddenly both corners were shouting. Time was running out on the round. Larter decided to give it one last try and went back to the scoop and kick. It was a mistake. Changing approaches may have delayed whatever Emily was planning earlier, but she had stored those measurements she took for later use.

Ali surged. Em teeped. Scoop, kick, block.

Then came the bonus.

As Larter started to retreat, Em lashed out with her left leg. The move had a few different names, but what it was a teep thrown sideways. With very little setup, Em shifted her weight to her right foot, then lifted her left and punched it into Ali's midsection.

Then Larter made it worse.

Though I do it myself, I really don't understand the reaction most people have to getting jabbed with the teep. The smart thing to do would be to go with it. To let the impact carry you away, then use the space to pull yourself together. But most fighters feel the undeniable urge to fight it. To get their feet under them and immediately get back into the fray. The harder and more surprising the teep, the more determined the reaction.

Ali did exactly that. Straightened right up and walked forward in a passive attempt to send a message.

See? You didn't hurt me.

Then she looked up and realized Em had immediately turned into a spinning backfist. By then it was way too late. Em's fist ripped across her face and Larter went flying... tripping and stumbling as she tried to right the ship.

I'll bet that hurt plenty.

The bell sounded to end the round, but Em's message had been delivered loud and clear. She was in this fight, and she was still just as much of a threat as Ali was.

Even more so if you ask me.

Even a pessimist like myself had to give this round to Emily. She hadn't done a ton of damage, but she had commanded the action nearly from bell to bell. By my count, that put the fight back to even. But there was more to it. Larter had already had her thunderous comeback, and Emily had overcome it. As far as I was concerned, the better fighter was back in the driver's seat, and she knew how to seal the deal.

Of course, I had been wrong about both of my post-round predictions so far, so pay no attention to me.

In the corner, there was nothing to see but practiced fluidity. With no new damage, Marcy focused on improving the repairs to her fighter's nose. Roz got to work on the gross stuff. Steph settled in front and divulged all she had to offer. Evolving the game plan for the round ahead.

Then the bell rang... and what happened next was nothing like what I'd expected.

Larter came out hard again, in a repeat of her first round strategy.

This time, Emily surged out to meet her... which was the one thing Ali hadn't counted on.

Larter readied the jab, but Em was way ahead. One big step and she uncorked the right hand - straight down Broadway.

BOOM!

Em's uppercut got there about two days before Larter's jab, and it was immediately clear the older fighter was no longer all there.

Emily poured it on.

Left, right, hook.

A shaken Larter did nothing but hold her hands up in front of her and get knocked around as Em's punches wormed their way through her guard.

Emily drew her hands back, got her balance, then lashed out with her right foot - pounding a kick into Ali's unprotected side.

Larter grunted and swayed with the blow, her guard instinctively coming down to protect the new injury.

Emily regained her stance, cocked her hips... and Daya put her fist in the air for no apparent reason.

Then Em unwound and the right foot came again. Higher this time.

CRACK!

The high kick landed with the force of a freight train, and Larter went down in a heap right in the middle of the cage. Gercke jumped in instantly to stop Em from doing more damage, but there was no need. Even the rookie knew this one was over. Knew it nearly a full second beforehand, in fact.

She might not be able to execute Emily's finisher with the same fluidity as her mentor, but Daya clearly had the 'book smarts' to know when it was coming.

The crowd reaction started with a brief period of stunned silence. They'd been full of anticipation for part four of this war, and now they couldn't comprehend that it was already over. Larter had gone from big trouble to on the way out to totally and completely gone in the span of thirteen seconds. There was nothing more to see.

The place went nuts when reality dawned, but I didn't cheer along with them. I was still busy shaking my head at the pure savagery I'd just witnessed.

Never take your eyes off the action when Emily is in the cage. Don't daydream. Don't take a sip of water. Don't even fucking blink, or you might miss everything.

The cage door flew open and the medics raced to the fallen fighter. Ali's team followed, and then came Daya... who had somehow slipped in front of the rest of the RSI group.

She flew toward Em like a heat-seeking missile, stopping only when she noticed the newly-crowned champion frantically waving her off.

Z's expression went puzzled, then a little sad. Emily didn't want her there?

I was pretty sure that wasn't the case. More likely, Em was still feeling the effects of the second round pounding, and wasn't up to being assaulted by her young pupil at that precise moment.

My theory gained credence when Emily gingerly walked over and threw an arm around Z's neck. Letting the rookie help hold her up for a while.

By then Steph and Roselyn had arrived. Em said something to Steph that drew a subtle nod. My guess was that it was something along the lines of, Thanks for the tip, because there was not a doubt in my mind that Steph was the one who noticed Larter was getting lazy with her defense each time she came blitzing out at the bell. I could almost hear her saying, If she does it again, knock her into the middle of next week.

Steph is many things, but subtle is not one of them.

Before too long, a groggy Larter was back on her feet. A pair of medical techs split off and wandered over to Emily. They checked her over briefly, then gave a nod to Steph along with the standard advice that her fighter should be examined more thoroughly as soon as possible. Not a problem. I was sure Trish was already waiting for her.

Another moment or two passed before the announcer grabbed the mic for the official announcement. I didn't hear a word he said. I smiled and clapped as I watched the ref raise Emily's hand, then smiled and clapped some more as she was awarded the championship belt. But all the while, my mind was looking ahead.

Two big fights still to come... with Danni's rematch with Cole drawing ever closer.
