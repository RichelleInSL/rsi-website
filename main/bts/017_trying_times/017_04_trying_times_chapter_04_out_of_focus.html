-4-Out Of Focus

Oceanport, New Jersey - Twelve days before the title fight in Melbourne

Steph McMahon stood beside the practice cage wearing the type of expression I might make if someone suggested I try deep fried rat for lunch. In other words, she looked totally disgusted and a little bit nauseous.

It was late afternoon, and I stood out on the floor with Danni observing from about fifteen feet away. All around us swirled the chaos that made RSI what it was. Emma and Tiffany working together on the heavy bags. Britney and Elisha dancing their way through a sparring session - tormenting each other with both physical blows and sharp verbal jabs. But we weren't seeing any of that. We were focused squarely on Steph and wondering just how long it was going to take for her to snap. I had my money on less than five minutes.

On the other side of the mesh, Dove Cameron and Olivia Holt circled near the center of the octagon. Both were set in their stance, bouncing rhythmically as they searched for openings in each others' defense. Liv tried to apply a little pressure with her jab. Despite the distance between us, I saw her mistake as clear as day. As she snapped out her left, her right hand came down, leaving her chin exposed. I knew it happened because she was already thinking about throwing the cross. I also knew it was pretty much an unforgivable sin. The response to such a mistake is nearly automatic and, sure enough, Dove immediately drove her right glove through the huge defensive hole. The impact snapped Liv's head straight back, but she quickly righted herself and did the only thing she could... using the space behind her to retreat out of harm's way. Dove chased after her, but Liv got her feet under her and circled out of danger.

"Keep your guard up," Steph said sternly.

Her volume level rose with each word. Like she had started out trying to be patient, but maintaining that patience through all four words proved impossible. Because this was the thousandth time she'd had to say the same damn thing to the same damn person.

I mentally adjusted my estimate. Couldn't be more than a minute, now.

The two fighters came back together near the center. Liv pressured again, pushing out the jab, then following with the cross. Dove kept her hands up and caught both punches on her gloves. Liv kept the combo going, but her right dropped again as she pivoted into the left hook. Again, it was a common mistake for a new fighter. She had her mind on the payoff punch and had simply forgotten about defense. Dove saw the mistake and launched her own left into the void where Liv's guard should have been. Her hook was shorter and tighter than Liv's, and she easily beat young Ms. Holt to the punch.

Steph growled as she reached up with her right hand and entangled her fingers in the mesh - squeezing like she was trying to crush the metal fencing into powder. Even from where I stood, I could see her hand turning pink from the force she was exerting.

"Up, Liv! UP!" she gritted.

Thirty seconds. Tops.

Olivia quickly shook off the blow and got her stance back together. As they circled again, I found myself thinking that Liv needed a lot of work, but Dove was looking pretty good in there. Then Cameron opened up with a little combo of her own, and I winced as I saw her lean into the cross - trying to put some extra juice into it. The effort caused her to rise up a little in her stance and completely ruined her balance. Liv slipped the punch and Dove's body followed her hand, with Liv clipping her on the ear as she stumbled forward.

And then the timer ran out.

"STOP!" Steph yelled. "Jesus, just... STOP!"

The fighters dropped out of their stances and turned to look at her Steph, who let out a long sigh as she turned her face down to the floor and ground the knuckles of her right hand into her skull. She stayed that way for a long time, but both fighters wisely kept their mouths shut. Then, once she was satisfied that she had regained a reasonable amount of self-control, RSI's Fighter Development Coordinator set out for the cage door.

"Do you two hear me when I talk to you, or are you too busy thinking about what shade of lipstick would go perfectly with that new dress you just bought?" she asked as she walked.

Despite her attempt to compose herself, her words had an edge to them that caused the two rookies to look at each other in a shared expression of concern. Like they wished the cage had a back door so they could make a run for it.

"Call me crazy," I said to Danni, "but I don't think this is going very well."

"Steph's losing it with these two," she replied. "She's gonna lay one of them out if something doesn't change."

I watched McMahon yank the door open and step through, letting it slam shut behind her. The way she was marching toward the blonde duo, I worried she might drop both of them right this second.

"Thoughts?" I asked.

"I think we took things too far the other way," Danni said.

I nodded. When we first signed Olivia, we quickly realized we had a problem. Liv was the first rookie we'd brought on in a long time, and it turned out we didn't have anyone appropriate for her to train with. Our early approach was to try her against a rotating schedule of sparring partners to see what the best fit would be, and the result was that there wasn't one. The other women on the roster were either too experienced or well outside of her weight class. Liv tried like hell to keep up, but it was a lost cause. The only thing she was learning was how to scrape herself off the canvas after every exchange.

We needed a fast, solid solution, and that's when Dove Cameron appeared on our radar. Same experience level as Liv. Same weight class. And to top it all off, the two of them were already close friends away from the cage. She was like an answer specially designed for us by divine intervention.

Except, it seems the design had a little flaw in it. Dove and Olivia may have been perfectly suited skill-wise, but that's because neither of them had any actual skills. While it's true that you can't learn much if you're constantly being knocked on your ass, it's also true that you can't learn from someone who has nothing to teach you. Danni was saying that, in our quest for a solution to the former, we had taken things too far and ended up with the latter.

"You have a plan?" I asked.

"I want to try rotating some of the others in with these two," she answered.

"Didn't we already try that?"

"I'm thinking of something different, this time."

"Such as?"

"More focus on technique, rather than just sparring."

I considered that for a moment or two.

"I don't follow," I said.

"We have a lot of talent on the roster," she said. "I want to see who's best at sharing it. Let the two of them continue to spar with each other, but have one of the veterans coach them through it."

I nodded as I started to get the idea.

"Like a series of mini-Stephs."

She arched an eyebrow at that thought.

"Not the words I would have chosen, but yeah, I guess that's about right. These two need to hear from someone other than Steph. And Steph needs a break."

"Before she breaks one of them," I said.

Back in the cage, Steph was trying to give instruction - arms flying wildly as her frustration showed through. I found myself wondering if she had a little Italian in her to go along with that Irish surname of hers.

Danni shook her head as she watched.

"That, or she's gonna have an aneurysm," she said. "Either way, somebody's gonna die."

We continued to watch while I let the plan turn over in my mind.

"You planning to pull Z into it, too?" I asked.

Zendaya Coleman joined the Rampage family a few months after Olivia and Dove. Though positive results were proving just as elusive for her, she was having an easier time training with her more advanced counterparts. That said, results where really the only thing that mattered. If we were going to pull top-level fighters in to help Dove and Olivia, it made sense to have Zendaya join in, too.

"I can't see why not," Danni said. "She could definitely benefit."

I nodded.

"You thinking about matching them with someone new each day?" I asked.

"Not sure yet."

I stopped and thought some more. I had a clear image of how the process would go and I mostly liked the idea. But something was nagging at me, and Danni picked up on it.

"You see a problem?" she asked.

"Two competing problems actually. If you try someone new each day, I don't know how much the rookies are going to learn. They'll need more repetition for the techniques to stick. But..."

"If we pull the veterans for multiple days at a time, it will mess with their own training," she said, finishing my thought.

I nodded, and now we were both quiet as we considered this new roadblock. It seemed to me this was something Danni would have thought about already, and I was sure she had both an idea on how to handle it and a supporting argument to go with it.

That's what I was waiting for, but it's not what happened.

"Maybe it's not the right approach," she said.

I turned and looked at her.

"I didn't say that."

She shrugged, but didn't look back my way.

"I'm not sure how to work it out," she said.

This was becoming a trend, recently. If I so much as hinted that I disagreed with her on something, she would just fold rather than argue with me. It felt a lot like the way we were when RSI had first started. Distant and uncomfortable. Like we just couldn't get on the same page.

"I'm sure we can come up with something," I said.

"Maybe."

Maybe? We'd been thinking about it for about thirty seconds. It's not like we'd burned through an exhaustive list of possible solutions.

That's exactly what I should have said to her, too. It's what I would have said, except... she's had me worried, lately. Something was wrong, and I had a strong theory as to what it was. Unfortunately, I really didn't know how to bring up. So, we just stood in silence until Danni eventually shifted us to the next awkward topic.

"You free tonight?" she asked.

From business to pleasure... only things haven't been all that pleasing. Not that they've been unpleasant. They've just sorta... been. We've tried to ease our way from friends to couple, but months have gone by and it doesn't feel like we're getting anywhere. We go out together, but we can't seem to bring ourselves to use the word date. Even if we did, I'm not sure it would matter. It's still just Danni and I spending time together. Not a bad thing. It just doesn't feel... different. Not like what it's supposed to be now.

Lately, we've been spending more time staying in than going out. She was the one to make the switch, and I think she was hoping a more intimate approach might push things along. It doesn't feel like it's working, but that doesn't mean it hasn't been fun.

"Sure," I said.

"Want to come over to my place after work?"

"You cooking?" I asked.

"No."

"Am I cooking?"

"Guess that's up to you," she said with a wry smile.

Well, at least some things hadn't changed.

"I suppose I can come up with something," I said.

"You can bring Tramp, if you want. I think Winnie misses him, a little."

I smiled and shook my head.

"Honestly. You have to stop calling him that in front of me."

"That's what I call him," she said. "It's not my fault you constantly have Disney on the brain."

"It's not anyone's fault. Fault implies failure or wrongdoing, neither of which applies."

"Whatever."

I smiled, but decided to let it go. Truth is, I recognize I may have a problem, so it's hard to defend myself.

"I can grab Tramp, but it's going to cost me a lot of time."

Danni lives only a few minutes from the RSI facility, while I live about a half-hour away. A commute of that length is not much of a sacrifice, but doubling it would be sort of a nuisance.

"So, stay over, then," she said.

Normally, a heavy statement, but we were something of a special case. The fact that I had already lived with her for a time took the sexual connotation out of it and turned it into something closer to a high school slumber party invitation. I admit, it was weird. But, while our relationship wasn't going very smoothly - and we were nowhere near ready to cross the physical intimacy barrier - nothing had changed the fact that being with her made me happy. So, I accepted the weirdness and left it at that.

"Sounds like a plan," I said.

She smiled, and the sight of that smile pushed aside the awkwardness that had come before. Maybe I was overreacting. Seeing problems that weren't there. So, she had backed down a little on the training idea. We'd talk again, and I had no doubt we could work things out. As for our romance... well, I didn't really have a way to measure the progress our relationship was making, and I didn't really want one either. It should develop however it developed, and I didn't need a scorecard to keep track of it.

A few minutes later, Danni excused herself to get ready for her own training session. I felt a little sad as I watched her go, but I told myself that was a good thing. I was sad because I wanted her here. That had to be the reason.

Then my self-appointed keeper snuck up behind me and let some of the air out of my balloon.

"How are things going?" Emma asked.

I suffered no confusion over what she meant by things, and, as usual, part of me wanted to tell her my relationship with Danni was none of her concern. But, given the way I was so rapidly waffling back and forth between all is well and total disaster, it felt like an outside opinion might be worth having. And, of course, it's not like Em would actually stay out of my business anyway, so...

"I don't know," I admitted.

And I didn't. On the one hand, here I was planning to head over to Danni's tonight for dinner and companionship. Another in the series of infinitesimally small steps we'd taken in the quest to figure out who we are to each other. On the other, it felt like everything was falling apart. There were too many stilted conversations like the one we'd just had. Too many times where I felt Danni pulling back on the reigns instead of just saying what was on her mind. Like she was afraid to say something I might not be happy to hear.

Was I also doing that to her?

I didn't think so, but I wasn't sure. I know I've been spending more time wondering about what I should and shouldn't do, lately. Stuff I never used to think about.

Overall, it felt like our new relationship was failing to take shape while the old one was melting beneath it. Now, we were left standing in a puddle of goo, wondering why we couldn't move forward.

So much for that confidence I'd been building just seconds ago.

"Problems?" Emma asked.

I sighed and said, "I don't know that, either."

"You don't know much, do you?"

"Don't start."

You could almost see the next wiseass comment forming in her head. That little, knowing smile creasing her lips. She decided against it, but the smile hung on longer than it should have. Like it was a really good one that couldn't be wasted, so she was saving it for later use.

"I don't know what's going on," I said. "It feels like we want this, but we don't know how to do it."

"What's so wrong with that? You'll figure it out."

I shrugged.

"Feels like it should be easier."

"Nothing worth doing ever is."

"Well... thank you, Teddy Roosevelt."

"You know what I mean. If you really want this, you need to work at it."

"I do want it," I said, "and I'll do whatever it takes. But I don't know what that is."

"Only Danni can answer that for you."

"I'm supposed to ask Danni?" I asked. "I don't think that's how it works."

She gave a little you never know shrug and said, "Sometimes, that's exactly how it works."

Then she gave me a smile and went back to her training.

Having no idea what the hell that was supposed to mean, I shook my head and moved on.

Late in the day, I caught Steph crossing the lobby on her way back to her office. She looked aggravated, but when did she not? Seeing an opportunity, I decided maybe the best way to get Danni's training idea back on track was to run it by Steph and get her on board.

"You got a second?" I asked her.

She nodded, then followed me into my office.

"I saw you out there with Liv and Dove today," I said once we were settled.

She didn't say anything, but the way she snarled was all the response I needed.

"I guess we need to do something," I said.

"I thought the rotation idea was a good plan," she said.

I paused, needing a few seconds to adjust. Danni hadn't mentioned that Steph already knew about the plan, so the revelation threw me off.

"You know about that?" I asked.

"Sure. I helped Danni hammer it out."

My eyes drifted to my desk as I once again found myself at a loss for words.

"But Danni said she talked to you, and you didn't like the idea," Steph continued.

"No," I said strenuously. "I thought of a few problems we need to deal with, that's all. I told her I was sure we could work it out."

"What problems?" she asked.

I detailed the concerns I'd shared with Danni earlier.

"We already covered all of that," Steph said. "We're planning week-long rotations for the veterans so the rookies get a healthy dose of each one, but we're going to keep the training sessions short to minimize the time impact. And we're going to synch it with the event calendar. If somebody has a fight coming up, we won't schedule them until after."

I spread my hands in front of me and let them hang there in the internationally recognized signal for what the fuck?

Danni had thought about the issues I'd raised, exactly as I'd expected. She had solutions. She'd discussed them with Steph. The two women I counted on to make RSI go had ironed out the whole thing. I couldn't have asked for a better approach. Beyond that, Danni knows me well enough to know that I'm not likely to resist when she and Steph are united. So, why hadn't she just said something?

This development, on top of my conversation with Emma, had pushed the needle on the relationship barometer back over to total disaster.

Way over.

Steph picked up on my expression.

"Are you two OK?" she asked gently.

Emma was one thing, but this was something else. I know she meant well, but the fact that the normally terse and practically antisocial Stephanie McMahon was now showing me compassion and concern did not help matters one bit.

"I don't know," I admitted again.

Again, I was left to wonder what was happening, and I kept coming back to the same thought. The day this new adventure began was also the day the saga with Powers had ended. Even as we were living through that nightmare, I found myself wondering how it would affect Danni in the long run. Now, it seemed, I was getting my answer. The same answer I knew I was going to get from the beginning. She'd been threatened, shot at and nearly killed. You don't go through something like that and come out unchanged on the other end.

Steph sat and watched me, the concern obvious on her face. Once again, I found this unsettling. Her usual response would be to say that Danni and I needed to knock off the crap and get our shit together. Where was that when I needed it?

What I didn't realize was that, in the course of doing her job, she was about to make things a whole lot worse.

"There's something else," she said.

"What?" I asked warily.

Instead of responding, she slowly stood, then went over and closed my office door. It was strange behavior from a woman who normally shares her thoughts instantly, directly and in the order they arrive. I'd never seen her so serious, and I've never known her to worry about her audience.

By the time she turned around, I was in a cold sweat. She was scaring the shit out of me, and I couldn't even begin to guess what she was going to say.

Had I tried, I would never have gotten anywhere close.

"Danni's not ready."

At first, I was confused. I thought maybe she was talking about our relationship, or something along those lines.

"Not ready for what?"

"Cole."

I couldn't see my own expression, but I'm sure surprised didn't even begin to cover it.

"What the hell are you talking about?"

The shock in my voice said more than my words ever could. Steph heard it, and her answer was in response to the unseen thoughts in my head, rather than the question I'd asked.

"I know," she said. "I can't believe it, either."

I shook my head slowly, unable to come to grips with the situation.

Danni?

Not ready?

The idea was ludicrous. In years of working together, I've never once had reason to to question Danni's ability, effort or work ethic in the cage. Through all the problems and catastrophes. All the ups and downs of making RSI run. No matter what was going on, Danni was always ready. Always. It was as automatic as breathing.

Instead of saying all that, I said nothing at all. I just sat and waited for the world to fix itself. But the expression on Steph's face told me that wasn't going to happen, and I should have known better than to think that it would.

When a fighter isn't prepared for a fight, disaster is the most likely outcome. Not only is she likely to lose, but the risk of serious injury goes up dramatically. Preventing that from happening is Steph's job, and she has never taken her responsibility lightly.

For her to tell me Danni wasn't ready meant that she had assessed the situation, considered how ridiculous it was and decided she had to tell me, anyway.

In other words, it meant that Danni wasn't ready.

"How bad is it?"

She shrugged.

"It's still Danni," she said. "Her skills are there, and she's putting the work in. She's just not focused. Not sharp."

She didn't have to explain herself any further. Still Danni would be enough for many opponents, but not for Cheryl Cole. She believed kicking Danni's ass and taking her title was her destiny. Danni had prevailed in their last matchup, but only because she had honed her skills to a razor's edge. Dull and unfocused wasn't going to get the job done in the upcoming rematch.

"How's she been in your sparring sessions?" Steph asked.

I took a deep breath and blew out an exasperated sigh. That was all the response she needed.

"You haven't been sparring," she said.

"No," I said.

"Why not?"

"I don't know," I said. "It just sort of went away. We started out skipping a few nights. Then it became more frequent, and then it just... stopped."

"And you didn't wonder why?"

I read the question as an accusation.

"Sure I did," I said defensively, "and I could give you a list a mile long with all the theories I came up with, if you want."

Her expression flattened and she said, "Relax. I'm not saying this is your fault. I'm just trying to see if you noticed anything."

I combed my fingers through my hair. I was angry now, but it only took me a few seconds to figure out who I was really mad at.

"I should have noticed," I said, mostly to myself.

"I never said that."

"That doesn't mean you didn't think it," I said. "Also doesn't mean it isn't true."

She didn't seem to have anything to say to that.

How had it come to this?  I asked myself.

Danni and I spent almost all our time together, both inside and outside this building. How had I not picked up on something so important?

The answer was simple. It was because I had my head buried too far up my ass to see anything. I had been so caught up in examining every little moment that happened between the two of us, searching for clues as to what she was thinking and what I should do and where we were headed. I had completely missed what was going on right in front of me.

Jesus Christ... not READY?

"What do we do?" I asked. "The event is less than two weeks out."

"I don't know," she said. "I'm pushing her as hard as I can. Trying to make her see what's happening. But if I say it flat out..."

"She's fucked," I finished for her.

The only thing worse than Danni going into the fight at less than her best would be going in knowing she wasn't right. Against an opponent of Cheryl's magnitude, confidence was half the battle.

"Can we claim an injury?" I asked.

The fact that I'd even asked the question revealed that I was grasping at straws. Worse yet, Steph's response told me she'd already had the same desperate thought.

"She'd have to go along," she said.

That was never going to happen. Danni would never fake an injury to get out of a fight.

"What the fuck do we do?" I asked again.

Another shrug.

"See if we can get in her head and figure out what's going on."

The implication was gentle, but clear. There were any number of things that could be throwing Danni off. Dig under most of them, and you'd find me.

The problem, of course, was that I didn't have many more angles of attack than Steph did. How was I supposed to tell Danni about our concerns without telling her about our concerns? Worse yet, I was now convinced I was right about what was going on. And there would be no quick fix for it.

"I'll try," was the best I could offer.

The words came out weak, just like my confidence. I didn't really believe I had any hope of getting Danni to open up, but, even if I did...what the hell was I going to do then?
