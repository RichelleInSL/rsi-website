-19-Getting Serious

Danni came into my office the next morning and plopped down in a guest chair.

"We need to go out to eat," she said.

"Like, now? I'm not really a big breakfast person."

"Tonight, I mean."

Between running RSI and our after hours training, the two of us have been leaving here exhausted each day. For a while, dinner was settling on what take-out we wanted, then deciding who's house we were taking it to. We got sick of that pretty quick, so, since she lives closer than I do, we stocked her kitchen and started dining in. Since I'm the better cook - an assumption I make because she's never tried - it's fallen on me to see what I can come up with, each night.

In other words, this sudden request sort of sounded like she was tired of my cooking.

"Just so we're clear, a more insecure person than I would be offended," I said.

"It's not that," she said. "We just need to go out."

Her tone seemed weirdly direct.

"Fine by me," I said. "What do you have in mind? Little Szechuan? Maybe Piero's?"

"I was thinking the Pier House."

My eyes widened a bit.

"Woah... that place is expensive."

"I know."

"And we'd have to get dressed up."

"I know."

I gave her a funny look as I puzzled over the request and the serious way she was making it. It didn't take long for reality to dawn on me.

Danni wanted to go on a date.

Despite what I'd said during our brief stop in New Orleans, our evening activities hadn't changed very much. Sometimes we went out and sometimes we had stayed in, but what Danni wanted now was something we'd never done before. Not a casual dinner between two friends, or a quiet evening at home. A full-scale, makeup and dresses, pick-you-up-at-seven kind of date.

It felt like things were getting serious in a hurry, and I was suddenly feeling more pressure than ever.

Danni mistook my silence to mean I was concerned about losing training time.

"C'mon, we've been at it for weeks," Danni said. "We can take a night off. Besides, it's Friday. This is when normal people go out and have fun."

"Really?"

"That's what I hear, anyway."

"You think we can handle being normal for a night?"

"We'll never be normal," she said, "but I'm a trained actress. I can pretend to be anything."

"I think I can wing it," I said. "I'll just follow your lead."

We both smiled. Hers was the same smile she'd given me on our trip. It was just as beautiful now, but it didn't have the same calming effect as before.

"I'm going to have to go home and get ready," I said. "You want to meet at your place?"

"No, I'll pick you up. Seven work for you?"

Huh.

"Seven? That leaves us enough time to squeeze in a quick spar..."

"Richelle..."

"I'm kidding!" I laughed. "Seven is fine. But why don't I pick you up? You live closer."

She shook her head.

"I asked, so I'll drive."

How very formal.

"Works for me," I said. "Plus, I get to ride in the Audi."

"See? It's a win-win."

"Think you'll ever let me drive it?"

"The Audi?" she asked as she got up to leave. "Sure. Yeah. You bet. One of these days."

Maybe it's just me, but I don't think she meant it.

After she left, I sat back and pushed a long breath between my pursed lips.

Dates? Dresses? Expensive dinners?

Most times I'd combined those things in the past, the evening's events had culminated in the bedroom.

Stupid Emma... doesn't know anything.
