Author's Note: The Spenser and Hawk characters belong to mystery novelist Robert B. Parker. Mr. Parker is one of my favorite authors, and his work is a major influence on the the way I write. Whether I even come close to mimicking his style is open to considerable debate, but I have chosen to use his characters in this story as something of an homage. My goal is to handle them as lightly as possible and to maintain them as Mr. Parker created them. Any failure on that front is completely my own.

-2-The Start of Nothing

Rumson, New Jersey - Three months before the title fight in Melbourne

I woke to a pair of large brown eyes peering at me intently over the edge of the couch cushion. For a moment, I didn't understand what I was looking at. I lifted my head and the eyes followed me up, with a sad, tan-furred face and dark muzzle coming into view behind them. Then, I understood.

"Go lay down, Tramp," I mumbled to my dog.

He stayed where he was and pressed his head forward over the cushion, looking for attention.

"Go away!" I said again.

He still didn't move, but a massive, dark shape suddenly rose up behind him. At full height, the shape was nearly a foot taller and far more menacing than my shaggy canine. Then, a pair of triangular ears perked up, and I recognized the shape to be Winzig, the mammoth Great Dane owned by Danneel Harris.

Why is Danni's dog next to my couch?

Because this is not my couch.

I pushed Tramp away and dropped my head back down. He snorted in displeasure as both dogs settled back to the floor, but I didn't feel bad about it. It wouldn't kill them to wait a little while longer, and it had been weeks since I'd woken up this calm and relaxed. I was going to make the moment last as long as I could.

Don't get me wrong. As the owner of Rampage Sports, Inc - better known as RSI - I'm pretty used to stress and craziness. When you run a stable of female mixed martial artists, you have to expect that sort of thing. These women are smart, tough and extremely self-confident. That's what it takes to be willing to step into a locked cage with an opponent who is hell-bent on destroying you. You have to know you can take whatever she's got. You have to absolutely believe you're better. When you fill a building with women who each possess this unique blend of brains, brawn and fearlessness... well, crazy is just par for the course. But things have been a little more nuts than usual, lately.

Ordinarily, my days are filled with event schedules and injury reports and media requests. As if that weren't enough, I have recently begun getting involved on the training floor, itself. That last part may seem a little strange, especially when you consider that I've never actually been a professional fighter. But I think it's safe to say that my amateur experience was a little more hardcore than most.

The decision to build RSI was a mere continuation of the years I'd spent in the violent and dangerous world of underground fighting. I'll never know for sure what drove me into that life. I tell myself that I wasn't much different than the women who work for me now. That, like them, I simply enjoyed the thrill and competition of fighting. It wasn't my fault that finding it then wasn't nearly as easy as it is now. MMA is a dangerous business, but it has rules and discipline and a measure of sanity that makes it a sport, rather than a back alley brawl. But the sport was in its infancy when I got my start decades ago. And that was only on the male side of things. The idea of women climbing in the cage was viewed as either a joke or a moral outrage, depending on who you asked. So, illegal, unregulated fights in seedy clubs and grimy alleys were the only option I had.

That's what I tell myself, but don't let the cover job fool you. Deep down, I don't buy it myself. The fact that I enjoy fighting wasn't enough to excuse or explain the fact that I freely chose to spend years living in a world where I easily could have been killed.

Clearly, I also have a screw loose.

I was pretty good at what I did. Maybe a little too good, to be honest. Because being good is not all you have to do to get paid in that world. The economics there revolved around the betting. After a while, taking on whatever opponents drifted into the shitty little club where I fought in the slums of Trenton wasn't working anymore. Everyone knew me and they knew what I could do. So I couldn't make any money because the club couldn't make any money. The odds on me were way too high.

With the local scene all dried up, I had begun to travel around a little... taking on big paying fights against fighters from other areas who had the same problem. Fighting outside of my home turf increased the risk a little, but the money prospects were a helluva lot better. It was all worth it in the end, right?

Not exactly.

I don't think there was ever any doubt that living that life was eventually going to catch up with me, and the inevitable finally happened in a dank and dingy dungeon of a club on the south side of Boston. The woman I was supposed to fight was one of the biggest names on the underground circuit, and, being a local, she was very popular there in Beantown. That was good news for me. These big matchups always attracted a lot of attention, which meant the betting would be heavy. And geography would make her the big favorite... meaning a sizable bonus for me if I won. Since I always expected to win, I was looking forward to a nice payday.

Then it all went to hell in a hurry.

My opponent was switched out only minutes before the fight. Injury, I was told, but I'll never know the real truth. What I did know was that my money had just gone bye-bye. There was no way the bettors would buy into whoever the replacement was. The crowd was already on-site, so the betting would still be heavy. But when they found out about the switch, the money was all going to go on me. That would erase most of the house edge, and my big bonus would disppear along with it. I was pissed, to say the least, and I was ready to pack it up and go home.

Enter King Powers - the slimeball who ran the makeshift club. He tried to persuade me to go through with the fight. Said he owed his patrons something for their trouble. He promised me big money, no matter what. All I had to do was make the fight look good. Maybe take a few punches to try and sell things. He just wanted the crowd to feel like they got a little of what they came for.

Yeah... it sounded like bullshit to me, too. But the money was tempting and the trip home was long. Making the journey with less money in my pockets than I'd started with would make it that much longer. And really... what could go wrong?

The answer, as I soon found out, was everything.

It all started just as I'd expected it would. The replacement they'd dug up was decent, but clearly in over her head. She was fit and strong and she knew a little about fighting, but not nearly enough. Within minutes, the crowd was growing restless ans tired of watching me play with the poor girl. I was confident I had everything well in hand, so I decided to do as I'd been told and lean into one.

For several minutes afterward, I would have no idea what was happening to me.

Her fist sank into my side with a force I'd never felt before. Before I could react, her other hand smashed into my mouth with an equally brutal impact.

Two punches, and I was down.

I got back up, but I was a mess. My head was scrambled and I couldn't defend myself. She laid into me a few more times and put me down again. A smarter person would have stayed down. Something was desperately wrong. She was hurting me badly in ways that made no sense. Better to just take the loss and get out of there than to risk being permanently injured or worse. But, for me, brains rarely ever rule the day. When the gloves are on and the battle is raging, I run on pride and white hot fury.

Despite the fog of the beating I'd suffered, I somehow realized the problem had to be her gloves. Fighting like a savage, I managed to get hold of her and rip one of them from her hand. Immediately, I knew I'd found my answer. A sap glove. It looked just like the ones I wore, but it was more than twice as heavy. That was because it was filled with a dense, metallic powder instead of the soft padding that was stuffed into mine.

From that point on, there was nothing rational about my behavior. I hit her. Then I hit her again. And again. My mind became consumed with the fact that she had cheated. That she had tried to kill me. I just kept swinging - beating her mercilessly. And no one did anything to stop me. Not until long after the damage was done. As they finally dragged me away, a small slice of my sanity returned. And, in that moment, I realized I had killed her.

There wasn't much time to reflect on what I'd done, though. With all the pieces of the puzzle now in place, it was clear I had been the victim of a plot cooked up by Powers. He wanted me to lose. If I had, the big upset would have meant a windfall for him. To make sure he got what he wanted, he had given her weapons to beat me with. She could have killed me, but hey... that was just the price of doing business. But I had fucked up his plan. In the process, I had cost him a small fortune. So, the reward for my victory was that he decided I should die, anyway. If not for an insane and completely lucky set of circumstances, I would have.

Among those circumstances was the involvement of a private detective named Spenser. I'm not a big believer in heroes and white knights, but, at a time when I desperately needed one, Spenser had been there to fill the role. He and a man called Hawk - whose background was less white knight and more dark and dangerous - managed to keep me safe and drive Powers away.

Though years went by without a hint of trouble, I could never shake the feeling that the ordeal wasn't over. Powers had been willing to risk my life just to make his little gambling scheme work. When it didn't go his way, he'd been willing to take my life, just to make himself feel better.

Sounds like the kinda guy who might hold a grudge.

Sure enough, my fears were eventually realized. Just a few weeks ago, King Powers reentered my life. His ultimate goal was the same as before: he wanted to kill me. But first, he wanted to destroy everything I cared about. To make things worse, he hadn't come after me, directly. Instead, he had threatened RSI's in-cage leader, Danneel Harris... using her as a pawn to get to me.

The weeks that followed had brought all sorts of terror and misery to both of us. But every step of the way, I kept thinking that I deserved it, while Danni did not. I had been the one who'd gotten myself involved with thieves and low-lifes just for thrills. I had been the one that killed that girl. Danni had done nothing but stand too close to me when karma had come to call.

Even now, I am furious with myself for what she had to go through.

But that was all behind us now. With a whole lot of help from a lot of people, we had survived. Despite shootouts and death threats and a mid-winter swim through an ice-glazed river, Danni and I were still here. Powers, on the other hand, was dead and gone.

Amazingly, a sliver of good came out of the whole hellish experience. Danni and I have known each other for more than three years now. It is a relationship that is nearly impossible to describe. Both of us are passionate - and also somewhat obstinate. We scream and shout at each other. We get in each others' faces. But, beneath it all, we trust each other. Each of us can do all that yelling and screaming with the knowledge that the other will always have her back. It's a bond unlike any I've ever had with anyone else. I cherish that relationship, and I know that she does too. But the ordeal of these past few weeks has pushed us even closer. Not just emotionally, either. Staying alive meant staying together. Literally. For weeks, we had been living together under her roof. With no room for retreat, I had expected we would drive each other crazy. Instead, the opposite had happened. And with the stress released and relief finally in sight, Danni had given me one of the biggest surprises of my life.

She had kissed me.

It was a simple, sweet moment. One that required a tremendous amount of courage on her part. In an instant, she had put our friendship at risk in the hope that it could become something more.

I had responded to her bravery by driving away without even saying a word.

I admit it was probably not the silver screen moment she'd been hoping for.

In my defense, I had a few things on my mind. Little things like, Do I feel the same way about her? and What the hell is happening to me? Not to mention, my favorite - When did I become a lesbian?

Strangely, it hadn't taken me long to get over those questions. I convinced myself I needed to keep it simple. I liked being with Danni. I was happier when I was near her. So I came back and decided to let the chips fall where they may. I owed her that much.

That was last night, though. Today - a day that was supposed to mark the beginning of our new adventure - something was troubling me. I couldn't quite put my finger on what it was, but I suspected it had something to do with the awkward realization that Danni's arm was draped around my waist.

Apparently, we had fallen asleep together on her couch.

A dull ache told me my hip was resting on the gap between the cushions. Trying to ease the pain, I shifted my weight gently - carefully trying not to wake her - but she stirred, anyway.

She quickly had the same awkward realization. Suddenly the arm was gone as I felt her tense behind me.

"Uhm..."

"Relax," I said as I sat up and swung my feet to the floor. "It's not a big deal."

I turned my head until her face came into view. The soft smile she gave me put a warm feeling in my chest, even as I continued to struggle with the subtle, nagging feeling that something was wrong.

She didn't help matters when she said, "This isn't exactly how I expected we would sleep together for the first time."

Oh, boy.

I don't know what expression played across my face. I only know she was quick and accurate in interpreting it.

"Too forward?" she asked.

"Just a little bit," I smiled back at her.

The nagging continued and I had to force the smile a little. As usual, Danni saw through it.

"Are you okay with this?," she asked.

"I think so," I said.

The answer surprised me. Last night, I had been so sure. So ready to explore this new branch of our relationship. I didn't hear that same certainty in my voice this morning.

Danni noticed it was missing, too. She sat up, hugged her knees into her and peered at me over the top. It was a soft, friendly look, yet it somehow silently asked me to explain myself.

"C'mon," I said, "you gotta give me a little time."

She thought a little, then her head started to bounce up and down in a gentle nod.

"Want some breakfast?" she said, changing the subject.

"You cooking?" I asked incredulously.

"Hell no," she answered.

Danni's house contained a kitchen the size of a small state, but it turned out she never cooked. I, on the other hand, was pretty handy with a spatula.

"So you were asking me if I wanted to make breakfast?"

"No, but... now that you mention it."

I tilted my head and arched an eyebrow at her.

"Fine," I sighed. "Just let me shower first."

Danni played with the dogs while I got cleaned up. I had basically moved into her spare bedroom during our ordeal, so my clothing and shower essentials were still there.

When I came back down, she went up as I gathered what I needed for breakfast: eggs, cinnamon, vanilla, a loaf of bread and a few slices of bacon. From the cabinet, I pulled out a long griddle pan and a shallow bowl. I beat the eggs, cinnamon and vanilla together in the bowl, then dipped the bread slices in the mixture. I placed them on the front half of the pan, then laid the bacon out on the back half. The dogs sat side-by-side to my left and watched me expectantly. I'm well aware that human food is supposedly bad for them, and that rewarding them for begging reinforces bad behavior. Unfortunately, a couple of slices of French toast and a few pieces of bacon accidentally fell on the floor while I was transferring them to the plates. Oops.

Danni returned as I was setting the food out on the island breakfast bar. She had her phone in her hand and wore an expression that said she was about to tell me something I wasn't going to like very much. But she put whatever it was on hold, stopping in the kitchen doorway to breathe in the aromas of the room.

How did she show her appreciation for my efforts?

"No banana pancakes?" she said disappointedly.

Danni has a serious weak spot for bananas. I've honestly never seen anything like it."Didn't I just make those yesterday?"

"Yeah," she winced, "about that."

She turned the phone so it faced me. There wasn't much for me to see because lock screen was up showing only the date and time. But the bad news was right there at the top.

Wednesday.

In the recent insanity, we had lost all track of time. Those pancakes I mentioned were served three days ago. Two days ago, we had come within moments of freezing to death. Yesterday, we had broken a whole lot of laws in the process of getting our lives back.

Today, we were supposed to be at work.

"Shit," I said.

"Yeah... Steph just called, wondering where we were."

As Development Coordinator, Stephanie McMahon would be the senior-most person at RSI until we arrived. Since Danni and I have been missing quite a bit these past few weeks, she's been basically running the show. We owe her a lot, and what we'd done this morning was not exactly the best way to start repaying her. We should have been the ones to call her, not the other way around.

"What did you tell her?" I asked.

"That I'd talk to you and get back to her."

I nodded. The obvious response was to simply say screw it, and that thought was definitely appealing. After what we'd been through, we deserved some time to relax. On top of that, spending the day with Danni would be fitting, considering the new path our relationship was taking. And I know the boss, so it's not like vacation time was going to be a problem.

"We could take another day," she said hopefully.

Then, she gestured toward the plates and added, "Besides, we wouldn't want to let all this go to waste."

No matter how I spun it, there was no logical reason to say no. But that didn't stop me from doing it, anyway.

"We've been missing a lot recently," I said.

I have no idea why I said it. Despite our recent absences, there was nothing pressing going on at the facility that required our attention. Nothing that couldn't wait until tomorrow, at least.

Her eyes clouded over as she looked away.

"But I think we can afford to have breakfast," I said quickly, trying to soften the blow by indicating we'd go in a little late.

It wasn't much of a sacrifice - we were already late - but her mood seemed to brighten, anyway.

"I'll call Steph and let her know," she said.

She had no sooner said it when my phone rang. For a brief moment, I had a thought that it was Steph, again. Then Danni and I looked at each other, and both of us simultaneously came to the same conclusion.

"That's not going to be Steph," I said.

"No, it is not," Danni said, shaking her head steadily.

I gave a little grunt of despair, then made the short walk to where I'd dropped my phone on the entryway table. It felt a little like being forced to march out and meet my executioner.

The name on the screen was exactly the one I expected.

Emma Watson.

Unlike Steph, Emma holds no official title whatsoever at RSI. So, it was really none of her god-damned business where I was. But that's not how our relationship works. She and I have quite a bit of history between us. Not all of it is good. Some of it is so bad, we never speak of it. Those things are behind us now, but the memories of what happened somehow bond us together. You wouldn't know it from the way we're constantly insulting each other, but we care a great deal about one another. She's certainly quick to stick her nose in my business. It's annoying at times, but I know she means well. And I figure it doesn't hurt to have her keeping an eye on me. Lord knows someone should.

Truth is, there are few people on Earth who garner my respect the way Emma does. With her astronomical popularity, she would seem the perfect candidate to suffer from diva-level self-absorption. Instead, she is as quiet and unassuming as the girl next door. She shows little interest in crafting the perfect image for the public. In part, that's because she has nothing to hide. But it also has a lot to do with the fact that she's comfortable in her own skin. She is who she is, and she's good with that. I'm not sure I could show that same level of of self-assurance if I were in her shoes, and the fact that she can impresses the hell out of me.

Oh, and I get the impression that, deep down, she thinks I'm OK, too.

At any rate, there was an incident last week where Danni and I disappeared for a while without telling anyone. I admit it was the wrong thing to do, but, in our defense, we really weren't thinking straight at the time. We were focused on not ending up dead, and nothing else. Such excuses didn't matter much to Emma, though. With everything that was going on, she assumed the worst. When we finally reappeared the next day, she was not happy. I know this because she told me so, in no uncertain terms. There was a point during her tirade where she became almost feral, stalking back and forth in front of my desk like a tiger that hadn't been fed in a week - and I was the only option on the menu. It was not a pleasant feeling.

This is probably where I should point out that Emma isn't just a fighter. She's an accomplished champion who ranks among the most elite warriors the league has to offer. Yes, she is exactly the sunny, friendly person everyone thinks she is. Push her around, though, and you will quickly discover she has talents most know nothing about. Destructive, violent talents. Those who have experienced that part of her can tell you some pretty horrifying stories. Trust me. I know.

Anyway... I didn't really want to go through the tiger thing again, so I was happy that, this time, I would be able to tell her the nightmare had come to an end. Sort of a lullaby to sooth the savage beast.

"Hi, Em," I said as I answered.

"Where are you?" she demanded. Like my mother coming down on me because I'd missed curfew.

"We're fine," I said. "It's over."

Her tone changed instantly.

"Really?"

"Yeah."

"Oh, thank god!"

There was no customary smartass comment, and the relief was clear in her voice.

See? I told you she likes me.

I gave her the basics of what had happened with Powers, with the most important detail being that he was dead.

"Fuck him," she said when I finished.

"Couldn't agree more," I said.

"So, it's really over," she said. Half-statement, half-question. Like she needed to double-check to be sure.

This heartfelt stuff was something new between us. I mean, I'm glad to know she cares, but this was weird. So, I tried to get things back to normal.

"Aww, Emma... were you worried about us?" I asked, deliberately setting myself up for her to take a shot at me.

"We all were," she said earnestly.

I had lobbed her an easy one, and she didn't even take the swing. Clearly, we had really scared her good.

Behind me, I could here Danni talking with Steph. Emma heard it too.

The downside of Emma keeping an eye on me is that she sometimes sees things I wish she didn't. That included whatever was going on between Danni and I. While we might not have noticed the way our relationship was evolving, Emma had. To her credit, she didn't meddle with it. When Danni started to sort things out for herself, Emma picked up on it. Still, she stayed out of it, trusting we would eventually figure it out. Then all hell broke loose, and she decided she couldn't wait anymore. With Danni still keeping her feelings to herself and me still typically clueless, Emma worried Danni might do things I wouldn't see coming. She tried to clue me in and, when I failed to get it, she went straight at Danni over it. Maybe she shouldn't have. Maybe she should have just minded her own business. She was right, though. At a moment when the danger was at its peak, Danni had put herself in harm's way to protect me. So, maybe Emma knew what she was doing, all along.

One thing I now knew for sure, Emma was tuned in to whatever was happening between Danni and I. And while she might not want to insert herself into the middle of it, she wasn't afraid to pry around the edges.

"You guys are still together?" she asked.

"We are," I answered.

Though we weren't in the same place, I could still see the tiny smile play across her face.

"Any particular reason?" she asked.

The answer was simple: Danni and I were together because we wanted to be. Because Danni had been brave enough to take a chance, and the moment Emma had been waiting for had happened. And there was not a doubt in my mind that Emma would know everything before the day was out, no matter what I said. So, there was no point in hiding the truth.

But I still tried.

"It was a long day," I said. "It was just easier for me to crash here when it was over."

That made the third time in less than forty-five minutes that I'd said something without understanding why, and it was starting to bother me.

"Oh," Emma said.

It wasn't the answer she wanted - or, you know, the truth - but she rallied like a champ.

"Are we going to see you guys today?"

"Yeah, we'll be in later."

"Then I'll see you then," she said. "And hey... I'm glad you're not dead."

"Me, too," I smiled.

We disconnected and I stood there wondering just what the hell was going on with me. Why did I feel the need to go to RSI today, when I could stay right here with Danni? And why did I just lie to Emma?

I brought the phone down and looked at it as though it might have some answers for me, but the screen just faded and then went dark.

Danni appeared in the kitchen doorway behind me.

"Food's getting cold," she said.

I shook off my thoughts and smiled at her.

"Then let's eat," I said.

We sat down at the island and dug in. Danni made a big deal out of how good the food was. I appreciated the compliments, but I couldn't agree.

For some reason, nothing tasted right to me.
