RSI Story #17Trying Times

-44-Dealing With Danni

I found Danni two rooms down with Marcy getting her prepped.

"How's Emma?" she asked as soon as I walked in.

"She's fine. I just left her."

Apparently, that answer was not sufficient for RSI's always-on-duty team leader.

"I saw what she did," Danni said. "Is someone keeping an eye on her?"

"Hey... I got this one, Harris. You can take the night off."

She wiggled the fingers of her already-gloved hand and said, "Yeah, sure. I'll just head home and catch up on some Netflix."

I smiled. She looked and sounded pretty good to me. Maybe a little tight, but that was to be expected before a fight of this magnitude. Even the most veteran fighters can get a little tense as they get closer to the opening bell, but Steph would be here shortly to handle the situation. She knew just what to do to loosen each woman up. Some liked distraction. Others liked something more physical, like working the mitts a little. But Danni was among the easiest. Steph would simply walk her through the strategy some more. Thinking about the technical stuff just before a bout would make some fighters freeze up, but Danni and Steph had a way of talking it through that helped her visualize what was coming. Neither was naive enough to think the real fight would work out anything like what they imagined, but that didn't matter. Focusing on the strategy calmed Danni. And when it all went to hell... well, there's no one who can improvise the way she can.

With Danni looking solid and Steph waiting in the wings to do her thing, I decided to make it brief.

"You're good," I said, "and since the odds of my saying or doing something stupid are astronomically higher than usual right now, I'm going to keep it short and sweet."

Danni smiled weakly and I realized there was something there I hadn't noticed at first. A little bit of a distant look in her eyes that said her head was somewhere else. To be honest, it was something I thought I'd see sooner. I expected it months ago before her return against Benz. Then I figured there'd be some sign of it in the preliminary rounds here. When that didn't happen, I started to wonder if I was worrying too much. But now, with Cheryl Cole in the offing, I could see she just a touch of worry creeping in on her.

It was not hard to figure out why. Not to put too fine a point on it, but Danni's career was basically on the line tonight. Another loss to Cole would be bad, but another lopsided loss would be catastrophic. If Cole took her apart that way again... there was a very good chance she would never make it back from that.

There was no part of me that honestly believed that would happen. Every mistake we'd made then had been corrected. Every hole plugged. Practically speaking, that night was ancient history.

But still... anything could happen in that cage tonight, and I could see that the worst-case scenario had found a place to hide in the back of Danni's mind.

"On second thought, can you give us a minute, Marse?"

The statuesque blonde gave me a little nod, finished whatever she was doing to Danni's right shoulder, then made her way outside.

Knowing I had seen past her facade, Danni gave me a little smile.

"I can't slip anything past you, can I?"

I smiled back and said, "I just thought it was time for a quick review of the facts."

The smile transformed into a sarcastic grin that said, Oh, I just can't wait to hear this.

I began to pace back and forth in front of her as I started my presentation.

"Danneel Harris is one of the premier fighters in this league."

"Oh, lord..." she groaned.

"If you don't mind, I'm going to have to request you not interrupt. I have a lot to get through, and not a whole lot of time."

She raised an eyebrow but said nothing.

"In a career that has spanned more than five years, Danni has won more than seventy-five percent of her fights. This includes the last few years, during which she has almost exclusively fought against the very best fighters in the league. During the time that I've known her, Danneel has not only achieved the top-ranking in her division, but she is also the top-ranked welterweight fighter in the entire league. She regularly finds herself going deep into brutal league-wide tournaments. Twice, she has won it all, and she has earned the opportunity to do it again, tonight."

The smile was gone now, and she was focused completely on me. I stopped pacing and looked straight back at her.

"I know Danneel better than... well, pretty much anyone. She is tough, strong and determined. She never gives excuses, and she steadfastly refuses to take the easy way out of anything. This includes refusing to acknowledge how difficult it is to be the best fighter in her weight class, while also being the greatest stable leader in this league."

"I question your impartiality..."

"Again, please don't interrupt."

Up went the eyebrow again, but I ignored it and continued.

"Nearly a year ago, Danneel suffered a terrible defeat against the very same opponent she will face tonight."

The eyebrow went down and the smile disappeared as the pain she held inside came right to the surface.

"The loss hurt her in ways most people can't even imagine. Privately, she told people she was thinking about quitting."

I paused there. I needed her to remember just how far down the rabbit hole she'd been before I continued.

"The thing is, Danni's friends and teammates wouldn't let her give up. No one who knew her believed for a second that what happened that night was the beginning of her end. So, they rallied around her. Veterans, rookies..."

I paused and waited until we were looking right at each other.

"...pit fighters," I added.

She gave me a thin-lipped smile, and I launched into the upside of my little speech.

"Everyone that cared about her dedicated themselves to setting things right. Danni tried to avoid it. She ducked and dodged and outright pleaded for people not to worry about her, but it was no use. Because the thing is, that group of people that care about her is enormous. She's earned that kind of dedication, simply by being who she is. She hates it, but seriously... none of us really give a shit."

The smile broadened.

"And so they pushed and they pulled and they prodded. They challenged her to get in the cage with them. They pretended to need help, just so they could work with her. Danni knew what was going on, but she let them get away with it because she secretly loved it."

She made no attempt to deny the accusation.

"Now, as her stable owner, it was my job to make sure that Danni was ready before she got back in the cage. So I enlisted the help of the best people I could find. Stephanie McMahon. Emily VanCamp. Emma Watson. Each of them tested Danneel, then reported back to me with what they found. And, of course, I made certain to test her for myself on a regular basis."

"This is the worst pep-talk..."

"Shush," I said. "I'm coming to the big finish."

She rolled her eyes.

"In the end," I continued, "the opinions were all the same. Danni was better than she had ever been."

"Are you done?"

"EVER, Danni," I said sharply. "Did you hear the list I gave? Steph, Emma, Emily... these are people who know their shit. People who would have spoken up loud and clear if they saw a problem, but none did."

I paused and waited for a reaction. My goal had been to reassure her with the level of expertise that had been brought to bear on the problem, but she took it in a direction I wasn't prepared for.

"They've been so good to me," she said of her teammates. "Good FOR me."

Then she looked up at me and said, "YOU'VE been good for me."

There was another beat of silence.

"I don't want to let anyone down."

For just a brief moment, I was worried. It was rare to see cracks in Danni's armor, and this was just about the worst time for it to happen. But then I thought of something I'd read way back in high school.

The world breaks everyone and afterward many are strong in the broken places. But those that will not break it kills.

The quote stuck with mostly because of when I'd read it. Because it so eloquently summarized a complicated truth I was only just beginning to understand at that time in my life.

Vulnerability is not a bad thing. It is a necessary thing. You have to understand where you are weak in order to be strong. Forging that strength is a process, not an exchange. You can't just swap out a weakness for a strength, like replacing a rotted beam in a house. Most of our weaknesses stay with us forever. We have to learn how to make up for them.

Danni would never forget what happened in her last fight against Cole. It was a scar on her psyche that would never disappear. But she had done what was needed to bridge over the wound. She had strengthened herself both physically and mentally. In doing so, she made those memories immaterial.

Or had she?

Were those memories coming back to haunt her now, or was it simply what she said? That she was worried that about what a bad result would do to the people who had helped her get back here.

It was impossible to tell with her, but it didn't really matter... because life had taught me another important lesson the hard way.

Vulnerability is not a bad thing... as long as you're careful about who you show it to. Show the wrong people, and they'll use your weakness as a weapon. Show it to people who truly care about you, and you'll quickly find out what it means to have real friends.

In her own way, Danni had shown her weakness to everyone at RSI, and her friends had been there for her. Giving her everything they could, while taking great pains to make it seem as casual as possible.

Now, she was showing a different weakness to me, and I wasn't going to let her down.

"We're your friends, Danni," I said. "Anything we did we did because we wanted to. So, don't put extra pressure on yourself because you don't owe anyone a damn thing."

I tried to gauge her reaction, but it was difficult to tell if I was making a difference.

I pressed on.

"I'm not going to bullshit you. Neither of us knows what's going to happen tonight. But I do know what won't happen. You won't be overwhelmed the way you were last time. You're going to go out there, put your gloves up and fight the way you know how. You're going to battle and scrap and claw for every inch you can get. You're going to put on a performance you can be proud of. One that's going to make all of us proud just to know you. And, win or lose, Cheryl Cole is going to leave this arena knowing she's been in a war."

We held on each other for a moment, and I could see she was hearing me.

"How was that for a pep talk?" I asked.

"Surprisingly, not bad."

I narrowed my eyes and said, "What the fuck do you mean surprisingly?"

She smiled broadly and I knew she was more than hearing me. I knew I was getting through.

I held my fist out in front of me.

"You got this," I said to her.

She nodded and bumped her fist to mine. We shared another long look but said no more because there was nothing left to say. Then I left and let the experts get in to do their work.

* * *

I could feel the tension in my gut as I took my seat in the RSI box. I hated it. A whole night of watching one RSI fighter after another put it all on the line, but none of the others got me worked up like this. Not even Emma.

Watching Danni was always hard on me, but this one was so much worse.

To my left - nearly a quarter of the way around the arena - I could see the owner of Cheryl's stable clustered with some members of his staff. If he saw me, he gave no acknowledgment. There was no particular animosity between us - which is a bit surprising, given how often our stables have come up against each other in big moments like this - but we weren't friendly, either. Truth is, we simply didn't know each other very well.

As for me, I was once again alone in the box. The only 'staff' I had were all hard at work.

I expected it would stay that way when there was a soft knock at the door.

I opened it to find the owner of Dragon's Den - who is simply known as Dragon - and his stable leader, Sophia Bush.

"Well," I said, "what has you two slumming in this part of the arena?"

"I was thrown out of my box," Dragon said curtly.

"I'm sorry... what?"

"Apparently, I had a drink too many. Perhaps more than that. Witnesses reported that I became belligerent, and I was eventually removed by security. I remember none of this, of course."

He was kidding. I was sure of it. Well, mostly sure of it. An incident like that is something I'm fairly certain I would have heard about. But I didn't say anything because, with Dragon, I can never be absolutely positive.

"Would you stop wasting time trying to be funny?" Sophia snapped playfully.

Dragon shrugged, then stepped aside and let his stable leader take over the conversation.

"How are you?" she asked me.

"Tense," I answered honestly. "And you?"

Sophia was much more than Danni's fellow stable leader. The two were friends, having worked together for years on the hit show One Tree Hill. It's a friendship that has sometimes given me agita, but it's also led to unprecedented cooperation between the stables that has worked to the benefit of all involved.

"The same," she answered, "but she's been rock solid recently, right?"

Another similarity between Danni and her friend is that they occupy the same weight class. In fact, the two had been contending for the same crown at this event. Sophia's bid was abruptly ended in round two by... you guessed it... Cheryl Cole.

Point is, thanks to RSI's cross-training agreement with the Den, Danni and Sophia often train and spar together. That put the Den leader in excellent position to know exactly how far Danni's game had come over these past months.

"I've never seen her better," I agreed.

Sophia smiled, then we all stood there looking at each other for a few moments.

"So, why are you here again?" I asked.

"We thought maybe you could use some company," Sophia said.

My true feelings showed on my face before I even had a chance to think about hiding them. The last thing I wanted right now was company. I was wound up so tight, it wasn't going to take much to cause me to lose my mind. My plan was to try and let it out in small increments throughout the fight, and I was thankful for the soundproof glass because some choice words were definitely going to be said. And if things really went wrong, I could retreat to the back of the box and throw shit where no one would see me.

In other words, I didn't need an audience for what was about to happen.

Sophia read my expression, and I could see she was disappointed. More disappointed than the situation warranted, to be honest. She had politely offered to keep me company, and I was about to politely decline. What was the big deal?

Oh... duh.

She wasn't just here for me. Like everybody else, she was worried about Danni.

I looked at her - her left eye still a little discolored from the thumping Cole had given her - and realized what she really wanted was a friend.

God damn Danni, I thought to myself. Makes everybody care about her, and then ties us all up in knots.

"Come on in," I said.

The two made their way in, with Dragon immediately noticing the small buffet table in the back was cold and empty.

"I wasn't expecting company," I explained, "and since I already feel like I'm going to throw up, I decided to skip the food. I think I've got some drinks back here, though."

"I'll take a water if you've got it," Sophia said.

I nodded then looked to Dragon, and I could practically see the words Do you have a beer in there? written on his face.

Then Sophia hit him with her elbow and he said, "Water would be fine."

I laughed as I fished three waters out of the fridge.

"Looks like you found someone to keep you on the straight and narrow," I said to Dragon.

He shrugged and said, "Someone probably should."

Need another similarity between Sophia and Danni? Well, it just so happens they're both dating their boss. While Danni and I were still unconfirmed, Dragon and Sophia were an official item.

Oddly, their relationship was viewed as somewhat scandalous, while it seemed the world was just itching to celebrate Danni and I.

Sounds to me like a gender parity issue. Or maybe a gay rights thing? A straight rights problem?

Regardless, I was sure there were lawyers somewhere praying that it all went to shit.

I tipped my bottle toward Dragon as we took our seats along the glass.

"Congrats on Josie's big win," I said.

"Thank you," he said. "Sorry it had to come at Jen's expense. She certainly made it interesting, though."

I shrugged.

"I'd have preferred a different result, but Josie deserved it. And Jen will be back, I think."

"Winning five titles isn't enough for you?" he asked. "You have to win them all?"

"Oh, I would never say something like that," I said.

Then I smiled broadly and added, "Doesn't mean I don't think it, though."

That drew a laugh from both of them as we settled in.

"Besides," I said as I turned my attention to the cage below, "it's going to be six titles soon."

My bravado was patently false, and Sophia saw right through it.

"Are you really worried?" she asked.

"I'm always worried," I said. "It's my natural state of being."

"I think she's got this."

"So do I," I said, "but that doesn't stop me from worrying."

Suddenly the announcer's voice filled the arena, and a little wave of panic washed over me.

It was time.

After rattling through the standard background stuff, the introductions began.

"Introducing first... she is the number one ranked welterweight fighter in the league and a muli-time divisional and tournament champion... fighting out of the red corner... the team leader of Rampage Sports, Incorporated... from Pelican Falls, Louisiana... she is... DA-neeeeeeeel HAR-riiiiiiis!

Danni's entrance song was an even bigger issue than Emma's. She had no productive thoughts on the matter. In fact, she seemed dismissive of the whole concept. Like having a song would be making too much of a fuss.

The others got involved and tried to inspire her. All suggestions were rejected. Soon enough, her teammates got fed up and the decision was taken out of her hands.

The process was complicated. The women felt a certain responsibility to get this one right, and capturing Danni's personality is not an easy thing. Many excellent choices were considered and dismissed. To the point where I was starting to worry we'd have to commission a brand new song just for the purpose... which would, in fact, defeat the purpose.

Eventually a consensus was reached, and Sorry Not Sorry by RSI's own Demi Lovato was selected.

As with Emma, the content of the song applies in no way whatsoever. But surprisingly enough, no one had to explain the decision to me because I got it right away. Just say the title out loud, and you realize it's Danni to a tee. As in, she's sorry she kicked your ass, but not really because that's her job. And it really isn't her fault she's so much better than you. That's just the way it is.

Danneel Harris... the apologetic conqueror.

Naturally, Danni fought the selection... and she can be quite the immovable object when she wants to be. But she stood no chance against the combined resolve of the women of Rampage Sports.

The song spun up at the bridge as Danni emerged from the tunnel, with Steph, Mandy and Marcy right behind her.

Now payback is a bad bitch, and baby I'm the baddest...

She looked good to me. Maybe I had made a difference. Maybe Steph had said something sage and comforting. Or maybe Danni was distracted from her concerns by the sizable roar she got from the crowd. Her devoted fans, letting her know they were behind her. She acknowledged them a bit on the way in, but mostly kept her eyes focused on the cage. An approach that was part professionalism and part humility. Though she takes the same business first attitude as Emily, she also doesn't really understand all that her fans see in her. I've resigned myself to the fact that she's never going to.

"And her opponent... also a multi-time tournament champion and the current divisional title holder... fighting out of the blue corner... the team leader of Savage Wolf MMA... from Tyneside, England... she is... CHER-yl Cooooooooooole!"

The pop the crowd gave Cheryl seemed noticeably less than Danni's... though I freely admit my opinion is biased. Regardless, Cole had plenty of fans, and they let her hear it. Though her entrance was more animated, she also seemed zeroed in on the goal.

Danni didn't ignore her, but she paid her no particular mind, either. She was busy talking last-minute strategy with Steph while she loosened up.

"She's all business," Dragon commented.

"She always is," Sophia and I said in unison.

With the intros finished, the referee - a role once again filled by Natasha Yi - brought the fighters to the center for final instructions. It was tense, but no more so than usual. I think Cole's hard look was meant to be intimidating, but it was wasted on Danni. Her plain expression was the perfect response because it was effortless. She wasn't trying to look bored with Cole's antics. She just was.

Yi finished her spiel, sent the fighters to their opposite corners, then motioned for the bell.

That moment had arrived again. That time when all the training and talking and build up stopped, and the only test that mattered began.

* * *

For nearly a full minute, there was little to talk about. Just a few light exchanges, with no significant damage done. The only thing worth noting was that Danni looked totally focused and in control. Her earlier misgivings hidden away in some emotional drawer. Not a small accomplishment, considering all she had riding on this.

Cheryl upped the pressure a bit. A quick jab, cross combo was followed by a left hook that would definitely have left a mark if it landed. But, while Danni's game was solid all-around, defense was still her strong suit. Instead of just blocking the hook she turned her body into it. It was a brilliant move. The punch landed harmlessly on her shoulder, and she was now in prime position to exploit Cole's miss. She already had her left handed cocked, ready to unload.

The problem? No Cheryl.

Cole had pulled the hook a bit, then gone low with what I had to admit was a pretty good takedown move. Coiling like a spring, she suddenly shot forward. Her shoulder slammed into Danni right around hip height and she moved to latch her arms around Danni's legs.

There was no earthly reason for Danni to expect a takedown from the strike-happy Cole. She hadn't attempted even one in their previous fights. I was fairly sure she hadn't attempted one throughout her run in this tournament. In fact, I'd be willing to bet she'd never attempted one. Ever. Which put the element of surprise squarely in her favor.

It also put a cold shiver down my spine. Cole had put together an impressive career by focusing on the things she does best, and she'd be the first to tell you so. So why try something now that she'd never done before? The most obvious answer was that she was looking to exploit some weakness she'd found in Danni's game.

One glance at Sophia told me I was not alone in this concern.

Cole surged forward and drove her shoulder into Danni's body. Running the shot, just as Fox had done. Only... not really.

First off - while Cole's move was good - she was no Megan Fox. Her technique didn't have the same speed, and certainly not the same crispness. On top of that, I suspected Danni had learned some hard lessons after being put through the wringer by Fox. So, despite the fact that she never could have predicted a move like this from Cheryl, she reacted to it beautifully. Immediately recognizing what was coming, she got her left leg back - improving her base to weather the attack. More importantly, she got the leg out of reach and denied Cole the wrap up she needed. What followed looked more like an outtake from a slapstick comedy than something that belonged in an MMA cage. Cole pushed and pushed, but Danni kept her balance by latching on to her opponent's body and simply going with the flow - literally hopping on her free leg as Cheryl plowed her along. It looked every bit as ridiculous as it sounds, but it kept Cole from getting the takedown she wanted. Trouble was, it also went on for much longer than it should have - the two of them tangled up and wobbling all around the cage. Almost as if Cheryl didn't recognize the futility of her efforts. Like she thought she just needed to push a little harder to get there.

She eventually caught on to the fact that it was never going to happen and slowed her momentum. That could have allowed Danni to try to improve her position and maybe even go for a choke - except Cole did something a little unorthodox. Keeping hold of her opponent's right leg, she lifted it high off the canvas. The move threw Danni completely off balance and Cole finally got that takedown after all... though, I don't really know why she bothered. Simply dumping Danni over accomplished little because she refused to give up her hold on Cheryl's body. The end result was that both fighters toppled to the canvas. Though Danni ended up on the bottom, the messy takedown allowed her to easily keep Cole from getting any sort of dominant position.

That didn't stop her from trying to dominate, though.

As soon as they landed, Cole tried to push her way up Danni's body.. and I was quickly losing the thread as to what she could be up to. Every move seemed to make less and less sense. The element of surprise was gone. In fact, there'd been nothing surgical about what she'd done so far, at all. No sense that she had some stunning tactical insight she was trying to make use of. Her takedown attempt had started decently, then devolved into a disaster. A mundane attack that had been turned aside with ease. So, why stay with it? I couldn't say for sure, but I found myself wondering if it had been more of a generic plan. It's a widely known fact that the canvas was a place most RSI fighters hated to be. We have some grinders like Tiz and Krystal Forscutt who are willing to go with the flow and scrap it out wherever the fight goes, but most of our fighters prefer to stay on their feet. Maybe Cole wanted Danni in a place she would be less comfortable?

If that's what Cheryl was thinking then she'd picked the wrong Rampager because Danni is the exception to the rule. She had always been a decent ground fighter, and had only recently demonstrated her markedly improved skills in the showdown with Fox. Did Cole really think she could do better than one of the most feared submission experts in the league?

It certainly seemed like she was going to try.

Danni read her opponent's move as any fighter would... that Cheryl was trying to get to mount position. The holy grail of ground fighting. If your opponent is able to gain full mount on you, you'd better hope the round is almost over because the danger is immense, escape is nearly impossible and you're not going to last long.

Knowing this, Danni scrambled to get her defense in place. Looking to catch Cole in what's called the guard position. Though very different from the 'guard' we talk about when striking, it's purpose is basically the same. It's how you defend yourself. The goal is to wrap your opponent with your legs. To control her and keep her from getting to you.

Danni was fast and technically perfect. Pushing and grappling to make room to get her legs up almost instantly. If Cole had seen an opening that would let her get to mount, Danni had slammed it shut and barred the door.

Trouble was, Cole wasn't trying to get to mount.

With impressive quickness, Cheryl ducked under and brushed Danni's upstretched legs aside as she moved up the RSI team leader's right side.

The warning light snapped on in my head.

Side control!

Not as bad as mount, but not good either. If Cole got solid position, she would have an array of options at her disposal. Arm bar. Elbow strikes. Hell, if she really got in deep, she could literally crush Danni into the mat.

Luckily Danni's warning light was better than mine because she was moving before the thought fully registered in my head.

As Cole moved in, Danni rolled up on her right side. At the same time, she spread her left leg out a bit and brought her right knee up. Her arms folded in to her center with her elbows tucked in tight. She pressed her palms together like she was praying then tucked them beside her head like she was going to sleep.

Just like that, Danni had expertly created a protective cage around herself.

By rolling up, she had avoided being caught flat on her back. Spreading the left leg out had given her an anchor to prevent Cole from pushing her around or trying to roll her, and bringing her knee up had invaded some of the space Cole wanted to occupy. Arms and elbows in would keep Cheryl from crushing down on her torso. Also, having the arms in and her hands together made it very difficult for Cole to go after the armbar, and the sleeping position kept Cheryl away from her neck.

In a grappling match, Cole probably would've be out of luck at that point. But here in the MMA cage, she still had some options. Punches and elbow strikes were still on the menu, and Cheryl gave it a go. But Danni didn't just lie there and hope she didn't get beat up. She was actively countering and trying to improve her situation. When Cole started with the elbows, Danni pulled her head down behind her hands and started to squirm away. Cole tried another elbow. Block. Squirm. They did it once more before Cole realized her prey was slowly escaping. She quit the elbows and focused on getting back in position. Meanwhile, Danni got her defensive cage back in place. Net result? They were right back where they started.

After a little pushing and pulling as they grappled for position, Cole tried the elbows again. Danni patiently defended and worked on her escape. Again Cole was forced to give it up, and they went right back to start.

After another cycle got them nowhere, Yi warned Cheryl that she needed to see some significant action or she was going to step in.

Cole went for broke. Showing her quickness again, she suddenly spun to reposition herself above Danni's head. The north-south position. The same spot Fox had taken before going for that fight-ending choke. No doubt that's what Cole was after, but Danni was having none of it. Cole had to give up a little control to make the move, and Danni squirted free instantly. Like she knew what was coming and had been waiting for it. Cole moved to go after her, but Danni was up on her knees and facing her opponent in the blink of an eye. Seeing how ready her opponent was, Cole wisely backed off and both fighters climbed to their feet.

I breathed a sigh of relief and unclenched my fists without realizing I had balled them up in the first place. Not that my angst wasn't well-founded. Ground fighting is a very intense thing... mostly because of how suddenly it can change. In the stand up game, you make a mistake and your opponent will likely make you pay. Certainly not a good thing - and if you make too many, your night will be over fast - but it would be very unlucky for a single miscue to cost you the fight. On the ground though... the slightest error could lead to all kinds of trouble. Sometimes it results in a sudden submission. More often it lets your opponent catch you in a terrible, unrecoverable position. Then comes the slow yet relentless march toward death as she patiently maneuvers you toward the finish. Either way, you're likely to end up screaming your submission before too long. So, despite the fact that Danni had never been in any real trouble, I knew she had been inches from it the entire time.

Even now - though disaster had been averted - I remained uneasy. Whatever secret Cole had hoped to exploit, Danni had denied her. But was it that simple? Maybe something Cheryl had seen in older footage? A flaw that was no longer a flaw because it was uncovered and corrected through the endless hours of sparring Danni's teammates had put her through?

Possible, but unlikely. If something like that had been discovered, Steph would have been aware of it.

That left a very unappealing alternative. That Danni had just been lucky with her defense, and the flaw remained. Which meant Cole was certain to go after it again.

The good news was that all that grappling had eaten just over three minutes of the round, leaving less than two to go. I really wanted Danni to be able to stop and get Steph's thoughts on the matter. The sooner, the better.

As the action resumed, it quickly became apparent that strangeness was going to be the theme of the fight. I mentioned earlier that the common thread binding all of Savage Wolf's fighters is strategy. Namely, attack, attack, attack!. But now, Cole was hanging back. Pawing here and there, but making no real effort to go after her opponent.

This quickly became a problem for the defensive-minded Danni. Not that she couldn't generate her own offense, but doing so was not really in the game plan tonight. With Cole normally preferring the hell-or-high-water approach, fights between them were a contest of strengths, and the basic question was always the same: could Cole overwhelm Danni with her relentless attacks, or would Harris kill her with defense and counters?
