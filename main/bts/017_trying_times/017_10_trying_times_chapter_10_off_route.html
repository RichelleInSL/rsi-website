-10-Off Route

I never got the chance to test Emma's resolve because it all went wrong too quickly.

It was just after eight-thirty and the place was starting to hum. A steady stream of fighters was trickling in. The sound of fists on the heavy bag and feet thumping off the floor of the practice cages was rising from the training area. Steph was yelling at somebody.

I've heard these sounds every day this past week, but they were different today. Instead of reminding me of my worries, they made me think of what it was going to be like to stand out there and take it all in with Danni at my side again.

Then I saw Trish come in, and I felt even better.

"Hey!" I called out with a little surprise. "I didn't think we'd see you today."

She gave a weary smile as she detoured into my office.

"I'm not staying long," she said. "I couldn't sleep because my rhythm's all out of whack. I wanted to check in with Marcy, anyway. So I figured I'd get it out of the way, then go back to bed."

I thought about asking whether out of whack was a technical term, but Trish was less than twenty-four hours removed from one of the longest non-stop flights in the world. Longer than any human being should ever be subjected to. So, I decided my humor might not be appreciated and chose to respond with a simple nod, instead.

"How's Danni doing?" I asked.

"She needs more time for a full recovery, but her symptoms are still minor," she said. "I guess I overreacted. There was no reason to keep her down there."

"I pay you to keep them safe, Trish," I said, "and I'll never second-guess the way you do it."

She knows I trust her, so the remark was little more than a token nod of confidence. But she smiled in appreciation, anyway.

"She say whether she was coming in today?" I asked.

It was an innocent question, and I didn't think the answer would be any cause for concern. Today, tomorrow... it didn't matter. All I knew was that she was going to be back here soon.

I could not have been more wrong.

The smile disappeared - replaced by shock that quickly dissolved into sadness.

"I thought you knew," she said.

"Knew what?" I asked warily.

"Danni didn't board with me in Los Angeles," she said.

"What are you talking about?"

"She took a different flight," she said. "She said she had something to do. I got the impression you knew about it."

I shook my head absently, clueless as to why Danni would choose to stay in L.A. Visiting friends from her TV days, maybe?

"Did she say what she had to do?"

"No, but..."

Every muscle in my body tightened.

"But what?"

"I think she changed her destination."

"To where?"

"I don't know," she answered. "I only know that she looked like she was heading for one of the other terminals."

My mind swam in a sea of questions.

Why would she change flights?

Where would she go?

Why didn't she TELL me?

I had no answer to the first two. I had no idea where she would go, or why. Worse, from a major airport like LAX, she could literally have flown anywhere in the country. Hell, she could be anywhere in the world.

On the other hand, the answer to the last question was as obvious as it was distressing. She didn't tell me because she didn't want me to know.

"I'm sorry. I should have called," Trish said.

Hell yes, you should have called!

But that was unfair. Danni was free to make her own decisions, and it was not Trish's job to watch her.

I took a deep breath.

"You didn't know," I said. "There was no way you could have."

She still had a guilty look on her face. Probably because I wasn't all that convincing.

"Richelle..."

"Forget it," I said. "Go see Marcy. I'll call Danni and see what's going on."

Reluctantly, she started to move on. Then another thought hit me.

"Hey, what about her recovery?"

Trish shrugged.

"We talked about it, a little. She doesn't really need any sort of treatment. Just time. She knows what to look for and she promised to call if her symptoms change."

"Okay," I said without enthusiasm.

Trish continued on her way. I grabbed the phone and punched in Danni's number. It rolled over to her voicemail almost instantly.

She had the phone turned off.

"Hey, what the hell's going on?" I said after the telltale beep. "Trish told me you changed flights. Where the hell are you, and why didn't you tell me? Give me a call back."

I hung up, then tried to sort through my emotions.

I was angry. What kind of bullshit move was this? How could she just disappear without telling me?

I was worried. Despite Trish's assessment of Danni's symptoms, there was still more recovering to do. She should be doing it here, where Trish and Marcy could monitor her.

But more than anything, I was scared. Because I was suddenly consumed with the fear that she might never come back.

It was an irrational thought. She had a home here. A life. She couldn't just leave and never return.

But suddenly taking off for parts unknown didn't seem like all that rational an act, so...

I lifted the receiver and dialed again. Again, the call went directly to voicemail.

"Look, Danni, I don't know what's going on, but call me, please. I know we both said things after the fight. I know I said things I didn't mean. So, c'mon... we need to talk."

My mind continued to whirl. Twisting the problem every way I could think of. I came up with plenty of credible, common sense explanations. Only, none of them addressed why she hadn't told me.

The longer it went on, the more distressed I became. How the hell did we get to this point? I had awoken this morning thinking we were on the verge of setting things right. Now, we were nowhere. And, until she decided to answer the damn phone, there was nothing I could do about it.

Anger and frustration and worry swirled together and became one emotion. One I could not contain.

On my desk, there is a blue rubber ball. When I'm thinking, I sometimes pick it up and roll it around in my hand.

Beside the ball sits a heavy, solid, marble square with a Mickey Mouse head etched into it.

I'll bet you can see where this is going.

Foregoing the ball, I grabbed the paperweight and hurled it across my office in frustration. It hit the bookcase, striking the third shelf not far from where it met the side panel.

Being both heavy and solid, the paperweight survived the impact.

The shelf did not.

The force of the blow caused the wooden dowel supports to snap, and the shelf collapsed down onto the one below it. Whatever contents didn't immediately fall to the floor slid toward the broken end. The added weight was too much for the shelf below, so that collapsed, too. The bottom shelf held taller books and binders which served as additional supports for its now useless counterparts, and the failure stopped there. But the damage was done. There was a prolonged, calamitous rumble as books, paperwork and decorative bric-a-brac slid and collided and tumbled to the floor. The initial torrent of debris subsided quickly, but additional items continued to fall one at a time for the next few seconds.

The sounds of destruction were enough to garner some attention, and Julianne Hough - who was unlucky enough to be crossing the lobby when I lashed out - stuck her blonde head into my office.

She surveyed the damage, then did a quick survey of me.

"You OK?"

I never looked up at her. I just stared absently at the now ruined shelves and thought about Danni.

"Not really," I muttered.

She gave a quick nod and disappeared. I thought she had simply decided to go while the getting was good, but I was wrong.

I don't know Jules that well. We've chatted plenty over the years, but, for whatever reason, the conversations never dig far beneath the surface. That lack of familiarity did not prevent her from taking action.

She had gone to get help. Help I did not ask for or want, but desperately needed.

I don't know who she told or how word spread that I was going off the deep end. I only know that it seemed like Emma was there in no time, with Tiffany right behind her. Both were wearing compression shorts and loose tank tops over sports bras. They had obviously been intercepted somewhere between the locker room and the training floor.

Emma looked at me for a minute, then at the mess I'd made.

"I like what you've done with the place."

"Not now, Em," I warned.

She took one of the guest chairs, and Tiff sat down beside her.

"What's going on?" Emma asked.

I didn't know whether to be angry at the intrusion or happy to have the help. I settled for morose.

"Danni changed planes in L.A.," I said. "She's not coming back."

"Ever?" Tiff asked.

"Well, as of five minutes ago, I thought she was already back here somewhere. So, I think it's now safe to say I have no idea what she's doing."

Steph arrived and observed the debris field without comment. With her usual spot unavailable, she decided to lean against the wall to the left.

Despite her best efforts, she looked concerned. Concerned for me? Probably. Concerned because she was certain whatever was going on had something to with Danni? Absolutely.

It was an understandable expression, but it looked out of place on Steph. Not to mention it was the third time I'd seen this type of dismayed countenance from her in as many weeks. It was freaking me out.

I told her about Danni changing her flight.

"Have you called her?" she asked.

I nodded sadly.

"The phone's off," I answered. "It goes straight to voicemail."

The group was quiet for a moment. Steph and Tiff just looked sad, but you could actually see the wheels turning in Emma's head.

"She didn't say anything to you?" she asked.

"Not a word."

"And you don't know where she went?"

"Nope," I said. "From L.A., she could be anywhere."

"Can you find out?"

I started to say no when a suprisingly clear thought pierced through my malaise. Danni's original ticket had been booked through RSI's regular travel agent. Had she rebooked through the same agency? It seemed likely.

"Probably," I answered.

"Do it."

It came out as a command. The sort of thing would normally cause me to respond in a less-than-pleasant manner, but I was distracted by the thought that she was on to something.

I made the call and quickly discovered Danni had used the same TA. A stroke of luck but only a small one. Like finding a tiny four-leaf clover in a field of tangled, overgrown weeds.

Not everyone was having such luck, though. As the agent relayed the flight information, I discovered things had not been going very smoothly on the west coast.

I hung up, then turned to share what I'd learned with the group. Emma held up a hand.

"Let me guess," she said. "She went to Louisiana."

I nodded, realizing Danni's choice of destination should have been obvious from the start. It was the one place you can count on to be safe. Where you should always be welcomed with open arms. Loved and supported and protected.

Danni was going home.

It made sense, and it was comforting to know she would be in the arms of people who cared about her. But it did nothing to quell the feeling that she was running away from me.

I snatched the receiver up and dialed her cell, again. Voicemail.

Running away, and acting like a child.

"God dammit, Danni!" I yelled at the sound of her prerecorded voice.

I slammed the receiver back down.

"Maybe she forgot to turn her phone back on after she landed," Steph offered.

I shook my head.

"She's still in L.A. The airline is jerking her around. They put her on the last flight to New Orleans yesterday, and that got held over due to mechanical issues," I said. "She doesn't take off until 9:30, L.A. time."

Steph spent a moment picturing the fun Danni was having, then shrugged.

"Maybe she busted the phone over somebody's head," she said.

We all nodded in agreement.

Danni been stuck on the ground for nearly twelve hours, so far. She'd probably spent at least half that time in the airport. Depending on how the timing had worked out and whether there were any hotel rooms to be had by the time she found out she needed one, it was even possible she'd been at LAX the entire time. And all of that was after the interminable flight from Melbourne.

The idea that she had snapped was a theory we could definitely buy into.

"So, now what?" I asked Emma.

"You go to her."

"Go to her?"

"Yeah," she said. "Get your ass on a plane and go to her."

She said it like it was the most obvious thing in the world.

"Just like that?"

She nodded.

"Just like that."

It sounded crazy. Just get on a plane, fly across the country and show up on Danni's doorstep? She had changed her flight to avoid me. What if showing up uninvited just pissed her off?

Emma must have seen the doubt on my face.

"You want her to know how you feel?" she asked. "You show up wherever she is and she'll know."

"You won't have to say a word," Tiff added.

I held on the blonde for a moment, then brought my eyes back to Emma. Saw nothing but seriousness in their faces.

They certainly seemed sure, and I suddenly had a pretty good idea of why that was.

The relationship between the two had gotten off to a rocky start on a disastrous night in Vancouver a few years ago. Tiffany was there to challenge divisional champion, Nadine Coyle. As her training partner, Emma was there to serve as Tiff's second. Anticipation and excitement were high. Tiff was in top form and in prime position to come away with the belt.

Then, it all went horribly wrong.

The backstory between Tiffany and Nadine isn't one we talk about much now because that shit is ancient history, but back then it was all still fresh and raw. When Tiff first came to us, she was coming off the worst beating she'd ever suffered. One that had come at the hands of Nadine Coyle.

The road back for her was one that was rarely traveled. Coyle had left her a bloody, beaten wreck. So thoroughly destroyed that most fighters would just quit. And, in truth, Tiffany did. When we brought her in to work her out, everything I saw screamed broken fighter. That included when she openly wondered why we'd even bothered to contact her. I was asking myself the same question when Danni stepped in and changed everything.

I had asked her to get in the cage with Tiff and put her through her paces. As expected, the blonde's performance was horrendous. In less than a minute, I knew all I needed to know. But Danni didn't stop. Not even when I practically begged her to.

You wouldn't think the way to fix a broken fighter would be to knock her all around the place, but that's exactly what Danni did. And she wasn't at all nice about it. Then, having reduced Tiffany to a pitiful, sobbing wreck, RSI's team leader sent me away so she could talk to Tiff alone. I admit I had no clue what she was up to, and I was extremely uncomfortable. But I had never gone wrong by putting my faith in Danni, so I left.

I do not now - nor will I ever - know what was said between them that day. I only know that Tiffany recommitted herself and embarked upon a comeback for the ages. She got back on the ladder and focused on what she needed to do to climb it, once again. With Emma and Danni there to back her, it became a true team effort. And it worked beautifully. The road wasn't smooth, but Tiffany was too thoroughly recommitted to get thrown. The bumps and bruises she suffered along the way were little more than fuel for her fire. She is like that to this day, and, as a result, she is also one of the most revered featherweights in the league. But it almost came undone that night in Canada's beautiful City of Glass.

Tiff had already made it back to the top, having taken the belt from Coyle nearly a year earlier. But Nadine stole it back with a narrow victory at their next meeting. No matter. Title or no, Tiff had already proven she could compete with anyone. Win or lose, Coyle would never be able to destroy her that way again.

But then, she did.

What happened that night in far-western Canada was, for a long time, a mystery to me. Tiffany's performance was a disaster. She seemed distracted and unsure. Showing shades of the fighter she'd been on that first day. A fighter I thought was long gone. And Coyle, of course, was only too happy to take advantage of her opponent's lack of focus. She pummeled the blonde from pillar to post, and enjoyed every minute of it. The only accomplishment Tiff could lay claim to was withstanding the beating and forcing the fight to the cards.

One look at her bloody, swollen face showed that to be a counterproductive achievement.

After the fight, Tiff would speak to no one. Not Emma. Not Danni. Not me.

With barely a word, she packed up her things and left the arena. She simply walked out the door, leaving me with no idea if she was ever coming back.

Then Emma found out Tiffany had gone, and she left, too.

It would be days before I saw either of them again, and, when they did return... well, let's just say the events that followed will forever be etched in the minds of those that experienced them. But that's a tale for another day. What's important here is that I eventually discovered the sequence of events that led to Tiff's disastrous performance against Coyle started when she revealed her romantic feelings to Em for the first time just before the fight.

Emma, in turn, flat out blew it.

Though she also had feelings for Tiff, she fumbled her response. Given the suddenness of the way it all went down, it was understandable. If she'd had a little more time to get herself together, she probably could have corrected her mistake. But then the fight happened and a clearly distracted Tiffany got her ass kicked. Emma put the blame squarely on herself and...

I pushed the memories aside as it all started to feel acutely current and familiar.

"That night in Vancouver," I said to Tiffany. "After you left the arena, where did you go?"

"Glasgow," she answered.

I nodded. Tiff had gone home. Just as Danni was doing now.

"You followed her all the way to Scotland?" I asked Emma.

She gave me the coy little smile.

"I called her cell, and she told me where she was going," she said. "When I couldn't talk her out of it, I decided to go after her. I made a wild dash to the airport, but I was too late."

"Didn't stop you, though," Tiff grinned.

"Nothing was going to stop me," Emma replied.

I studied them both again. I saw the way they looked at each other and thought about the way they were together. I never have to do much for these two because they are entirely self-sufficient. Whatever one of them needs, the other provides. Support, love, understanding, competition... two strong, independent women had willingly become dependent on each other, and each of them was better for it. I wondered what it must be like to be joined to someone you could rely on that way, and I thought I might like to have that, too.

Then realized I did  have it, and I was letting it get away.

Again, it was Emma who deciphered my expression.

"I don't know what's eating Danni," she said, "but, trust me... the minute she sees you, you'll know if it can be fixed."

I hesitated some more. Wondering what I would do if I found out it couldn't  be fixed.

"Look, I'm not going to make promises about how it will turn out," Emma said, "but you know I've been watching you two."

Then Steph jumped in and made Emma's point for her.

"We all have," she said.

My eyes went wide in disbelief as I slowly turned my gaze to the normally taciturn - and deliberately uninvolved - McMahon.

"Just because the two of you were too dumb to see it doesn't mean the rest of us were, too."

Well, fuck me.

Hearing it from Steph was like having a bucket of cold water dumped over my head.

Had Danni and I really been that oblivous? Had we been trying too hard to reach for what everyone else saw was right in front of us?

"Richelle, you need to go," Emma said.

Her expression was resolute, and both Tiffany and Steph were nodding steadily.

After a few more seconds, I nodded too. My mind made up, I transitioned into action.

I didn't reach for the desk phone because I wasn't calling the TA to book a flight. Instead, I dug my cell phone out of the desk drawer and called a number from my contacts list.

"Blue Bullet Air," a friendly female voice answered.

"Isa, it's Richelle. I need your help."

The voice was silent for a beat or two. Waiting. Because my reputation is well-known and well-earned.

"No joke or smartass remark," she observed. "You must be serious. What do you need?"

"A plane," I said.

"Hot shit," she said. "I think I got one of those around here somewhere."

Like I need this.

"How fast can you get me to New Orleans?" I asked.

"How fast can you get your ass down here?"

"I'm on my way. Be there in less than an hour."
