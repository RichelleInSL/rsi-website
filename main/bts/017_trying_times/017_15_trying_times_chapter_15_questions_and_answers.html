-15-Questions and Answers

Early progress was slow, but steady. While Danni split her days between her roles as fighter and mentor, I spent mine getting my ass kicked by a Murderers' Row of sparring partners. Jules, Emily VanCamp, Catherine Bell, Daniela Hantuchova, Britney Spears... I even let Emma have a whack at me. Basically, the better they were, the more I wanted to work with them.

I'm not going to lie. It hurt. A lot. And I think they all enjoyed it a little too much. But the pain brought results. For now, Danni and I settled for spending our nightly sessions running through pad drills and heavy bag routines. But, with the help of my cadre of highly-skilled tormentors, I was starting to think we'd be back to our old selves, soon.

Meanwhile, the approach was also helping to reign in the circus around Danni. After the initial wave of concern and support, the fervor around her leveled off but did not go away. As expected, the women of RSI were collectively determined to boost their leader back up. All around her, her teammates were there, offering help in the form of sparring, advice or just an ear to bend. But my strategy had given our elite fighters a way to help without being a nuisance. As a result, life was starting to return to normal - both for Danni and the stable as a whole. Though her plight was still on everyone's mind, her next fight was months away. And there were a million other things we needed to worry about before then.

The first big item on the calendar was the ESL event in - you guessed it - Australia. The good news was that - while Danni and I were lounging on some beach, somehwere - Steph had been hard at work doing what she does best. So our fighters were ready, and it showed in the results.

Emily retained her crown, beating back the legendary Irina Sheik in yet another chapter of their never-ending war. Jules was the model of technical perfection, keeping the Bantamweight title from falling into the grasp of the decorated Keira Knightley in a fight that was more chess match than brawl. Tiz dug deep to pull out a win over Julianne's usual foe, the aforementioned Vikki Blows. And Danni's fellow welterweights were phenomenal, with both Amanda and Anna winning big against tough competition.

Overall, it was a banner night for RSI, which should have made the follow-up press events a breeze.

Should have, but it didn't.

Though I've been known to grab a microphone and speak my mind when it's warranted, I prefer to let Danni and Steph be the public face of the organization. If for no other reason than they're good at it. One or the other is always present when an RSI fighter fields queries after an event. They step in and answer questions that rise to the management level, while also helping to shield our fighters from reporters who get too aggressive or out of line.

The trouble here was that Danni was the one who needed shielding. Her injuries had prevented her from meeting with the media after the loss to Cheryl, so they never had the chance to rake her over the coals. No doubt, they would find a way to sink their teeth into her now, if given the chance.

My solution to the problem was simple. I would step in and share the press duties with Steph, while Danni directed the action in the locker room.

As you might expect, Miss Harris was having none of it. She would have to face the questions eventually, and ducking the press would only make the whole thing a bigger issue than it was. She preferred to just stick her head in the lion's mouth and get it over with.

I'll give the press credit. They tried to be civil about it. Though Danni was right there when Emily was taking her turn, they kept the questions on topic and let the lightweight champion have her moment.

Tiz evidently hasn't earned that same level of respect because it was during her press conference that ESPN reporter Rachel Nichols went off script and turned her questions directly on Danni.

This development was both good and bad. Nichols was known for her no-nonsense style of reporting. She kept her questions focused and treated the athletes with respect. However, she also demanded a similar level of respect in return. She asked straight-forward questions, and she expected straight-forward answers. Trying to dance around the truth would only make her press harder.

Right from the start, something felt odd. It started when Ash didn't protest in an effort to defend Danni. Instead, she politely ceded the microphone and let her teammate field the question.

After making apologies for basically hijacking Tisdale's press conference, Nichols cut right to the chase - simply asking Danni for her thoughts regarding the loss.

"I'm disappointed, obviously. I don't want to take anything away from Cheryl. She clearly worked hard to get ready for that fight, and it showed. There's no doubt she's a great fighter, but I'm usually able to give her a better fight than that. So, overall, I'm not happy with the way I performed."

"What do you feel went wrong?" Nichols asked.

"There's no specific thing," Danni answered. "I just wasn't as sharp as I needed to be."

Danger, Will Robinson! Danger!

Though there was nothing technically wrong with the answer, it was too open-ended. Exactly the sort of response that would cause Nichols to dig deeper.

"Do you feel there was something missing from your training?" the redheaded journalist asked. "Something that distracted you?"

The way she allowed for the possibility of a distraction being the problem caused beads of sweat to pop out of my forehead.

The women of RSI were usually excellent about keeping their mouths shut, but I was getting the distinct impression Nichols knew something she shouldn't have. And that would be a disaster.

Publicly-speaking, the relationship between Danni and I had risen no higher than the level of rumor, so far. We had no illusions about keeping it quiet forever, but we also had no plans to advertise our personal business.

If it came out that we were not only romantically involved, but that I was the distraction that had led to her demise...

"I'm not going to stand up here and make excuses," Danni said directly. "I'm also not going to spend a lot of time talking about this. Cheryl was the better fighter, and now she's the champion. Simple as that. As for me, I know I can be better than I was that night. I think I've shown that over the years, and I expect to show it again."

Her answer couldn't have been better. First, no excuses was textbook Danni, so Nichols would have no reason for suspicion. On top of that, the line about not talking gave the whole group a new direction to pursue. Because, while Danni might not be big on handing out juicy quotes to the media, the new champion had no such reservations. With the belt secured and the paper champion  unceremoniously dispatched, Cole was suddenly willing to offer her keen insights to anyone who asked.

Hands shot up all around the room, with everyone eager for an opportunity to follow-up. It was a chance for Danni to steer away from the demanding Nichols, but I got that odd feeling again when she elected to go right back to Rachel.

"Do you have any response to the comments Cheryl made after the fight?"

"I'm not sure what comments you're talking about."

That was a little white lie. Danni was well aware of the things Cole had been saying, mostly because her teammates have been losing their minds over it. But feigning ignorance was her subtle way of showing the disinterest she genuinely felt.

Always well-prepared, Rachel paged through her notes to find the exact quote she wanted.

"When asked to comment on your long reign as champion, she said, 'Danni's a great person, and she's very deserving of the praise she gets for the kind of champion she was. On top of that, she does a fantastic job representing her stable. She's a genuine leader, and she gives those RSI girls someone to look up to. But sometimes, I think people get confused. They like her, and they want her to win. But nice doesn't win championships. Don't get me wrong... she's a good fighter. Better than average, for sure. But you saw the way she folded up tonight. She barely even fought back. I'm sure she wanted to, but she just couldn't. That's because she's good, but not great. Once we ended up in the same division, I knew it was only a matter of time before I took the title from her. Now the belt is where it belongs.'"

For the record, while Danni may not be very interested in Cheryl's comments, I can't deny that I've been spending a fair amount of time dreaming of ways I could punch the bitch in the mouth without anyone knowing. I haven't come up with anything yet, but I haven't given up on the idea, either.

As Nichols finished, the room turned its collective attention to Danni. I was watching closely, too. The others were hoping she would lash out at Cole and say something controversial or incendiary. I was wondering if she might shy away and offer no reaction, at all.

Such a non-response would be understandable, given what she'd been through. But it would also become a story, all it's own. It would lead to questions about her state of mind and whether she really had what it would take to mount a comeback. The press would openly muse about whether or not her heart was still in it.

"I'm not sure what you want me to say," she answered. "I can't deny what she said about my performance. The rest..."

She stopped, then shrugged again.

"So you have no response?"

Danni kept her eyes riveted on Nichols as though they were the only two in the room. Then she leaned out slowly toward the microphone.

"It's like I said. I'm not going to spend a lot of time talking."

It was an answer that was so completely and beautifully Danni, I actually pumped my fist in response.

The words she'd said were totally inane and forgettable, but the message in her tone was unmistakable. There was a dark, hard edge to her voice that carried with it the implication that her response to Cole would be physical, not oral.

I smiled as another brick went back into the wall of her confidence. What she'd just done was more of a challenge to herself than anyone else. Everyone in the room had heard those words she didn't say. Anyone who saw the video later would hear them, too.

She was putting the burden on herself to back them up.

She had to be feeling pretty good to do something like that, and I suspected I knew where it was coming from. Her teammates might be annoying, but they were also effective. The treatment was working. Danni was already starting to believe in herself again.

The answer seemed to satisfy Nichols. A few other reporters tried to follow-up, but Danni quickly made it clear that she meant what she'd said. She was done talking.

The group broke up, and Danni and Tiz were chatting back and forth as they made their way out the side door. I wanted to go after them, but I also needed to make my way over to the next room for Julianne's question and answer session. Danni had other things to get to as well, so I was forced to let her go.

When I finally caught up with her later, I found her in the hall outside the RSI locker room - chatting with none other than Rachel Nichols.

I quickened my step when I saw them. Nichols might be one of the most professional of the group, but a reporter is still a reporter. I worried that she might be pressuring Danni in some way, but it quickly became clear that the conversation was cordial.

Danni caught sight of me first, and I shot her a look that asked, what the hell is going on?

I wiped it away as Nichols turned to follow Danni's gaze.

"You badgering my fighter?" I asked playfully.

"I'm trying," Rachel quipped, "but I can't get her to crack."

"Yeah, it's tough to get past Danni's defenses."

"That's not what I hear," she said teasingly.

Excuse me?

The sweat on my brow reappeared, but Nichols quickly moved on.

"Quite a night for you guys," she said.

I did a quick internal sigh of relief, then pulled myself together.

"Well, when you have fighters like Emily and Jules leading the charge, there's a good chance things are gonna go your way."

"Not to mention a world-class stable leader running the show," Danni offered.

"No credit for me?" I asked.

She shrugged.

"You pay the bills. That's something."

Excuse me... again?

"I... pay...? That's all you've got to say? At the very least, I should get credit for hiring you."

"Even a broken clock..."

"Alright," I cut in. "Let's not fight in front of the reporter."

Rachel wore a bemused grin.

"You two are gonna make some couple," she said.

I was instantly sweating again. Nichols obviously knew something, but still made no effort to press for details.

"Well, I've got a flight to catch," she said. "Congrats again on the big night."

"Thanks," I managed as the newest ripple of panic subsided.

"Thanks, Rach," Danni said. "See you in Brisbane."

Brisbane was the next stop on the league's extended - and hellacious - tour of the Land Down Under.

"Rach?" I said to Danni once the reporter was out of earshot. "Since when are you so chummy with the media?"

"Not sure what you mean," she answered. "Rachel and I have always had a good relationship."

"Uh, huh," I said suspiciously.

I started to piece together the evening's events. The way Tiz had so willingly turned her press conference over to her stable leader. The way Danni had gone back to Rachel when she'd had the opportunity to move on to someone less demanding.

"Why do I suddenly get the feeling that your earlier exchange with Ms. Nichols was some sort of setup?"

"Oh, I wouldn't call it a setup," she countered. "More of a controlled conversation."

"I see. And how, exactly, did you control it?"

"Rachel and I were talking before the event," she said. "We both knew I was going to have to face the firing squad, and I told her all I wanted was to keep the questions on the level. We agreed that she would do that, and in return, I would be straight with her. But there were no limitations on what she could ask."

It sounded like an easy deal for Danni to agree to since straight  was the only way I'd ever seen her deal with the press.

So, maybe it hadn't really been a setup. But, still...

"You knew what was going to happen," I said. "So did Tiz."

"Like I said, it was controlled, but not scripted."

I believed her. Trying to play the media was not her style. It also wasn't something a reporter like Rachel would go for.

But speaking of Ms. Nichols...

"She knows about us," I said.

"Everybody knows about us," she replied quickly. "I think the rumors started before the actual relationship."

"No, I mean she knows. You confirmed it for her."

"I did," she admitted. "She asked, and... I was trying to get her to trust me. Lying to her face didn't seem like the way to do that. And like I said, everybody knows anyway. She'd have know if I was bullshitting her."

"But she didn't reveal it."

"I asked her not to."

"Mmm," I said, "and what did she ask for, in return for her secrecy?"

"Nothing. But I got the impression some future consideration might be welcome."

I kept my expression flat as I considered the situation. Truth is, if I was going to be indebted to a reporter, Nichols would be my choice.

Meanwhile, Danni's expression told me something else was coming.

"I also got the feeling that, if we decided to take things public, she might want to be the one to bring the story to light."

I couldn't stop the look of annoyance that crossed my face. I had visions of the three of us sitting down for an informal interview with some sort of cutesy angle about the way our love had flowered in the shadow of the cage, or some shit like that.

Nichols might be a straight-shooter, but she wasn't above spreading a little schmaltz around to score some publicity.

Danni mistook the look for anger and got defensive.

"Look, I'm sorry," she said. "I should have said something to you. But it's not something I really planned. I wanted to get this over with. I wanted someone to ask me some tough-but-fair questions, and there was Rachel and I knew she would do it without a lot of bullshit attached. So, I took a shot. She tested me with the thing about us, and it didn't seem like a big deal. It's not like we're trying to hide. But I shouldn't have done it without talking to you."

"It was a smart play," I said.

She recoiled a little in surprise.

"This would have become a distraction if you let it fester," I continued. "It also would have been a problem if you and Cheryl got into a war of words. Hell, the whole stable probably would have gotten into it. But you shut it down in the most elegant way I can think of."

She cocked her sideways and asked, "Really?"

"Seeing what's coming. Thinking on your feet. Protecting the stable. That's what I count on you for. If getting it done means extending some courtesies to the likes of Rachel Nichols down the road... well, there are worse things."

"And the thing about us?"

I shrugged.

"If everyone knows, then they know. If not... well, they will eventually. It was a pretty small sacrifice."

A small smile tugged at the corners of her mouth.

"Thanks."

I waved her off. There was nothing to thank me for. I should be thanking her for doing her job and doing it well.

"But don't think I'm going on one of those weekend afternoon filler shows for a chat about our personal lives."

Danni winced.

"I'm pretty sure that's exactly what Rachel's gonna want."

I grumbled in disapproval for a few seconds, but eventually relented.

"Fine," I said, "but I'm not wearing a dress."

She shrugged and said, "You can wear your pajamas, for all I care."

I paused a second while I imagined that scenario.

"Yeah, I'm not doing that, either."

"Good," she said. "Cause I've seen your pajamas, and I don't know how well Tinker Bell is going to play on national television."

"What's wrong with Tinker Bell?"

"It's kind of... juvenile."

"You watch your mouth."
