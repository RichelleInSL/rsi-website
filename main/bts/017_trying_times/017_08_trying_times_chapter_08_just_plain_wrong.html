-8-Just Plain Wrong

Rod Laver Arena, Melbourne, Australia

I sat on a bench at the end of a hallway that connects the rooms used by the training staff. The rooms where the fighters are wrapped and readied for their upcoming fights. The rooms to which they later return to be examined when the fight is over... the length of the exam determined by how well things had gone in the cage.

Danni was with Trish, Marcy and the medical team in the third room on the right. And they had been in there for a very long time.

I sat perfectly still, leaning back against the row of lockers behind me as I studied the ceiling and waited for that door to open. My stillness was not owed to some Zen-like inner calm that I had achieved. It was the result of a concerted effort to will myself to stop fidgeting like a worried parent waiting outside the doors of the pediatric surgical wing.

Unsurprisingly, my emotions were all over the place, but I was doing a reasonable job of walling them off. I assured myself that, no matter the damage, Danni would be okay. Everything else could be sorted out later.

Behind me, the locker room was quiet. Far too quiet, given the circumstances. Despite Danni's disastrous outcome, RSI had much to celebrate. Not the least of which was a dominating performance by Britney Spears, who finally... finally... tore the middleweight belt loose from Laura Vandervoort's iron grip.

But that was the thing. There was no despite. Danni's loss overshadowed everything. The women of RSI had been forced to watch their leader destroyed. Not only that, she had looked lost and nearly defenseless as it was happening. A shadow of the champion they knew her to be. It was something they had never seen before, and they weren't sure how they were supposed to deal with it.

As my thirtieth minute of waiting neared, Steph stepped around the corner to my left and sat down next to me. Neither of us said anything, at first. She didn't ask what I knew about Danni's condition - probably because she could already tell I knew nothing. So, we just sat there, each of us wrestling with our own thoughts.

When she did speak, her tone was noticeably subdued.

"About before," she began. "I shouldn't have jumped on you that way. I know this was hard on you."

"Don't worry about it," I said. "She's your friend, too. You were going through the same thing I was. And let's face it. You handled it better than I did."

"Not really."

"You were able to do the job," I said. "You were there for her. That's more than I managed to do."

She left that one alone, and we went back to our separate reflections. I kept trying to smooth out my emotions. To remind myself that it was over. It had been as bad as we'd expected - maybe even worse - but it was behind us, now. We would deal with it, and we would make sure it never happened again.

Ever.

I wasn't sure if my efforts were working or if I was just deluding myself, but I seemed to be holding it together, so far. But, no matter how hard I tried, there was one thought I could not let go of.

"Did we fuck up, Steph?" I asked. "Should we have stopped her?"

She didn't answer right away. Instead, she stared at the floor for a long while. Thinking.

"Probably," she concluded. "I still don't think she would have been willing to do anything we suggested, but we probably should have tried."

I didn't reply because there was nothing to say. I had known the answer before I asked the question. Yes, Danni would have resisted. In fact, she would have flatly refused. But, given the outcome, not saying something directly to her felt like a huge mistake.

Hindsight is like that. So damned perfect all the time.

More thoughts floated through my head. Bad thoughts that reminded me that the real problem ran much deeper than anything we had or hadn't done in the last two weeks.

That night when Danni kissed me, it had all seemed so innocent and easy. Why not give it a try and see what came of it? What was the worst that could happen?

Now, I realized there were possible outcomes I'd never considered. Outcomes that, at this moment, did not seem worth the risk.

"We can fix this," Steph said.

"I know," I said. "Shouldn't have happened, though."

"It won't happen, again."

"No," I said. "It definitely won't."

Something about the way I said it put a touch of concern into her expression.

Before she had the chance to voice that concern, the door opened.

As the crowd filed out, Trish and Marcy headed straight for us - both looking particularly grim. I took a deep breath and steeled myself for whatever news they had to share.

The last person out turned off the lights, leaving Danni alone and in the dark. I was too busy worrying to notice... or to realize what it meant.

"How is she?" I asked.

"She's banged up pretty bad," Trish said.

"What does that mean?"

"Her visible injuries aren't as bad as they looked," Marcy offered. "She'll be sore for a while, but she'll heal up fine."

I think she was trying to take the sting out of things, but it didn't work. Most of what she said sailed right past me because two words had hit me like a freight train.

"Visible injuries?" I asked.

The two shared a quick glance, then Trish dropped the bad news.

"I don't like the results of her ImPACT test."

I closed my eyes and slowly shook my head.

ImPACT - formally known as Immediate Post-Concussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing - is the tool used to assess the presence and severity of a concussion. It's a field test of sorts. A way to assess the situation in the immediate aftermath of a fight.

It was no surprise Danni had suffered a concussion. Though they are not the same thing, concussions and knockouts pretty much go hand-in-hand. But for Trish to bring it up this way meant she was more concerned than usual.

When I spoke again, my tone was matter-of-fact. My cadence unnaturally steady and even. The way you speak when you're fighting to stay calm, but barely hanging on. And every word you hear shoves you closer to the edge.

"How bad are we talking?" I asked.

"We won't know for a while," she said. "We'll need to keep an eye on her for a couple days, at least."

"Give me an estimate."

"It doesn't work like that."

"Trish..."

"What?" she said with some annoyance. "ImPACT is quick and informative, but also highly imperfect. It gives us a place to start, but that's all. There's no way to guess how things will turn out. We won't know until we know."

"Worst case scenario, then."

She glared angrily at me. She usually does a pretty good job of putting up with me - and putting me in my place - but she was truly pissed now. A reaction I would later recognize as her own emotions coming through. Danni was the center of everything at RSI. She was everyone's friend, and Trish was no exception. Hard to play the dispassionate clinician when you're talking about someone close to you.

"You want worst case?" she snapped. "Fine. Worst case, she's done. She'll never fight again."

I closed my eyes again. The image of a battered Danni lying motionless on the canvas was now made worse by the knowledge that she had suffered a devastating - and career threatening - injury. Suddenly, I was confronted by the possibility that she might have lost much more than just a fight. All the effort I'd put into convincing myself that the worst part of this was over had been wasted because now there was a real chance that wasn't true.

I looked at Steph and saw on her face the same hollow feeling that currently occupied my stomach.

"Can I finish now?" Trish asked.

I nodded. She was calmer - the tiny outburst having made just enough room for her to pack her emotions back down.

"Concussions are tricky," she said. "ImPACT is not meant to provide a definitive diagnosis. The only thing it tells us for sure is that we need to take a closer look. But concussions are still very much an amorphous thing, and there is no one-size-fits-all approach to diagnosing or dealing with them. So, this could end up being a disaster, or it could be nothing. False positives are even a possibility."

"False positives?"

"The test is meant to evaluate for symptoms of a concussion," she said. "It's a cognitive test. We compared her results to her latest baseline screening, and we don't like the results. Now, obviously, she's not at her best, right now. So, it's hard to tell exactly what's going on or how bad it is. She's suffering some cognitive impairment. Is she just out of it, or is it something serious? We don't know, yet."

"So, you're saying maybe it's not a concussion?"

"I'm saying we don't know," she answered. "We need to do more thorough testing, both physical and cognitive. More importantly, we need to keep testing for a few days to see how things change."

I watched her face for signs that she was just trying to give me hope that things weren't as bad as they seemed. She seemed sincere. I checked Marcy, and saw no reservations there, either.

"If you don't know, then why are you so concerned?" I asked.

"You saw the same fight I did, right?" she answered. "I think a little extra caution is warranted."

I swallowed that little dose of reality without further comment.

"So, what do we do?" I asked.

"Emotionally? We calm the hell down," Trish said. "There's no point in assuming the worst."

I nodded again. Trying to get on board with the positive thinking.

"And medically?"

"We treat it like a disaster," she said, "and hope it's not."

I took another deep breath, had a little moment of relapse, but fought it off and redoubled my efforts to go positive.

"That's fine," I said. "We'll get her back home, and you'll do whatever you need to do. And you can look forward to me pestering you constantly for updates."

It wasn't the world's funniest line, and, to be honest, my delivery was a bit stiffer than usual. But, when neither medical professional so much as cracked a smile, I realized the bad news wasn't over.

"There's something else," I prompted.

"The treatment protocol for concussions varies, but the basics are to keep the patient relaxed," Trish said. "Dim the lights. Keep noise to a minimum... and, in severe cases, restrictions on flight."

I took a moment to let the implication sink in. None of what she'd said had come as a shock. MMA is a violent sport, and Danni was far from the first RSI athlete to suffer a concussion. So, I knew the drill. But the ban on flying was a step Trish only took on rare occasion.

In severe cases.

"She can't come home," I said.

"Not yet."

"You really think it's that bad?"

"I already told you, I don't know. But again, you saw the fight, right? I think there's good reason to play it safe."

I didn't answer because I didn't have to. Yes, I had seen the fight, and I wasn't going to get those images out of my mind anytime soon.

"Any idea how long?" I asked.

"At least a few days. After that, we'll see."

"So, anywhere from a few days to forever?"

She shot me a stern look, but managed to keep her voice even.

"It'll take as long as the testing dictates," she said. "You're just gonna have to just trust me and let me do my job."

I nodded. The statement was meant as a reminder, not an instruction. I trust Trish the way I trust Danni and Steph. Implicitly. No matter how badly Danni was injured, Trish was the one I wanted there to get her through it.

I raised both hands and pressed my palms into my forehead.

What a night this had been for Danni. She'd lost her title and suffered a full-on beating in the process. Had her brains scrambled who knows how badly. And now she had days of testing to look forward to while sitting in the silent darkness ten thousand miles from home.

Fuck positive. This blows.

"AAAAAAAHHH!!!" I vented. "This SUCKS!"

"I know," was all Trish could say.

I let my right hand drop, then turned to look at Steph as I rubbed at my neck with my left.

"I'm gonna stay, too," I said.

"No problem. We can handle things back home."

I nodded my thanks, then turned back to Trish.

"Can I see her?"

"Sure."

As I went to stand, Steph put a hand on my arm. That made twice she'd touched me in the same night. This was obviously a very different gesture from earlier, but still... weird.

"I know you're upset," she said. "Just... be careful in there."

In other words, stay calm. Don't let my emotions overwhelm me. Don't say anything stupid.

All things I'm so naturally good at.

* * *

I had to pause as I stepped into the darkness of the room. The only active light source was a small lamp near the far wall. Enough to see by, but not without a period of adjustment.

Eventually, I was able to pick out the shadowy outline of Danni, lying prone on the trainer's table.

Her eyes were closed, and she didn't react as I stepped up beside her. Slowly, her features came into focus, and what I saw broke my heart. Swelling and bruising still distorted her face, and the gauze and bandages Marcy had used to patch her wounds together only seemed to magnify the damage Cole had done.

I swallowed hard and fought the urge to reach out and caress her face.

God, Danni...

I pushed those thoughts away, straightened up and pulled myself together. She needed better from me, and she was going to get it.

"Nap time?" I joked softly.

Her eyes popped open.

"I'm sorry," were the first words out of her mouth.

She tried to push herself upright, went too fast and lost her balance halfway up.

"No, no," I said as I tried to ease her back down.

But she refused. Once she got her bearings, she sat all the way up, then needed another moment to steady herself.

"I'm sorry," she said again.

"You don't owe any apologies, to me or anyone else," I said. "It wasn't your night. We forget it and move on."

"No," she said abruptly.

Her tone was sharp and direct. A one word declaration to remind me that she wasn't stupid. Reality had hit her just as hard as Cole's fists in that cage. She knew what happened was more than luck gone awry.

"I was bad," she admitted. "I wasn't... I don't know, I just... wasn't good enough."

"That's not true."

"Yes it is," she said. "She beat my ass, and there wasn't a damn thing I could do about it. I just wasn't right. And I don't know why."

The reaction I needed to give was anger and disbelief. To strenuously protest the way she was blaming herself. To remind her of how good she was. To tell her we would not rest until she had that belt around her waist again.

It should have been easy because I believed those things, but I was slowed by other thoughts. I did  know why she hadn't been right, and I was too busy tearing myself to pieces over my part in it. Silently screaming at myself for failing her.

Clearly, I wasn't as pulled together as I thought I was.

My slowness cost me, as Danni read the silence perfectly.

"You knew," she said.

I raised my eyes to hers - certain the truth was written all over my face.

"You knew I didn't have it," she said.

I nodded solemly because there was no point in denying anything.

"I knew you weren't as sharp as usual," I said. "I hoped you might find a way, but, yeah... I had a real bad feeling."

"When did you know?"

"Steph picked up on it. She told me about it a couple of weeks ago."

"Steph knew, too?"

"Of course. That's her job. Mandy, Cobie, Anna... they all knew, too. They know you. They know what you're capable of, and they knew something was off."

"Why didn't somebody say something?"

"Like what?" I asked. "Hey Danni, you're gonna get your ass kicked in two weeks. Have a nice day."

"So you just let it happen?"

"Well, let me ask you this. If we had said something, what would you have done?"

"I could have worked harder."

"For two weeks? After a training program designed to work over almost half a year, you were going to get it all together in just two weeks?"

She didn't say anything.

"The only workable solution was to try to delay the fight," I continued. "Would you have even listened if we'd suggested that?"

Again, she let the silence stand as her answer.

"The only option left was to try and push you," I said. "That was mostly Mandy and Steph. They tried to turn it up. Trying to get you to respond. And you did, a little bit. But it was too little, too late."

She stared off into space for a while, and I studied her while she did it. I looked past the bruises and the bandages and saw the real pain in her face. I found myself wondering exactly when she'd figured it out. At what point in the beating did she realize she was overmatched? And how long did she have to go on with that knowledge in her head?

I've been through moments like that. When you've tried everything you can think of to find a way, and you realize that you can't deny it anymore. When you comprehend that you're opponent is better than you, but you have to face the torment of fighting on, anyway.

I wouldn't wish that feeling on my worst enemy.

"If I was so off, then Steph must have noticed something earlier," she said. "Why did she wait so long to say anything?"

"She's never dealt with anything like this before," I said. "I don't think she really knew the best way to go."

"What are you talking about? She deals with this stuff all the time."

I didn't say anything.

"You mean she's never had to deal with it from me."

I shrugged.

"Everyone believed you would pull it together because you always do," I said. "When that didn't happen, no one knew how to handle it, and it was too late, anyway."

She thought about that for a little while.

"Steph would be the first to admit she fucked this up, and I'd be right behind her in line," I said. "I know she thinks she should have been more forward with you and gotten on your case earlier. She's probably right. As for me, I think... well, I think a lot of things, but mostly I think we should have been more direct with you these past couple of weeks. Steph and I made that decision together, but it was my call in the end. At the time, we worried about putting doubts in your head. Did we do the right thing? I thought so before. Now, I think we made a mistake."

"I'm the one who fucked up," she said.

You didn't do it alone, I thought.

"There's no point in torturing yourself with this now," I offered.

It was an idiotic thing to say. I knew how this worked. I knew the worst moments from the fight were already stitching themselves together to make a short, disjointed horror film that would play through her mind in an endless loop for days to come. But I said it for the same reason people say shit like, it is what it is. Because I'm sitting here and I'm trying to help her through this while feeling angry and guilty about my own role in it. I felt I needed to say something, and that was the best I could come up with.

"How did I not realize?" she asked.

"You were going through the motions. Doing what you always do to get ready. You were just distracted, so it wasn't as effective."

"Distracted?"

"Yeah, distracted," I said roughly.

Woops.

In an instant, I had done exactly what I set out not to do. This was not the time for us to discuss the role our relationship had played in this mess, and I should have kept my mouth shut. But I couldn't help it. I understood that she probably didn't realize how the King Powers affair was affecting her, but hadn't she noticed everything else that was going on? Was she saying she wasn't aware of the way things between us had been falling apart? Or was she trying to tell me she didn't think that was the problem?

Remembering Steph's warning, I quickly got hold of myself and tamped my anger back down. But it was too late. Danni's face hardened, and I knew she'd picked up on my tone.

With Plan A out the window, I quickly formed a Plan B and tried to cut the conversation short before we got into a full-blown fight.

"I know it's hard," I said, "but you need to let this go for a little while."

"Yeah, like that's gonna happen."

"I know, but going through it now isn't going to help. Better to process it later. Back at RSI. Where you can think about what went wrong and do something about making it right."

She waved me off without even hearing me.

"I want to talk about it now," she said.

I have zero medical training and at least twice as much incidental knowledge, but, the longer the conversation went on the more I saw what Trish was talking about. Danni looked off, but there was no way to tell what was emotional and what was physical. Her thoughts seemed clear, and, in the traditional fashion, she was arguing with me at every turn. But she also wasn't right. Her expression had been distant, and her eyes wandered all around the room. Like she couldn't get them to stay focused on any one particular thing.

But she suddenly found her focus now as those brown eyes zeroed in on me.

So much for Plan B. She obviously wasn't going to let this go.

"Look, we both know what the problem was," I said, taking another shot at deflecting the conversation. "I don't think this is the time or place to talk about it, though."

"Why not?" she asked. Then she gestured down over her damaged body and said, "It looks like I'm going to have a lot of free time coming to me."

"More than you think," I muttered.

"What does that  mean?"

Call it lucky stupidity. Though I hadn't meant to bring up her test results so callously, I quickly realized the mistake might work in my favor. I mean, she was going to find out, anyway. And broaching the subject now might let me switch the topic, altogether.

As Plan B went down in flames, Plan C had risen from its ashes.

"Trish didn't like what she saw when she did your ImPACT."

She groaned audibly.

"How bad?"

"She said she doesn't know. That the test doesn't indicate severity," I said. "It's bad enough that she's concerned, though."

She just shook her head.

"It gets worse," I said.

"Of course it does."

"Trish doesn't think you should fly."

The news landed with visible effect. Her head came up and she rocked backward. Like she'd been nudged off-balance by some unseen physical force.

"How long?"

"She doesn't know," I said, "but you can look forward to at least a few days of quiet reflection. It sounds like you'll be alternating between bouts of rest and testing."

"Fantastic."

"Trish and I are both going to stay," I said.

"You don't have to do that."

"I want to."

"You have things to do."

I didn't know why she was arguing with me, and I didn't really care. I raised a hand to put a stop to it.

"It's all taken care of," I said dismissively. "Don't even worry about it."

It was hard to tell, but I thought she gave a tiny nod. Like she'd heard what I said but was already thinking about something else. Probably relishing the joy that would be her next few days.

I sorted through my thoughts, trying to come up with something to say that wouldn't lead to trouble. Something that would navigate the conversation safely away from darker waters.

Once again, I took too long, and Plan C became a casualty of my slowness.

"What did you mean before when you said I was distracted?" Danni asked.

I sighed and made a silent vow to stop making plans. They never work out.

"Danni..."

"Just answer the question."

There are moments when I feel my patience leave me. When the gears in my head seem to grind together, and the angry, grating sound that results seems to come straight out of my mouth.

This was my fault. I never should have hinted at why I thought Danni had been unprepared for her fight. I should have waited for a more appropriate time. But I said what I said and - despite my efforts to put the cork back in the bottle - she wouldn't let it go. And it was pretty obvious she was spoiling for a fight. So, fuck it.

"I meant that you were distracted," I snapped. "Are you trying to tell me you haven't noticed what's going on with us?"

"Yeah, I've noticed," she shot back, "but what does that have to do with anything?"

I spread by hands out in disbelief.

"Are you fucking kidding me?" I asked. "You drop the ball on your training for the first time EVER  at the same time you and I are dancing around each other with this relationship stuff, and you don't think it's related?"

Drop the ball.

She'd just suffered the worst loss I'd ever seen her take. She was sitting here on the trainer's table with blood from her injuries still oozing into the bandages that covered her face. And I flat out told her she'd failed.

If this RSI thing doesn't work out, I think maybe I have a future as a high school guidance counselor. Imagine how many dreams I could crush in just one day on the job.

"It's my job to be ready to fight," she said. "It doesn't matter what else is going on."

"And it's my job to help you with that. To make sure you have what you need. At the very least, I need to stay out of your way."

"Do you even hear yourself?" she asked. "How did this become about you?"

"It's not about me."

"Really? Then why do you automatically assume you were the cause of my distraction?"

"I don't. I mean, I do... but it's not automatic. It's just the truth."

"So, do you think the relationship was a mistake?"

"We really shouldn't be having this conversation, right now."

"Why not? It's a simple question. Do you think it was a mistake?"

"Right now?" I asked angrily. "Yeah... I do!"

At that point, I was thinking I probably should have just stayed out in the hall because I wasn't doing any good here.

"Great," she said. "That's great. Guess it was a total waste of time, then."

"I didn't say that," I shouted, "but, since you brought it up, how do you think things are going?"

"Not that well."

"There's an understatement. Truth is, they're not going at all."

"So, what? We just give up?"

"No, I..."

I stopped short. Unsure of what to say.

"You what?" she snapped.

"I didn't feel this way before."

Once again, wrong answer. Despite the swelling and bandages, I could see the disappointment on her face.

"Before what?"

I wanted to stop. To rewind about ten seconds and take back what I'd said. But there was nowhere to go but forward.

"Before I knew what the consequences would be."

If she'd been disappointed before, then I was going to need to invent a new word for the expression she was wearing now.

"I can't believe you said that."

I felt trapped by my own big mouth.

"Danni... I already told you, this is the wrong time for this."

Wrong time. Wrong place. And I was giving all the wrong answers.

"No, it's the perfect time," she said. "We should just lay it all out there before we figure out how to keep lying to each other."

"That's not fair!" I shouted.

I didn't understand why she was pushing me this way, but, if pissing me off was her goal, it was working. I was starting to worry less and less about measuring my words.

Either she didn't notice or she didn't care because she only pushed harder.

"You want to know what I think?" she asked.

"Not really."

"I think you love to blame yourself for things," she said. "You almost have to because it means you can fix whatever's wrong. You need to be able to think that."

"Holy shit," I said. "I forgot you're a fuckin' psychologist! Please... lay some more psychobabble bullshit on me."

"It's not bullshit. It's the way you are. You think you can fix anything. No problem too big. Not when you're around!"

We were getting pretty loud, but neither of us seemed too concerned about it.

"What the fuck is that supposed to mean?" I asked.

"You constantly take responsibility for things that you have nothing to do with. You see your hand in everything! Any time something goes wrong, you figure out a way to make it your fault!"

"You don't know what the hell you're talking about! I didn't say it was my fault. But what do you mean, things I have nothing to do with? Are you nuts? Maybe you forgot, but there are two of us involved in this relationship."

"But only ONE of us was in that cage!" she yelled. "ME! I'm the one who wasn't ready! Everyone else did their part, and I didn't!"

"Yeah, but why was that?!"

"Who gives a shit, why? It was still my responsibility, not yours!"

"How can you say..."

"HEY!" a third voice interrupted.

I turned to find an angry-looking Trish standing in the doorway.

"You two need to knock it off!" she snarled. "This is not exactly part of the treatment protocol!"

Behind her I could see Steph, giving me a stern yet understanding look. Some what the fuck did I say to you before? mixed with a little I knew you'd never be able to do it.

What can I say? I am who I am.

I looked back to Danni, both of us still seething, but also embarrassed by the way we'd lost it. We came to a silent agreement to do what Trish was asking.

"Sorry," I said to Trish, with my eyes still on Danni. "Just give us another minute."

"Are you sure you can..."

I cut my eyes to her.

"Trish..."

I have an awesome angry glare. I've used it countless times to wordlessly put fear into people, and it usually works instantly.

On Trish, it doesn't work at all. It never has.

Maybe I should spend some time practicing it in the mirror.

Instead of wilting, Trish stood taller.

"Behave yourselves," she warned, "or I will make you sorry."

And then she was gone. Steph shot me another look, then followed behind her.

I brought my eyes back to Danni.

"That woman is scary," I said.

She didn't react. Not a word. Not a smile. Nothing.

"Look, I'm sorry," I said. "We're both a little messed up right now, and that got outta hand."

"It's alright," she said, though her expression said it was not.

"We just need to give it time," I said. "Let things cool down. Get you right. Then we can think things through, and figure this out."

"Sure," she said.

It was an agreeable response, but the look didn't change. Which made me think she wasn't feeling very agreeable, at all.

"Look, some of what I said," I began. "I didn't mean..."

"You should go back with the others," she said.

It was just a statement. No anger in it. Flat and almost emotionless. But, it hit me hard.

"I'm not going anywhere," I protested. "I want to be here."

"You can't do anything here," she said, "and you need to be back there. You need to put the article together."

"I can write anywhere," I said.

"You're gonna have a lot of press surrounding Britney's title."

"The press can wait. Or I can give interviews from here."

"And what will that look like?" she asked. "Are you going to tell them you decided to take a vacation and hang out in Australia for a while? Maybe take in the sights? How hard will it be for them to figure out that you're waiting around for me? The one who got her ass kicked all over the fucking cage. The one who got fucked up so badly she couldn't even fly home."

The harsh self-assessment caught me flat-footed. It also made me feel like an idiot for the way I had overlooked the obvious.

How could I be so stupid?

How could I have let the conversation go this way? How is it we talked about the damage she'd suffered and the treatment process and had a full-blown argument about how we got here in the first place... but we never brought the focus around to what the fight had really done to her? I had thought about it. It had been weighing on me the whole time. But we never talked about how it had to be weighing on her.

"Danni, I..."

"There's no point in you staying here," she said. "Trish will do her thing, and that's what I need, right now. There's nothing you can do."

My throat went dry as I realized what was happening. The wasn't about the press or my responsibilities. She was telling me to go away.

It was upsetting and surprising, but, more than anything, it hurt like hell.

"I don't want to leave things this way," I said hoarsely.

"You said before we need time to think things through."

"This isn't what I meant."

"Well, it's what I need," she said.

I didn't know what to do. Every ounce of me wanted to stay here, even if I was totally useless. But I was being more than useless. I was making things worse.

"I don't think this is the right thing to do."

"I do," she said. "We're just yelling at each other, which isn't helping anything."

I took that as an acknowledgement from her that this conversation had been a waste. Just the two of us venting, and nothing more. A small glimmer of hope that we could forget it ever happened.

I didn't know if it was real, but it was all I had.

"Yeah," I said, "we'll give it some time."

"Yeah," she said.

I thought I heard a little optimism in her voice, too. Or maybe she'd realized how harsh her tone had been and she was just trying to go a little easier on me.

Despite the tiny ray of light, I still felt like shit. But I had already done enough damage. Damage I would not be able to undo for a while.

"I'll call," I said hopefully, "and I'll be touching base with Trish about a hundred times a day."

"Yeah," she said again.

I stepped slowly toward the door. Not wanting to go, but knowing I didn't have much of a choice.

I turned back to her.

"We can fix this," I said.

She nodded, but didn't say anything.

And then I left.
