-18-The Shadow Knows

Later that afternoon, I found myself all dressed up with nothing to do. Danni was busy with her fellow welterweights, winding down yet another tutoring session for Cobie and Anna. I was dressed for action and mostly recovered from a grueling encounter with Torrie Wilson. I was becoming a little disappointed in myself. Recovery was something that was getting harder and harder the older I got. I knew that was natural, but Torrie and I are nearly the exact same age and she was clearly having a lot less trouble with it than I was.

Not good.

In an effort to speed things along, I stood off to one side and worked through a series of stretches. It didn't take long for me to realize I was kidding myself, so I settled into a pattern of absently looking over the training area. Seeing things without making any note of them. My eyes clicking back to where Danni was after each circuit. I didn't really know why it kept happening. I tried to put my focus elsewhere and succeeded in small intervals, but whenever I let my attention drift my stare found its way back to her.

"How are things going?" Emma asked from behind me.

I had been so lost in thought, the sound of her voice made me jump.

"Jesus, Emma!" I said as I worked to temper the little adrenaline shot. "Wear a bell or something, would ya?"

"What's wrong with you?" she said. "It's not like I jumped out of a closet."

"I know," I sighed. "I was just... thinking."

"Mmm-hmm," she hummed knowingly. "Thinking about what?"

Apparently my shadow had been watching me again.

"I was thinking about all the things I don't understand about what I'm thinking."

She stepped up and looked into the training area with me.

"It upsets me a little that I understood what that meant," she said.

"Kinda bothers me, too," I agreed.

We stood and watched for a while. Again, I found myself looking in Danni's direction. And again, Emma noticed.

"You didn't answer my original question," she said.

"Which was?"

"How are things going?"

"Things are good," I said. "I was a little worried at first, but I think she's handling everything very well. I mean, look at her... she looks as comfortable as ever."

In the cage, Danni had pulled Anna aside for another one-on-one conversation. Ms. Kendrick has been one to watch for a while now, and the talk around her has been getting louder. Her most recent performance was her best yet, which only further convinced the prognosticators that this girl was going places. Of course, this is exactly the time when so many budding superstars flame out. When a fighter tends to get a little chip on her shoulder as she begins to believe her own hype. But Anna wasn't showing any signs of that so far. The expression on her face now said she was completely focused on what her stable leader was saying. She knew she still had things to learn, and she trusted Danni to teach them to her.

"And what about outside the cage?" Emma asked.

I sometimes wonder if Em is this meddlesome with other people. It would almost seem impossible.

"Don't you have some training to do?" I asked. "Shouldn't you be busy trading punches with your girlfriend, or something?"

"She's working with Shelby," she said. "Now stop trying to change the subject."

I slid my eyes her way with an annoyed look on my face. She knew from past experience that the look was there, so she felt no need to turn and confirm it now.

I don't particularly like Emma nosing into my life, but I've come to realize I need it from time to time. So, I don't know why I keep putting on this act. As with Danni, I guess I just like to make sure I meet everyone's expectations.

"I don't really know how things are," I admitted.

"Trouble?" she asked.

"Not particularly. I just... I feel like I don't know what I'm doing."

Her trademark smirk appeared.

"You probably shouldn't say stuff like that to me."

"Yeah, I know," I sighed.

I've figured out that Emma is something of a professional at this. She seems to sense when she needs to pry and when to just wait me out. She opted for the latter this time, and it didn't take long.

"Everything between us is good. We spend almost all our time together, and we seem to enjoy it. I know I do, for sure. But, I don't know... something is missing."

Emma's head bobbed lightly as she considered what I'd said.

"Have you kissed her?"

Beyond being nosy, she is also direct.

Though the technically correct answer was yes, I knew that wasn't what she was asking.

Had I kissed her? Had I touched her? Had I done the things that would make us lovers instead of good friends?

I was certain Danni wanted those things, but I still wasn't sure how I felt. On top of that, I had already said things that made it seem like I was. Telling Danni it didn't matter if everyone knew about us? How stupid was that? Of course, it mattered. How could everyone else know when I didn't know? And the worst part of all was that I didn't even know how to make up my mind.

My silence gave Emma her answer, but, once again, she chose to wait and see what else I had to say. She knew the question would get the gears rolling in my head.

And really, what was I waiting for? There wasn't anyone I knew that was better equipped to help me figure this out.

My eyes scanned the training area until I found Tiffany and Shelby. I thought about the way the relationship between Emma and Tiff had evolved. Though they were now two halves of a whole, they started as nothing more than training partners. The transition happened much more suddenly than it has for Danni and I, but it was also more than a little chaotic. Still, it had worked.

"How did you know?" I asked.

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw her look at me briefly then follow my gaze to where Tiffany was.

"Know what?" she asked.

"That it wasn't just friendship. That there was something more, and that you wanted that something more."

She thought about it for a moment.

"I didn't know exactly," she said.

"Gee, thanks for the help."

"Well, I'm sorry but it doesn't work that way," she said. "You can't know in advance that it's going to work. You have to just take the chance."

"That's not what I meant," I said. "I'm perfectly willing to take the chance. I already have, to some degree, and I've gotten my answer. Being with her makes me happy."

"But?"

"But you asked if I had kissed her, and the answer is no. Not the way you mean. I don't know how to get past that. I don't even know if I want to."

"Have you talked to her about it?"

"No."

"Not talking is how you got yourself in trouble the first time," she said.

"I know, but I don't even know how to bring it up. And I don't think she can answer this for me, anyway. The problem is me, not her."

She stopped and thought some more.

"First of all, it's not a problem," she said. "It's a decision."

"You know what I meant."

"I do, but I also think it sounds like your starting to blame yourself."

"You're starting to sound like a shrink."

"Maybe you need one."

I paused and shot her a look.

"Did you just call me crazy?"

"Wouldn't be the first time."

I grunted my agreement and tried to get the conversation back on track.

"This time, you're the one who didn't answer the question," I pointed out.

Emma kept her eyes on Tiffany as she formulated her response.

"I understand what you're going through," she said. "I can't say exactly when or how it happened. But I can tell you this relationship didn't develop like any I'd had before. It was almost the reverse, actually. The physical attraction... that's usually where it all starts. You see a guy. You talk to him. Decide to go on a date. Like that."

I stayed quiet. Already seeing where this was going, and thinking about how much sense she was making.

"With Tiff... we were basically two strangers thrown in a cage," she continued. "It didn't take us long to gain some respect for each other, but we didn't have any idea where things were headed. I didn't, anyway. I just got to know her and, the more I knew, the more I wanted to know. And I guess that's what makes it strange. I didn't fall in love with someone I was physically attracted to. I became attracted to her BECAUSE I was in love with her."

"But how did you know?" I asked. "I feel like Danni's waiting on me, but... I just don't feel it."

"Well, I think the first thing you need to do is stop trying. I guarantee that Danni will wait as long as she has to. So the pressure you're feeling is coming from you."

"How do you know she'll wait?"

She gave a little sigh of frustration as she tried to decide how to word things.

"Do you remember when your parents used to tell you there were things you couldn't understand until you were older?"

"Ohhh," I moaned. "I can't wait to hear where this is going."

"Shush," she said. "This is hard enough to explain as it is."

I wanted to hear what she had to say, so I fought the urge to make another wiseass comment. It's a temptation that's not easy to resist with Emma, but I managed.

"That's what this is like," she continued. "You can't understand how you'll know until you feel it. But when you feel it, you'll know."

"So, basically, I'm still as lost as I was when this conversation started. Thanks, Em. You've been a huge help."

"Just be open to it. As long as you're willing to let it happen, it will."

None of this was making me feel any better, and that fact showed clearly on my face.

"Trust me," she said, trying to reassure me. "You're going to look back on this and realize that I know everything."

I thought about that for only a second, then shook my head firmly.

"Just so we're clear, there is zero chance that's going to happen."

"You'll see."
