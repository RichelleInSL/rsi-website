RSI Story #16Haunted By The Past

Author's Note: The Spenser, Hawk and Susan Silverman characters belong to mystery novelist Robert B. Parker. Mr. Parker is one of my favorite authors, and his work is a major influence on the the way I write. Whether I even come close to mimicking his style is open to considerable debate, but I have chosen to use his characters in this story as something of an homage. My goal is to handle them as lightly as possible and to maintain them as Mr. Parker created them. Any failure on that front is completely my own.

-18-Home Movies

The first sign of trouble came when I arrived the next morning to find the building locked and empty. Danni usually gets in earlier than I do, simply because geography gives her a head start. But the difference between her travel time and mine is less than twenty minutes, and it doesn't take much to push her arrival time past my own. So, I told myself it was probably nothing.

She arrived about a half-hour later, with Hawk in tow. Just from her appearance, it was clear it was definitely something.

Her hair was pulled back in a loose, messy ponytail gathered high on her head. Her outfit consisted of basic shades of grey and black — no doubt selected deliberately to make matching nearly automatic — and it looked like it had been put on by a blind person. Her blouse was loose and slightly askew, as if she'd managed to get it on, but couldn't muster the energy to straighten it out. Her eyelids were permanently stuck at half-mast, and she was walking like a zombie — her right arm bent up in front of her, coffee cup in hand.

I've seen Danni in a variety of different states. Formal, casual, jeans and sneakers... whatever. Aside from the sweaty, spent look she works hard to earn in the cage, she is the mistress of effortless perfection.

Now, she literally looked like something the wind blew in.

"Whaa-at the hell happened to you? I asked.

She stood in front of my guest chair and let gravity do the rest... dropping like a rag doll, legs and arms flopping independently.

Not a drop of coffee spilled, though.

"Something is wrong with my alarm system," she said. "It must have gone off six times, last night."

My antennae went up immediately.

"Is everything alright?" I asked urgently.

She shrugged.

"Hawk checked each time. He didn't find anything."

I looked to Hawk.

"No one tried to get into that house last night," he said definitively.

"Then what happened?" I asked.

"I don't know," he said. "The alarm company is coming out today. Spenser will be there."

I'm not sure if Danni noticed, but Hawk's accent had disappeared.

I noticed, and I eyed him warily because I knew what the change meant.

"I'm sure it was just a problem with the system," Danni said dismissively.

I kept my eyes on Hawk... waiting to hear his take.

"We'll know more later," he said. "Until then, we will be more cautious."

"You follow me everywhere I go," Danni protested. "How much more cautious can we get?"

Neither Hawk nor I so much as cracked a smile.

Hawk's job was to protect Danni, and he always takes his job seriously.

As for me, after days of a whole lot of nothing, something had now happened that brought all of the fears I'd tamped down rushing back to just under the surface.

I held on Hawk for another beat, then stood and picked up a pile of miscellaneous paperwork from the left side of my desk. I carried it to the other side of my chair and dropped it unceremoniously to the floor.

I turned and met Danni's confused expression.

"That's your spot for today," I said, nodding toward the newly emptied end of the desk.

Her eyebrow went up.

"Are you kidding?"

"No," I said as I nudged my laptop to the right to create a little more space.

Danni turned to Hawk, then back to me.

"You don't think you're being a little dramatic?" she asked.

"Probably," I said, "but do me a favor and humor me."

She continued to eye me as if I were a little bit deranged.

Well, more than usual, I suppose.

"I think you're nuts," she said finally, "but okay."

She got herself squared away pretty quickly. My desk is open on both sides, so we were able to gain space by sitting in opposite directions. Before long, we were both busy with our own work, and the strangeness of the situation faded away.

Hawk, meanwhile, had commandeered one of my guest chairs and set it just outside my office door. He then split his time between the chair and his favorite position holding up my wall.

An hour or so later, Steph came by with the injury reports and training schedules for the upcoming event. She never said a word about the seating arrangement or Hawk's presence. Just handed over the reports and assumed her spot against the bookcase.

Danni and I each grabbed a report and looked it over. After a moment or so, I became acutely aware of Hawk standing silent and emotionless against one wall as Steph seemed quietly disinterested against the other. My eyes traveled back and forth between them several times as I absorbed the similarities.

Finally, I couldn't stand it anymore.

I caught Danni's attention, then nodded toward the the other two.

She looked over one shoulder at Hawk then observed Steph over the other. She didn't immediately pick up on my meaning, so she shot me a confused looked. I nodded again, and she turned around completely and took a harder look. This time it clicked.

She did the same repeated back and forth I had done a few times, then spun back to me.

"I honestly don't know what to say."

"I don't think words can begin to describe what we have seen," I said.

"Could you two stop fucking around and finish so I can get on with my life?" Steph said.

We finished with the reports. After a few minutes of discussion, Steph headed off to the training area. It was time for Danni's morning session with Amanda, so she followed along. With the two of us separated, Hawk sought out the best spot to defend us both at once — a quest that put him back in the lobby.

Nearly two hours later, Spenser arrived. His expression was gaunt and serious, and he didn't even have a lame wise crack to lob my way.

Translation: He came bearing bad news.

"What is it?" I asked.

He lowered himself into a chair.

"Miss Harris should be here for this."

I could have called the training floor and had Danni sent over, but those repressed worries of mine were right there now... waiting for the chance to grab hold of me. I had no patience for any sort of delay.

"She's training. We can bring her up to speed later. Now, what is it?"

From his pocket, he produced a tiny flash drive and handed it to me without a word.

I plugged it into my computer and took a look at the contents. It contained only a single video file, which I opened.

Hawk made his way into the office just as my media player finished loading.

The footage was dark, and I couldn't immediately tell what I was looking at. After a few moments, I figured out it was an open field. More study revealed a line of trees on the left of the screen and a structure that appeared to be a house — Danni's house, no doubt — on the right.

Putting the pieces together, I realized I was looking at security footage. The camera appeared to be mounted near the front of the house, looking back onto the lawn on the left side.

The video ran for just over a minute and absolutely nothing happened.

"The first alarm occurred at 11:43 PM last night," Spenser said.

Almost the moment he finished his sentence, the video showed a figure emerge from the tree line. I glanced at the time stamp in the lower right corner.

11:43

The figure crossed the strip of lawn quickly and purposefully, his features shrouded in darkness. He went directly to the side window of the house and tried to open it.

Immediately, the lawn was bathed in light, and for just an instant, the figure was clearly visible. He swiftly scurried back into the tree line and disappeared.

There was no sound on the video, but it was obvious that Danni's alarm system had been tripped at the same time the lights snapped on. Within thirty seconds, Hawk strode into the frame, gun in hand. He surveyed the scene, then disappeared behind the house. Within a minute, he was back, moving more deliberately this time. He stopped and looked at the window for a moment, then continued on.

Hawk spoke up and answered my question before I asked it.

"I didn't see anything because there was nothing to see," he said. "No markings or signs that anyone had tried to force their way in."

I stayed quiet and kept my eyes on the video. There was a quick cut, and then the scene was dark again. I glanced at the the corner to see that the video had jumped ahead to 1:17 AM.

"The second alarm sounded at 1:18," Spenser said.

Sure enough, the figure reappeared within a minute, and the whole scene played out in an almost identical fashion.

After the action concluded, the video jumped ahead to 3:02. Spenser skipped the narration, because the story was the same. This time, I stopped the video just after the lights snapped on... the figure clearly visible.

Height wise, he looked to be about five-nine or five-ten. He wasn't overweight, but he was thick and broad, which put his weight around two twenty-five or so. In his sneakers, jeans and dark winter coat, he was not dressed for burglary. I skipped ahead a few frames to get a good look at his face. No stranger to a computer, I knew how to use the video software to magnify the image. Clearly, Danni hadn't skimped on her security, as the quality of the video was excellent. His eyes — small and dark — were clearly visible. His face, too, seemed small — much too narrow for his broad frame. His features looked compressed and pinched in toward his nose. His lips were pressed tightly together, which further accentuated the look.

Give him some whiskers, and he'd have looked like a giant rat.

"Who is he?" I asked darkly.

"I don't have a name," Spenser said, "but I've seen him at the club. When Powers comes and goes, this guy is with him."

My suspicion confirmed, I could feel my emotions condense into a small, cold stone in the pit of my stomach.

"If he wasn't breaking in, what was he doing?" I asked without taking my eyes from the screen.

I was making no effort to disguise the rage that was building in me, and I was sure Spenser and Hawk could hear it. They didn't seem to know quite what to do with it, though.

"We think setting off the alarm was his only goal," Spenser said.

I nodded once, slowly and deliberately.

There were only two reasons I could think of for such behavior. Either Rat Face was an adolescent moron who thought he was cool, or he was trying to intimidate the homeowner. And I didn't have to think too long to decide which motivation I believed was at play, in this case.

King was fucking with Danni. The one thing I had been most concerned with. The one thing I simply would not allow.

I closed the video and snatched the drive from the front of the machine. I yanked open my desk drawer, threw the tiny device in and slammed it shut again.

Then I stood and pulled my jacket and purse from the coat stand.

"Where are you going?" Spenser asked.

"Trenton," I said as I stormed out of the office.

"Hey, wait a second," he called after me.

I ignored him and kept walking toward the front door. The patience I'd shown in responding to Steph the previous day was gone. She had been right. It was time to go confront the problem.

And maybe snap it's slimy little neck while I was at it.

"Richelle, STOP!" Spenser said, as he and Hawk emerged from the office behind me.

I turned and looked at him.

"Why?" I asked angrily.

He gave me a disbelieving look.

"You don't think maybe this is a bad idea?"

"No," I said. "We want to know what Powers is up to. I'm going to go ask him."

"And you think he's just going to tell you?"

"Can't hurt to try."

"Actually, I think it can. There's a good chance he wants you dead."

"If he wanted to kill me, he would have done it by now."

"What's going on?" Danni asked as she emerged from the training area — hair still damp from her post-workout shower.

Spenser looked at me and I thrust my hand out disgustedly.

"Go ahead. Tell her."

He recounted for her what the video had shown. She listened silently, and I watched the sad reality weigh her down, bit by bit.

Each bit served as fuel for the flames of my anger.

When the story finished, Danni patiently and methodically digested what she'd just heard. Then, her eyes narrowed on me.

"Where the hell are you going?" she asked.

"To see an old friend," I said.

She nodded lightly, and I readied myself for the expected argument. Which is why I was so unprepared when she didn't protest.

"I'm coming with you," she said.

People talk about waiting for the other shoe to drop. I didn't have to wait, because the shoe had just hit me right between the eyes.

Just a few minutes ago, Spenser had been trying to impress upon me the reasons my plan was a bad one. Now, I had to impress those reasons and more upon Danni, all while maintaining my original position for myself.

Needless to say, I didn't have much success.

"Not a chance in hell," I said, "Powers sent a man to terrorize you. Now you're going to just walk in and offer yourself to him?"

"Isn't this the same man who wants you dead?" she asked. "You don't seem to have a problem walking in there."

"If he was going to kill me, he'd have done it already."

"Ah, yes. It must be wonderful to know everything, the way you do."

"Fuck off, Danni."

"Kiss my ass," she said. "By the way, since you're so well informed, then tell me what he does want if he's not here to kill you?"

"I don't know yet," I said. "That's what I intend to find out."

"And you think he's just going to tell you? If that's the case, then what the fuck have we been waiting for? You should have dropped in on him weeks ago."

"I already had this lecture from him," I said, pointing to Spenser, who was strangely silent as he and Hawk took in the festivities.

"Oh, I see," Danni said, "and did he tell you you were a fucking idiot, too?"

"I don't have to take this shit, and I don't have to explain myself," I said.

"You don't think maybe getting you to walk right into his lap might be part of his plan?" she continued.

"Look," I shouted, "I'm going and you're not, and I don't give a fuck if you like it!"

She stared defiantly at me, but said nothing.

"As for you two," I said to the men, "feel free to come, or don't. But, if you want to stop me, you're going to have to shoot me."

I spun on my heel, steamed out the door and got in my car. Danni was the first one out behind me, and I could see her standing in front of the building as I backed out. She made no effort to stop me... just stood there with her hands on her hips and watched me drive away.
