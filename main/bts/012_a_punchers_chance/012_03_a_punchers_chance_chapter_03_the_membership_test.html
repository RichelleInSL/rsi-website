RSI Story #12A Puncher's Chance

-3-The Membership Test

It was late as I made my way into the women's locker room at RSI. Yes, it's necessary for me to specify the women's locker room, because there is a small men's locker room across the hall. When I was laying out the plans for the facility, I felt it just made sense to put one in. In the years since then, no one but the cleaning crew has set foot in there.

That makes sense, right?

The crowd in the locker room was thin, but the subject of my interest was still there, sitting sullenly in front of her locker.

"Hey there," I said.

"Oh shit," Brittany Tacy replied. "Was I that bad?"

"What do you mean?"

"I get my ass kicked, and now I get a visit from the boss?"

I shook my head.

"I'm not here because you lost."

"Oh? Then, why?"

"I just wanted to see how you were doing."

"That's funny," she said sharply. "You've never checked up on me before."

I blew out a sigh.

"Fine. I'm checking up on you because of the fight, okay?"

"Yeah, well... I'm great, thanks for asking."

10... 9... 8... 7...

"Let's try this again. You got got your ass kicked a few days ago, and I'm here to see how you're handling it."

She started to speak, but I stopped her.

"I know you're in a shitty mood. I get it. But, if you don't drop the attitude, you're going to get your ass kicked again right here in this room."

She gave me a hard look as she considered her options.

I was pretty familiar with what happens when a fighter gets this kind of angry. I had been there many times, myself. The desire to lash out and hit something... or someone... can be pretty strong. By pushing her this way, I had basically volunteered to become her target.

On the other hand, I had shown in the past that I could back up my threat. So, lashing out at me would have some consequences attached.

While she mulled it over, I decided to make her decision a little easier and soften the blow.

"I didn't come here to get in a fight with you," I said. "I saw what happened, and I know how it feels. So..."

"You wanted Steph to stop it," she said, cutting me off.

I had expected that. The moment I tried to paint myself as a kindred spirit, she slapped me down with the fact that I had broken the unwritten rule. Clear proof in her mind that I was not a legitimate member of the club.

"I did," I said plainly.

"So then you don't really know."

"Don't hand me that bullshit," I said. "I've been through it, and I DO know. But, I also remember I was pretty stupid back then. Too stupid to know when I was beaten."

My choice of words gave the impression I wasn't stupid, anymore. I know a few people who might take issue with that.

In fact, if this were a movie, this is the point where Danni would walk into the room and make just such a comment. I even went so far as to take a quick glance around, but there was no sign of her.

I took a moment to lament the lost opportunity.

"I knew how bad it looked," Brittany said, "but it only takes one punch."

I couldn't help but smile.

"The 'puncher's chance,'" I said wistfully. "I told myself that same thing quite a few times."

"There's a reason they call it that."

"Yeah," I said, "it's meant to describe the last desperate hope of someone who's doomed to fail."

She laughed a little... reality chipping away at her veil of obstinacy.

"That's not a very hopeful way to look at it."

"Shocking, I know. Not at all in tune with normal jaunty personality."

That drew another laugh from the blonde fitness beauty.

The chances to mock me were coming fast and furious, now. Danni was really missing out.

"What about Steph?" Brittany asked as her smile faded.

"We're fine," I said.

"You were giving it to her pretty good."

I shrugged.

"I was pissed, and I took it out on her. I probably shouldn't have, but she's done it to me, too. It happens. We're past it."

"You let Steph yell at you?"

I smiled again as I tried to imagine what my other options might be. I don't think anyone 'lets' Steph do anything. She either does, or she she does not. Then, you decide if you can live with it. On it's face, that might not sound very appealing. But, I've found Steph's decisions are never selfish or arbitrary. Call her principled. Call her obstinate. Or, just do what I do and call her a pain in the ass. Whatever your preference, it doesn't change the fact that the reasons behind her decisions are always good.

In other words, I decided long ago that I can live with it.

"If you have any suggestions as to how I could stop her, I'd love to hear them," I joked.

Brittany nodded as she saw my dilemma.

"I could fire her," I continued, "but that feels a little like shooting myself in the foot."

"We'd be lost without her."

I nodded.

"I trust Steph. I count on her. And, I know I put her in an impossible situation the other night."

"Then, why do it?"

I paused a moment to consider my answer.

"I know what it feels like... that promise you make to yourself to never give up. Every one of you does it. I get it, but I hate it, at the same time. Since I know you won't watch out for yourselves, I sometimes feel like it's my job to do it for you."

"Did you have someone watching out for you?"

"No."

"Then, why?"

"Because maybe I should have. Maybe things would have been better if I did have someone keeping an eye on me."

That wasn't the answer she expected, and she really didn't know what to say after that. So, we just dropped it and moved on.

"You got someplace to be?" I asked her.

"Not really."

"Feel like putting the gloves back on?"

"With you?"

I shrugged.

"I'm sure we can find some way to pass the time."

"Okay," she answered, a little too eagerly.

No matter the profession, there's always something appealing about the idea of punching your boss in the face.

"Just let me get changed," I said.
