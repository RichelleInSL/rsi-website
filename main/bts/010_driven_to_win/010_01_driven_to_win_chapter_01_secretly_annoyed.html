-1-Secretly Annoyed

Thump, thump!

I hit the bag with a quick one-two. Brought my guard back up and kept moving... dancing to my right. Doubled-up on the jab, stepped back and unloaded a middle round kick that caused the bag to fold in half around my leg. I got my feet under me and moved in for the kill. Hooking my hands around the top of the bag as if it were an opponent's neck, I drove my right knee into the center of the synthetic leather surface. One... Two...

Suddenly, everything went black.

"Hey!" I called out. "I'm still in here!"

The lights snapped back on. I reset my stance and started hitting the bag again. A moment later, Richelle Winterfeld's head popped out from behind the unused bag to my left.

She spread her hands out wide. "Emma?" she asked. "What the hell are you still doing here?"

I pivoted and put my weight into a crisp right that thudded heavily into the bag. Then, I stopped and came out of my stance.

I had been at it for a while, and my legs burned from the constant bob and weave movement so crucial to staying alive in the cage. My arms were heavy with fatigue... a fatigue that weighed on my whole body. I rolled my shoulders, unconsciously trying to loosen the tight, exhausted muscles. Every inch of me was slicked with sweat... I could feel it running down my face and dripping from my chin.

It had been a good workout.

I had draped a towel over the support bar of the bag stand, expecting I would need it when I was done. I grabbed it and mopped my face.

"What does it look like I'm doing?" I said.

Richelle narrowed her eyes. "You know, your little smartass routine is very different from the image you project for the public."

"I don't have to worry about that here, right?" I said with a smirk. "I believe you told me I could feel safe here."

Richelle nodded. "Yeah... safe from prying reporters and withering public opinion," she answered. "But you're not safe from me slapping you upside your pretty little head."

"I'll take my chances," I said.

She didn't reply, but she gave me a look that said I was going to run out of chances, one of these days.

"So, what gives?" she asked.

"Just trying to get a little extra work in," I said.

"A little?" she said as she looked down at her watch. "Everybody else has been gone for hours."

That caught me by surprise. "What time is it?"

"Almost seven-thirty."

I shook my head. "I'm sorry. I hadn't realized it had been that long," I said. "I'll go shower up and get dressed."

Richelle looked a little annoyed. She would have to wait for me to finish before she could lock up. But, she let it pass quickly.

"Okay," she said, "just give a knock before you go."

I cleaned up as quickly as I could, and it was about a half-hour later when I stopped into Richelle's office.

"I'm outta here," I said. "Sorry again for making you wait."

"That's alright," she said, "but I still don't understand. Why are you here?"

"Just trying to get a little extra work in."

"Yeah, you said that already. I'm asking you why."

I shrugged. "Just felt like I needed it."

"Right," she said, "because your recent results show you've clearly lost your edge."

I shrugged again. She slipped her coat on, and we made our way out to the parking lot together.

"Emma, c'mon," she said. "You're one of the best there is. There isn't anyone you can't beat."

"There's one," I chirped.

Richelle raised an eyebrow. "Who?"

I didn't say anything, sorry that I'd opened my mouth in the first place. But, Richelle knew what my next event was. It didn't take her long to figure it out.

"Kristen Stewart," she said.

I gave another shrug. She correctly took that as confirmation.

"Are you serious?" she said. "She's two weight classes above you. Of course you're going to struggle against her."

"Doesn't seem to be a problem for other people," I said.

"Ah, geez," she said. "I hate this Open Fight Night crap. You know I do."

"That's great," I said, "but I still have to fight."

Richelle stopped for a moment, then nodded.

"You're right," she agreed. "You're absolutely right. Sometimes, I look at things a little too 'big picture.' But I've been the one taking the punishment. I should know better."

"Don't worry about it," I said. "I understand."

"So, what's your plan?" she asked as she pushed the front door open.

"I don't know," I said. "I just... I need to beat her, you know?"

She latched the door, removed the key and gave a firm tug. The door didn't move. Satisfied, she slipped her keys into her coat pocket, and we walked on.

"I didn't realize you hated Kristen that much."

"I don't hate her," I said. "I don't have any problem with her, really. I just..."

We stopped beside her car.

"You know how I feel about you. I expect the best from you, and I always get it. But, it's not like you never lose."

"She doesn't just beat me, Richelle."

I looked her straight in the eye as I gave voice to what was really bothering me.

"She kicks my ass," I said. "Nobody does that to me. Ever."

Richelle smiled. "My little perfectionist," she said.

"Kiss my ass," I said.

Richelle kept smiling, but I could see in her face she knew what I was feeling.

"Well, standing around hitting that bag isn't going to make you better," she said.

"It's all I've got," I said. "I get my work in with Tiff and Steph during the day. But, once everybody else is gone... I can't just stop. I'm not going to feel comfortable unless I'm putting everything I have into this."

She nodded. "Go home. Get some rest. We'll talk about it tomorrow."

I nodded back. "Okay," I said.

It felt good to know she understood me, but I really didn't know what there was to talk about. What kind of training could I do when I was in the gym all alone?
