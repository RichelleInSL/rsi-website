-1-So Easily Lost

Life is good, I thought to myself as I slipped a powder blue cotton sweater over my head and stepped into the bathroom.

Rampage Sports, Incorporated - the women's MMA stable I own and try desperately not to screw up - was humming along on a level I never could have imagined. That's not just my feeling. It is now a proven fact. The most recent league tournament started with two hundred and fifty-six fighters, divided evenly into eight weight classes, with each weight class producing a single champion.

There are over thirty stables in the league. Many of the them much larger than Rampage. And yet, we produced five of the eight champions.

How did we achieve such a stunning result? Well, the truth is, we're not entirely sure. There's been a lot going on these past months. Veterans in crisis. Rookies going astray. Things that knocked us out of our normal day-to-day groove and left us feeling like we could tip into a full-blown disaster at any moment.

Instead, it all led to a whole new way of doing things. One that isn't quite as organized or strategic, but somehow feels more intrinsic and natural.

One of the things I've always understood is that, for this stable thing to work, it has to be about more than just fighting. I came from a background where the only person I could rely on was me. I made it workWith so much going on, the established leadership didn't hold... and yet, the group kept on. Without ever being asked, others stepped up to fill the void. All throughout the stable, Hard work and preparation are obviously still major components, and opponent-specific game-planning remains essential. But the glue that holds it all together is more about relationships and camaraderie than training plans and rigid routine. The women here have become a kind of family, and they've figured out how to be there for each other.

It sounds a little weird, but the results... well, they speak for themselves. And the fact is that it doesn't look all that strange when you watch it happen. Established champions are pushing each other to become better. Rookies are hooking up with veterans to tap into their experience and learn new techniques... and the seasoned fighters are even learning a thing or two from their younger counterparts. When it comes down to it, everything is working about as well as I could hope.

The thing is, not knowing exactly why it's all working so well leaves me wondering what we need to do to keep it going.

I have, however, identified one key factor.

"I don't understand," Danneel Harris said defensively. "Why do you need to take over my duties?"

I stood in front of the mirror and pulled a brush through my auburn hair.

"I'm not taking over anything," I said. "I'm simply lightening your load a little."

"You're not happy with the way I'm doing things?"

I gathered my hair in a high ponytail and stepped back out into the bedroom.

"That may be the dumbest thing I've ever heard come out of your mouth," I said nonchalantly. "How long has it been now? You don't think I'd have said something by now if I had a problem?"

Danni has been the Stable Leader of Rampage Sports since the day I opened the doors, and I've never regretted that decision for an instant. She is everything I could have hoped for, and then some. She's genuinely invested in giving each fighter exactly what she needs to reach whatever goal is next. Committed and determined in the gym, she sets the standard for the rest of the group. Oh, and it probably doesn't hurt that she is supremely talented in the cage. She was a long-reigning champion when I hired her, and she's only gotten better since.

Exactly how good is she? Well, it's a big league with a lot of divisions, and determining the pound-for-pound queen is a never-ending debate. But Danni's name is sure to come up in every conversation.

To say I was happy with her performance - both as a fighter and a leader - would be the mother of all understatements. The fact is, I don't have any clue how I got this lucky or where we would be without her.

"Then what's the problem?" she asked.

"There's no problem," I said. "I want to free you up to spend more time on the training floor. That's all."

"And why do I need to do that?"

"Because you are the straw that stirs the drink, my dear."

She stepped over to the full length mirror, tugging at her blouse to adjust the fit.

"Wow," she said, "what a load of crap."

I didn't argue because it was no use. Another thing to know about Danni is that she is humble to a level that is almost beyond human understanding. She treats everyone as an equal. From the greenest of rookies to the most decorated of champions, they all get the same level of respect from her - provided they respect her, as well. And nothing about it is fake or forced. I've come to believe that she really doesn't comprehend how good she is.

I pressed up close behind her, wrapping my arms around her waist as I put my chin on her shoulder.

"Neither of us really knows what happened at the tournament," I said. "All we know is how it turned out."

"We already talked about this," she said. "What happened in Australia was special. If you think we can somehow recreate or recapture that, you're nuts."

"I know, and I'm not looking to bottle up any kind of secret formula. But we both know the reason things were so different last time."

She narrowed her eyes at me in the mirror.

"Oh, no, no," she said. "I don't need to be anyone's pet project, anymore. I'm good now, thanks."

Those veterans in crisis I mentioned? Well, Danni's name was at the head of that list. A rare loss had put everything she'd worked for in jeopardy. Because it wasn't just a loss. It was a disaster in which she was absolutely mauled by Cheryl Cole.

The reasons behind the poor performance are complicated, but the fact was that Danni wasn't ready for that fight... and I bear a lot of the responsibility for that. But none of that really matters. Fans and pundits don't talk about fighters' personal lives because they rightly don't care. That's not what they're here for. What matters to them is what they see in the cage, and on that night they saw Danni take a vicious, one-sided beating from one of her most talented rivals. Suddenly, a fighter who many counted among the best in the league was facing hard questions from every direction. What was she going to do now? Could she come back after such a terrible failure? Was she still good enough to even try, or was this the beginning of the end for her?

In typical fashion, Danni had very little to say. She admitted Cole had been clearly better, and she offered no excuses for her rare letdown. Meanwhile, Cheryl simply couldn't seem to keep her mouth shut. If there was a microphone around she would find it, and she was always ready with a juicy quote to share regarding the 'former champion.' The torment seemed to drag on and on... mostly because Cole refused to let it die.

But through it all, the only question the women of RSI asked was what could they do to help? Danni had done so much for each of them over the years, and now they stood ready to do whatever was needed. She didn't make it easy for them, but her teammates refused to be denied... and they quickly discovered the way to help Danni was to use her generous nature against her. Suddenly, everybody needed Danni's help. Everyone was eager for a slice of her time. And once they got their hooks in, they turned it all around on her. Using the sessions to ignite a little friendly competition. To push her and challenge her and hit her with everything they had. Doing whatever they could to sharpen her and make sure she was ready.

The end result? An unbelievable tournament run that included a sound thrashing of Cheryl Cole in the championship round. A complete victory that has left many wondering

There has been no further talk of Danni's end since then. There has also been precious little comment from Cheryl Cole. But there has been plenty of talk

Mission accomplished.

The funniest part is that Danni had to know what her teammates were doing. Probably knew it from the very start. But she let it continue. Putting up with it for the simplest of reasons.

Because she loved it.

I hugged her a little tighter.

"You can't lie to me, Harris," I said. "Watson already blew your cover."

"Oh, yeah? How's that?"

"She made me see how rare it is for you to really enjoy yourself on the training floor," I said. "She said you never get to just be 'one of the girls.'"

"Just cause Emma said it..."

"Don't even bother," I cut in. "After she said it, I couldn't help watching you. You knew those girls were messing with you, but you still let them do it. And we both know why you did."

She offered no denial.

"So this is Emma's fault," she said with a sigh.

"Tell me she's wrong."

Again, no answer.

"And there you have it," I concluded.

She shook her head lightly, and a little smile creased her lips. Her way of admitting I'd scored a point in the never-ending tennis match that is our relationship.

I stepped around to face her, with my arms looped around her waist, and...

"I believe your hands are on my ass," she said.

"Indeed, I believe that is correct."

"You need to remove them."

"Why would I do that?" I said. "I mean, it's such a fine ass."

"Be that as it may, I can't have you grabbing my ass while we're arguing.

"First of all, I'm not grabbing anything. I think caressing would be a more appropriate term."

"Uh, huh..."

"And second, I really don't see the need to continue this disagreement. I'm right. You know I'm right. So, let's just agree on that, and move on."

"But, alas... I do not agree."

I raised my face and nibbled her ear.

"Mmmmm... now you're just cheating," she said.

Perhaps I forgot to mention the biggest reason I've been so happy lately.

After years of working side-by-side - years that have taken us through more heartbreak, disaster and triumph than I can say - the two of us have finally wrapped our arms around what everybody else seemed to know all along. Because we are much more than co-workers, and much more than friends. We are in love. Deeply and completely.

I can see now why it was so obvious to everyone. No matter the situation, Danni is the first person I want beside me. I can count on her for anything, and I trust her more than I trust myself. It makes perfect sense that a bond that strong would evolve into a relationship like this. But that didn't stop us from fighting it all the way. And when I say us, I mean me. Jesus... what an ass I was.

The fact that it all worked out might be the greatest proof of what we have together. I wasn't able to screw it up, no matter how hard I tried. It was almost like the universe wouldn't let me.

She turned to face me, and we kissed. I brought a hand up behind her head and pulled her into it. She went with it just long enough to rouse that familiar hunger in my chest. Then she gently forced me back.

"We have to get to work," she said.

I didn't want to stop. My body ached for more of her. But she was right, and I knew it. It wasn't just about maybe being late. If we gave in to this, we were unlikely to ever get out the door.

I kissed her again.

"To be continued?" I asked.

"Count on it," she smiled.

Like I said, life is good.

* * *

We managed to keep our hands off each other long enough to get going, but it also allowed us to resume the playful bickering.

I tossed the ball at her and missed by a mile. It glanced off the back of her chair and headed toward the open door. Then, just as I thought I was going to have to get up and retrieve it, it was ricocheted off Fighter Development Coordinator Steph McMahon's leg as she walked into the office.

"Oooooohhh... kick save, McMahon!" I shouted.

She had no reaction. I turned to Danni.

"She doesn't seem impressed by my limited hockey knowledge."

"Or any other area of your limited knowledge, for that matter."

"OK, settle down there, Harris," I said. "Your ass may be fine, but I will still kick it."

She didn't ignored it completely, and the look on her face sobered the two of us up immediately.

"What's wrong?" I asked.

She let out a deep sigh. Like she already regretted what she was going to say.

"Demi's no-call, no-show."

I shared a worried look with Danni, then we both looked back at Steph. Because we had all been down this road before.

"You tried calling?" Danni asked.

Steph nodded.

"Emily, Tiz, Aly... nobody knows anything?"

As the best member of our lightweight group, Emily served as a mentor and frequent sparring partner for Demi. Tiz and Aly were the RSI athletes she was closest with... probably because they'd all followed a similar path to get where they were in life.

Steph shook her head solemnly.

We looked at each other some more. All of us wondering if this would end the same as it had before, or would it finally be something worse?

"I'll call her agent," I sighed.

"I'll start checking the media sites," Danni said.

She headed over to her own office, while Steph went back to the business of getting the women of RSI ready for battle.

I hadn't even dug up the agent's number before Danni was back.

"Check your email," she said.

I checked and found a message from her. The body contained nothing but a link to an article on the TMZ website.

A cesspool of ugly rumor and tawdry celebrity gossip, the site is roundly despised around here... but the information is usually reliable.

I clicked the link and the headline appeared in huge, bold type.

Demi Lovato Suffers Drug Overdose, 'Awake and Responsive' in Hospital

I scanned through the article. The details registered as a jumble of misery.

Police were called... treated with Narcan... still unconscious when paramedics arrived... love, prayers and support...

"Son of a bitch," I mumbled.

I was upset and angry... and yet I couldn't help feeling a sense of relief. This was definitely worse than the usual, but still not the worst outcome you imagine when it comes to addiction.

I sat back and stared absently at my desk... thinking the same illogical thoughts everyone thinks in a moment like this.

God dammit, Demi... how could you do this?

Like coming within inches of dying was something she'd planned when she got up yesterday.

Danni's voice broke into my reverie.

"What do you want to do?"

Do? I thought. She's an addict. There's nothing anyone can do.

"She's on the card in two weeks," Danni clarified.

I dropped my head into my hands and rubbed my eyes with my palms. Demi's RSI schedule seemed trivial given what had happened, but at least it was something we could do. And the sooner we did it, the quieter we could keep it.

"Pull her," I said.

"That it?" she asked.

I looked up at her. I understood exactly what she was asking. Wondering whether we should go the next step and take Demi off the active roster entirely.

"For now," I said.

We shared a long look. Both of us knew I was only delaying the inevitable. Demi was going to have to be suspended. The road back from this was going to be long, physically and emotionally. It would probably be months before she was ready to even think about returning to training. Who knows when she would be in any shape to actually fight?

But neither of us said another word. Mired in a shared sadness we couldn't do anything about.

She eventually went to make her calls while I went back to looking up the agent's number. He would need to be kept in the loop about what we were doing, even if it was just a formality.

After a moment or two, I stopped and stared at the desk some more.

It was amazing how my seemingly unassailable good mood had been so quickly and easily lost.
